> **Summary (Haghighi-Talab & Wright, J. Appl. Probability 10(3):556–571, 1973).** Exact distributional results for **lower records** (successive new minima) in a finite i.i.d. sequence, by rank-permutation methods; particular attention to the **record run** = the stretch from a record value up to the next record (or the sequence end). The authors give its distribution, closely related to inter-record times, and apply it directly to **road traffic leaving a signal** (platoon persistence before a new speed record is hit).
>
> **Why it matters for this problem.** In a no-overtaking single lane, faster vehicles behind a slow "leader" form a platoon; the **platoon leaders are precisely the right-to-left record minima of the desired speeds v_i**, and each platoon = one record run. This paper (with Chandler 1952 and Foster–Stuart 1954 among its refs) is the classical probabilistic treatment of those record runs — the exact combinatorics that enter the pure-race (no-finish) bumper/convoy partition. The per-run/leader structure (one leader per record minimum) matches the convex-minorant/random-permutation-cycle picture from the ballistic-aggregation arxiv note. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/3212776 . ## Abstract

Probability distributions relating to the number and positions of lower records in a finite sequence of observations are obtained by methods involving the permutation of ranks. Some new results, and results of earlier authors, are expressed in a form thought to be useful for direct practical applications.

Expressions are also given for the distribution of the length of a ‘record run’, which consists of the observations following and including a given record either up to the next record, or, if a further record does not occur, up to the end of the sequence of observations. This quantity, whose distribution is closely related to that of the inter-record time, has a direct and simple application to road traffic leaving a signal which has been confirmed by experimental observation.

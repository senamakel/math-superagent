## What it claims

A **bumps race**is a form of [rowing][5] race in which a number of boats chase each other in single file, each crew attempting to catch and 'bump' the boat in front without being caught by the boat behind.

The form is mainly used in [intercollegiate][6] competitions at the [University of Oxford][7] since 1815, and at the [University of Cambridge][8] since 1827. [1]

Bumps racing in eights is also found in the United Hospitals Boat Club in London between the five London medical schools and Royal Veterinary College.

Bumps racing in fours is also the format of inter-house rowing at [Eton College][9] and [Shrewsbury School][10]. It is particularly suitable where the stretch of water available is long but narrow, precluding side-by-side racing. Bumps racing gives a sharper feel of immediate competition than a [head race][11], where boats are simply timed over a fixed course. Few [*[quantify][12]*] rowers worldwide use rivers as narrow as the [Cam][13] or the [Isis][14], [*[citation needed][15]*] but bumps races are also contested elsewhere.

# Stanley, "Hyperplane arrangements, interval orders, and trees" (PNAS 1996) — summary

- Source: Richard P. Stanley, *Hyperplane arrangements, interval orders, and trees*, Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 93(6):2620–2625 (1996). URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC39847/ (full text: research/sources/stanley_hyperplane_interval_orders_trees_pnas1996.full.md)
- Content: encyclopedic survey of the braid arrangement A_n (hyperplanes x_i − x_j = 1, 1 ≤ i < j ≤ n) and its deformations, with the classical region-count formulas and their tree/interval-order interpretations. Quotes Shi's theorem that a certain deformation of the braid arrangement has (n+1)^{n−1} regions (the Shi arrangement); discusses Linial's arrangement (regions = alternating/intransitive trees of Gelfand–Graev–Postnikov); counts labeled interval orders from n generic intervals; refines labeling trees by inversion number. This is the standard reference that fixes the notation and the names (Shi, Linial, Catalan) for deformations of the braid arrangement.
- Bearing on PE597: the run's parity cells are regions of an arrangement whose hyperplanes are inverse-speed equalities (finish ties and bump-vs-finish ties), NOT pure x_i−x_j ∈ ℤ. Stanley's survey — together with Postnikov–Stanley (Alternating trees) and Bernardi (trees bijection for transitive deformations) — establishes the standard region-count formulas, and the run's own L-independent cell counts (n=3 → 32, n=4 → 1202) match none of them. Rules out the "known braid deformation counts our cells" route. See the closed thread `research/threads/finish_line_as_absorbing_wall.md`.
- Restriction: the survey covers x_i−x_j ∈ ℤ deformations; the torpids arrangement has a different (inverse-speed) shape.

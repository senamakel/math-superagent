# Dudeney — "The Torn Number", the puzzle statement (1917)

Source: https://bookofproofs.github.io/branches/fun/dudeney/arithmetical-and-algebraic-problems/various-other-aa-problems/the-torn-number.html
Full text: `research/sources/dudeney-torn-number.full.md` (the raw problem page;
the solution page is `research/sources/dudeney-torn-number-solution.full.md`).
Original: H. E. Dudeney, *Amusements in Mathematics*, The Authors' Club, 1917,
Problem 113 (public domain; Project Gutenberg #16713).

**This is the problem statement**, not the solution. A label bearing 3025 is
torn in half so that 30 | 25 remain; 30 + 25 = 55 and 55² = 3025. Puzzle: find
another 4-figure torn number, with all four digits different, which may be
divided in the middle to give the same effect.

**Bearing on PE 719.** This is the historical origin of the *two-block* special
case of the S-number rule. Its full analysis (the general solution and its
relation to the Kaprekar unitary-divisor formula) is in
`research/summaries/dudeney-torn-number-solution.md` and the claim
`dudeney-torn-number-two-block`. It is the two-block subcase only and does not
give T(10^12); retain it as the primary historical source.

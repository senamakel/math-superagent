# NRICH — "Recent Developments on S.P. Numbers" (distinct class)

Source: https://nrich.maths.org/articles/recent-developments-sp-numbers
(`research/sources/nrich-sp-numbers-survey.full.md`).

**Subject.** The **S.P. (S·P) numbers** thread from the Mathematical Gazette,
raised by S. Parameswaran (Gazette 81, Jul 1997): integers equal to the
**product of their digits times the sum of their digits**. The 1998–99
exchanges established any S·P number has at most 60 digits, and a computer
case-check (plus case reduction so the search is feasible) proved the **only
S·P numbers are 0, 1, 135 and 144** (published Gazette 83, Mar 1999).

**Why it does NOT help this run.** This is the same *distinct* S·P-number class
as the Belcher/Kominers note (`sp-numbers-distinct-class`): 
`digits-product × digits-sum`, not the split-a-square's-digits-into-blocks-summing-to-the-root
rule of PE 719. The "S" here is *S·P* (a name), not the run's "S-numbers". It
shares nothing with the problem except the letters; its completeness result
(only four such numbers) has no bearing on T(10¹²). Discard as a lead; do not
re-fetch. See `research/notes/librarian-dead-ends.md` §4 for the full
disambiguation of the three name-collisions (S·P, Kaprekar two-block, SSPDS).

# Sources: Calkin-Wilf tree, hyperbinary counts, Stern's diatomic sequence

Question: confirm that f(n) (number of hyperbinary representations: sums of
powers of 2 with each 2^k used 0,1,or 2 times, f(0)=1) satisfies
f(2n)=f(n)+f(n-1), f(2n+1)=f(n), f(2n-1)=f(n-1), and that
r_n = f(n)/f(n-1) (root r_1=1/1) is generated by
r_{2n} = r_n + 1 (trailing 0), r_{2n+1} = r_n/(r_n+1) (trailing 1),
bijecting positive integers n with positive rationals (Calkin-Wilf enumeration).

## Verification (mechanical, against recurrence f defined per PE175)
- f(10)=5; f(241)/f(240)=13/17; first n with that ratio is 241; SBE=[4,3,1]
  (prior brute.py + solution.py runs, recorded in memory.md).
- User's 3 recurrences hold for n=1..199 (script scratchpad_verify2.py).
- r_{2m} = r_m + 1 and r_{2m+1} = r_m/(r_m+1) hold for m=1..199;
  the tree walk from root 1/1 over binary digits after the leading 1 with
  0 -> r+1, 1 -> r/(r+1) reproduces r_n = f(n)/f(n-1) for n=1..99.
- solution.py: for all n=2..5000, peel(f(n),f(n-1)) == bin(n)[2:], i.e. the
  inverse walk (Euclidean subtraction) recovers n from the ratio. So the
  bijection n <-> positive rational holds for all tested n.

## Key facts and sources

### 1. Calkin-Wilf tree enumerates each positive rational exactly once
- Original paper (proved, Theorem 1):
  https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~wilf/website/recounting.pdf
  (Calkin & Wilf, "Recounting the rationals", Amer. Math. Monthly 107 (2000)
  360-363; mirror: http://www.math.clemson.edu/~calkin/Papers/recount.pdf)
  "Every positive rational occurs once and only once in this list."
  Tree: root 1/1; children of i/j are i/(i+j) and (i+j)/j; breadth-first rows
  give b(0)/b(1), b(1)/b(2), ...; recurrences f(2n+1)=f(n), f(2n+2)=f(n)+f(n+1).
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calkin%E2%80%93Wilf_tree
  "The tree is rooted at 1/1 ... Every positive rational number appears exactly
  once in the tree."
- https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Calkin-WilfTree.html
  Describes the tree, the level-order sequence and its link to Stern's diatomic
  series (A002487); "Both trees generate every rational number."
  (Note: MathWorld and Wikipedia label left=0, right=1 for the binary path;
  the user's statement assigns 0->r+1, 1->r/(r+1). Numerically the user's
  assignment holds for r_n=f(n)/f(n-1) exactly; only the 0/1 edge-labeling
  convention differs between sources. The bijection property is independent
  of the labeling.)

### 2. Recursion of the hyperbinary partition function (PE175's f)
- https://oeis.org/A002487 (Stern's diatomic series)
  "a(n+1) is the number of ways of writing n as a sum of powers of 2, each
  power being used at most twice (the number of hyperbinary representations of
  n) [Carlitz; Lind]."
  G.f. in OEIS: x * Product_{k>=0} (1 + x^(2^k) + x^(2^(k+1))) [Carlitz].
- Calkin-Wilf paper (above): b(2n+1)=b(n), b(2n+2)=b(n)+b(n+1); b(n) is the
  hyperbinary count; f(n)=A002487(n+1). PE175's form f(2n)=f(n)+f(n-1),
  f(2n+1)=f(n) is the same recurrence re-indexed.

### 3. Stern's diatomic sequence
- https://oeis.org/A002487 : a(0)=0, a(1)=1, a(2n)=a(n), a(2n+1)=a(n)+a(n+1);
  "a(n)/a(n+1) runs through all the reduced nonnegative rationals exactly once
  [Stern; Calkin and Wilf]."
- https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SternsDiatomicSeries.html : Stern's diatomic
  series (also fusc, Dijkstra 1982); quotients of consecutive terms enumerate
  the rationals via the Calkin-Wilf tree.
- https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/45350 (Steuding, Hofmann &
  Schuster, "Euclid, Calkin & Wilf - playing with rationals", Elem. Math. 63
  (2008) 109-117): states Stern's 1858 recursion, coprimality of consecutive
  terms, and that a=s(n), b=s(n+1) gives a unique n for every coprime pair
  (a,b); notes Stern's enumeration of Q+ predates Cantor.
- Survey: https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.4169/000298910x496714 (Northshield,
  "Stern's diatomic sequence 0,1,1,2,1,3,2,3,1,4,...", Amer. Math. Monthly 117
  (2010) 581-598) - covers hyperbinary representations and rational enumeration.

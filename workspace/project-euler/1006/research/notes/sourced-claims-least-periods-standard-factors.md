```claim
id: fibonacci-least-period-set
statement: The set of least periods of factors of the Fibonacci word is exactly the set of Fibonacci
numbers. (Currie & Saari, "Least periods of factors of infinite words", RAIRO-ITA 43 (2009) 165-178,
Cor. 4; period set of a Sturmian word characterized in Cor. 3. Lemma 8 (de Luca-De Luca): the least
period of factor w equals the length of a longest unbordered factor of w. Cor. 6: a finite word is a
factor of a Sturmian word iff its fractional root is a conjugate of a standard word.)
hypotheses: Fibonacci word = characteristic Sturmian word c_alpha of slope alpha=(3-sqrt5)/2;
least period = smallest p such that w is a power of a length-p word.
holds-here: true — the problem's S_n limit is that word; the period-set fact constrains which
length-k factors can repeat with small period.
status: sourced
follows-from: governing-sturmian
bearing: Replaces the Wikipedia-carried fact "least period of a subword is a Fibonacci number" with a
primary peer-reviewed anchor; supports the finite factor structure at k=F_n-1 (rotations of standard
words have Fibonacci least periods when primitive).
anchor: research/sources/currie-saari-least-periods-factors.full.md
(https://www.numdam.org/item/ITA_2009__43_1_165_0.pdf)
```

```claim
id: fibonacci-standard-factors-l0l1
statement: Characterize the standard words that occur as factors of the Fibonacci word: the set is
{phi^n(1), phi^n(10), phi^n(101), phi^n(0010010) : n >= 0}, phi the Fibonacci morphism (0->01, 1->0)
(Richomme, Saari & Zamboni, "Standard factors of Sturmian words", RAIRO-ITA 44 (2010) 159-174,
Example 3.3; Theorem 3.1 in general). Standard words are the images of {0,1} under the monoid
generated by L0: 0->0, 1->01 and L1: 0->10, 1->1 (Lemma 2.1). The Fibonacci word is the
characteristic word of slope alpha=(3-sqrt5)/2, directive sequence (1,1,1,...) — a third
independent confirmation of the slope.
hypotheses: standard words per directive-sequence construction s_{-1}=1, s_0=0,
s_k = s_{k-1}^{d_k} s_{k-2}; characteristic Sturmian words.
holds-here: true — the problem's word is c_alpha.
status: sourced
follows-from: governing-sturmian, conjugate-christoffel-factor-sturmian
bearing: This is the finite standard-factor structure behind directive 1's k=F_n-1 factor set (the
k+1 factors = F_n truncated cyclic rotations of the standard word q_n): standard factors of the
Fibonacci word are exactly the images of the S-set under the L0/L1/E monoid, and the length-k
truncated rotations of the standard word have the standard-word/property structure.
anchor: research/sources/richomme-saari-zamboni-standard-factors-sturmian.full.md
(https://www.numdam.org/item/ITA_2010__44_1_159_0.pdf)
```

```claim
id: fibonacci-zeckendorf-parity-characterization
statement: The Fibonacci infinite word f = 0100101001001... is the sequence f(n) = (rightmost bit /
parity) of the Zeckendorf representation of n; equivalently the finite words
f_n = f(0)f(1)...f(F_n-1) satisfy f_n = f_{n-1} f_{n-2} (the S_n of PE1006), with
f_{2n+1} = p_{2n+1} 01 and f_{2n+2} = p_{2n+2} 10 for the central words p_n (Fici,
"Factorizations of the Fibonacci infinite word", J. Integer Seq. 18 (2015) 15.9.3,
arXiv:1508.06754, §2).
hypotheses: Zeckendorf representation = unique sum of non-consecutive Fibonacci numbers F_n, n > 1.
holds-here: true — f is exactly the problem's S_n limit word.
status: sourced
follows-from: governing-sturmian
bearing: Gives the Ostrowski/Zeckendorf side a direct anchor on the word's digit definition: the
digit at position n is determined by the Zeckendorf representation of n, which is the numeration
system behind directive 1's O(log) lag-sum recursion and Ostrowski-based factor-location results.
anchor: research/sources/fici-factorizations-fibonacci-infinite-word-ar5iv.full.md
(https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1508.06754)
```

```claim
id: fibonacci-unique-special-factor-reverse
statement: The Fibonacci word f has exactly n+1 distinct factors of length n; exactly one of them is
right-special (both extensions x0 and x1 are factors), and it is the reverse of the length-n prefix,
f[0..n-1]^R. (Du, Mousavi, Schaeffer & Shallit, "Decision Algorithms for Fibonacci-Automatic Words",
arXiv:1406.0670, Theorem 18; the n+1 count is Theorem 57.)
hypotheses: Fibonacci word f = 01001010... = fixed point of 0->01, 1->0.
holds-here: true — f is the problem's S_n limit.
status: sourced
follows-from: governing-factor-complexity
bearing: Gives a precise structural description of the k+1 length-k factor set's special element;
useful as an independent cross-check on the mechanical-word/arc-factor enumeration (one factor is
the reverse of the k-prefix).
anchor: research/sources/mousavi-schaeffer-shallit-fibonacci-automatic-ar5iv.full.md
(https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1406.0670)
```

```claim
id: fibonacci-squares-conjugate-finite-word
statement: If uu is a square occurring in the Fibonacci word f, then u is conjugate to some finite
Fibonacci word; all finite Fibonacci words are primitive. (Du, Mousavi, Schaeffer & Shallit,
arXiv:1406.0670, Theorems 32 and 33, after Seebold and de Luca respectively.)
hypotheses: conjugate = cyclic rotation; finite Fibonacci word X_n = f[0..F_n-1].
holds-here: true.
status: sourced
follows-from: conjugate-christoffel-factor-sturmian, fibonacci-standard-factors-l0l1
bearing: Supports directive 1's k=F_n-1 statement that the k+1 factors are truncated rotations of a
single standard word: squares in f being conjugates of finite Fibonacci words and the primitivity of
the finite Fibonacci words constrain the repeated/rotational structure of length-k factors.
anchor: research/sources/mousavi-schaeffer-shallit-fibonacci-automatic-ar5iv.full.md
(https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1406.0670)
```

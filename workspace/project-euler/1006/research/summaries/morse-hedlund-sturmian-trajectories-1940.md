Source attempted: https://doi.org/10.2307/2371431 (American Journal of
Mathematics 62 (1940) 1–42, MR0000745).

**Status: paywalled.** The DOI resolves to a JSTOR page; the download returned
only a "Client Challenge" page, no article text. No free legitimate full text
exists (JSTOR/AMS archival; IAS archive offprint is physical-only, open for
research request but not digitized). Reported so nobody re-searches.

**Not a gap for the run.** Every result this paper introduces is already held
in-library by primary full texts that state it precisely:

- Perrin & Restivo, "A note on Sturmian words" (`perrin-restivo-note-sturmian-words.full.md`):
  Theorem 1 — an infinite word is Sturmian iff it is mechanical of irrational
  slope — plus the definition of a mechanical word and the factor-complexity
  characterization.
- Lothaire, *Algebraic Combinatorics on Words*, ch. 2 "Sturmian Words"
  (`lothaire-sturmian-words-C2.full.md`): the Morse–Hedlund factor-complexity
  theorem (p(n)=n+1 characterization), mechanical words, standard words.
- Berstel, "Recent results on Sturmian words" DLT'95 survey
  (`berstel-recent-results-sturmian-words-dlt95.full.md`) and Berstel's 2007
  survey (`berstel-sturmian-episturmian-survey-2007.full.md`).
- Encyclopedic Morse–Hedlund theorem entry (MathWorld,
  `research/summaries/mathworld-morse-hedlund-theorem.md`):
  right-infinite word ultimately periodic iff p(n) ≤ n for some n; aperiodic
  implies p(n) ≥ n+1, sharp; Sturmian = aperiodic binary with p(n)=n+1.

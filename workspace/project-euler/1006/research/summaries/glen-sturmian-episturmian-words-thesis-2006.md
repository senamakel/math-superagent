# Glen — On Sturmian and episturmian words, and related topics

**Source:** Amy Glen, "On Sturmian and episturmian words, and related topics",
Bulletin of the Australian Mathematical Society 74(1) (2006) 155–160,
DOI 10.1017/S0004972700047559. Published abstract of her PhD thesis
(University of Adelaide, January 2006; supervisors A. Wolff and R. Clarke).
Downloaded from the Murdoch research portal PDF:
`research/sources/glen-sturmian-episturmian-words-thesis-2006.full.md`.

## What it establishes

This is the 6-page published outline of a 191-page thesis. It is a survey-tier
source (not a new theorem here): it fixes the standard history and names, and
maps the thesis's results, which include:

- **History of Sturmian words**: Bernoulli III (1772), early work by Christoffel
  (1875) and Markoff (1882); the term "Sturmian" and the first detailed study
  are due to Morse–Hedlund (1940, symbolic dynamics). Standard surveys: Berstel,
  Berstel–Séébold, Allouche–Shallit's book, and the Lothaire volumes.
- **Chapter map of the thesis** (each is a pointer to the real theorems, held
  elsewhere in this library where noted):
  - Ch. 2: notation, background (standard sequences, characteristic Sturmian words).
  - Ch. 3: decompositions of the Fibonacci word — palindromes (Berstel's
    question), **singular words** (Wen–Wen's construction), **circular words**
    (Hegedüs–Nagy). This is the same singular-word machinery the run's
    `pe1006-christoffel-conjugacy-rotation-sum` approach was built on.
  - Ch. 4: conjugates of characteristic Sturmian words generated by morphisms,
    singular words for c_α.
  - Ch. 5: occurrences of palindromes in c_α, return words.
  - Ch. 6: Tribonacci sequence, episturmian morphisms, powers.
  - Ch. 7: divisibility/division properties of (epi)Sturmian words —
    **generalizes de Luca's "A division property of the Fibonacci word"
    (IPL 54 (1995) 307–312)** to the lexicographic order on episturmian words.
  - Ch. 8: transcendence results for episturmian continued fractions.

## Why it matters here

- Corroborates the singular-word / circular-word / division-property cluster of
  sources the run already holds (Wen–Wen 1994, Hegedüs–Nagy 2014, Fici 2015,
  de Luca 1995 via Fici's statement). Gives the de Luca division property a
  survey-level citation in the episturmian generalization (Glen Ch. 7).
- Confirms the standard-history statement the run uses for the "Fibonacci word
  is the canonical Sturmian word" sentence (Morse–Hedlund 1940).
- Boundary: survey abstract, no proofs and no new arithmetic; it does not bear
  on the floor-sum primitive or the decimal-weight evaluation. Use it as the
  reference-tier entry for the singular-word/division-property vocabulary, not
  as a computational source.

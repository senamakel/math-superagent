# Du, Mousavi, Schaeffer & Shallit — *Decision Algorithms for Fibonacci-Automatic Words, with Applications to Pattern Avoidance* (arXiv:1406.0670)

Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1406.0670
(full text: [[mousavi-schaeffer-shallit-fibonacci-automatic-ar5iv.full]])
Published: RAIRO-ITA 52(3) 2018 (parts I–II), IJFCS 2016 (part III).

## What this source establishes

A **decision procedure** (implemented in the Walnut tool) for Fibonacci-automatic
sequences — those whose n-th term is a finite-state function of the (Zeckendorf)
Fibonacci representation of n. The Fibonacci word **f = 01001010…** (the
problem's S_n limit) is generated by a **2-state DFAO** reading the canonical
Fibonacci representation: f_i = output of the last state reached from
(i)_F. Addition in Fibonacci representation is performed by an explicit
**17-state automaton** (the correctness proof identifies states with Lucas/Fib
sequences). Theorem 1: decidability for k-automatic sequences (Presburger +
Büchi V_k(n)=k^e); Fibonacci representation is a Pisot system so the same holds.

**Results on the infinite Fibonacci word** recovered/proved mechanically:

- **Theorem 12 (Chuan)**: palindromes of every length occur in f.
- **Theorem 13 (Droubay)**: exactly one palindromic factor of each even length,
  exactly two of each odd length.
- **Theorem 18**: the unique right-special factor of length n is the **reverse
  of the length-n prefix**, **f[0..n−1]^R**. (Because there are exactly n+1
  factors of length n, exactly one is right-special.)
- **Theorem 19 (Saari)**: the least period of any nonempty factor of the
  Fibonacci word is a Fibonacci number F_n, n ≥ 2, and every such occurs — the
  same result Currie–Saari Cor 4 states; reproved by automaton here.
- **Theorem 20 (new)**: ℓ(n) := smallest integer that is a least period of some
  length-n factor equals F_j iff L_{j−1} ≤ n ≤ L_{j+1}−2 (L_j = Lucas).
- **Theorem 11**: f has exactly four antisquare factors: 01, 10, 1001, 10100101.
- Theorem 9/10: automata classify maximal repetitions (least-period structure);
  Theorem 4–8: squares/cubes and their positions/orders automata.

**Results on the finite Fibonacci words (Section 4)** — directly relevant to the S_n:

- The finite words X_n = ε, 1, 0, X_{n−1}X_{n−2} (|X_n|=F_n) are f[0..F_n−1];
  φ(X_n)=X_{n+1}, φ the Fibonacci morphism.
- **Theorem 32 (Séébold)**: every square uu in f has u conjugate to a finite
  Fibonacci word (directly supports directive 1's k=F_n−1 conjugate structure).
- **Theorem 30/31 (almost-commutative)**: X_{n−1}X_n = η(X_n X_{n−1}) (swap last
  two letters), and this characterizes Fibonacci-indexed prefix pairs.
- **Theorem 33 (de Luca)**: all finite Fibonacci words are primitive.
- **Theorem 34 (Chuan)**: X_n (n≥5) is a product of two palindromes in exactly
  one way (factors of lengths F_{n−1}−2 and F_{n−2}+2).
- Borders of f[0..F_n−1] are f[0..F_{n−2k}−1] for 2k<n (Cummings–Moore–Karhumäki).

## What it implies for PE1006

1. Gives an independent, mechanical (automaton-based) confirmation of the
   factor-structure facts the run uses: n+1 distinct length-n factors, unique
   right-special factor = reverse of the n-prefix, least periods are Fibonacci.
   This corroborates directive 1's k=F_n−1 conjugate machinery (Theorem 32:
   squares are conjugates of finite Fibonacci words).
2. The unique-special-factor fact (Theorem 18) is exactly the kind of structural
   statement behind the factor-set description; useful as a cross-check on the
   k+1 factor enumeration.
3. Fibonacci representation = Zeckendorf, the numeration underlying the
   O(log) floor-sum/Ostrowski evaluations — the same system Fici and Chuan–Ho
   use. The explicit addition automaton is a reference implementation of
   Fibonacci-representation arithmetic.

## Claims anchored here

`fibonacci-least-period-set` (Currie–Saari) is independently re-derived here
(Theorem 19); new claim `fibonacci-unique-special-factor-reverse` recorded in
the cycle note.

## What it does NOT establish

- Nothing about reading factors as decimals or summing squares.
- Nothing about the universal-Euclidean floor-sum primitive (the run's actual
  evaluator); this is the structure side, not the evaluation side.
- The avoidability/Rote-Fibonacci parts (§5–8) are not relevant to PE1006.

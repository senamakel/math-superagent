191–238 (1994), DOI 10.36045/bbms/1103408547. Full text obtained from the
Verimag mirror (UMR-CNRS/UJF) at

Cobham–Semenov overseeing survey on base-dependence of automatic (p-

For this run it is chiefly a **negative result** (the Cobham obstruction),
parallel to Frougny's linear-numeration version already held.

## What it establishes (precise statements, verified in full text)

- **Theorem 4.1** (p-automaticity): for p ≥ 2 and s : N → A, s is generated by
  a p-substitution iff s is p-recognizable iff p-definable (iff p-algebraic for
  p prime). Same in N^m (Thm 5.1). Cobham's theorem is the base-dependence
  sharpening.
- **Theorem 7.7 (Cobham–Semenov):** Let m ≥ 1 and p, q ≥ 2 be
  **multiplicatively independent** (p^k = q^l only for k = l = 0). If
  s : N^m → A is both p-recognizable and q-recognizable, then s is definable
  in ⟨N, +⟩ (equivalently: ultimately periodic / Presburger / semilinear in
  the 1-dimensional case).
- Consequence quoted in the text: the characteristic sequence of powers of 2
  is 2-recognizable and 2^k-recognizable, but not 3-recognizable — the
  canonical illustration of base dependence. Sets recognizable in **all** bases
  are exactly the ultimately periodic ones (Cobham 1969, Thm 7.3(1)⇔(3)). In
  fact the only sets recognized in bases that are powers of one fixed integer
  are the ultimately periodic sets.
- **Theorem 6.1 / Corollary 6.3:** Th(⟨N, +, V_p⟩) decidable; the set of
  squares is not p-recognizable for any p — the standard "recognizable sets
  are arithmetic progressions modulo finitely many exceptions" content.
- Proof of Thm 7.7 in §8.2 follows Michaux–Villemaire's paper
  (Ann. Pure Appl. Logic 1996) and Muchnik's definability criterion.

## Hypotheses and whether they hold here

- Functional: the theorem concerns sequences definable/recognizable in integer
  bases p, q that are multiplicatively independent. PE1006's two "bases" are
  10 and φ = (1+√5)/2 (φ the Pisot number of the Fibonacci numeration). These
  are multiplicatively independent (10^k = φ^m would force φ rational [or of
  algebraic degree 1], impossible), so the *1-dimensional* Cobham theorem does
  not literally apply to a φ-numeration (φ is not an integer base; the
  Frougny/linear-numeration generalization covers that), but the **same
  obstruction** — no finite automaton can convert between φ- and 10-representations — is the Bès–Frougny/Frougny result already held.

## What it is **not** to be used for

- It does **not** say anything against the universal-Euclidean floor-sum monoid:
  that route carries exact integer arithmetic in a *single* base (decimal digit
  weights), not an automaton conversion between two bases. The monoid is
  unaffected by Cobham's theorem.

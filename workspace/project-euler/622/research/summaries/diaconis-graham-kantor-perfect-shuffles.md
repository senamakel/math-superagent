# Diaconis–Graham–Kantor, "The Mathematics of Perfect Shuffles"

Source: https://pages.uoregon.edu/kantor/PAPERS/PerfectShuffles.pdf (Adv. Appl. Math. 4, 1983) · full text: [[diaconis-graham-kantor-perfect-shuffles.full]]

## What it establishes

The group-theoretic structure of the shuffle group ⟨I, O⟩ generated by the in
and out shuffles acting on 2n cards. The load-bearing result for this run is:

**Lemma 1** (verified at line ~140 of the full text):
- The order of the **in** shuffle on 2n cards is the order of 2 mod (2n+1).
- The order of the **out** shuffle on 2n cards is the order of 2 mod (2n−1).

Proof (out only; in is the same with 2 more cards): label positions 0..2n−1;
one out-shuffle sends card j to position 2j (mod 2n−1); after k shuffles it is
at 2^k·j (mod 2n−1); all cards return when 2^k ≡ 1 (mod 2n−1). The paper's own
opening remark: "A pack of 52 cards requires 8 out shuffles or 52 in shuffles
to recycle."

## Consequences for this problem

The statement's riffle shuffle fixes the top and bottom cards, so it is the
**out**-shuffle, and s(n) = ord_{n−1}(2). (The in-shuffle's order would be
ord of 2 mod n+1 — the wrong variant, excluded by the "top and bottom
preserved" clause.) The rest of the paper (Weyl group B_n structure, Mathieu
group exceptions at 2n = 12, 24) is irrelevant here.

## Does not settle

- The exact numerical sum for order 60; that requires enumerating divisors of
  2^60−1, which without the Wieferich lift and lcm machinery is a raw scan.
  Those come from Packard and Chappelon.

## Status

Proved in the source (main theorem of the paper's Section on perfect shuffles
structure). Hypothesis n even holds here. This is the single most important
claim in the whole library.

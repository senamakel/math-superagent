# OEIS A002326 — least m>0 such that 2n+1 | 2^m − 1

Source: https://oeis.org/A002326 · full text: [[oeis-A002326.full]]

## What it establishes

a(n) = least m>0 such that 2n+1 divides 2^m − 1, i.e. a(n) = ord_{2n+1}(2).
Comments: number of riffle shuffles of 2n+2 cards required to restore a deck;
a(1)=2 because a riffle shuffle of [1,2,3,4] needs 2 iterations:
[1,2,3,4]→[1,3,2,4]→[1,2,3,4].
Given sequence: a(0..?) = 1,2,4,3,6,10,12,4,8,18,6,11,20,...
Note intercourse: "if 2n+1 is prime then 2n is a multiple of a(n)".
Conjecture (Ordowski): if p odd prime then a((p^3−1)/2) = p·a((p^2−1)/2)
unless p is Wieferich (p^3 | 2^{p−1}−1); no such p known — i.e. no Wieferich
base-2 prime below the tested range.

## Consequences for this problem

For n even, deck size = 2n+2, so s(2n+2) = ord_{2n+1}(2) = a(n). The sequence is
A002326 exactly. Confirms s(52)=8 (52=2·25+2, n=25: a(25)=8) and the small-case
cross-checks. The "no Wieferich base-2 prime" data supports the `wieferich-lift`
claim status for the primes of 2^60−1 (none of the known Wieferich primes — 1093,
3511 — divide 2^60−1).

## Does not settle

No closed-form enumeration; purely the sequence and its interpretations.

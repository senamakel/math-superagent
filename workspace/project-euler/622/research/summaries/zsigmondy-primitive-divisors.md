# Zsigmondy's theorem / primitive prime divisors of a^n − b^n

> Source: Graham Everest, Shaun Stevens, Duncan Tamsett, Tom Ward, "Primes Generated by
> Recurrence Sequences," arXiv:math/0412079 (section 1).
> Full text: `research/sources/zsigmondy-primitive-divisors.full.md`
> Source URL: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/0412079

## What it establishes

The classical Zsigmondy theorem, stated cleanly in the Mersenne context that PE622
needs. A *primitive prime divisor* of a term S_n of an integer sequence S is a prime
factor of S_n that divides no earlier nonzero term S_m (m < n). The theorem:

- For the **Mersenne sequence** M_n = 2^n − 1: every term M_n with n > 6 has a primitive
  prime divisor. The **sole exception is M_6 = 63 = 3² · 7**, which has no primitive
  prime divisor (both 3 and 7 already divide earlier terms). Verified in the source's
  Table 1 (M_2=3, M_3=7, M_4=3·5, M_5=31, M_6=3²·7 with no bold/primitive entry,
  M_7=127, ...).

- General form for U_n = a^n − b^n with positive coprime a > b: U_n has a primitive
  divisor unless (a,b,n) = (2,1,6) or a+b is a power of 2 with n = 2. (Bang proved
  the a=2,b=1 case; Zsigmondy [Monatsh. Math. 3 (1892) 265–284] the general one; the
  theorem was independently rediscovered by Birkhoff–Vandiver.F.)

## Consequences for this problem

For the Zsigmondy classification approach (research/approaches/zsigmondy-prime-power-classification.md),
this is the missing certification rung. A primitive prime divisor p of 2^d − 1 is
characterised by the property that it divides 2^d − 1 but no 2^k − 1 for k < d — which is
*exactly* the condition ord_p(2) = d. So:

```
{Zsigmondy primes of 2^d − 1}  =  {primes p : ord_p(2) = d}.
```

To classify every m with ord_m(2) = 60, the run needs, for each d | 60, at least one
prime of order d (then the Wieferich lift ord_{p^a}(2) = d·p^{a−1} handles prime powers).
Zsigmondy certifies each class is nonempty for d | 60, d ≠ 1, **except d = 6**: there the
only divisors of 63 = 2^6−1 are 3 (order 2) and 7 (order 3), so no prime has order 6.
That exception is harmless for order 60: the lcm of prime-power orders must reach 60,
which requires a factor of 4 (from d=4, e.g. p=5), 3 (from d=12,20,60...) and 5 (from
d=5,20,60...), all of which do have primitive prime divisors. The missing order-6 class
contributes nothing needed.

## Status

Proved in source (classical theorem, cited from Zsigmondy 1892). The characterisation
"primitive prime divisor of 2^d−1 ⟺ ord_p(2)=d" is immediate from the definition and
Conrad Thm 2.1 (a^k ≡ 1 mod m ⟺ ord | k). Hypothesis gcd(2, m) = 1 holds here since all
moduli are odd. This makes the prime-order classes {p : ord_p(2)=d} citable as a named
theorem, closing the `_unchecked_` precedent on the Zsigmondy approach.

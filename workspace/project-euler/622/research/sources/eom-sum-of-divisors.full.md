# Sum of divisors

From Encyclopedia of Mathematics

Jump to: navigation, search

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: *Primary:*[11A25][23]*Secondary:*[11A51][23] [[MSN][24]][[ZBL][25]]

*of a natural number $n$*

The sum of the positive integers divisors of a natural number $n$, including $1$ and $n$: $$ \sigma(n) = \sum_{d | n} d \ . $$ More generally, the function $\sigma_k$ is defined as $$ \sigma_k(n) = \sum_{d | n} d^k \ . $$ so that $\sigma = \sigma_1$ and the [number of divisors][26] function $\tau = \sigma_0$.

These are [multiplicative arithmetic functions][27] with [Dirichlet series][28] $$ \sum_{n=1}^\infty \sigma_k(n) n^{-s} = \prod_p \left({(1-p^{-s})(1-p^{k-s}) }\right)^{-1} = \zeta(s) \zeta(s-k)\ . $$

The [average order][29] of $\sigma(n)$ is given by $$ \sum_{n \le x} \sigma(n) = \frac{\pi^2}{12} x^2 + O(x \log x) \ . $$

There are a number of well-known classes of number characterised by their divisor sums.

A *[perfect number][30]*$n$ is the sum of its [aliquot divisors][31] (those divisors other than $n$ itself), so $\sigma(n) = 2n$. The even perfect numbers are characterised in terms of [Mersenne primes][32] $P = 2^p-1$ as $n = 2^{p-1}.P$: it is not known if there are any odd perfect numbers. An *[almost perfect number][33]*$n$ similarly has the property that $\sigma(n) = 2n-1$: these include the powers of 2. A *quasiperfect number*is defined by $\sigma(n) = 2n+1$: it is not known if any exists. See also [Descartes number][34].

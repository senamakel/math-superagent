# L0.0 seal — the counting/CGT foundation and two dead-end artifacts

Seals the ten original notes of batch **L0.0** (CGT framework + arithmetic
engine, plus accidental downloads). Together they establish the two pillars the
whole run stands on — **how the game reduces to counting**, and **how A(n),B(n)
are computed in polylog** — and they record that three of the ten are misfiled
material with no bearing on the problem.

## Pillar 1 — the game's no-skip value is A−B
- [[cgt]] (Conway ONAG / Winning Ways): the board is a **disjunctive sum** of
  one short game per number; normal-play values add.
- [[disjsum]]: each number with a 1-bits, b 0-bits is the integer game **a−b**
  (the simplest surreal between its two options G(a−1,b) and G(a,b−1)), so the
  whole board's no-skip value is **A−B** (A total 1-bits, B total 0-bits).
- Consequence recorded in MEMORY: A−B>0 ⇒ One can always win without skips ⇒
  skips are what let Zero ever win, so **S(n) ≠ A−B**.

## Pillar 2 — the arithmetic is polylog
- [[bitcount.full.md]]: OEIS A000788, summatory 1-bit count → **A(n)** =
  Σ k·popcount(k), via O(log n) recurrences.
- [[a083652.full.md]]: OEIS A083652, summatory binary length, exact O(1)
  closed form (n+1)⌈log₂(n+1)⌉−2^⌈log₂(n+1)⌉+2; third leg = ones+zeros, and
  via A059015 = A083652 − A000788 gives **B(n)** = Σ k·zerocount(k).
- [[flajolet_weighted_digitalsums]] (Cheung–Flajolet–Golin–Lee, arXiv:1003.0150):
  weighted binary digital sums have exact main-term + 1-periodic-fluctuation
  closed forms — the warrant that k·-weighted A(n),B(n) stay polylog at
  n=10⁵ scale. (Two files: the abstract plus the full text; the doubled
  `.full.full` name is a mis-save of the same source, covered once.)

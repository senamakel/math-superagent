idea: Pontryagin's Maximum Principle (optimal control) — formulate the escape as a continuous-time optimal control problem and derive the critical speed from the necessary conditions of optimality, without assuming the homothetic-staging ansatz. The adjoint (costate) equations characterize the optimal dash direction, and the transversality condition at the dash-initiation point yields an equation for v.

mechanism: The swimmer's dynamics are ẋ(t) = u(t) with ||u(t)|| ≤ 1, starting at x(0) = (0,0) (center). The runner's position s(t) on the boundary evolves as ṡ = ±v (choosing the direction that minimizes escape chance). The game ends at terminal time T when |x(T)| reaches the boundary at azimuth φ, with the runner at s(T). Escape succeeds if the swimmer's boundary arrival point is distinct from the runner's. The value is the minimum over runner strategies of the maximum over swimmer strategies of [escape success indicator].

Applying PMP: the Hamiltonian is H = p·u + q·(±v), where p ∈ ℝ² is the costate for the swimmer's position and q is the costate for the runner's boundary coordinate. The optimal swimmer control is u*(t) = p(t)/|p(t)| (bang-bang structure: the swimmer always moves at full speed in the direction of the costate). The costate evolves as ṗ = −∂H/∂x = 0 in the interior, so p is constant along straight-line dash segments. During the staging phase (if any), the swimmer moves to maintain a positional advantage; the costate is not constant because the Hamiltonian depends on position through the boundary-interaction term.

The critical insight: the optimal trajectory consists of (1) a staging phase where the swimmer moves along a curve that keeps them "opposite" the runner in a sense defined by the costate dynamics, followed by (2) a terminal dash along a straight line (constant p). At the switching point between these phases, the costate p must be tangent to the staging curve (transversality). The critical speed v is the value where the maximum over all possible dash directions (parameterized by the terminal costate direction) of the escape margin equals zero.

For the hexagon, the staging phase is constrained by the polygon geometry: the runner's motion is piecewise-constant-velocity along edges, creating piecewise dynamics for the adjoint. The critical condition reduces to: find v such that max_{dash direction, staging endpoint} [runner perimeter time − v × swimmer dash time] = 0, where the staging endpoint is constrained to lie on a curve determined by the adjoint equations. This is a different equation from the stewbasic max-ratio formula because the staging endpoint is not assumed to lie on a 1/v-scaled homothetic copy — it comes from the ODE.

```approach
idea: Schwarz-Christoffel conformal mapping — transform the regular-hexagon game to the unit-disk game where the critical speed is known exactly (V_circle = 1/cos B, tan B = π+B, ≈ 4.60333885). The hexagon's boundary is mapped to the circle; the runner's uniform speed v along the hexagon edges becomes a non-uniform speed v·|f'(z)|⁻¹ along the circle, and the swimmer's straight dash in the hexagon becomes a geodesic in the pullback metric. The critical-speed equation becomes: find v such that the maximal ratio of (distorted runner boundary time) to (distorted swimmer distance) equals v. Since the Schwarz-Christoffel map for the regular hexagon to the unit disk has an explicit integral representation involving (1−z⁶)⁻¹/³, the conformal factor at each boundary point is known in closed form, and the critical condition reduces to a 1D maximization along the circle.

mechanism: The hexagon game (uniform speeds, polygonal boundary) and the disk game (uniform speeds, circular boundary) are the same pursuit-evasion problem on conformally equivalent domains, but with a position-dependent metric. The known disk solution gives the structural template; the conformal factor supplies the hexagon-specific distortion. Structurally a "solve for the fixed point of a 1D max" problem, independent of the K-index — complex analysis rather than the trigonometric inequality that defines K.

status: refuted
killed-by: The critical speed is NOT a conformal invariant. Disk and hexagon are conformally equivalent (an SC map exists — the premise is true), yet V_disk = 4.60333885 ≠ V_hexagon = 5.05505046. The run's OWN two oracle values for conformally-equivalent domains are a direct counterexample to the idea that the disk answer "pulls back" to the hexagon. Reason: the game's data are Euclidean distances (swimmer straight dash) and boundary ARC LENGTHS (runner time). Conformal maps preserve only angles, not either of these — they do not preserve the swimmer's straight-line distance (hexagon chords) or the runner's perimeter arc length. After any conformal map the game lives in a non-Euclidean Riemannian metric, and there is no Euclidean-interpretation relation between the two games. The Schwarz-Christoffel map exists but maps the Euclidean game to a curved-metrized game whose solution is a NEW problem, not the known flat-disk one. Searching the literature (web, research papers, and all 20 held primary sources) returns NO work applying conformal/Schwarz-Christoffel methods to escape or critical-speed pursuit games — this is an absence: the premise that the flat-disk answer transfers is false on its face (the two oracle values falsify it), independent of whether anyone has attempted it.
```

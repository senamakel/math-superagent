## Not yet taken to the literature

Nobody has checked whether these are known theory. Grounding one is cheaper than pursuing it: a named theorem arrives with its hypotheses, and a reformulation somebody already tried arrives with the reason it failed.

- [[angular-speed-safe-region]]: This approach tests whether the stewbasic homothetic-scaling ansatz is the true optimal safe region or merely a convenient lower bound. On a polygon edge, the runner at position s (arc-length) has angular speed ω(s) = v / ℓ(s) where ℓ(s) is the distance from the center to the line through the edge point perpendicular to the radius — a computable geometric quantity. The region {r ≤ 1/ω_runner(φ)} is the true region where the swimmer can maintain opposition. For the hexagon, compute this region exactly (it is piecewise-defined by six arc-of-circle segments — the safe boundary consists of six hyperbolic/trigonometric arcs, one per edge). Then run the same "max over dash landing point" optimization on this region. The circle case: ρ(φ) ≡ 1, ω_runner ≡ v, safe region = disk of radius 1/v — recovers the known circle solution. If the hexagon result matches 5.05505046 to 8 dp, the stewbasic formula is confirmed by a genuinely independent physical model; if it differs, the stewbasic ansatz is suboptimal and the true V_hexagon is larger.
- [[david-k-hexagon-construction]]: The hexagon is the last regular n-gon whose trigonometric functions of π/n are all expressible in real radicals (√3 only, no nested roots), and n=6 is one of only three n (with n=3,4) for which the minimal polynomial of V(n)² is quadratic — established fact from the run's own `code/v2_quadratic_test.py`. This is not a coincidence: the quadratic closure reflects the fact that the geometric equal-time condition involves only linear and quadratic expressions in the coordinates when all angles are multiples of 30°. A David-K-style construction for the hexagon would make this algebra explicit: pick coordinates, parameterize the critical dash exit point on the hexagon boundary, write the two distances (swimmer chord, runner perimeter) in terms of the parameter, maximize the ratio, and solve the resulting algebraic equation for v. The result must equal 2+2√21/3, but the route to it is entirely geometric — no K, no acos, no trig inequality.
- [[schwarz-christoffel-conformal-mapping]]: The hexagon game (uniform speeds, polygonal boundary) and the disk game (uniform speeds, circular boundary) are the same pursuit-evasion problem on conformally equivalent domains, but with a position-dependent metric. The known disk solution gives the structural template; the conformal factor supplies the hexagon-specific distortion. The critical-speed equation becomes max_{φ} [arc_length_on_circle(φ) / chord_length_in_pullback_metric(φ)] = v, where the pullback chord length involves integrating |f'(z)| along the straight segment. This is structurally a "solve for the fixed point of a 1D max" problem, independent of the K-index — it uses complex analysis rather than the trigonometric inequality that defines K.

```approach
idea: Number-theoretic / CRT-based formula for g(c,s,p,q) from discrete contact-point phase bookkeeping
mechanism: The "perfectly meshing" condition for a planet meshing both an internal ring (c teeth) and an external sun (s teeth) can be stated purely in terms of tooth-count congruences without geometry. Consider the planet rolling without slip around the ring C: one full revolution of the planet relative to C covers c teeth on C and t teeth on the planet, so the planet's tooth phase advances by t/c of a full turn per radian of travel along C. Similarly, rolling along S advances the planet's phase by t/s per radian along S. For a planet at a fixed position (tangent to both C and S simultaneously), the arc of C that the planet "covers" is the C-contact arc from some reference tooth to the contact point — this is determined by the geometry. In discretized form: label the c teeth of C by 0,…,c−1 and the s teeth of S by 0,…,s−1. A planet of t teeth meshes with C at tooth i_C ∈ Z_c and with S at tooth i_S ∈ Z_s. The planet itself has t teeth, and the two contact points are separated by k planet teeth (k = ⌊t·ψ/(2π)⌋ or similar). The meshing condition is that the same planet tooth that meets C at i_C also meets S at i_S (or vice versa) — i.e., the planet-tooth labelling must be globally consistent. This gives, for each planet:
  i_S ≡ i_C + δ (mod gcd-like quantity depending on c, s, t).
Since the planets are independent (overlap allowed), the four planets give four such constraints. The ring and sun orientations are global degrees of freedom, absorbing two of these constraints. The remaining two give the discreteness. The count g(c,s,p,q) is then the number of integer solutions to a system of linear congruences modulo c and s, with the planet tooth counts entering through the offset δ_t. This is a Chinese Remainder Theorem / Smith-normal-form problem: count the number of (i_C^(p+), i_C^(p−), i_C^(q+), i_C^(q−)) ∈ (Z_c)⁴ (ring contact teeth for the four planets) that satisfy the sun-contact consistency conditions. Since C and S are offset by d, the relation between i_C (position on ring) and i_S (position on sun) involves the geometry — but after fixing the discrete tooth indices, the geometry determines d uniquely, so counting tooth-index assignments is equivalent to counting d values. The key structural claim: g(c,s,p,q) depends on c,s,p,q only through gcd/lcm structures, making it a multiplicative function of the tooth counts. If true, G(500) can be computed by a sieve over integer partitions of n = s+p+q with a closed-form evaluation of g at each triple, costing O(n²) rather than O(n³).
status: proposed
first-step: Write down the exact tooth-labelling congruences for one planet meshing both C (internal, c teeth) and S (external, s teeth) at a fixed geometry, in terms of the tooth indices i_C ∈ Z_c, i_S ∈ Z_s, and the planet tooth offset k ∈ Z_t. Eliminate the planet phase to get a single congruence relating i_C and i_S. Then check whether the four-planet system (with global sun/ring orientations as free variables) reduces to a solvability condition whose number of solutions is the observed g(16,5,5,6)=9 when c=16, s=5, p=5, q=6.
```

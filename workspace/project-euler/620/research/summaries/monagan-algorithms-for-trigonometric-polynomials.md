## What it establishes (the algebraic substrate of candidate tangent-half-angle)

The **tangent half-angle (t-) substitution** θ → t = tan(θ/2):

    sin θ = 2t/(1+t²),   cos θ = (1−t²)/(1+t²)

is a ring morphism ψ_t : Q[sin θ, cos θ] → Q(t) whose kernel is the principal
ideal generated by s²+c²−1 (Lemma 3). Consequences:

- **Degree bound (Lemma 4):** a trigonometric polynomial p of trigonometric
  degree d satisfies ψ_t(p) = a(t)/(1+t²)^d with a(t) ∈ Q[t] and deg_t(a) ≤ 2d.
  So the t-substitution at most *doubles* the degree.
- **Theorem 1 (inverse):** any rational polynomial a(t)/(1+t²)^n with
  1+t² ∤ a(t) and deg a ≤ 2n is the image of a unique trig polynomial of degree
  n — the substitution is reversible on the right class.
- Division, factorisation, and GCD of trig polynomials reduce to the same
  problems on the numerator a(t) (Lemmas 5–6, Theorems 2–3).

## Implication for PE620's candidate `tangent-half-angle`

Confirms that substituting t = tan(E/2) into the trig expressions for the
phase invariant is algebraically sound and that the resulting object is
rational in t, with degree at most double the trig degree. Combined with Sturm's
theorem (exact real-root counting on a square-free real polynomial), this is a
*bona fide* exact route to counting roots without numerical scanning — the INRIA
ALIAS system uses exactly this (trig equation → polynomial via half-angle
substitution → root count), and Sturm/Encyclopedia-of-Math give the counting
theorem.

**What it does NOT settle:** the t-substitution turns the *arithmetic* of the
phase congruence into a polynomial, but only if the phase model itself is the
right discreteness. The candidate presumes the W-invariant model of the thread
`offcentre-mesh-phase-model` — s·φ + c·χ − t·γ pairwise congruent — which is
**still oracle-unverified** (does not yet reproduce g(16,5,5,6)=9). Also the
candidate's phase terms are arccos of rational functions, so tan(W) is a sum of
terms √(rational)·(Chebyshev), and clearing square roots can raise degree well
above 2(c+s) and introduce spurious roots. So the technique is sound and exact;
its *guarantee* for THIS problem is upstream of the (unverified) meshing model.

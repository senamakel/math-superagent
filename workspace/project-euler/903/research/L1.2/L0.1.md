## 1. Hultman, Permutation statistics of products of random permutations
[[../L0.1/hultman_products_random_permutations.full.md]] (arXiv:1301.0430, full
text is the arXiv abs page). Curated: [[L1.0/hultman_products_random_permutations]].
**Method:** expected value of a statistic s on a PRODUCT of t independent
permutations, each uniform on a union Γ of conjugacy classes. Requires the mean
statistic s̄ (the class function equal to the mean of s over conjugacy classes) be
expanded as a linear combination of irreducible S_n characters. Explicit
expansions are given for excedance, inversion, descent, major index, and k-cycle
numbers, yielding closed-form expectations on such products.
**Hypotheses / do they hold here?** Products of t class-distributed permutations —
this run sums rank over the CYCLIC subgroup {π^i} of a single π, which is neither a
product distribution nor class-uniform; and rank is a Lehmer/factoradic weight, NOT
a class function (its value is not constant on conjugacy classes). So the character
method does not directly apply.
**What it lets the run bound/rule out:** it is the standard replacement for
enumerating S_n when confirming an A_n/B_n cycle-type sum: per-conjugacy-class
weighted sums via characters could cross-check class-uniform averages. But it does
NOT reach Σ_{τ∈<π>}rank(τ). Methodological neighbour, clean negative on the core.
Verdict: tooling/reference only.

## 2. Leaños, Moreno, Rivera-Martínez, On the number of m-th roots of permutations
[[../L0.1/leanos_mth_roots_of_permutations.full.md]] (arXiv:1005.1531v4, full
text). Curated: [[L1.1/leanos_mth_roots_of_permutations]].
**Definitions:** σ∈S_n of cycle type a=(a_1..a_n), a_ℓ=#ℓ-cycles. σ has an m-th
root τ (τ^m=σ) iff for every ℓ the count a_ℓ is divisible by ((ℓ,m)) :=
∏_{p|ℓ}p^{ν_p(m)} (the part of m supported on the primes of ℓ) — Theorem 3 here,
due to Wilf/Knopfmacher–Warlimont (Theorem 4.8.2 of Wilf's Generatingfunctionology).
For prime p that reads: cycles of length a multiple of p must come in batches.
**Theorem 1 (explicit count):** r^(m)(a) = ∏_{ℓ:a_ℓ≠0} a_ℓ! · Σ_{ε∈E_m(ℓ,a_ℓ)}
∏_i ℓ^{(g_i−1)ε_i}/(g_i^{ε_i}ε_i!) where G_m(ℓ,a)={g∈N: g≤a_ℓ, gcd(gℓ,m)=g},
E_m(ℓ,a_ℓ)={ε∈N_0^k: Σg_iε_i=a_ℓ}; r=0 iff some E_m is empty.
**Theorem 2 (EGF):** coeff of t_1^{a_1}⋯t_n^{a_n}/(a_1!⋯a_n!) in
exp(Σ_ℓ Σ_{g∈G_m(ℓ)}(ℓ^{g−1}/g)t_ℓ^g) counts m-th roots of type a.
**Prop 9 (prime-power block-constancy):** for m=p^r the probability p_m(n) that a
random n-permutation has an m-th root is constant on each block
n=jp,…,jp+p−1.
**Hypotheses:** m fixed positive integer; root counts are conjugation-invariant
(constant per cycle type). **Hold here trivially.**
**What it lets the run compute/rule out:** counts of preimages τ of a σ under the
power map τ↦τ^m — the m-th-root count of a given cycle type. **Caveat (clean
negative):** Q(n) is not an m-th-root count; root-counting gives the number of
τ with τ^m=σ, not Σ_{τ∈<π>}rank(τ). A reformulation Q(n)=Σ_τ rank(τ)·N(τ) with
N(τ)=#{(π,i):π^i=τ} is type-structured but needs Σ over n! exponents and p(10^6)
cycle types — it does not beat enumeration. Confirms cited fact (2); does not
advance the efficient method.

## 3. Legendre, The Number System of the Permutations Generated by Cyclic Shift
[[../L0.1/legendre_number_system_cyclic_shift.full.md]] (abs page, arXiv:1007.2870)
and [[../L0.1/legendre_number_system_cyclic_shift.md]] (excerpt stub — both files
carry the same source). Curated: [[L1.1/legendre_number_system_cyclic_shift]].
**What it establishes:** a number system coding for the permutations generated by
CYCLIC SHIFT — the orbit {σ_i} of a one-line word under left-rotation of its
entries (n permutations). It ranks a permutation given how it was generated, and
unranks a rank; the code encodes the symmetry of the whole cyclic set, conjectured
(not proved) to be a combinatorial Gray code — an Hamiltonian path of minimal
weight in the associated regular digraph.
**Hypotheses:** none needed for the framework; the Gray-code claim is a conjecture.
**What it lets the run bound / why it matters here:** it is the CLOSEST located
treatment of "rank a permutation inside the subset generated by one map on S_n" —
exactly the shape of the open core (Σ rank over the subgroup {π^i}).
**Clean negative (limitation):** the map is a rotation of the one-line word (an
n-cycle acting on positions), orbit size n; PE 903 sums over {π^i}, the order-d
subgroup generated by the permutation-POWER map, with d=lcm of cycle lengths and
orbit size d (=ord π). Same question shape, different algebra; the Legendre rank
code is for rotation orbits, not power orbits, and yields NO closed form for
A_n,B_n. Related framework only — this is the "rank-inside-a-cyclic-orbit"
negative that framework literature confirms and power-variant solves nowhere.

## 5. Zawiślak, The Lehmer factorial norm on S_n — abs page
[[../L0.1/lehmer_factorial_norm.full.md]] (arXiv:2111.03951). Curated:
[[L1.1/lehmer_factorial_norm]].
**What it establishes (body, see §6):** Lehmers digits c_i(σ)=#{j>i:σ(j)<σ(i)}
of a uniform σ∈S_n are independent with c_i uniform on {0,…,i} (Lemmas 3.1, 3.3,
Cor 3.2). This is the factoradic-rank digit structure rank(σ)=1+Σ_j a_j(n−1−j)!
already used by this run.
**Wedge:** the abs page alone carries only the title/abstract (a norm family,
metrics differentiating first/last position changes); the math this run needs is in
the body (§6). Verdict: a re-proof/confirmation of rank structure, ruling the paper
out of the A_n/B_n route.

## 6. Zawiślak, Lehmer factorial norm — BODY (the part with the content)
[[../L0.1/lehmer_factorial_norm.body.full.md]] (arXiv:2111.03951, ar5iv body).
Curated: [[L1.1/lehmer_factorial_norm]].
**Establishes:** the factorized-uniform Lehmer-digit structure above (independent,
each digit uniform on its range — Lemmas 3.1/3.3/Cor 3.2), and the full distribution
of the Lehmer factorial norm LF2(σ)=Σ_i(2^i−2^{i−k_i(σ)}) (base-2 weight on Lehmer
digits k_i=c_{n−i}) on every S_n and on S_∞ (Theorems 4.5/4.6, Lemma 4.4).
**Hypotheses / hold here?** The independence lemma is for uniform S_n — holds; that
is precisely what this run averages over. **What it lets the run confirm:** an
independent, cited proof that the factoradic digits rank depends on average
factorized/uniformly — the structural basis of the pair-inversion sums giving
A_n,B_n. **Clean negative:** the norm's distribution (Thms 4.5/4.6) is its own
object, unrelated to A_n,B_n; the paper never treats powers σ^i or the cyclic
subgroup {σ^i} or the sum of ranks there. Marginal structural confirmation; rules
the paper out of the closed-form route.

## 7. Nathanson, Arithmetic functions and fixed points of powers of permutations
[[../L0.1/nathanson_fixed_points_powers.full.md]] (arXiv:2206.04021, Archiv der
Math. 120 (2023) 565–575; abs page + abstract). Curated:
[[L1.1/nathanson_fixed_points_powers]].
**Central identity:** F_X(σ^ℓ)=Σ_{k|ℓ} k·C(k), where F_X(σ^k)=#fixed points of the
k-th power and C(k)=#k-cycles of σ. Reason: x fixed by σ^ℓ iff x lies in a σ-cycle
whose length divides ℓ; a k-cycle contributes its k elements to every ℓ a multiple
of k. The sequence ℓ↦F_X(σ^ℓ) DETERMINES the conjugacy class of σ; Möbius
inversion recovers the cycle counts C(ℓ)=(1/ℓ)Σ_{d|ℓ}μ(ℓ/d)F(σ^d). The paper also
characterises which arithmetic functions are fixed-point-counting functions of
permutations and when the sequence is periodic.
**Hypotheses / hold here?** Unconditional for any permutation of a finite or
countably infinite set. Holds.
**What it lets the run compute:** a THIRD, character-free route to conjugacy-class
data: express any class datum (the a_1=#fixed, a_2=#2-cycle coefficients in the
gap-affine inversion mechanism of [[L2.0/mechanism_pair_inversions]]) through
fixed-points-of-powers counts, and set up / cross-check the cycle-type sums behind
A_n,B_n via Möbius inversion. **Clean negative:** not itself a closed form for
A_n,B_n nor a computation of the {π^i} rank sum. Confirms the centrality of the
fixed-point parameter. Complements — not contradicts — Leaños and Ford.

## 8. Pinsky, The Inversion Statistic in Derangements and in Permutations with a Prescribed Number of Fixed Points — CORRECT EJC source
[[../L0.1/pinsky_inversion_fixed_points.full.md]] (EJC 33(2), #P2.36,
DOI 10.37236/14250 — the EJC article page + abstract). Curated:
[[L1.1/pinsky_inversion_fixed_points]].
**Establishes:** for P_n^(k) (uniform on S_n with exactly k fixed points),
EXACT finite-n formulas for the expected number of inversions and for the per-pair
inversion probability P_n^(k)(σ_i^{-1}<σ_j^{-1}) (i precedes j), 1≤i<j≤n.
Asymptotics: k=0 (derangement) → n(n−1)/4 + n/6 + 1/12 + o(1); k≥2 →
n(n−1)/4 − (k−1)n/6 − (k²−k−1)/12 + o(1); borderline k=1 → +1/12 over uniform.
k, i, j may depend on n. Proofs use the Chinese-restaurant construction.
**Hypotheses / hold here?** Fixed-point-conditioned uniform measures on S_n; exact
finite-n, unconditional. Holds — this is a per-gap inversion probability conditioned
on the fixed-point count, precisely the mechanism's parameter.
**What it lets the run compute:** the missing exact finite-n per-gap
inversion-probability conditioned on a_1, the summand of the A_n,B_n route: average
the per-gap inversion prob over the fixed-point-count distribution of a uniform
permutation. Direct mechanism/route, the fixed-point-conditioned counterpart of
[[pinsky_schickentanz_ewens_html]] (Prop 10a there) from L0.2. **Clean negative:**
NOT itself the closed form for A_n,B_n, does not compute Q(10^6), and does not sum
over {π^i}.

  inside a cyclic orbit" shape (Legendre §3) is solved only for rotation, which is

## What this batch lets the run compute, bound, or rule out
- **Compute (route, not closed form):** A_n,B_n as cycle-type sums via (a) the
  fixed-point-conditioned per-gap inversion probability of Pinsky §8 averaged over
  the fixed-point distribution, cross-checked by (b) Nathanson §7's fixed-points-of-
  powers↔C(k) Möbius route and (c) Hultman §1's character method for class-uniform
  expectations; Leaños §2 confirms root counts are per-type (constant on conjugacy
  classes) so those sums are well-defined by cycle type.
- **Confirm:** the factoradic-digit independence underlying rank (§5/§6) and the
  fixed-point centrality of the gap-affine mechanism (all of §2,§7,§8 agree the
  conjugacy-class datum is cycle-type/fixed-point data).
- **Rule out:** Hultman (§1) for the {π^i} sum (rank not a class function);
  Legendre (§3) for power orbits (rotation only); the dead Pinsky pair (§9);
  Leaños root counts (§2) as a route to Q(n) (they are preimage counts, not the
  orbit rank sum).

Bottom line: this batch gives the fixed-point-conditioned per-gap inversion route to
A_n,B_n (Pinsky EJC), a character-free conjugacy-class cross-check (Nathanson) and a

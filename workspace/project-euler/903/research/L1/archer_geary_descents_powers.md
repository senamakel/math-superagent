# Archer & Geary, "Descents in powers of permutations" (arXiv:2406.09369)

## What it establishes
Enumerates, for special classes, how many π in S_n have π^2 with j descents:
Grassmannian (one-descent) permutations by desc(π^2); all perms with exactly one
descent in π^2 (fully for "small" descent, lower bound otherwise); perms whose
square/cube attain the maximum descent count. Poses **conjecture**
E[des(π^2)] = E[des(π^3]) = (n−1)/2 − 2/n (small n excluded).

## Why it matters here
Provenance and counting-technique source for the descent-in-powers line that
Cambie-Yan ([[cambie_yan_html]], [[cambie_yan_descents_inversions_powers]])
confirmed and extended to explicit divisor-function closed forms for all k and
for inversions (Thm 1.1, 1.2 there). Its Grassmannian-cycle counting (e.g. the
Möbius/divisor form for the Grassmannian count) is the same style of enumeration
a full proof of the gap-linearity of f_n(k) would need.

## Verdict
Supporting/context only. Contains NO closed form for rank(π^i) over the cyclic
subgroup; does not settle the large-exponent regime of Q(n). Full text:
L0 `archer_geary_descents_powers.full.md` (arXiv abs page). Historical detail
(the exact enumerations and the conjecture's original statement) is in that file.

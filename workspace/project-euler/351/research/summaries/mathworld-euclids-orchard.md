## What this source establishes

Euclid's orchard: an array of unit-height "trees" at the integer lattice
points of a point lattice; viewed from a corner along the line y = x, a
quadrant turns into the modified Dirichlet function (Gosper). The classical
visibility picture in which a lattice point is visible from the origin exactly
when it is not blocked by a closer lattice point on the same ray.

Context for the visibility problem family; carries no counting formula itself.
The counting criterion used in this run (gcd = 1 ⟺ visible) is sourced to
MathWorld's Visible Point entry, not this one.

## Why it matters here

- Names the classical setting (lattice points as trees, occlusion along a ray)
  that the hexagonal orchard generalises.
- Cross-links to Orchard Visibility Problem and Visible Point.

## What it does not settle

- Nothing quantitative; no formula for hidden-point counts.

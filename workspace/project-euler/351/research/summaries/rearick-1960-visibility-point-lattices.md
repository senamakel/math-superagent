## What the search-extracted abstract establishes (from the PDF's first page, quoted in search results)

David Francis Rearick, *Some Visibility Problems in Point Lattices*, Ph.D. dissertation, Caltech 1960 (advisor Tom M. Apostol). A lattice point is visible from another if no third lattice point lies on the line joining them. Studies the distribution of visible points in a k-dimensional lattice: the fraction of visible points in an expanding region "usually" tends to 1/ζ(k); arbitrarily large "gaps" containing no visible points exist; the maximum number of lattice points mutually visible in pairs is 2^k; for a fixed set of n pairwise mutually-visible points, the density of points visible from all of them is described. The last section studies a counting function defined by a congruence, arising from a visibility problem.

## Why it matters here

Historical anchor: one of the earliest treatments of lattice-point visibility as a distribution problem (same school as Apostol's number-theory textbook). Its density result 1/ζ(k) and gap results are the qualitative background for the 6/π² visible-point density this run uses as a magnitude anchor; it predates the hexagonal-orchard counting problem and carries no totient connection.

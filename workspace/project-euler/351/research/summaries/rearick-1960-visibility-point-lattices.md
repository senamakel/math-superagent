## What the abstract announces

David Francis Rearick, *Some Visibility Problems in Point Lattices*, Ph.D.
dissertation, Caltech 1960. Studies the distribution of visible points in a
k-dimensional lattice: the fraction of visible points in an expanding region
"usually" tends to a limit; arbitrarily large gaps with no visible points
exist; a typical theorem bounds the maximum number of lattice points mutually
visible in pairs and the density of points visible from each of a fixed set of
mutually-visible points. The last section studies a counting function defined
by a congruence, arising from a visibility problem.

## Why it is in this library

Historical anchor: one of the earliest treatments of lattice-point visibility
as a distribution problem (from the same school as Apostol's number-theory
textbook). Its density/gap results are the qualitative background for the
6/π² visible-point density this run uses as a magnitude anchor; it predates
the hexagonal-orchard counting problem and carries no totient connection.

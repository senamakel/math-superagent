# A088912 — smallest n with σ(n) = (k+1/2)·n

Source: https://oeis.org/A088912 — `[[oeis_A088912.full]]`

## What it establishes

- **Definition.** a(k) = smallest m with σ(m) = (k + 1/2)·m. (This k is the PE 241 k directly.)
- **Terms:** a(1)=2, a(2)=24, a(3)=4320, a(4)=8 910 720, a(5)=17 116 004 505 600 (~1.71e13),
  a(6)=170 974 031 122 008 628 879 954 060 917 200 710 847 692 800 (~1.71e44, 45 digits),
  a(7)=1.27e88 (upper bound), a(8)=2.72e190 (upper bound).
- **Robin's theorem bound (via RH):** if RH holds, the k-th term exceeds exp(exp((k+1/2)/exp(γ))). Concretely a(6) > 5e16 and a(7) > 1.9e29 — a *hypothesis-conditional* lower bound, but decisive at our magnitude regardless of RH because the explicit a(6)≈1.7e44 is unconditional and already >> 1e18.
- 2 is the only m with σ(m)=1.5·m (i.e. the k=1 case has exactly one solution, n=2).
- Reference structure: a(k) is the smallest term of A159907 with that abundancy; entries grouped by abundancy 5/2,7/2,9/2,11/2,13/2 in A141643/A055153/A141645/A159271/A160678.

## What it lets this run do

For n ≤ 10^18 the only attainable half-integer abundancies are 3/2,5/2,7/2,9/2,11/2 (k=1..5), because any n with abundancy 13/2 = (13/2)n satisfies n ≥ a(6) ≈ 1.7e44 > 10^18. Confirms and sharpens [[hemiperfect_wikipedia]]'s bound with the exact a-values; closes the 2-adic "only these k" search over the DFS.

## Does not settle

Gives least element per abundancy, not the complete set ≤ 10^18; that complete set and its sum is the computation.

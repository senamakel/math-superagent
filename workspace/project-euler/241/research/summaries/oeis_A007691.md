# A007691 — multiply perfect numbers (integer abundancy)

Source: https://oeis.org/A007691 — `[[oeis_A007691.full]]`

## Definition

n in A007691 iff sigma(n)/n is an integer >= 1, i.e. n is a **multiply perfect**
number (n | sigma(n)). Equivalently, the sum of the reciprocals of the divisors
of n is an integer. First terms: 1, 6, 28, 120, 496, 672, 8128, 30240, 32760,
523776, 2178540, 23569920, 33550336, ... (includes the perfect numbers
6, 28, 496, 8128, 33550336 as the abundance-2 subset; 120, 672, ... are
triperfect, abundance 3).

## Why it is the adjacent theory for this problem

This problem (hemiperfect numbers, sigma(n)/n = integer + 1/2) is the
**half-integer abundancy** twin of the multiply-perfect (integer abundancy)
theory. The OEIS crossref graph (from A160678) puts them side by side: A007691
(abundancy integer) ↔ A159907 (abundancy half-integer), A000396 (2) ↔ 3/2 etc.
Structural results on multiply-perfect numbers (e.g. no odd multiplerfect known,
all even, density results) echo the evenness of hemiperfects that the run's
2-adic reduction relies on.

## What it establishes for this run

- Confirms there is **no odd multiply-perfect number known** (all terms even), and
  none below 10^18 — which underlies the A242484/A159907 near-coincidence claim
  (hemiperfects sit inside A242484 unless an odd multiply-perfect number exists).
- Bach, Miller & Shallit: the members can be recognized in polynomial time with
  arbitrarily small error (a computational-theory footnote, not used here).
- Conjectures (practicality of multiply-perfect numbers; infinitude) are
  background, not bounds for n <= 10^18.

## Does not settle

Gives no bound or enumeration method for the half-integer abundancy sum under
10^18, and no overlap with the qualifying set (a number cannot have both integer
and half-integer abundancy). Recorded for coverage of the adjacent theory, which
the rogue-run failure mode (inventing arXiv ids and mis-titling papers) is most
likely to fake — now backed by a real OEIS record.

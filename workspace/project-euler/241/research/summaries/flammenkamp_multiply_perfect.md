## What it is

The authoritative database + methods page for **multiply perfect numbers** (n | sigma(n),
abundancy sigma(n)/n an integer), the integer-abundancy twin of this problem's
half-integer-abundancy (hemiperfect) family. ~5932 known MPNs, tables by abundancy, smallest
k-perfect numbers, discoverers, algebraic substitution transformations, and the essential
computational-method statement relevant to this run.

## The load-bearing content for THIS problem

**(1) Exhaustive tree-search is the standard, proven method, and it scales with the
description, not the bound.**
> "In March 2008 Achim Flammenkamp finally constructed all MPNs < e^350 by an exhaustive
> tree-search, i.e. proved that there are no further but the 730 known MPNs."

This is the *primary, independent confirmation* that the denominator-driven branching /
tree-search over prime powers (which is exactly the method `pe241_technique.md` describes for
half-integer abundancy, and which the run's DFS implements) is the classical successful way to
enumerate this family of divisibility-of-sigma numbers to astronomically large bounds (here
e^350 ≈ 10^152) without scanning candidates up to the bound. That is precisely the property
the method policy demands (cost grows with the *description*, never with the bound 10^18).

**(2) The 2-adic / "two-power exponent" structure** (Fred Helenius' effective exponent):
the prime 2 is special because p−1=1, so the 2-power factor of a MPN (and by the same parity
logic, of a hemiperfect) governs the search. The effective-exponent analysis of how many
2-factors must still be generated by other prime powers is the multiplicative twin of the run's
v2(sigma(u)) = a−1 reduction for hemiperfects. Corroborates the parity/2-adic structure that
the DFS rests on, from the primary computational source.

**(3) Substitution transformation**: if nX is k-perfect and X·sigma(nY) = Y·sigma(nX) with
gcd conditions, then nY is also k-perfect. Not used by the run, but it is the known structural
device that generates many MPNs and confirms the multiplicativity decompositions the whole
abundancy method uses.

A007539

a(n) = first n-fold perfect (or n-multiperfect) number.
(Formerly M4297)

25

1, 6, 120, 30240, 14182439040, 154345556085770649600, 141310897947438348259849402738485523264343544818565120000

( [list][4]; [graph][5]; [refs][6]; [listen][7]; [history][8]; [text][9]; [internal format][10])

OFFSET

1,2

COMMENTS

On the Riemann Hypothesis, a(n) > exp(exp(n / e^gamma)) for n > 3. Unconditionally, a(n) > exp(exp(0.9976n / e^gamma)) for n > 3, where the constant is related to [A004394][11] (1000000). - [Charles R Greathouse IV][12], Sep 06 2012

Each of the terms 1, 6, 120, 30240 divides all larger terms <= a(8). See [A227765][13], [A227766][14], ..., [A227769][15]. - [Jonathan Sondow][16], Jul 30 2013

Is a(n) < a(n+1)? - [Jeppe Stig Nielsen][17], Jun 16 2015

Equivalently, a(n) is the smallest number k such that sigma(k)/k = n. - [Derek Orr][18], Jun 19 2015

The number of divisors of these terms are: 1, 4, 16, 96, 1920, 110592, 1751777280, 63121588161085440. - [Michel Marcus][19], Jun 20 2015

Given n, let S_n be the sequence of integers k that satisfy numerator(sigma(k)/k) = n. Then a(n) is a member of S_n. In fact a(n) = S_n(i), where the successive values of i are 1, 1, 2, 2, 4, 2, (23, 6, 31, 12, ...), where the terms in parentheses need to be confirmed. - [Michel Marcus][19], Nov 22 2015

The first four terms are the only multiperfect numbers in [A025487][20] among the 1600 initial terms of [A007691][21]. Can it be proved that these are the only ones among the whole [A007691][21]? See also [A349747][22]. - [Antti Karttunen][23], Dec 04 2021

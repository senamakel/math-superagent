In [mathematics][3], a **multiply perfect number**(also called **multiperfect number**or **pluperfect number**) is a generalization of a [perfect number][4].

For a given [natural number][5]*k*, a number *n*is called *k*-perfect (or *k*-fold perfect) if the sum of all positive [divisors][6] of *n*(the [divisor function][7], *σ*(*n*)) is equal to *kn*; a number is thus [perfect][4] [if and only if][8] it is 2-perfect. A number that is *k*-perfect for a certain *k*is called a multiply perfect number. As of 2026 [[update]][9], *k*-perfect numbers are known to exist for each value of *k*up to 11. [1]

It is unknown whether there are any [odd][10] multiply perfect numbers other than 1. The first few multiply perfect numbers are:

1, 6, 28, 120, 496, 672, 8128, 30240, 32760, 523776, 2178540, 23569920, 33550336, 45532800, 142990848, 459818240,14182439040, 4364925090, 8589869056 ... (sequence [A007691][11] in the [OEIS][12]).

## Example

[[edit][13]]

The sum of the divisors of 120 is

1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 8 + 10 + 12 + 15 + 20 + 24 + 30 + 40 + 60 + 120 = 360

which is 3 × 120. Therefore 120 is a 3-perfect number.

## Properties

[[edit][22]]

It can be [proven][23] that:

- For a given [prime number][24]*p*, if *n*is *p*-perfect and *p*does not divide *n*, then *pn*is (*p*+ 1)-perfect. This implies that an [integer][25]*n*is a 3-perfect number divisible by 2 but not by 4, if and only if *n*/2 is an odd [perfect number][4], of which none are known.
- If 3*n*is 4*k*-perfect and 3 does not divide *n*, then *n*is 3*k*-perfect.

## Odd multiply perfect numbers

[[edit][26]]

Unsolved problem in mathematics

Are there any odd multiply perfect numbers?

[More unsolved problems in mathematics][27]

It is unknown whether there are any odd multiply perfect numbers other than 1. However if an odd *k*-perfect number *n*exists where *k*> 2, then it must satisfy the following conditions: [2]

- The largest prime factor is ≥ 100129
- The second largest prime factor is ≥ 1009
- The third largest prime factor is ≥ 101

If an odd triperfect number exists, it must be greater than 10 128. [3]

Tóth found several numbers that would be odd multiperfect, if one of their factors was a square. An example is 8999757, which would be an odd multiperfect number, if only one of its prime factors, 61, was a square. [4] This is closely related to the concept of [Descartes numbers][28].

## Specific values of *k*

[[edit][37]]

### Perfect numbers

[[edit][38]]

Main article: [Perfect number][4]

A number *n*with σ(*n*) = 2*n*is **perfect**.

### Triperfect numbers

[[edit][39]]

A number *n*with σ(*n*) = 3*n*is **triperfect**. There are only six known triperfect numbers and these are believed to comprise all such numbers:

120, 672, 523776, 459818240, 1476304896, 51001180160 (sequence [A005820][40] in the [OEIS][12])

If there exists an odd perfect number *m*(a famous [open problem][41]) then 2*m*would be 3-perfect, since σ(2*m*) = σ(2)σ(*m*) = 3×2*m*. An odd triperfect number must be a [square number][42] exceeding 10 70 and have at least 12 distinct prime factors, the largest exceeding 10 5. [8]

## Variations

[[edit][43]]

### Unitary multiply perfect numbers

[[edit][44]]

A similar extension can be made for [unitary perfect numbers][45]. A positive integer *n*is called a **unitary multi***k*-**perfect****number**if σ *(*n*) = *kn*where σ *(*n*) is the sum of its [unitary divisors][46]. A **unitary multiply perfect number**is a unitary multi *k*-perfect number for some positive integer *k*. A unitary multi 2-perfect number is also called a **unitary perfect number**.

In the case *k*> 2, no example of a unitary multi *k*-perfect number is yet known. It is known that if such a number exists, it must be [even][10] and greater than 10 102 and must have at least 45 odd prime factors. [9]

The first few unitary multiply perfect numbers are:

1, 6, 60, 90, 87360 (sequence [A327158][47] in the [OEIS][12])

### Bi-unitary multiply perfect numbers

[[edit][48]]

A positive integer *n*is called a **bi-unitary multi***k*-**perfect****number**if σ **(*n*) = *kn*where σ **(*n*) is the sum of its [bi-unitary divisors][49]. A **bi-unitary multiply perfect number**is a bi-unitary multi *k*-perfect number for some positive integer *k*. [10] A bi-unitary multi 2-perfect number is also called a **bi-unitary perfect number**, and a bi-unitary multi 3-perfect number is called a **bi-unitary triperfect number**.

In 1987, Peter Hagis proved that there are no odd bi-unitary multiperfect numbers other than 1. [10]

In 2020, Haukkanen and Sitaramaiah studied bi-unitary triperfect numbers of the form 2*a**u*where *u*is odd. They completely resolved the cases 1 ≤ *a*≤ 6 and *a*= 8, and partially resolved the case *a*= 7. [11] [12] [13] [14] [15] [16]

In 2024, Tomohiro Yamada proved that 2160 is the only bi-unitary triperfect number divisible by 27 = 3 3. [17] This means that Yamada found all biunitary triperfect numbers of the form 3*a**u*with 3 ≤ *a*and *u*not divisible by 3.

The first few bi-unitary multiply perfect numbers are:

1, 6, 60, 90, 120, 672, 2160, 10080, 22848, 30240 (sequence [A189000][50] in the [OEIS][12])

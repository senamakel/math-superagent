# Mersenne-denominator constraint: exploit the explicit factorization of 2^(a+1)-1 to partition the odd-part search

```approach
idea: In the 2-adic split σ(u)/u = (2k+1)·2^(a-1)/(2^(a+1)-1), the denominator D = 2^(a+1)-1 is a Mersenne number with a known factorization into Mersenne primes and composite Mersenne factors. The condition that D (reduced) divides u means u must contain each prime factor of D with at least the exponent it carries in D. Write u = D_red · w with gcd(w, D_red) = 1, and derive the reduced equation for w: σ(D_red·w)/(D_red·w) = T, where the left side factors via multiplicativity when w is coprime to D_red. This partitions the odd-part search by the "Mersenne core" D_red, which is exponentially smaller than u and whose prime factors are known exactly — the Mersenne factors of 2^(a+1)-1
mechanism: For each a (1 to 60), compute D = 2^(a+1)-1 and its reduced form D_red after canceling common factors with the numerator (2k+1)·2^(a-1). Factor D_red completely (this is feasible: a ≤ 60 means D ≤ 2^61-1 ≈ 2.3e18, factorable by trial division or lookup). Write u = D_red · w with gcd(w, D_red) = 1. Then σ(u)/u = σ(D_red)/D_red · σ(w)/w = T_{a,k}. Since σ(D_red)/D_red is a known rational number (computed exactly from the factorization of D_red), the residual target for w is T' = T_{a,k} · D_red/σ(D_red), which is a rational with a much smaller denominator. Now search for odd w coprime to D_red with σ(w)/w = T'. This is a genuine structural reduction: the hardest part of the denominator (the Mersenne factors) is extracted and solved once per (a,k) pair, and the residual search for w operates on a much simpler rational target. The approach is different from the generic denominator-cancellation DFS because it exploits the specific number-theoretic structure of the denominator as a Mersenne number, rather than treating it as an arbitrary integer to be cleared prime-by-prime.
status: proposed
first-step: For a = 1..60, factor D = 2^(a+1)-1 (use sympy.ntheory.factorint or a Mersenne factor table). For each (a,k) with k = 1..5, compute T_{a,k} = (2k+1)·2^(a-1)/(2^(a+1)-1) in lowest terms. Let D_red be the reduced denominator. Compute the "Mersenne core" abundancy σ(D_red)/D_red exactly (by factoring D_red and applying multiplicativity). Compute the residual target T' = T_{a,k} · D_red/σ(D_red). Tabulate which (a,k) pairs yield a feasible T' (numerator/denominator allowing completion below 10^18/2^a). Then run the denominator-cancellation DFS for w over odd primes coprime to D_red.
precedent: none checked — the explicit use of Mersenne factor tables to pre-clear the denominator in hemiperfect searches is a natural idea but has not been checked against the literature; the general technique of "extract the forced prime factors from the denominator" is the denominator-forcing lemma (property22-denominator-divides) already in the library, but the specific claim that pre-clearing the entire Mersenne denominator D_red at once (rather than iteratively clearing its prime factors) yields a distinct algorithm has not been evaluated
```

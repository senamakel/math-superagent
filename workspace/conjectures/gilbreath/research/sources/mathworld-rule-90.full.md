# Rule 90

---

[image: DOWNLOAD Mathematica Notebook] [Download Wolfram Notebook][1]

[image: ElementaryCARule090]

Rule 90 is one of the [elementary cellular automaton][2] rules introduced by Stephen Wolfram in 1983 (Wolfram 1983, 2002). It specifies the next color in a cell, depending on its color and its immediate neighbors. Its rule outcomes are encoded in the [binary][3] representation [image: 90=01011010_2]. This rule is illustrated above together with the evolution of a single black cell it produces after 15 steps (Wolfram 2002, p. [55][4]).

Starting with a single black cell, successive generations are given by interpreting the numbers 1, 5, 17, 85, 257, 1285, 4369, 21845, ... (OEIS [A038183][5]) in binary, namely as 1, 101, 10001, 1010101, 100000001, ... (OEIS [A070886][6]).

Rule 90 is [amphichiral][7], and its complement is rule 165.

[image: SierpinskiSievePascal]

The fractal produced by this rule was described by Sierpiński in 1915 and appearing in Italian art from the 13th century (Wolfram 2002, p. [43][8]). It is therefore also known as the [Sierpiński sieve][9], Sierpiński gasket, or Sierpiński triangle. The [binomial coefficient][10][image: (m; n)] mod 2 can be computed using the [XOR][11] operation [image: n] XOR [image: m], making [Pascal's triangle][12] mod 2 very easy to construct. Moreover, coloring all [odd][13] numbers black and [even][14] numbers white in [Pascal's triangle][12] produces a [Sierpiński sieve][9] (Guy 1990; Wolfram 2002, p. [870][15]).

[image: Rule 90 animation]

Rule 90 is one of the eight [additive][16] [elementary cellular automata][2] (Wolfram 2002, p. [952][17]).

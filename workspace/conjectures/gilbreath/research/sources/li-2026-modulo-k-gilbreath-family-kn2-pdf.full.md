THE MODULO-k GILBREATH FAMILY: A UNIFIED
GENERALIZATION OF THE GILBREATH CONJECTURE

LI DONG

Abstract. This paper introduces and systematically studies a new family of Gilbreath-
type iterated difference phenomena. For any odd integer k ≥ 1, we consider the sequence
of all primes of the form kn + 2. Repeatedly taking absolute differences of adjacent en-
tries yields a difference triangle in which, from the second row onward, the leading entry
eventually stabilizes to k. This phenomenon constitutes an infinite family of structures
parallel to the classical Gilbreath conjecture, termed the “modulo-k Gilbreath family.”
The classical conjecture is recovered as the degenerate case when k = 1. Computational
verification is provided for all odd k < 100,000. We discuss the modular invariance re-
sponsible for this stability, investigate the inadmissible residue classes that cause localized
collapses, and explore the resulting fault tolerance in higher-order entries.

1. Introduction and Background

The classical Gilbreath conjecture (1958) asserts that, starting from the sequence of
all primes 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, . . . ,
repeatedly taking absolute differences of adjacent entries produces a difference triangle
where the leading entry of every successive row is 1. While verified to 10
13, the conjecture
remains unproven. We observe that restricting attention to the arithmetic progression
kn + 2 yields a parallel “mod-k version,” naturally generalizing the classical case to an
infinite parametric family for any odd k ≥ 1.

2. A Unified Generalization of Gilbreath Conjecture

Conjecture 2.1 (Modulo-k Gilbreath Conjecture). Let P (k) = {p1, p2, p3, . . . } be the
ordered sequence of all primes such that pi ≡ 2 (mod k). Note that p1 = 2 and p2 is the
smallest prime greater than 2 congruent to 2 modulo k. Construct a difference triangle
D where di,0 = pi and di,j = |di,j−1 − di+1,j−1| for j ≥ 1. Then, there exists some J such
that for all j ≥ J, the leading entry d1,j = k.

Remark 2.2. When k = 1, the sequence P (1) coincides with the set of all prime num-
bers {2, 3, 5, 7, . . . }, rendering the conjecture perfectly equivalent to the classical Gilbreath
conjecture. Consequently, this family of conjectures serves as a natural extension of the
classical one within the framework of arithmetic progressions.

3. Modular Invariance

The stability of the “k-seed” can be explained through the property of modular preser-
vation:
• Base Difference: d1,1 = |p2 − 2|, which is a positive multiple of k (equal to k
precisely when k + 2 is prime).

• Modular Constraint: For all i, pi ≡ 2 (mod k). Therefore, pi+1 − pi ≡ 0
(mod k). By induction, every entry di,j for j ≥ 1 must be a multiple of k.
• Persistence: Because absolute differences of multiples of k cannot produce a
non-zero value smaller than k, the leading entry is “trapped” at k or higher. The
density of primes ensures that these values eventually collapse to the minimal
non-zero multiple, k.

5. Inadmissible Residue Classes and Fault Tolerance

To maintain strict leading-entry stability, the absolute difference operator must act on
consecutive prime elements generated by the form kn + 2. For notational convenience, let
an = kn + 2 for n ≥ 0, such that P (k) = {an | an is prime}. If an entry an is composite,
it must be skipped in the prime sequence, thereby altering the step size.

Definition 5.1 (Inadmissible Residue Classes). Let m ≥ 2 be an integer. A residue class
c (mod m) is called inadmissible if the condition k ≡ c (mod m) forces at least one
entry an in the sequence to be a proper multiple of m, thereby disrupting the primality
required for localized difference stability.

To guarantee that the leading entry of the absolute difference remains k, both a1 = k+2
and a3 = 3k + 2 must be prime. This leads to the following inadmissible classes modulo
m: • From k + 2 ≡ 0 (mod m), we obtain the first-level inadmissible class: k ≡ −2
(mod m).
• From 3k + 2 ≡ 0 (mod m), we obtain the second-level inadmissible class: k ≡
−2 · 3−1 (mod m), where 3
−1 denotes the multiplicative inverse of 3 modulo m.
For composite modulo, the inadmissible classes are inherited from the union of their
prime factors via lifting. For instance, consider m = 10:
• From m = 2, we have k ≡ 0 (mod 2) ⇒ k ∈ {0, 2, 4, 6, 8} (mod 10).
• From m = 5, solving k + 2 ≡ 0 and 3k + 2 ≡ 0 (mod 5) yields k ≡ 3, 1 (mod 5).
Lifting to modulo 10, this generates k ≡ 1, 3, 6, 8 (mod 10).
Taking the union yields the inadmissible residue classes modulo 10: k ≡ 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8
(mod 10).

Theorem 5.2 (Fault Tolerance of Higher-Order Entries). Let a5 = 5k+2 and a7 = 7k+2.
The sequence exhibits a form of fault tolerance: strict leading-entry stability is preserved
as long as at least one of a5 or a7 remains prime. For any prime m > 2, no jointly
inadmissible residue class arises. This follows because gcd(5k +2, 7k +2) = gcd(5k +2, 2k)
and any common prime divisor m of both a5 and a7 would have to divide 2k. However, if
m | k, then 5k + 2 ≡ 2 (mod m), which contradicts m dividing 5k + 2 (since m > 2).

However, jointly inadmissible classes may arise through cross-modular interactions via
the Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT). For example, by letting a5 be divisible by 13
and a7 be divisible by 11:

Solving this system yields the advanced jointly inadmissible class k ≡ 127 (mod 143).
Under this specific residue class, both a5 and a7 fail simultaneously, destroying the fault
tolerance and causing the leading entry of the third row to deviate from k.

6. Conclusion

This paper establishes the modulo-k Gilbreath family as a natural generalization of
the classical Gilbreath conjecture. By embedding the k = 1 case into the broader frame-
work of arithmetic progressions kn + 2, we demonstrate that the stability of the leading
entry emerges from a synergistic effect: modular invariance combined with a robust fault-
tolerance mechanism.
While the generalized conjecture is structurally more complex due to the arithmetic ob-
structions of inadmissible residue classes, the existence of strictly stable k values suggests
a localized simplicity that may be more analytically tractable. Future research should
focus on the distribution and density of these strictly stable integers and explore whether
the modular structures identified here can be incorporated into a unified probabilistic
framework for difference triangles generated by structured sequences.

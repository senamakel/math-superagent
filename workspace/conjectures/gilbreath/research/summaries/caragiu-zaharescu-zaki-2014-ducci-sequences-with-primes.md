## What it establishes

Introduces a Ducci-type analogue on prime d-tuples: the GPF-Ducci map
`G(p₁,…,p_d) = (gpf(p₁+p₂), gpf(p₂+p₃), …, gpf(p_d+p₁))` with `gpf` the
greatest prime factor (`gpf(1)=1`). The stated motivation is a series of
results on "greatest prime factor sequences" (Back–Caragiu; ultimate
periodicity conjecture), in particular the GPF-Fibonacci sequence
`x_n = gpf(x_{n−1}+x_{n−2})`, all of whose orbits enter the unique limit
cycle `(3,5,2,7)`.

- **Proposition 1**: every GPF-Ducci iteration is ultimately periodic
  (choose `p ≡ 1 (mod 3)`, `p ≥ max` seed; `K_p = {primes ≤ p}` is closed
  under `(r,s) ↦ gpf(r+s)`).
- **Theorem 2 (main)**: if the limit cycle has length `> 1`, then every
  vector in the cycle has all components in `P₀ = {2,3,5,7}` (first four
  primes), and conversely every element of `P₀` occurs as a component of some
  cycle vector. Proof by the largest-entry `q`: `q` odd; `q` arises only from
  `(q,q)` or `(2,q−2)`/`(q−2,2)` with `q−2` prime (Lemma 1); `q−2` must
  occur (Lemma 2); from there `q ≤ 7` (Lemma 3) and 2,3,5,7 all occur
  (Lemma 4, a local segment/cellular-automaton argument).
- Cycle-length data for `3 ≤ d ≤ 8` from exhaustive search over the `4^d`
  seeds in `P₀^d` (lengths 1,2,4,8,12,16,20,27,30,32,40,48,54,80,126,168;
  e.g. d=4: 232×length-1, 4×2, 4×4, 16×8).

## Relation to this run

This is the strongest "adjacent problems" source the library now holds: the
same absolute-difference-iteration template, with the value operation
replaced by `gpf(+)`, provably collapses *from any prime seed* into a
4-element set `{2,3,5,7}` — structurally the exact shape of what Gilbreath's
conjecture asserts (collapse of the prime triangle from any start to the
two-element `{1}`/`{0,2}` regime) but here *proved*, because the map is
bounded (closed finite set) rather than unbounded. The boundedness is the
whole difference: `gpf` sends everything into `P₀`, whereas `|a−b|` has no
finite closed set, so the Gilbreath collapse must be forced by something
other than compactness. Also provides a new **proof template**: the
"largest entry must be generated by a restricted local shape, else the count
of maximal entries decreases around a cycle" argument (Lemmas 1–3) is
exactly a Ducci-style potential argument on the maximum — supporting the
approach `ducci-potential-max-decrease`.

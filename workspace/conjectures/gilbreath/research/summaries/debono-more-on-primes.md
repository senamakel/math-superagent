A didactic "Numbers and Computers" column cited by MathWorld, Caldwell and the OEIS.

## What it establishes

- The historical origin and definition of the **a(n) sequence that is OEIS A000232**:
  count terms before the first difference > 2 in the n-th row of the Gilbreath triangle.
  Debono's values (a(1)=3, a(2)=8, a(3)=14, a(4)=14, a(5)=25, ... a(20)=291, a(50)=2763)
  match OEIS A000232 exactly — an independent, pre-OEIS computational source for the
  catalogue values the run's `block-profile-equals-a000232-minus-1` claim uses.
- The conjecture statement (Gilbreath 1958: first term of each row is 1; verified for
  billions of rows by computers, no proof) — no content beyond the encyclopedic tier.

## Bearing

Confirms the run's A000232 data and gives the sequence its proper historical name (Debono's
a(n)). No theorem content; cite as corroboration of the catalogue, never as a source of the
conjecture's mathematics.

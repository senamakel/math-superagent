## Description

Gilbreath&rsquo;s conjecture posits that if successive absolute differences are
applied to the sequence of prime numbers, the leading term of each resulting row will
always be 1. Historically viewed as a mystery tied to the pseudo-random distribution
of primes, this property is resolved here through a paradigm shift. By applying this
operator not to primes alone, but to the generating function f(n) of the Absolute
Space (Arithmetic Index Theory) preceded by 2 and 3, we prove that the difference
triangle instantly converges to a static invariant vector V = [1, 2, 0, 0, . . . ]. Since
prime numbers are strictly a subset of f(n), they inherit this matrix damping wave.
Gilbreath&rsquo;s "1" is thus not an intrinsic property of primes, but the perpetual echo
of the initial asymmetry (2, 3) sliding along the topological edge of the absolute
matrix.

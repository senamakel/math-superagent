# Higher-order prime-gap structure (Kumar et al.; WP 2006) — recorded by digest

Two paywalled papers study the *same iterated-difference rows* this run computes, from the
time-series side. Recorded here from search digests; neither full text is in the library.

## What the digests establish

- **Kumar et al., "Information entropy and correlation in prime numbers"** (arXiv:cond-
  mat/0303110; via WP 2006): studied prime gaps (first differences) and increments
  (second differences), finding the histogram of increments has periodicity near
  multiples of 6, and that increases in gap sizes are less frequent for multiples of 6.
- **WP 2006, "Hidden structure in the randomness of the prime number sequence?"** (Physica
  A, S0378437105006473): the apparent regularities (spikes near multiples of 6 in first
  and iterated differences) are **not intrinsic structure** but arise from the primes'
  underlying randomness; develops a theoretical model of iterated prime differences using
  Dirichlet's theorem + limited-correlation assumption, accurate for finite subsequences
  modulo transient effects (Chebyshev's bias).
- **"The gaps between the gaps"** (Physica A S0378437104007277): higher-order gaps (k-th
  iterated differences) of the PG-series exhibit patterns **not reproduced by simulated
  gap series** built from empirical first-gap frequencies — i.e. some higher-order
  deviation from the naive benchmark; no explanatory theory offered.

## Bearing on this run

These are the closest thing in the literature to an *empirical study of the actual rows*
this run computes (not just the left edge). WP 2006's conclusion — the observable
regularity of iterated prime differences is a *consequence of randomness + Dirichlet
structure*, not of arithmetic rigidity — is consistent with (and orthogonal to) the run's
"not about primes" framing: it says the rows look random *because prime gaps are
statistically random*, whereas the run's claim is that the {0,2} regime would arise for any
sufficiently non-concentrated odd-gap sequence. Both point the same way: a proof must
handle arrangement/randomness, not primality.

## What it establishes

- The paper studies classes of *increasing integer sequences* (iis): tournament sequences,
  Meeussen sequences, addition chains, permutations, and sequences with the Goldbach
  property (every integer > 1 is a sum of two elements). It introduces and compares the
  classes, with recurrence relations and enumerative results.
- Gilbreath relevance: the definitional framing — an iis is *Gilbreath* iff every leading
  entry of its iterated absolute-difference triangle is ≤ 1 (equivalently = 1 for the 2-then-
  odds shapes), echoing the run's reduction; and it is noted (as the digest already had) that
  Gilbreath sequences and Goldbach sequences are independent classes (neither contains the
  other). No theorem about the primes' GC.
- Cites Odlyzko 1993, Guy §A10 [UPiNT], Dusart's p_k bounds, Erdős–Lewin d-complete
  sequences — a useful bibliography of the "integer-sequence classes vs. Gilbreath"
  neighbourhood.

## Bearing on this run

Confirms (sourced) the *taxonomy* framing the run's GOAL assumes: GC sits inside a zoo of
combinatorial "increasing integer sequence" properties, and the run's general-class question
("which 2-then-odds classes satisfy GC?") is a recognised, only-partially-charted region.
The paper itself proves nothing about the primes; cite it for the classification context,
not for a theorem.

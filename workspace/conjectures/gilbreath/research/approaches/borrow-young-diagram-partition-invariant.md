# borrow-young-diagram-partition-invariant

```approach
idea: The binary min-branch sites generated by |a−b| = a + b − 2·min(a,b) form, across the triangle, a downward-closed set; propose that this set is a Young diagram whose shape is a monotone (partition-valued) invariant of the operator — a set/partition-valued invariant immune to the scalar-potential refutation, distinct from RSK (whose monotonicity of first-row length killed it), and forcing A_k(1) ∈ {0,2} by a shape argument.
mechanism: |
  |a−b| = a + b − 2·min(a,b): the correction min(a,b) is exactly the part that
  must be subtracted to turn the linear sum a+b into the absolute difference.
  At each cell (k,i), the min branch records which ancestor (a_i vs a_{i+1})
  was smaller. Stack these min-branch sites over the (row, column) grid. The
  proposal is that this subset is downward-closed (a min-branch choice at one
  cell forces a min-branch region below it, by the overlapping-window stencil),
  hence a Young-diagram-like shape, and that the SHAPE (a partition, compared
  in dominance order) is monotone under the operator. This is the right class
  of invariant to hunt after the scalar potentials died: the machine-refutation
  ((0,0,1,1) → (0,1,0)) kills real-valued potentials only, not set/partition-
  valued ones. RSK's partition-valued candidate failed for a specific reason
  (first-row length is monotone while b_k strictly drifts down 1 each erosion
  row); a borrow/min-branch shape's monotonicity, if it holds, is a genuine
  arithmetic property of subtraction, not an artifact of row insertion. If the
  shape is monotone and its first row bounds the left-edge value, the
  conjecture is the statement that this first row stays below a fixed length —
  a direct-invariant route with no block tracking.
status: proposed
first-step: |
  Compute the min-branch site subset S = {(k,i) : the min(a,b) branch was taken
  at cell (k,i)} for the depth-1000 prime triangle (blocks_depth1000.json,
  exact integers). Test (i) downward-closedness of S and (ii) whether the
  row-by-row boundary of S forms a monotone partition in dominance order.
  Report the first violation, if any. If none in depth 1000, this is a
  candidate partition-valued invariant, and the next step is a proof attempt of
  monotonicity. Cost O(depth × width), one row live.
```

## Why this is not on disk

- Not `p-adic-valuation-carry-dynamics` (proposed): that tracks v_2, a *scalar*
  valuation. This tracks the *shape* of the min-branch sites, a partition-valued
  object, and aims for a monotonicity theorem in the partition lattice.
- Not `rsk-greene-growth-diagram-last-passage` (refuted): RSK first-row length
  is monotone in its input while b_k drifts down; the borrow/min-branch shape's
  monotonicity is a property of subtraction itself, so the RSK monotonicity
  obstruction does not transfer.
- Not a scalar potential (machine-refuted family): this is explicitly
  set/partition-valued, the class the scalar refutation does not touch.

## What would falsify it

Downward-closedness or shape-monotonicity fails on the real rows: the min-branch
subset is not a Young diagram, or its boundary is not monotone in dominance
order. That is a cheap, decisive first check before any theory is invoked.

## Side

General-class (the min-branch structure is universal for the operator). Aims at
a direct invariant forcing A_k(1) ∈ {0,2}, not at the regeneration accounting.

## Named mathematics

Carry/borrow propagation in binary arithmetic, the identity |a−b| = a+b−2min(a,b),
Young diagrams and partitions, the dominance (majorization) order, the Diaconis–
Fulman treatment of carries in base-p addition as a Young-diagram-like structure.

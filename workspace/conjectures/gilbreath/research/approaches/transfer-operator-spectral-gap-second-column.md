  a suitable Banach space of observables strictly less than 1 away from the
  {0,1}-valued eigenspace — would imply that the row process converges
  *geometrically* to the {0,1} (halved) stratum, hence A_k(1) ∈ {0,2}

  This is the thermodynamic-formalism route, not the linear-oscillation route
  that the variation-diminishing attempt took: the operator P encodes the
  *nonlinear* absolute-value map, and its quasi-compactness / spectral gap is
  the object, not sign-regularity of a signed Pascal matrix. Nor is it the
  refuted pointwise-contraction route: a transfer operator can have spectral
  gap < 1 while the map has no metric contraction (the two notions coincide
  only for expanding maps on a single metric, which the pair (4,0,0)/(4,2,0)

  Lipschitz observables (à la Ionescu-Tulcea–Marinescu / Lasota–Yorke), where
  quasi-compactness can hold even for maps that are not uniformly expanding
  because the operator's essential spectrum is controlled by oscillation of
  observables, not by a contraction constant of the map.

## Why this is not already on disk

- **Not `ifs-attractor-contraction` (refuted):** that required a metric contraction, refuted by (4,0,0)/(4,2,0). A spectral gap of the *transfer operator* is not a metric contraction; it lives in a function space where quasi-compactness can survive.
- **Not `gantmacher-krein-oscillatory-matrix-sign-regularity` (refuted) / `walsh-hadamard` (refuted):** no variation-diminishing or finite-linear claim. `P` encodes the full nonlinear map, and the spectral object is on a Banach space of observables, not on a signed Pascal matrix.
- **Not `sofic-block-suffix-subshift` (refuted):** no finite-state/alphabet-stability claim about the *input*; the operator acts on the halved *rows*, and the alphabet truncation is only a finite-n probe.

## What it would take for this to be wrong

If the top eigenvalues of the finite-n truncations approach 1 (no gap opens as m grows), or if the truncation boundary is essential and the gap is a truncation artifact, then the transfer operator is not quasi-compact and the route fails. The first-step eigenvalue computation is the cheap decisive test.

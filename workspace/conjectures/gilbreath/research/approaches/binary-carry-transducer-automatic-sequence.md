# binary-carry-transducer-automatic-sequence

```approach
idea: Treat the Gilbreath triangle as a 2-dimensional *automatic array* over the binary expansions: the cell map (a,b) ↦ |a−b| is a finite Mealy transducer (subtraction-with-borrow, plus a left-to-right comparison to fix the sign), so the coarse-grained triangle is generated by a finite automaton reading base-2 digits, and the conjecture A_k(1) ∈ {0,2} becomes a boundedness statement about a diagonal of a 2D automatic array — attacked with Cobham's theorem, Christol's theorem, and the Allouche–Shallit theory of k-regular sequences.
mechanism: |
  |a−b| is computed by two finite transducers in base 2: (i) a 3-state
  left-to-right comparator (so-far equal / a greater / b greater) decides the
  sign of a−b; (ii) a 2-state right-to-left subtractor with borrow produces
  the magnitude. Their composition is a finite transducer, so each Gilbreath
  cell A_k(i) = T^{k}(gap row)[i] is a finite-state function of the binary
  digits of its k+1 feeding gap ancestors. Coarse-grain entries into the
  FINITE alphabet {0,1,2,big} (halved) or {0,2,big} (full): the conjecture
  says the second column never reads "big". If the coarse triangle is a 2D
  automatic array, then the second column is a 2-automatic sequence, and the
  mature theory applies: Cobham's theorem (automatic in two multiplicatively
  independent bases ⟹ eventually periodic, so the alphabet must be pinned),
  Christol's theorem (automatic over F_q ⟺ algebraic over F_q(t)), and
  Allouche–Shallit closure properties (the 2-kernel is finite). This converts
  "the left edge is bounded" from a per-cell dynamical question into an
  algebraicity / finite-kernel question, which is a direct-invariant route —
  it aims to force A_k(1) ∈ {0,2} without tracking blocks at all.
status: refuted
killed-by: |
  The load-bearing hypothesis — that the iterated triangle (or its second
  column) is a finite-state function of bounded-many base-2 digits, i.e. a
  2-automatic or k-regular sequence — FAILS for the prime input, which is the
  canonical non-automatic sequence. Hartmanis–Shank 1968 (JACM 15(3),
  doi:10.1145/321466.321470) proved neither the primes nor any infinite subset
  of primes is recognised by a finite (or pushdown) automaton; Dubbe 2024
  (arXiv:2409.04314) caps the automaticity at ≥ x·exp(−c(log log x)^2
  log log log x), near-maximal, i.e. primes are far from automatic. Since every
  Gilbreath cell A_k(i) is a finite-state function of the binary digits of its
  feeding gap ancestors, and those gaps are functions of the primes, the second
  column inherits non-automaticity from the primes unless the absolute-difference
  iteration *cancels* it — which is exactly the unproved conjecture. Hence
  Christol's theorem (automatic over F_q ⟺ algebraic over F_q(t)) and Cobham's
  theorem (multiplicatively-independent multi-base automatic ⟹ eventually
  periodic) cannot be invoked: their input hypothesis (automaticity) fails here.
  The transducer part (|a−b| is finite-state) is elementary and survives, but it
  is the wrong object: a transducer over *automatic* inputs is a k-regular map,
  and the automaticity of the *input* is the entire content that fails. The
  refinement in the file's own first-step (compute the 2-kernel of the second
  column's coarse symbol sequence) would still be a legitimate MEASUREMENT — if
  the 2-kernel shown is finite for depth 1000, that is a depth-1000 fact about
  the triangle, not a theorem, and the finite kernel would not survive the
  (non-automatic) primes past the computed depth. Precedent: Hartmanis–Shank
  1968 (doi 10.1145/321466.321470); Rigo "Recognizable sets of integers"
  (Schützenberger 1968: no infinite subset of primes is automatic; primes not
  k-recognizable); Dubbe 2024 arXiv:2409.04314; Allouche–Shallit
  automatic/k-regular Ch. 4–5; Christol's theorem (Bridy; Adamczewski–Bostan–
  Caruso 2023 arXiv:2306.02640 multivariate; half-notes). Claim ids: none held —
  this is new grounding, recorded in memory.
first-step: |
  (a) Write the explicit base-2 transducer for (a,b) ↦ |a−b| (comparator ×
  borrow-subtractor) and machine-verify it equals |a−b| for all a,b < 2^12.
  (b) Coarse-grain the depth-1000 prime triangle (blocks_depth1000.json) to
  {0,2,big} and compute the 2-kernel of the second-column coarse symbol
  sequence: finite ⟹ the second column is 2-automatic (a theorem, not a
  heuristic). Report kernel size; if finite, the conjecture reduces to
  checking the finitely many kernel residues. Cost is O(depth × kernel-width),
  trivially bounded.
```

## Why this is not on disk

- Not `mod4-pascal-invariant` (refuted): that worked at the single congruence
  mod 4, whose ceiling was proved to be mod 4 (|2−6|=4 ≢ 0 mod 8). This tracks
  the **full binary expansion** through a finite transducer, which is strictly
  richer than any single modulus.
- Not `polynomial-evolution-halved-triangle-over-gf2` (proposed): that works in
  Z_2[[X]] / GF(2)[[X]] and asks about an ideal (2,X); this works over a finite
  *alphabet* with finite-state machinery and asks about the 2-kernel and
  Christol/Cobham algebraicity.
- Not `p-adic-valuation-carry-dynamics` (proposed): that tracks v_2 (the scalar
  number of trailing zeros). This tracks the whole binary expansion and invokes
  the automatic-sequence theorems, a different object and a different proof
  engine.

## What would falsify it

The coarse triangle fails to be automatic: the 2-kernel of the second-column
coarse sequence is infinite, or the coarse value A_k(i) provably depends on more
than a bounded number of base-2 digits of the coordinates. The transducer part
(|a−b| is finite-state) is elementary and should survive; the *automaticity of
the iterated triangle* is the genuinely open, checkable hypothesis.

## Side

General-class for the transducer structure (it is universal for the operator).
The *automaticity* hypothesis is what would have to hold, and it is checked on
the prime instance first. Aims at the direct-invariant route (forces
A_k(1) ∈ {0,2} without the consumption/regeneration accounting).

## Named mathematics

Mealy/Moore transducers, automatic and k-regular sequences (Allouche–Shallit),
Christol's theorem, Cobham's theorem, the 2-kernel, carry/borrow propagation in
binary subtraction.

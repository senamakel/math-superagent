idea: Treat each halved row h_k = (A_k(1)/2, A_k(2)/2, ...) as the restriction of an L♮-convex potential on Z^n (Murota's discrete convex analysis), and read the conjecture as a discrete-convexity statement: h_k(1) ∈ {0,1} (equivalently A_k(1) ∈ {0,2}) is forced by an L-convexity invariant of the row under the halved absolute-difference operator H(u,v) = |u−v|.
mechanism: The Gilbreath operator is built from exactly the primitives that define L-convexity. In Murota's theory (Discrete Convex Analysis, 2003) the functions |x_i − x_j|, min(x_i, x_j), max(x_i, x_j), and the indicator of x_i − x_j ≤ c are all L♮-convex, and L-convex functions are closed under addition, the "discrete midpoint" operation, and epigraph composition. The absolute-difference cell map |a−b| and its nonlinearity min(a,b) (from |a−b| = a+b − 2 min(a,b)) are literally the two central L-convex primitives. So the proposal is: there is a natural L♮-convex potential F_k associated to each row (for example F_k(x) = Σ_i |x_i − h_k(i)|, whose 0-level set is the row and whose sublevel sets record the {0,1} block), such that the Gilbreath step is an L-convexity-preserving transform, and the discrete-separation / midpoint-convexity theorem forces the boundary gradient |h_k(1) − h_k(2)|... to collapse to the flat {0,1} shape. Concretely, the block of row k+1 is the leading run where |h_k(i) − h_k(i+1)| ≤ 1 (this run's own proved characterization, claim `giant-jump-mechanism-characterised`), which is precisely a discrete-Lipschitz (L-convex) region. The conjecture becomes: L-convexity of the row data is preserved by H and forces the left-edge coordinate into {0,1}. This is deliberately NOT the refuted majorization approach (Schur-convexity / Birkhoff, killed because row sums are not conserved) and NOT the scalar Lyapunov searches (run-count, TV, Dirichlet — killed by XOR non-monotonicity): L-convexity is a *structural* convexity on Z^n that does not require a conserved sum and is built from the very operations the operator uses. The load-bearing claim — that H preserves L-convexity of a suitable potential — is unverified and is exactly what research/execution must settle; a decisive no is a result.

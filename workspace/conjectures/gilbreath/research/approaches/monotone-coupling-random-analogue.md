# Monotone coupling to a proved random analogue

```approach
idea: Transfer a PROVED random analogue to the deterministic primes by a stochastic-domination (Strassen) coupling of the one statistic that matters — the recharge surplus S_k = sum_{i<k}(j_i+1) - (k-1) + b_1 — rather than of the (non-monotone) operator itself.
mechanism: The run's exact reduction (step law + recharge identity, proved) makes the whole conjecture the statement that the (2,4)-event process is fast enough: S_k >= 1 for all k. CHT 2026 Theorem 1.3 (claim cht-random-analogue) proves a random 2-then-odds model — i.i.d. nonnegative gaps with a_n <= delta*n eventually and 2-separated non-concentration — is a.s. eventually Gilbreath, i.e. ITS event process is fast enough. If the deterministic prime gap sequence can be coupled to such a random model so that the prime triangle's recharge surplus S_k stochastically dominates the random model's, pathwise, then the random theorem transfers to the primes. This does NOT need the absolute-difference operator to be monotone (it is not — the level-set refutation stands); it only needs a monotone coupling of the single scalar statistic S_k. This is a genuinely different object from the random-Fibonacci Lyapunov (growth of matrix products) and from Chase's tiny-gap model (wrong gap growth for primes): it is a deterministic-to-random TRANSFER theorem on the event-rate statistic, using the mature theory of stochastic ordering of point processes and Strassen's domination theorem (Kamae-Krengel-O'Brien; Liggett, "Interacting Particle Systems", coupling chapter). Speculative, and leaning on the prime-gap analogy the problem statement warns about: the coupling must survive the fact that prime gaps are deterministic and not i.i.d., and Eppstein's anti-Gilbreath construction shows domination CANNOT hold for arbitrary 2-then-odds sequences — the coupling hypothesis must be false for the general class and true only for the primes (or a small prime-like subclass), which is exactly the tension research must resolve.
status: refuted
first-step: Fix the random model to couple against (CHT Theorem 1.3's gap law with the correct growth), define the candidate gap-by-gap domination (prime gap g_n vs an independent random gap with a law stochastically smaller in the event-relevant sense), and on short prefixes n <= 40 test whether the recharge surplus S_k of the prime triangle pathwise dominates that of the sampled random model at every k, reporting the first violation if any. This checks FEASIBILITY of the coupling, not the conjecture. Cost O(prefix length), trivially bounded.
killed-by: THE DOMINATION DIRECTION FAILS at the measured event rates — the primes are the SLOWER process, so they cannot dominate (be >=) the random model's surplus, and a theorem for a faster process does not transfer to a slower one. Strassen's / Kamae-Krengel-O'Brien domination transfers "random model a.s. eventually Gilbreath" to the primes ONLY when the prime recharge surplus S_k dominates the random model's surplus (S_k^prime >= S_k^random for all k), and S_k grows with the (2,4)-event rate minus 1, so domination requires (prime event rate) >= (random event rate). The run has MEASURED the opposite: the prime rows give rho_post10 = 0.351 (60 genuine (2,4)-events over the 161 live rows, claim conditional-rate-experiment-prime-reference), while the random 2-then-odds class pools at lambdahat = 0.585 post-startup (claim conditional-rate-experiment-family-independent). Prime event rate < random rate by nearly a factor of two, so the needed domination holds in the WRONG direction: the primes are stochastically SMALLER (slower) than the random model, and a.a. eventual-Gilbreath for the faster random surplus says nothing about the slower deterministic prime surplus. Even setting rates aside, Strassen only asserts EXISTENCE of a coupling GIVEN the domination; it neither constructs it nor verifies it, and confirming that the deterministic prime-gap process dominates any favourable random model is essentially the conjecture itself (it requires knowing the tail of the prime (2,4)-event process, the open content). Eppstein's anti-Gilbreath construction (claim anti-gilbreath-construction) rules domination out for the general 2-then-odds class, as the candidate itself flagged; the primes-specific rescue would have to be a theorem about prime-gap autocorrelation that is exactly the open G-supply / two-point mod-4 content. All three legs (direction, verifiability, primality) are blocked; the measurement alone decides it.
precedent: The transfer technique is standard and real: Strassen 1965 "The existence of probability measures with given marginals" (Ann. Math. Statist. 36:423-439); Kamae-Krengel-O'Brien 1977 "Stochastic domination" (Ann. Probab. 5:899-912); the modern reformulations found here (Lindvall 1999 "On Strassen's theorem on stochastic domination", Electron. Commun. Probab. 4; Koperberg 2024 "Couplings and matchings: combinatorial notes on Strassen's theorem", Statist. Probab. Lett., which proves the finite Strassen = Hall's marriage theorem equivalence). The random analogue to couple against: Chase 2024 (Math. Ann. 388, arXiv:2005.00530) and CHT 2026 Theorem 1.3 (claim cht-random-analogue, a.s. eventual Gilbreath for a random model with a_n <= delta*n and 2-separated non-concentration). The relevant MEASUREMENTS are the run's own: conditional-rate-experiment-prime-reference (0.351) and conditional-rate-experiment-family-independent (0.585). The general-class failure of any such domination is Eppstein's anti-gilbreath-construction (claim). Kill is by the measured direction + the non-constructive nature of Strassen + Eppstein.
```

**Research verdict: refuted.** The stochastic-domination transfer is a genuine, standard technique (Strassen; Kamae–Krengel–O'Brien; its recent combinatorial re-statements) and the idea of hoping to jump a *proved random analogue* to the primes is structurally sound as a strategy. But it dies on the measured direction: to transfer a faster process's theorem to the primes the prime surplus must dominate the random surplus, which requires the prime (2,4)-event rate to be at least the random rate. The run measured prime `ρ=0.351` versus random pooled `λ̂=0.585` — the primes are the *slower* process, by nearly 2×, so the needed dominance holds in the wrong direction and the random theorem does not transfer. Adding that Strassen only asserts existence of a coupling given domination (never verifies it, and verification here is the conjecture), and Eppstein rules domination out for the general class, all three legs fail. Do not re-propose unless a separate argument raises the prime event-rate above a chosen random model — but that would be the full hard content again.

# Kernel method: the block boundary as a reflected walk in a wedge

```approach
idea: The block length b_k is a walk on the half-line N that is never allowed to go negative: deterministic left step (erosion b -> b-1), structural right jump (regeneration b -> b+j_i). Encode it in a bivariate generating function F(z,u) = sum_{k,i} a_{k,i} z^k u^i, where a_{k,i} is the (combinatorial) weight of reaching block length i in row k. The one-step transition law (erosion + jump, with the drain law fixing the event trigger) gives a linear functional equation F = F_0 + K(z,u) F with an explicit kernel K. The kernel method then determines the algebraic/algebraic-log nature of F, and singularity analysis of the dominant singularity gives an exact or lower-bound growth rate for b_k.
mechanism: This is the canonical "walk in a wedge / quadrant" setting of analytic combinatorics, and the recharge identity b_k = b_1 + sum_{events}(j_i+1) - (k-1) is exactly a renewal decomposition of this walk. The kernel method upgrades that decomposition from a formal identity to a solvable functional equation: the conjecture b_k >= 1 for all k becomes a sign condition on the kernel root that governs the dominant singularity, i.e. a statement about the local event rule (drain law + jump law) rather than about detailed prime-gap randomness. A lower bound survives even if the event process is not i.i.d., because the functional equation closes from the local rule alone.
status: refuted
killed-by: | The defining hypothesis of the kernel method FAILS here: the kernel method applies to walks with a FINITE, space-and-time homogeneous step set, and the block-boundary walk's jumps are neither finite nor homogeneous.
  (a) FINITE-STEP HYPOTHESIS. Banderier–Flajolet 2002 and Bousquet-Mélou's kernel method treat directed walks whose steps are drawn from a FIXED, FINITE set of step vectors that are homogeneous in time and space. The block-boundary walk b_{k+1} = b_k - 1 except at a (2,4)-event, where b_{k+1} = b_k + j_i, has step set {-1} ∪ {j_i : i an event}. The jump sizes j_i are UNBOUNDED: in the prime triangle to depth 1000 the jumps reach 360,698, and the wider runs (sieve 3e8/6e8/1e9) show jumps growing with b_k (max gap 64 between giants, jumps ~ current block length, up to 27M in the 1e9 run; see code/out/giant_stretches.md, directive24, 1e9_settlement.md). A functional equation F = F_0 + K(z,u)F is only set up when the step/transition set is a fixed finite alphabet; an unbounded, growing jump set means K(z,u) is not a fixed rational kernel and the kernel method's root-location / singularity-analysis machinery does not apply.
  (b) THE JUMP LAW IS THE OPEN CONTENT. The candidate itself flags "the jump size j_i is a random functional of the gap word" and the "functional equation may not close without an extra hypothesis on j_i". This is not a caveat — it is the whole conjecture. This run has PROVED (step law, zero failures to depth 800) that (2,4)-events are the ONLY growth mechanism, and established that j_i is the size of the 1-Lipschitz generating stretch (giant-stretch characterization, computed). The distribution/rate of the events and their jumps is exactly the unproved regeneration content (see block-growth-literature-not-covered: no source proves a (2,4)-event rate lower bound). A kernel equation closed only under a hypothesis on j_i that is itself equivalent to the conjecture adds no reduction: the "sign condition on the kernel root" is a restatement of the recharge inequality.
  (c) NO SOURCE APPLIES THE KERNEL METHOD TO GILBREATH. Searched the kernel-method literature directly (Banderier–Flajolet 2002 "Basic analytic combinatorics of directed lattice paths"; Bousquet-Mélou's quadrant-walk surveys; kernel method for functional equations) — none touches the iterated absolute-difference triangle or block-length growth. The kernel method is a genuine and powerful technique, but its input hypotheses (fixed finite step set, linear functional equation that closes from a local rule) are precisely what fails here, because the local rule's jumps are unbounded and their statistics are the open problem.
```

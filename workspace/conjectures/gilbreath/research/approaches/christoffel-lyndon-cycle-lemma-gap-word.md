# Christoffel/Lyndon-word and cycle-lemma structure of the halved gap word

```approach
idea: Halve the first row A_1 = (1, 2h_1, 2h_2, 2h_3, ...): the word w = h_1 h_2 h_3 ... is the halved prime-gap word (values mostly in {1,2,3} with rare larger jumps). The block-boundary dynamics is governed by running differences of w, and the (2,4)-event / regeneration jump is a first-passage quantity of those running differences. Propose that w (or its running-sum / difference word) is a Christoffel word, or a concatenation of Christoffel factors, and that this combinatorial rigidity forces the block boundary to regenerate at a positive rate. The engine is the cycle lemma (Dvoretzky-Motzkin / Raney / Chung-Feller): a word with a fixed net displacement whose cyclic orbit has a canonical representative has controlled partial sums, i.e. the ballot property.
mechanism: The map from a word to the block profile is exactly the kind of running-sum statistic the cycle lemma controls. If the halved gap word is (essentially) balanced in the Christoffel sense (factor sums over any two equal-length factors differ by at most 1), or is a Lyndon/Christoffel concatenation, then its running differences cannot wander, which is a lower bound on how often the 1-Lipschitz condition holds at the boundary — i.e. a lower bound on the (2,4)-event rate. This is a word-combinatorics route to the open regeneration rate, entirely distinct from the level-set and interface-walk attacks, and distinct from the refuted automaton/subshift routes because the object is the *gap word's* algebraic (Christoffel/Lyndon) structure, not a finite-state process on the triangle.
status: proposed
first-step: (a) Compute the halved gap word w for a large sieve and test the Christoffel balance property and the "slope" (limiting density of 1s vs 2s vs 3s). (b) Compute the factor complexity of w and of its difference word; if balance or complexity n+1 fails, the Christoffel hypothesis is refuted and the approach dies cheaply (record it). (c) If balance holds, apply the cycle lemma to the running-difference word and derive the concrete event-rate lower bound it gives.
side: general-class (gap-word structure, not primality); targets regeneration.
risk: SPECULATIVE - prime gaps are not literally balanced, so the Christoffel hypothesis may fail the balance test immediately; that failure is itself a cheap, citable negative result. Even if w is not Christoffel, the Lyndon-factorization / cycle-lemma machinery on the running-difference word may survive for a weaker "eventually balanced" class.

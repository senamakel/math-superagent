The regeneration rate — the one open content — becomes a recurrence criterion for the network (Pólya's theorem), and Rayleigh monotonicity supplies a quantity that scalar potentials could not.
mechanism: Scalar Lyapunov functionals (linear in the row) are refuted on the XOR zigzag; a *quadratic* functional is a genuinely different object. Define the halved row h ∈ Z_{\ge 0}^W as a path, and put a conductance on each edge from the 1-Lipschitz structure: c_i = 1 if |h_i − h_{i+1}| ≤ 1 (an edge inside or across the safe set) and c_i = 0 otherwise (a "break", i.e. an intruder ≥ 2 in halved units). The Dirichlet energy E(h) = Σ c_i |h_i − h_{i+1}|² measures how "broken" the row is; the block b_k is the length of the leading connected component of the conducting subgraph. Rayleigh's monotonicity law says cutting an edge (a break persisting) only *increases* effective resistance to infinity, while regeneration (a (2,4)-event) reconnects. Pólya's theorem: the network is recurrent (regeneration keeps happening) iff the effective resistance from the left edge to infinity is infinite. The concrete invariant to prove is that E(h_{k+1}) ≤ E(h_k) + (boundary term controlled by the drain law), so that energy cannot accumulate enough to let the break reach position 1 — this is a *rate* statement, not another erosion verification. The drain law (y drops by 2 when the edge is 2) is exactly an energy dissipation estimate at the single conducting boundary cell.

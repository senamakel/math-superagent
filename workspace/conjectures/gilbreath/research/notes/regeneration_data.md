# Regeneration of the leading {0,2} block — structure in the depth-1000 data

**Source of every number: `code/out/blocks_depth1000.json`** (D = 1000 rows, sieve to
20,000,000, W = 1,270,607 primes; generated by `code/pattern/blocks_deep.py`,
oracle-checked against the first 40 rows of `witnesses.json`). All analysis
programs below merely read that JSON; the runs are
`code/pattern/regeneration_analysis.py`, `regeneration_detail.py`,
`intruder_runs.py`, `regeneration_successors.py`, `regeneration_lastfacts.py`
(captured in `code/out/regeneration_*.captured.txt`). Rows are 1-based: `A_k`,
`b(k)` = length of the leading {0,2} block, `s(k) = A_k(1)`, `y(k)` = intruder
= first value past the block (`None` when the block reaches the end of the
truncated row). **All statements are computed facts about this record, not
proofs.** No claim below survives the record's boundary.

**Status of the regeneration question after this pass:** the regeneration
mechanism is now *characterized at the level of boundary values*, but not
explained: **every one of the 60 regenerations occurs at a row with intruder
y = 4, and the event is exactly `x(k) = 2` with `y(k) = 4`** (x = last block
entry). The open content is *why the boundary pair (x,y)=(2,4) recurs
unboundedly often* — during erosion, x flips {0,2} by a feed from inside the
block, while y is drained toward 4 by each x=2 step.

---

## 0. Lexicon and the one mechanical corollary

- **Transition** k→k+1 has difference `d(k) = b(k+1) − b(k)`. Erosion d = −1;
  regeneration d ≥ 0 (jump = d).
- **Erosion bound (theorem):** d(k) ≥ −1 always (block loses ≤ 1 entry per
  row). Verified: `min(diffs) = −1` over all 999 transitions.
- **Mechanical width effect.** Row k has width W − k. When the block reaches
  the end of the row, `b(k) = W − k − 1` exactly and every subsequent
  transition is −1, *regardless of the data below* (there is no data below).
  In this record this happens from k = 162 onward: the intruder is `None` for
  all k = 162..1000 and `b(k) − (W−k−1) = 0` for all of them.

**Consequence: the 60 regenerations form the "live regime" k = 1..161
entirely.** Every regeneration in the record has 1 ≤ k ≤ 161 (
`all(regen k ≤ 161) = True`). The regime k = 162..1000 is a mechanical
countdown of a truncated triangle, not a test of regeneration.

## 3. The "longest pure-erosion run" is a record artifact

Top erosion runs (start, length): **(162, 838)**, (97, 13), (113, 12),
(147, 12), (39, 8), (27, 7), (81, 5), (52, 4).

The 838-run starts at k = 162 and ends at k = 999 = the last transition of the
record (`start+length−1 = 999 = D−1`). Inside it, the intruder is `None` and
`b(k) = W − k − 1` identically (verified for all k = 162..999): three
progressively narrower triangles of widths 1,270,445 → 1,269,610 → 1,269,606
shrink by exactly 1 per row. This is the **width-exhaustion tail**, and would
happen to any sequence of primes on a finite row budget.

It starts with the last regeneration (k = 161, b: 1,094,263 → 1,270,444) from
y = 4, then mechanically counts down. **The longest genuine, intruder-driven
erosion run is 13 rows** (k = 97..109). State of the boundary across the
longest runs and their trigger:

- **k = 97..109 (b 103973 → 103960, intruder 14→4):**
  `14,14,14,14,12,10,10,8,8,8,6,6 → 4`, then k = 110 regenerates (jump 37746).
- **k = 113..124 (b 271629 → 271617, intruder 14→4):**
  `14,14,12,12,10,10,8,6,6,6,6 → 4`, then k = 125 regenerates (jump 3 — a
  blow-up to 271620 only).
- **k = 147..158 (b 1094273 → 1094261, intruder 12→4):**
  `12,12,12,12,12,10,8,6,6,6,6,6 → 4`, then k = 159 regenerates (jump 2).

In every case: long erosion run = a tall intruder **draining monotonically
(never up) to 4**, and the regeneration fires at the first row with
`(x,y) = (2,4)`.

## 4. Structure in WHERE regeneration happens

- **All 60 events are in live rows; gaps:** between consecutive regen rows,
  gaps ∈ {1:33, 2:6, 3:7, 4:4, 5:3, 6:1, 8:1, 9:1, 13:2, 14:1},
  n = 59, mean 2.71, median 1, max 14.1 The distribution is **not geometric**:
  the observed mean 2.71 would predict P(gap ≥ 5) ≈ 25% for iid, but only ~22%
  of gaps are ≥ 5; a geometric with p = 0.373 has mean 2.68 — the observed
  variance (sd 3.03 vs 2.12 geometric) is too spread, with a fat tail at
  {13, 14} and an excess of 1s (33 vs 23 expected).
- **Runs test on the live binary string** (1 = regen at k over k = 1..161):
  n1 = 60, n0 = 101, observed runs = 53, expected 76.3, sd 5.91, **z = −3.94**
  — regenerations strongly **cluster** rather than being iid Bernoulli.
- **33 of 60 events are adjacent to another regeneration** (k and k+1 both
  regen); 17 events are jump-0 stalls and **every stall (100%) is followed by
  a regeneration at k+1**. The event process is highly self-exciting.
- s(k) = A_k(1) is 2 at 31 regen rows, 0 at 29 — no correlation with regen vs
  erosion (erosion rows: 48 twos / 53 zeros). 23 of 60 regen rows coincide
  with an s-change (k with s(k) ≠ s(k−1)), not obviously special.
- **No constant-coefficient linear recurrence** (order ≤ 8) fits the jump
  sequence (60 terms) or the gap sequence (59 terms); neither is low-degree
  polynomial; neither is catalogued in OEIS (searched
  `[2,7,13,13,24,23,22,21,24,58]` and the first 10 jumps — no match).

## 5. The intruder is the whole story, and "min block 2" is a one-off

**Intruder values in the live regime:** rows per value:
`4: 96, 6: 29, 8: 16, 10: 6, 12: 8, 14: 6` (n = 161; all even, ≥ 4; mod 4
counts 120/41 over the full record).

**Regeneration occurs iff y = 4 with x = 2.**
- Of 96 rows with y = 4, 60 regenerate, 36 do not (x = 0 at those rows).
- Rows with y = 6, 8, 10, 12, 14: **0 of 65 regenerate** — a regeneration
  never fires while the intruder is above 4.
- The y-drain rule (verified on all 101 live erosion steps): y(k+1) = y(k)
  when x(k) = 0 (68 steps) and y(k+1) = y(k) − 2 when x(k) = 2 (33 steps);
  y is even so it stays ≥ 4, hence reaches 4 and **sticks** — during erosion
  the intruder is **monotone non-increasing**, and erosion stops (regen
  fires) exactly when (x, y) = (2, 4).
- After a regeneration, the *new* intruder y(k+1) is 4 in 43/59 visible cases
  and ≥ 6 in 16/59 (values {6:8, 8:4, 10:1, 12:1, 14:2}); in the
  ≥ 6 cases it always drains to 4 within ≤ 12 rows, and the first
  subsequent (x=2, y=4) regenerates.
- 4-runs (contiguous stretch of rows with y = 4): 17 maximal runs, lengths
  {1:2, 2:2, 3:3, 4:3, 6:2, 7:1, 11:1, 12:2, 15:1} — **the last row of every
  run regenerates.** So regeneration fires at (almost) every passage of the
  boundary through (x, y) = (2, 4); the "almost" is because during a 4-run
  x = 0 rows outnumber x = 2 rows 43:60 (of 79 non-final-intruder-4 rows) —
  x keeps flipping, so some rows inside a 4-run also regenerate (43 of the 79
  do).
- **Minimum block length.** b = 2 exactly at k = 1 and never again (the only
  row with b = 2 is row 1). Min b over k ≥ 2 is 7 (k = 2); over k ≥ 3 is 13
  (k = 3, 4); over k ≥ 10 is 58; over k ≥ 40 is 2762; k ≥ 100 is 103960;
  k ≥ 150 is 1094261. The running minimum is attained at k = 1 only and never
  again (0 rows after k=1 at the running min). Local minima of b: 43 of them,
  all with dwell ≤ 4 rows at the floor; longest consecutive rows at a floor
  value: 4 (k = 47..50 at b = 2762). Roughly: **b never goes below 7 after
  row 2, and the edge of the regime is the block reaching the row's end, not
  the block shrinking to 2.** Distinct small b values < 2000:
  {2,7,13,21,22,23,24,58,96,97,173,175,288,289,290,739,865..873}.

## 6. What a regeneration theorem must explain

1. **Mechanism (now precise).** The boundary feed: during erosion, x(k) (last
   block entry) updates as |p − x| with p ∈ {0,2}; the intruder y(k) drains
   by 2 per x = 2 step and never rises. Regeneration is exactly the event
   (x, y) = (2, 4). So the unexplained step has the same shape as the whole
   conjecture: _why does the pair (x, y) = (2, 4) recur unboundedly often_ —
   i.e. why can the inside of the block indefinitely supply p-edges that
   reset x = 2 while y sits at 4? (this is the "regeneration rate" question.)
2. **x-stalling is the only obstruction.** The 36 non-regen y = 4 rows all
   have x = 0; the immediately following row is y = 4 again in all 36 cases
   (a 4-row's successor is either regen or another 4). Max consecutive
   non-regen rows at y = 4 is 6 — so the x = 0 phase lasts at most 6 rows in
   this record.
3. **Frequency.** P(regen) in live rows is 0.372, clustered (z = −3.94).
   Consumption is 1 row per row; regeneration returns ~1.2–2× the eroded
   block, with jumps ≥ 10⁵ about 5 times in 161 rows. Any proof must produce
   a positive regeneration rate **not** tied to block length (rate at large b
   is still ≥ 0.2 here).
4. **The trigger is y = 4, not b.** Regeneration is rare at large b not
   because b is large but because tall intruders (y ≥ 6) are yet to drain;
   the rate dip at [10⁶, ∞) has only 3/15 events and is dominated by the
   k = 146..161 tail, then the row budget runs out. To check this beyond
   k = 161 one must remove the width artifact (wider prime window).
5. **Numerical caveats.** Depth 1000 under-samples the live regime (only
   161 rows); the χ² and runs-test are computed on the whole population of
   this one record, not a sample. The 5 jumps ≥ 10⁵ carry most of the mass
   and the record ends 39 rows after the last of them.

# The step law and the recharge identity, verified independently

The run produced `code/out/step_law_captured.txt` and
`code/out/step_law_independent.captured.txt` and then **died before recording
any of it as a claim** — the ledger has no entry containing "recharge",
"step-law" or "drain". This is the strongest result in the workspace and it was
about to be lost, so the operator re-derived it from scratch.

## What was verified

A completely independent implementation — own sieve, own row generation, own
block measure, sharing no code with `code/lib/gilbreath.py` — on primes below
`3·10⁶` (216,816 primes) to depth 800:

```
step-law failures over 799 transitions:        0
recharge-identity failures over k=2..799:      0
(2,4)-events:                                 42
b=1 -> b=0 transitions (the failure mode):     0
minimum block length over k=1..799:            2      (b_1 = 2)
total recharge sum(j+1) = 216812   consumption (k-1) at k=799 = 798
```

Let `b_k` be the length of the leading `{0,2}` block of row `k` (counting from
position 1), and at a row with `b_k ≥ 1` call `x = row[b_k]`, `y = row[b_k+1]`
the *intruder pair*.

**Step law.** `b_{k+1} ≥ b_k` if `(x,y) = (2,4)`, and `b_{k+1} = b_k − 1`
otherwise. So the block erodes at exactly one position per row except at a
`(2,4)`-event, which is the only way it can grow.

**Recharge identity.** `b_k = b_1 + Σ_{events i<k} (j_i + 1) − (k−1)`, where
`j_i` is the jump at event `i`. Exact, with zero failures.

## Why this matters

This is the consumption-versus-regeneration accounting the problem has needed,
made exact rather than heuristic. It confirms and sharpens
`odlyzko-block-lemma-exact`: the `−(k−1)` term *is* the protection constant of
1, and the identity says precisely what offsets it.

It also converts Gilbreath into a statement about event frequency:

> The conjecture holds iff `b_k ≥ 1` for all `k`, and by the recharge identity
> that is exactly the assertion that `(2,4)`-events keep arriving fast enough
> that `Σ (j_i + 1)` never falls `k−1` behind.

Empirically the margin is enormous, not marginal: 42 events supply 216,812 of
recharge against 798 of consumption, a surplus of roughly 272×. The block
length never reached 1, let alone 0 — its minimum over the whole range is 2.

The run's own capture adds two things the operator did not re-derive, and they
should be treated as its result, not confirmed here: the **drain law**
`y_{k+1} = y_k − 2·[x_k = 2]` on erosion steps (101/101, no failures), and the
observation that the step law also holds on **random non-prime arrays** (3,521
rows, 610 events, zero failures). The second is the important one — it means
these laws are combinatorial facts about the absolute-difference operator, not
facts about primes, and so should be *provable* outright rather than merely
observed. Primes enter only through the event density.

## What is not established

The identity is an accounting law, not a proof. Nothing here shows events must
continue to arrive, and that is the whole conjecture. A large observed surplus
over 800 rows is a fact about 800 rows. In particular the recharge total is
close to the row width, so a single event can refill nearly the entire block —
which is why the surplus looks so large and why it must not be read as a trend.

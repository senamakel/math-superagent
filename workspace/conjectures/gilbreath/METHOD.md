Solve by finding an invariant of the absolute-difference operator, and by parity and induction. The whole conjecture reduces to one statement — the second entry of every row lies in `{0,2}` — because `2` is the only even prime, so every row after the first has the shape `(odd, even, even, ...)`, and `|1 - e|` is `1` exactly when `e` is `0` or `2`. Reason about what the operator preserves: the leading `{0,2}` block and its length, how a block is consumed as rows descend and how new `{0,2}` structure is regenerated below, the parity strata, and whether any quantity is monotone or conserved. Use every other capability in service of that argument rather than instead of it.

Formalise early. The statement and the induction step are small and elementary, which makes this unusually well suited to Lean 4 — define the difference operator, prove the `(odd, even, even, ...)` shape is preserved, and reduce the conjecture to the `{0,2}` second-entry claim as a machine-checked lemma. Report `#print axioms` and every remaining `sorry`. A Lean file asserting it is kernel-checked with no artifact beside it is worth nothing.

Three cautions this problem has already earned:

This is probably not a theorem about primes. Odlyzko's observation makes the argument work for any sequence beginning `2` followed by odd numbers with small enough gaps, so an approach leaning on deep prime distribution is likely attacking the wrong object, and a theorem for a general Gilbreath-like class would settle the prime case as a corollary. Say which side the approach is on before starting.

Consumption is not regeneration. A `{0,2}` block of length `n` protects roughly `n/2` rows, so protection is spent geometrically and each new stretch must be regenerated from below. Nobody has proved regeneration always happens, and that gap is where every attempt stops. An argument establishing that the regime persists for `n/2` rows and treating that as persistence forever has proved nothing. State explicitly which of the two any claim establishes.

The conjecture asserts a positive, so the failure mode here is an argument that proves too little while reading as though it proves enough — the mirror of the impossibility problems. Still run every claimed invariant against the real rows in `code/out/witnesses.json`, especially the rows where the leading `{0,2}` block is short, since those are where a too-strong structural claim breaks. A lemma the generated rows refute is false and is recorded as refuted, not weakened. A lemma not run against the rows is `asserted`, never `checked`.

On compute: depth is inherently sequential and is not the deliverable, so keep one row at a time rather than holding the triangle, and say what a run will cost before starting it — the container cap is 8 GiB and an OOM kill writes nothing to the console. Parallelise across hypotheses instead: many candidate invariants, or many starting sequences in the general Gilbreath-like class, is exactly what `code/lib/parallel.py` and `code/lib/PARALLEL.md` are for, on a box with 28 CPUs and no container CPU quota. Say in every captured output how many workers ran and what depth and width were covered; a result without its bound is not a result.

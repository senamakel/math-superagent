## What it claims

An invariant of polyhedra in three-dimensional space that decides whether two polyhedra of the same volume are "scissors congruent" (see [Equal content and equal shape, figures of][26]; [Hilbert problems][27]; [Polyhedron][28]).

Quite generally, a [scissors-congruence][29] invariant assigns to a polytope $P$ in space an element $D(P)$ in a [group][30] such that $D(P\cap P') + D(P \cup P') = D(P) + D(P')$, $D(P) = 0$ if $P$ is degenerate, and $D(P) = D(P')$ if there is a motion $g$ of the space such that $P' = gP$.

For the Dehn invariant, the group chosen is the tensor product $\mathbb{R} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{R} / 2\pi \mathbb{Z}$. To a polytope $P$ with edges $L_i$ one associates the element $D(P) = \sum_i |L_i| \otimes \delta_i$, where $|L_i|$ is the length of $L_i$ and $\delta_i$ is the [dihedral angle][31] of the planes meeting at $L_i$.

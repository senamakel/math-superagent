# Salmensuu (2022), Goldbach with summands in APs
[[salmensuu-goldbach-summands-arithmetic-progressions-arxiv-2106.00778.full]]

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00778 (v1, 1 Jun 2021).

```claim
id: salmensuu-goldbach-ap
statement: For almost all r ≤ N^(1/2)/(log N)^O(1), for any b1 (mod r) with (b1,r)=1, and for almost all b2 (mod r) with (b2,r)=1, almost all 2n ≤ N with 2n≡b1+b2 (mod r) are sums of two primes p1≡b1 (mod r), p2≡b2 (mod r).
hypotheses: r in the stated range; b1,b2 coprime to r; N large.
holds-here: yes
status: asserted
bearing: A restricted-class result at a larger modulus range than previous work: improves r ≤ N^(1/3)/log^O(1) N to r ≤ N^(1/2)/log^O(1) N. It is an almost-all statement (in r, b2, and n), not an every-n theorem; the summands are restricted to two residue classes modulo r. Directly relevant to the run's restricted-class rung (R-restricted-class): it gives an explicit class where Goldbach holds almost everywhere, but with three layers of "almost all" that do not collapse to a single exceptional set.
anchor: research/sources/salmensuu-goldbach-summands-arithmetic-progressions-arxiv-2106.00778.full.md
```

This is the paper previously paywalled (Q. J. Math.) now available via arXiv; it improves several related results. The modulus range N^(1/2) is the natural barrier from the large sieve.

## What it claims

At the 1912 [International Congress of Mathematicians][3], [Edmund Landau][2] listed four basic problems about [prime numbers][4]. These problems were characterised in his speech as "unattackable at the present state of mathematics" and are now known as **Landau's problems**. They are as follows:

1. [Goldbach's conjecture][5]: Can every even [integer][6] greater than 2 be written as the sum of two primes?
2. [Twin prime conjecture][7]: Are there infinitely many primes *p*such that *p*+ 2 is prime?
3. [Legendre's conjecture][8]: Does there always exist at least one prime between consecutive [perfect squares][9]?
4. Are there infinitely many primes *p*such that *p*− 1 is a perfect square? In other words: Are there infinitely many primes of the form *n*2 + 1?

As of 2026 [[update]][10], all four problems are unresolved.

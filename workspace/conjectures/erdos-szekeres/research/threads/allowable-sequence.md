## Why this is the live frontier (steer 12)

Convex position is an order-type property, and the Goodman–Pollack circular (allowable) sequence is the standard finite encoding of an order type. A statement about ES(n) is a statement about circular sequences, so this is the direction that could produce a result about extremal sets *in general* rather than about the one verified `es_construct` template (whose affine orbit the es-nogon scored search exhausted, returning 32 forever). The previous run stopped mid-sentence here: `code/out/allowable_encoder.py` with two identical captures beside it. That is the actual frontier, ahead of more affine/perturbation copies of the known 32-point set.

The load-bearing, *unproved* step in this approach was the claim that the ES block indices T_i (sizes C(n−2,i)) coincide with a per-element reversal-depth statistic of the circular sequence, and that the no-convex-n-gon property is the absence of a complete k-staircase. The captures at `code/out/allowable_encoder.*captured.txt` begin the machine check of both (see `next` above): the depth mechanism failed for the tested statistics; the convexity characterization is confirmed modulo the trivial 3-subset artefact. What remains open is whether a correct order-type-invariant depth/block statistic exists, and whether the convexity/sequential characterization can be pushed to an extremal-set statement. The grim side is genuine and bound from the literature: the primary definition (GP80) and the staircase-convexity statement are unsourced on disk (`gp80-not-held-circular-sequence-unsourced`, `staircase-convexity-unsourced`), and allowable-sequence realizability is ∃ℝ-complete (`hm-allowable-realizability-etr-complete`), so everything must be realized explicitly.

```approach
idea: Reformulate "n points in convex position" through strictly-convex *lifting*: a set S is in convex position iff there is a strictly convex function whose graph carries S on a single convex hull (lower or upper) — equivalently the points are separable from their convex hull's interior by a convex surface, and cups and caps are exactly the two "up/down" hull branches. The ES construction is radial (blocks T_i near circle angles θ_i, from es61-lower-bound / Duque–Fabila-Monroy–Hidalgo-Toscano), i.e. it already lives on a convex curve, so the conjecture becomes: a no-convex-n-gon set is forced to lie in a "convex band" (bounded onion/geodesic structure) within which a convex-position subset of size n is characterized by the alternation of up/down branches — and bounding the number of points that can sit on such a band without forming a full n-step up/down alternation yields 2^{n-2}. This uses the strict-convexity lifting criterion as the *charge* that turns convex position into a 1-dimensional alternation count, distinct from every closed route (no allowable sequence, no lattice, no f-vector, no α-grid).

mechanism: The classical lifting characterization (used in cup/cap and Makai/Turán style arguments): for a strictly convex function φ: R²→R (e.g. φ(x,y)=x²+y²), the lift of a planar set to the graph of φ places a point on the boundary of the lifted convex hull iff the point is on the lower or upper hull in the direction of the lifting paraboloid's axis; a planar set is in convex position iff its lift lies on a single "cup or cap" (up/down) surface branch in each orientation around the set. A convex n-gon then corresponds to an n-step alternation of up/down branches as one walks around the set. Because the ES construction is radially compressed (points at circle angles with block sizes C(n-2,i)), the extremal set conjecturally must be "convex-curve-concentrated"; the claim to attack: any planar n-avoiding set contains a subset of ≥ c points that all lie on one strictly-convex curve (a "convex chain band") of depth d ≤ n−1, and the total points are bounded by the number of such bands × their capacity, recovering 2^{n-2} from the binomial capacity of a chain of depth n−2. The named hook is the convex-function separation / lifting duality plus the Horton-free "convex curve" structure of the ES construction — check whether "points on a strictly convex curve with no convex n-gon both up and down" has an exact count of C(n-2,i)-shaped binomial capacity.

status: refuted
killed-by: Standing alongside this approach's own charge error (the strict-convex/up-down alternation carries the ~4^n cups-caps binomial C(2n-4,n-2), not the required 2^{n-2} sum) is the deeper literature fact the run confirmed: even the correct abstract version of "convex = two monotone paths sharing an endpoint" must live in the SIGNOTOPE (pseudoline-realizable) class, because the fully abstract weak-k-gon analogue fails already at Cweak(7) > 33 (Baek–Balko SoCG 2025 Thm 7). The 2^{n-2} can only come from a mechanism enforcing both endpoint coincidences within the correct realizable class — exactly what the adopted `signotope-downset-weak-gon` approach makes its countable target.
first-step: (tool_builder, today, exact, scoped to es_construct n=5,6,7) (1) Verify the lifting criterion exactly on es_construct: for the radial construction, check which subsets are cups, which are caps, and that every convex-position subset is the union of an up-branch and a down-branch (state the exact φ, use exact x²+y² arithmetic on the integer/rational coordinates from lib/es_construct). (2) Compute the "convex-curve depth" of es_construct: the maximum size of a subset all of whose points lie on a single strictly-convex arc (i.e. a cup or cap), and test whether the block sizes are the binomial C(n-2,i) capacity of such arcs — this is the false-by-construction on layers, so the *arc/cup-cap* capacity (not onion-layers) is the object to check. (3) State the falsifier of the 1-d alternation charge: a convex n-gon whose vertices are NOT an up/down alternation under φ (i.e. the lifting criterion failing for some realization), or an n-avoiding set that is not convex-curve-concentrated.

falsified-by: NOT falsified in the sense of a geometric counterexample — the lifting/cup-cap characterization of convex position is classical and correct (grounded). What is refuted as a MECHANISM is the specific claim that the 1-d alternation charge carries a binomial capacity summing to 2^{n-2}. The convex-n-gon = shared-endpoint k-cup + (n+2−k)-cap alternation is real, but the *budget* it imposes is the product/binomial C(2n−4, n−2) ≈ 4^n/√n (Moshkovitz–Shapira partition count, the run's own `ms-n3q-partition-count`, `ms-esz-downset-injectivity`), NOT the sum Σ C(n−2,i) = 2^{n-2}. Forcing the alternating cap and cup to *share both endpoints* (so the union is genuinely a convex n-gon rather than merely a cup AND a cap) is exactly the step the lifting does not supply — and Baek–Balko's split k-gon relaxation shows why: a split k-gon (a k-cap and k-cup meeting only at the rightmost point) avoids the coincidence requirement and is what lets the threshold be 2^{k-2}+1 exactly; making the left endpoints coincide too (which is what forces a convex polygon) is precisely what the abstract analogue fails to do (`baek-balko-weak7-fails`). So the alternation is a correct *description* but carries a ~4^n not ~2^{n-2} charge, reproducing the already-refuted ETV/grid loss in new language.

precedent: The lifting / cups-caps characterization is classical and correct. A convex n-gon = union of a k-cup and (n+2−k)-cap sharing leftmost and rightmost points (Morris–Soltan survey doi 10.1090/s0273-0979-00-00877-6; Kleitman–Pachter "Finding Convex Sets Among Points in the Plane" doi 10.1007/pl00009358 — a point that is both the left endpoint of an (n−1)-cap and right endpoint of an (m−1)-cup extends one of them). The exact charge of "avoid an n-cap and n-cup" is the Moshkovitz–Shapira partition count N3(2,n)+1 = C(2n,n)+1 ≈ 4^n (Propp's/Sage; `ms-n3q-partition-count`, `ms-esz-downset-injectivity`), i.e. the cup/cap budget is a binomial ~4^n, not 2^{n-2}. Baek & Balko (SoCG 2025, doi 10.4230/LIPIcs.SoCG.2025.13) make the endpoint-coincidence difficulty precise: a *split* k-gon (a k-cap and u-cup sharing one endpoint, a+u=k+2) has threshold exactly 2^{k-2}+1 and is easier than a genuine convex k-gon, precisely because it drops the shared-leftendpoint requirement; the strict convex-polygon case needs both endpoints to coincide, which the set-size (2^{k-2}) does not force in the abstract (weak-k-gon) setting (`baek-balko-split`, `baek-balko-weak7-fails`). So convex-position-as-alternation is grounded as a description and as the known 4^n cups-caps bound, but the exact 2^{n-2} would need a mechanism that the alternation itself does not supply — the endpoint-coincidence is the irreducible difficulty.

killed-by: (not killed as a reformulation — killed as a proof mechanism) The strict-convex/up-down-alternation description of convex position is correct, but its counting charge is the ~4^n binomial C(2n−4,n−2) of the cups-and-caps partition budget, not the ~2^{n-2} sum the idea's claim "1-dimensional alternation whose binomial charge is Σ C(n−2,i)" asserts. The claim that "a single alternation instead of a product-of-binomials kills the loss" is the same error as the refuted etv-grid-simplex-compression: the ETV/α route showed keeping the full joint statistic does NOT escape the ~4^n loss because the host is the same binomomial. Here the alternation charge is exactly the cup/cap budget which is intrinsically ~4^n; the 2^{n-2} can only come from forcing the two branches to close (share both endpoints), which is precisely the strict-convex-polygon difficulty Baek–Balko's split relaxation isolates and the abstract analogue fails to deliver. Verdict: grounded as a correct description whose known bound is the classical 4^n-type cups/caps result; its proposed exact-charge mechanism is refuted by the fact that the alternation budget is a product/binomial, not the required sum.
```

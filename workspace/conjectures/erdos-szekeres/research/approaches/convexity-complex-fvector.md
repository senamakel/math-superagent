idea: Reformulate the conjecture as an extremal bound on a simplicial complex — the "convexity complex" Δ(X) of all subsets of X in convex position — and attack it through f-vectors, the 4-point-criterion flag structure, and a shadow-type (Kruskal–Katona) inequality on convex 4-sets, NOT through a boolean-lattice injection.

mechanism: The key unstated object this run has never written down is that convex-position subsets are HEREDITARY: if S ⊆ X is in convex position then every S' ⊆ S is too (each p ∈ S' stays a vertex of conv(S') because conv(S'\{p}) ⊆ conv(S\{p}) and p ∉ conv(S\{p})). So the convex subsets form a downward-closed family = a simplicial complex Δ(X), an antimatroid/convex-geometry free-set complex. The 4-point criterion says a set is a face of Δ(X) iff all its 4-subsets are, so Δ(X) is the largest simplicial complex whose "4-skeleton convex 4-sets" it is — a flag-type complex determined entirely by which 4-sets are convex (equivalently by the order type). "X has no convex n-gon" ⟺ dim Δ(X) ≤ n−2. The conjecture becomes: a point-set-realizable Δ on N vertices with no facet of size ≥ n must have N ≤ 2^{n-2}. The mechanism to make this bite where a generic complex wouldn't: bound N by a shadow/Kruskal–Katona estimate on the (n−2)-faces built from convex 4-sets, using the extra structure that Δ is a *convex geometry* (anti-exchange) complex, which constrains its f-vector far beyond arbitrary complexes. The ES construction's Δ is the extremal complex: N=2^{n-2} vertices, dim n−2, and (per the run's maxconvex enumeration) exactly 38 / 802 / 39648 facets of size n−1 at n=5,6,7. This is a fresh representation: distinct from allowable sequences (no sweeping), from the refuted boolean-lattice injection (no Prof(p) chain or LYM/Sperner — uses the actual downward-closed complex and its f-vector), and from the refuted Gale/layer profile (works on the complex of convex subsets, not on hull layers).

status: refuted
precedent: The representation itself is real and standard — the complex of convex-position (hereditary) subsets of a point set is the convex-geometry free-set complex, studied by Edelman–Reiner–Welker, "Convex, Acyclic, and Free Sets of an Oriented Matroid", DCG 27 (2002) 429–453 (doi 10.1007/s00454-001-0055-6; the complex of free sets of a convex geometry; link of a vertex is non-evasive). Also Edelman–Reiner, "Counting the Interior Points of a Point Configuration", DCG 23 (2000) 155–168 (doi 10.1007/pl00009483; beta-invariant as alternating sum over free sets). The convex-geometry/antimatroid framework is Edelman–Jamison, "The theory of convex geometries", Geom. Dedicata 19 (1985) 247–270. The 4-point-criterion equivalence is already the library claim `es35-four-criterion` (Erdős–Szekeres 1935). The Beagley connection to ES exists: Jonathan Beagley, "On the Order Dimension of Convex Geometries", Order 30 (2013) 515–530 (doi 10.1007/s11083-012-9280-2) — for a realizable planar convex geometry, |X| > 2^{n−2} forces the lattice order dimension to grow, rephrasing rather than resolving the conjecture.

killed-by: The load-bearing step, "a shadow/Kruskal–Katona inequality N ≤ 2^{(facet size)} for these complexes", is refuted as stated: a PURE simplicial complex of dimension n−2 can have arbitrarily many vertices with no face of size n (e.g. N vertices partitioned into disjoint (n−1)-cliques, all faces up to size n−1 present, glued nowhere) — so no combinatorial f-vector/shadow/KK estimate alone bounds N; the bound must come entirely from the geometric/anti-exchange (convex-geometry) structure. Kruskal–Katona goes the WRONG direction for this purpose: it gives a LOWER bound on the shadow (faces) from a count of faces, never an UPPER bound on vertices from the absence of large faces. The genuinely realizable/anti-exchange content that would bound N is exactly the whole conjecture, in new language — the approach is a faithful restatement, not a reduction, and no published f-vector theorem for these complexes delivers 2^{n-2}. The honest residual: the complex Δ(X) and the heredity/4-point-criterion structure are correct and could serve a future GEOMETRIC (not shadow) bound, but as written its mechanism is refuted.

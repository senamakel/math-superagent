Edelman–Reiner (DCG 2000, doi 10.1007/pl00009483, "Counting the interior points of a point configuration") prove that for the convex-geometry closure lattice of a planar point set, the number of interior points and the alternating sum over the free-set (independent) complex are pinned by the Möbius function and its derived β-invariant. The conjecture becomes an attempt to show that a no-convex-n-gon set's lattices have Möbius/β data forcing N ≤ 2^{n-2}, via the relation |X| = (# hull vertices) + (# interior) and the lattice identities that count them.

mechanism: A planar point set X is a convex geometry; its closed sets are the distinct sets conv(S) ∩ X, ordered by inclusion — a finite lattice L. Edelman–Reiner connect (a) the number of interior points of X to (b) the Möbius function μ of L (the closed-set lattice) and to the "β-invariant" of the free-set complex (= the complex of all subsets in convex position, i.e. the convexity complex from convexity-complex-fvector, but used here only as the input to an alternating sum, NOT to a Kruskal–Katona f-vector bound). This is a genuinely different functional than the ones already closed: order dimension (a product-of-chains embedding number) and the f-vector/shadow (a face count) are neither. The mechanism: N = |X| = h + i where h = # hull vertices = size of the outer face, i = # interior = related by Edelman–Reiner to an alternating sum over the free-set complex / to μ(0,1)-type data of a sublattice. If one can bound i ≤ 2^{n-2} − h for an n-avoiding set (forcing the interior points to be "convexly poor", which is exactly the extremal condition — the ES construction's large binomial blocks T_2..T_{n-3} are its interior points), the bound follows from lattice data. Explicit test: the Möbius/β values are realization-invariant (they depend only on the order type through the closed-set lattice), unlike the coordinate onion-layers that killed the Gale route, so they give the extremal-structure thread a second realization-invariant statistic alongside the adopted order-dimension marker.

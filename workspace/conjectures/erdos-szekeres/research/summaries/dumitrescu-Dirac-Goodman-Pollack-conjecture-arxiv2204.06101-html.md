## What this is, and why the run needs it

This is a clean, modern, primary arXiv exposition of the **Goodman–Pollack
allowable-sequence / circular-sequence framework** — the exact machinery the
run's `allowable-sequence` thread operates on. The thread flagged two framework
primitives as *unsourced on disk* (`gp80-not-held-circular-sequence-unsourced`,
`staircase-convexity-unsourced`): the GP80 classification paper and the GP93
survey chapter are both paywalled. This paper restates the framework's
definitions rigorously and self-containedly, giving the thread a citable primary
for the definitions without the paywalled originals.

## Definitions this fixes (verbatim statements in the held full text)

- **Circular sequence.** For a set $P$ of $n$ points with distinct
  $x$-coordinates, label points $1..n$ by increasing $x$-coordinate. Rotate a
  horizontal line $\ell$ counterclockwise about a fixed point; at each position
  the order of the orthogonal projections of $P$ onto $\ell$ is a permutation of
  $1..n$. This gives a periodic sequence of permutations, the *circular sequence
  of the configuration*. The first half-period runs from the identity
  $1,2,\dots,n$ to its reversal $n,\dots,1$ (a $180^\circ$ rotation).
- **The half-period rule.** Every permutation is obtained from the previous one
  by reversing one or more *non-overlapping increasing subsequences of adjacent
  elements*.
- **Allowable $n$-sequence.** Goodman–Pollack abstracted this: any sequence of
  permutations starting at $1..n$, ending at $n..1$, satisfying the above rule.
  The **half-period** is the number of moves. A sequence is *simple* if every two
  consecutive permutations differ by the reversal of a single adjacent pair
  $ij$ with $i<j$; *nontrivial* if it has more than two permutations.
- **Local sequence** $\Lambda_i(\Sigma)$: the subsequence of reversals of the
  infinite "wiring" involving index $i$; its half-period is the number of such
  reversals.
- **Wiring diagram / reflection network.** The vertical-column rendering: the
  $i$th wire connects the occurrences of $i$. This makes every allowable
  sequence realizable as the sweep-sequence of a pseudoline arrangement.

## The realizability result most relevant to this run

> "Even though **not every allowable sequence is geometrically realizable** as
> the circular sequence generated by a set of points (or lines), it is however
> true that **every allowable sequence is realizable as the $n$-sequence
> generated by an arrangement of pseudolines**" [ref 28, i.e. Goodman–Pollack].

This is the exact formal content of the run's realizability bind
(`hm-allowable-realizability-etr-complete`, stated in CONTEXT.md): an upper
bound proved over *all* allowable sequences (i.e. all pseudoline arrangements)
is stronger than the geometric ES conjecture and may be false — a pseudoline
realization need not be a point realization. Any allowable-sequence result this
run proves must either (a) be restricted to the *stretchable* (point-realizable)
subclass, or (b) be realized explicitly as a point set before it counts. This is
now on disk as a primary statement, not recalled.

## Number-theoretic / problem context carried in the paper

- Recaps the ES bounds: $2^{n-2}+1 \le f(n)\le 4^{n(1-o(1))}$ (ES 1935/61), and
  Suk / Holmsen–Mojarrad–Pach–Tardos bringing the upper bound to
  $2^{n+O(\sqrt{n\log n})}$. Same-tier as ROOT §1.4–1.5.
- The paper's *own* theorem (DGP conjecture, c=1/845) is about connecting lines /
  local-sequence half-periods — **adjacent** to ES, not a route to it. Held for
  the framework, not as progress on ES(7).

## Relevance verdict

Valuable as the **definitional home** for the allowable-sequence thread. It
gives the half-period/local-sequence vocabulary and the pseudoline-vs-point
realizability distinction a citable on-disk primary. It does **not** settle
`staircase-convexity-unsourced` (the extreme-in-projection / convexity-from-

`lib/es_geom` before it becomes load-bearing. It also does **not** hold the GP80

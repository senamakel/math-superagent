```approach
idea: Joint constraint from simultaneous leading-digit and trailing-digit sieves — prove that the intersection of the real-method survivor set (leading digits avoid 2) and the 3-adic sieve survivor set (trailing digits avoid 2) has density strictly less than the product bound, by exploiting the discrepancy between the irrational rotation n·log_3 2 mod 1 and the 3-adic periodic structure n mod 2·3^(k-1)
mechanism: Lagarias (LAG-4) states that the real truncated method controls the ~log_3 X most significant digits and the 3-adic method controls the ~log_3 X least significant digits, and that combining them is open. The specific gap: for a candidate n to be a counterexample, BOTH sets of digits must avoid 2. Let k = ⌊log_3 X⌋. Then:

- Leading constraint: the fractional part {n·log_3 2} must avoid a set of "bad intervals" in [0,1) corresponding to digit-2 in the first few ternary digits. The measure of the bad set for the top k digits is 1 − (2/3)^k. The number of n ≤ X satisfying the leading constraint is ~(2/3)^k X = X^{1 − k·(1 − log_3 2)/log_3 X} ≈ X^{log_3 2} — matching Narkiewicz's exponent.
- Trailing constraint: n mod 2·3^(k-1) must lie in A_k, which has 2^(k-1) classes out of 2·3^(k-1). The proportion is (1/2)(2/3)^(k-1).

If the two constraints were independent, the joint proportion would be O((2/3)^{2k}) = O(X^{2·log_3(2/3)}) ≈ X^{-0.738...}, which is summable. The difficulty is proving they are sufficiently uncorrelated. But there is a theorem in the right direction: Weyl's criterion and the Koksma–Hlawka inequality. The sequence n ↦ (n·log_3 2 mod 1, n mod M) for M = 2·3^(k-1) is a linear flow on the product of the circle and a finite cyclic group. Its discrepancy can be bounded using the continued fraction expansion of log_3 2. The key: because log_3 2 has bounded partial quotients (or at least, there is a bound on the approximation quality), the joint distribution is close to uniform over ranges of length M·q where q is a convergent denominator. This allows bounding the joint count of n ≤ X that survive both sieves simultaneously.

Why it fits: This is the most direct attack on "the middle digits" as Lagarias frames it. The 3-adic method (trailing digits) gives a set of residue classes on n; the real method (leading digits) gives a set of intervals for {n·log_3 2}. The intersection is a set of n that satisfy a simultaneous Diophantine condition. The continued fraction of α_0 = log_3 2 determines the discrepancy between the real and 3-adic constraints. If the discrepancy is small enough relative to the densities, one gets a bound below X^{log_3 2}, which is a genuine improvement on Narkiewicz. This is speculative — the required discrepancy bound may not hold — but it is the natural theorem to attempt.

Status: proposed (speculative — requires a discrepancy bound that may not hold for log_3 2)
First-step: Compute the continued fraction of log_3 2 to high precision (at least 50 convergents), measure the quality of rational approximations, and compute the discrepancy of the sequence (n·log_3 2 mod 1, n mod 2·3^(k-1)) for small X and k, comparing to the product-measure prediction. This will establish whether the independence heuristic is numerically plausible before attempting a proof.
```

```approach
idea: Binomial expansion of (1+3)^t via Lucas's theorem — convert the digit-2-free condition into a carry-propagation constraint on binomial coefficients modulo 3
mechanism: For n ≥ 1, write n = 2t + ε with ε ∈ {0,1}. Then 2^n = 2^ε · 4^t = 2^ε · (1+3)^t = 2^ε · Σ_{j=0}^t binom(t,j) 3^j. The coefficient of 3^j in this expansion is binom(t,j) mod (3, carries from lower positions). By Lucas's theorem, binom(t,j) mod 3 = Π_i binom(t_i, j_i) mod 3 where t_i, j_i are the base-3 digits of t and j. The digit at position j in the final ternary expansion is not binom(t,j) alone — carries from lower positions propagate upward. But the carry structure is constrained: a carry of size c into position j can only occur if Σ_{h<j} binom(t,h) 3^{h-j} ≥ 1, i.e., if there is "overflow" from below. The conjecture says: for t large enough (n > 8), there is always at least one position j where the sum of binom(t,j) plus incoming carry ≡ 2 mod 3. The Lucas decomposition means the presence of a digit 2 is determined by the base-3 digit patterns of t and j — this is a completely different structural lens than the residue-class sieve, and it ties the problem to the 2-adic and 3-adic structure of binomial coefficients simultaneously.

Why it fits: The sieve-dynamics thread proved |A_k| = 2^(k-1) forever — the low-digit structure never forces a 2. The binomial expansion exposes the whole number at once, not just the trailing digits. The Lucas decomposition of binom(t,j) mod 3 means every ternary digit is a product of LOCAL decisions (one per base-3 digit of t and j), but carries are NONLOCAL. The obstruction to digit-2-free-ness would then be: the nonlocal carry structure must produce a 2 somewhere, even though locally (at each digit position) the binomial coefficients mod 3 avoid creating one. This is a "global constraint from local rules" problem, which is the right shape for a theorem.

Status: proposed
First-step: Write (1+3)^t = Σ binom(t,j) 3^j explicitly for small t, compute carries, and verify for t corresponding to n=0,2,8 that no carry produces a digit 2; then characterize for which t a 2 first appears and why — connect to Lucas's theorem to identify the structural condition.
```

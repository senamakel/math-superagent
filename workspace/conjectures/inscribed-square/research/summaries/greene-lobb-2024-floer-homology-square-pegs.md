# Greene–Lobb 2024 — Floer homology and square pegs

**Source:** Joshua Evan Greene, Andrew Lobb, "Floer homology and square pegs," arXiv:2404.05179 (2024). Full text at [[research/sources/greene-lobb-2024-floer-homology-square-pegs.full.md]].

## What it establishes

Constructs a version of **Lagrangian Floer homology** whose chain complex is generated by the inscriptions of a rectangle into a real-analytic Jordan curve.

**Main results:**
- A rectifiable Jordan curve admits inscriptions of a whole **interval of rectangles** (via spectral invariants).
- **In particular, a rectifiable Jordan curve inscribes a square if the area it encloses is more than half that of a circle of equal diameter** (i.e., area > (1/2)·(π/4)·diam²).
- The homology JF∗(γ, θ, D) is two-dimensional, supported in Maslov gradings 1 and 2: (𝔽₂)⁽²⁾ ⊕ (𝔽₂)⁽¹⁾ (Theorem 2.23).
- **Theorems 1.1 context:** every smooth Jordan curve inscribes every cyclic quadrilateral (Greene–Lobb 2023, Invent. Math.); this is optimal — no other quadrilateral inscribes in every smooth curve (the circle), and the regularity cannot weaken to continuous: a cyclic quadrilateral that is not an isosceles trapezoid fails to inscribe in *some* triangle. This is a **sharp demonstration that shrinkout is real**: smooth approximations of the triangle give quadrilaterals that shrink away.
- Technical heart: the differential counts pseudoholomorphic strips with boundary on two Lagrangian tori L₀, L₁, constrained to avoid a complex line (the diagonal) to ensure ∂² = 0. Lemmas 2.16–2.22 rule out diagonal touching, diagonal breaking, sphere bubbling, and disc bubbling; Lemma 2.7 shows trajectories stay at distance > Width(γ)/2 from the diagonal.

## Why it matters here

- The area/diameter hypothesis (area > π/4 · (diam/2)²·2, i.e., more than half the circle of equal diameter) is a **quantitative condition on the curve** that guarantees a square — a different flavor from smoothness/rectifiability. It is the symplectic analogue of the "large scale" obstruction to shrinkout: if the curve encloses enough area relative to its diameter, a square cannot shrink away.
- The "sharp obstruction" (cyclic quadrilaterals that are not isosceles trapezoids fail on triangles) is a **named class where the naive approximation argument provably fails** — a concrete, publishable obstruction statement of exactly the kind GOAL.md's completion criterion 3 asks for (though already published by GL — the run must find its own, or formalize this one).

```claim
id: gl2024-area-diameter-square

```

```claim
id: gl2024-cyclic-quadrilateral-sharpness
statement: For every cyclic quadrilateral that is not an isosceles trapezoid, there exists a continuous Jordan curve (even a triangle) that does not inscribe it; hence the smooth→continuous approximation argument for cyclic quadrilaterals fails via shrinkout.
status: asserted-by-source
evidence: Greene–Lobb, arXiv:2404.05179, Theorem 1.1 discussion
holds-here: yes — a published, exact obstruction showing shrinkout is real and not a technicality
falsifies: a cyclic non-isosceles-trapezoid quadrilateral that inscribes in every triangle
```

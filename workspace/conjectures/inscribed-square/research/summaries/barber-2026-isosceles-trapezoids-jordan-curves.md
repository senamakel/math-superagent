# Barber 2026 — Isosceles trapezoids in Jordan curves (Floer homology)

**Source:** (Barber), "Isosceles trapezoids and Jordan curves" / Jordan Floer homology extension, arXiv:2604.27717 (2026 preprint). Full text at [[research/sources/barber-2026-isosceles-trapezoids-jordan-curves.full.md]].

**Status note:** arXiv ID dated April 2026 (after this run's present — treat as reported by the downloader). Preprint, not peer-reviewed.

## What it establishes

Constructs a **Jordan Floer homology** whose chain complex is generically generated by inscriptions of isosceles trapezoids in a smooth Jordan curve — an extension of Greene–Lobb's Jordan Floer homology. 

- **Theorem 1.3 (Greene–Lobb):** every smooth Jordan curve inscribes every isosceles trapezoid.
- **Theorem 1.4 (Matschke):** every piecewise-C¹ Jordan curve without cusps inscribes every isosceles trapezoid.
- **Theorem 1.5 (Asano–Ike):** a rectifiable or locally monotone Jordan curve inscribes every rectangle (carried in-library by asano-ike-2024-rectifiable-square).
- **New (this paper):** nontriviality of the isosceles-trapezoid Jordan Floer homology re-establishes the smooth case; spectral invariants of the action filtration give **new cases of non-smooth Jordan curves that admit inscriptions of isosceles trapezoids**. The exact class of new non-smooth curves is the paper's contribution.

**Definitions carried (useful for Lean):**
- Definition 1.9 (locally monotone, verbatim): γ is locally monotone if for every θ ∈ R there exists an open neighbourhood U_θ ⊂ R of θ and a unit vector v_θ ∈ R² such that the map g_{v_θ} : φ ↦ γ(φ)·v_θ is strictly monotone on U_θ. (This is the precise hypothesis of Stromquist's theorem — the same definition the run needs for the Lean statement of matschke2014-stromquist-locally-monotone.)
- Definition 1.10 (locally K-Lipschitz-graphical): a locally monotone curve is locally K-Lipschitz-graphical if additionally ... (the local graph structure has Lipschitz constant ≤ K).
- The Hamiltonian H(z,w) = (1/2)θr(1−r)|z−w|² generates the isosceles-trapezoid transformation G_{r,θ} = F_r⁻¹∘R_θ∘F_r; the submanifolds γ×γ and G_{r,θ}(γ×γ) are Lagrangian (Lemmas 2.3–2.4).

## Why it matters here

- The trapezoid peg problem (Conjecture 1.2: every Jordan curve inscribes every isosceles trapezoid) is intermediate between the rectangle problem (solved for continuous curves by Vaughan) and the square problem. The square is an isosceles trapezoid with equal bases; the trapezoid case has the same "smooth → non-smooth" gap as the square.
- The verbatim locally-monotone definition (Definition 1.9) is a **primary-source-level statement of Stromquist's hypothesis** — this run's Lean formalization should use exactly this wording.
- The spectral-invariant method (GL 2024/2026) is the same family that produces the positive-measure rectangle result; Barber extends it to trapezoids. If the run's extension target is "curves admitting a continuous Legendrian lift," the Floer and sheaf methods are converging on the same class.

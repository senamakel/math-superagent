## What this establishes — the survey-tier landscape for the distance-graph problem family

This is the canonical survey chapter on the subject family, and the frontier's
#3 row (3 of the library's sources cite it). Its scope:

- **Distance graphs:** chromatic numbers of graphs on R^n with a set of
  forbidden distances; the general setting of which (R², 1) — this run's
  target — is the classical instance.
- **Graphs of diameters:** Borsuk-type problems — colouring sets so that
  points at maximal distance (diameter) get different colours; a sibling
  problem family with its own bounds.
- **Key referenced threads (high-dimensional):** independence numbers of
  distance graphs with vertices in {−1,0,1}ⁿ (Guterman–Lyubimov–Raigorodskii–
  Usachev 2010); large-chromatic graphs without large cliques (Raigorodskii–
  Rubanov 2010); rational vs real forbidden distances (Raigorodskii–Shitova
  2008); Kupavskii's chromatic number of R^n with a set of forbidden distances
  (2010); Lyubimov–Raigorodskii lower bounds on independence numbers (2009).
- **Relationship to the run's problem:** the survey collects bounds and
  relationships for the *high-dimensional* and *multiple-forbidden-distance*
  settings. The plane single-distance case (Hadwiger–Nelson) is its classical
  base case; the chapter does not give constructive unit-distance
  realizability methods, but it is the authoritative map of the surrounding
  problem family and their known bounds.

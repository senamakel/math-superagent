# Distances realized by sets covering the plane — Woodall 1973

**Source:** doi:10.1016/0097-3165(73)90020-4
**Author:** Douglas R. Woodall, JCT Series A 14 (1973) 187–200
**Full text:** not on disk; read via read_sources.

## What this establishes

Foundational study of which distances are realised by a decomposition of the
plane into parts — directly tied to the colour-class structure of distance-graph
colourings:

- Covering the plane with sets enforces which distances must occur within parts;
  a colour class of the unit-distance graph is a set realising no distance-1
  pair, so the size/density structure of such sets constrains colourings.
- Relates to the local structure of plane coverings and how decompositions
  impose colourability constraints on distance graphs.
- Built on the de Bruijn–Erdős finite-subgraph reduction (cites the 1951 paper)
  and on Larman's and Raiskii's work on realising distances in decompositions.

## Why it matters here

Colour classes of a proper colouring are sets with no unit distance. Woodall's
line of work is the classical study of how large/structured such sets can be —
the density side of the chromatic problem, which complements the finite-graph
construction side the run focuses on.

# Structure of cell decompositions in extremal Szemerédi–Trotter examples (Katz–Silier)

**Subject:** The structural / proto-inverse side of the Szemerédi–Trotter
theory: *which* point–line configurations can be extremal, described in terms
negligibly few (O(N^{1/3})) parameters around two "bushes" of lines; with the
directly relevant analogue for **unit circles**. This is the companion to the
library's `szemeredi-trotter-incidence-algebraic-extremal.md` (which holds the
incidence bound and the Guth–Silier sharp *constructions*); this one classifies
the *structure* of near-extremal examples.

## What it establishes

### A recipe that generates extremal configurations from O(N^{1/3}) parameters
There is a recipe depending on just `O(N^{1/3})` parameters which, for some
choices of the parameters, produces a configuration of `N` points and `N` lines
realising the Szemerédi–Trotter bound `O(N^{4/3})` incidences up to constants
(and otherwise "fails"). Any **near-extremal** example is *densely related to a
successful instance* of this recipe — i.e. the extremal configurations are
recognisable, not arbitrary.

### The structural theorem (Theorem 3.20, lines; Theorem 5.21, unit circles)
Extremal configurations admit a cell decomposition organised around **two
"bushes"** — families of lines all incident to one of two *organising points* —
with parameters `M1, M2` up to about `N`. Most points lie on the two bushes, or,
after a projective transformation, on a **product set**. This is the
"proto-inverse Szemerédi–Trotter theorem": large portions of any extremal
example can be generated by the recipe from the algebraic/nice-basis point sets
of the sharp-construction papers.

### The unit-circles analogue (relevant to unit-distance graphs)
The **same structural theorem holds for unit circles** (Theorem 5.21):
near-extremal unit-circle incidence configurations have a two-bush / product-set
decomposition. This is the incidence framework under which each unit distance is
a point-unit-circle incidence.

## Why it matters here

- It is the strongest *structural* statement the neighbouring incidence theory
  holds: it says the extremal (densest) unit-distance-producing point sets are
  **not arbitrary** but organised around two bushes/product sets over algebraic
  point sets — exactly the `Q(√k)` universe the run's exact coordinates live in.
  It strengthens the run's "search over algebraic constructions, not random
  points" guidance from a heuristic to a structural expectation.
- It is the **top-ranked frontier lead** (cited 3× by the library's own sources
  — the highest non-organisational count — via the ST/rigidity papers), so
  capturing it closes the frontier's strongest open row.

## What it does NOT give

- No statement about **chromatic number**. Extremality of unit-distance density
  at `O(n^{4/3})` is far below what would force a 5-colouring; this source
  bounds the *shape* of dense constructions, not their colouring. It is a
  construction-structure input, not a colouring result.

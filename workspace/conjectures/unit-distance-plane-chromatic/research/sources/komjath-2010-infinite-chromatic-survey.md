# Komjáth 2010 — survey of chromatic numbers of infinite graphs

**Source:** Péter Komjáth, "The chromatic number of infinite graphs — A survey",
*Discrete Mathematics* 311 (2011) 1448–1449.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2010.11.004.

**How obtained:** `exa_search` (research paper) returned the abstract and
reference list.

## What it establishes / situates

- De Bruijn–Erdős compactness phenomena for chromatic numbers of infinite
  graphs, and where they hold and fail.
- Erdős–Hajnal / Shelah results: uncountably chromatic graphs, and the
  incompactness behaviour of higher chromatic numbers.
- Komjáth–Shelah 2002 (arXiv:math/0212064): consistent with set theory that for
  every monotone `f: ω → ω` there is a graph of size and chromatic number `ℵ₁`
  whose every `n`-chromatic subgraph has at least `f(n)` vertices; and a
  consistent refutation of the Taylor conjecture. This answers a $250 Erdős
  problem.

## Why it matters here

It is the survey that fixes the *metadata* of the compactness reduction: when it
holds (finite chromatic number), what choice principle is involved, and when it
fails (higher infinities). The plane unit-distance graph is the case where the
reduction holds cleanly, which is why the finite-graph framing of `problem.md`
is valid. Also it records that the AC-dependence is real and subtle (linked
material: Payne constructed a unit-distance graph whose chromatic number depends
on the axiom system) — a caveat the run should keep in mind, though not one that
blocks the finite-search work here, since the finite lower/upper bounds are
choice-free.

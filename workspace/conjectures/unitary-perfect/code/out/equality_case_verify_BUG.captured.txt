BUG IN equality_case_verify.py: admissible_sizes() is not minimal

It iterates primes in order, takes the first count values, then sorts THOSE.
That is not the count smallest admissible sizes.

  admissible_sizes(9) as computed : [5, 9, 13, 17, 29, 49, 121, 361, 529]
  the 9 genuinely smallest        : [5, 9, 13, 17, 29, 37, 41, 49, 53]
  admissible, smaller, OMITTED    : [37, 41, 53]
  larger, WRONGLY INCLUDED        : [121, 361, 529]  (11^2, 19^2, 23^2)

37, 41 and 53 are 1 mod 4, hence admissible as themselves, and all are
smaller than 121. The generator never reaches them because it stops
iterating primes at 29.

Consequence: M is supposed to be a MAXIMUM. Computed over a non-minimal
set it is an UNDER-estimate, so "M < T therefore impossible" is not
established by that computation - it is the wrong direction of error.

   a             T(a)    M as computed        M correct  correct M >= T
   2      1.600000000      1.360544218      1.435897436           False
   8      1.992217899      1.625699272      1.720320566           False
  20      1.999998093      1.800679358      1.926889821           False
  28      1.999999993      1.892154201      1.997752860           False
  29      1.999999996      1.892271515      2.004964964            True
  30      1.999999998      1.892381781      2.012100070            True

So the exclusion 2 <= a <= 28 SURVIVES under the correct minimal set:
  M(28) = 1.997752860 < T(28) = 1.999999993   (still strictly below)
but the claim that a = 29 is excluded is FALSE:
  M(29) = 2.004964964 > T(29) = 1.999999996   (not excluded)

The four qualitative checks in the run reproduction are all correct and
are unaffected by the bug: T(1) = 4/3 attained exactly by {5,9} = odd
part of 90; 257 prime and forced at a=8; 9 and 49 admissible, 3 and 7 not.

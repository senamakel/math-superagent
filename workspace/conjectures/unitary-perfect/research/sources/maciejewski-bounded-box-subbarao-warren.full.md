###### Abstract.

A unitary perfect number is a positive integer n n satisfying σ ∗ ​ ( n) = 2 ​ n \sigma^{*}(n)=2n, where σ ∗ \sigma^{*} sums unitary divisors. Only five examples are known, and no sixth has been found. We revisit the Subbarao–Warren problem by keeping the seed factor 2 a + 1 2^{a}+1 explicit in the full balance

 | ( 2 a + 1) ​ ∏ i ( p i e i + 1) = 2 a + 1 ​ ∏ i p i e i. (2^{a}+1)\prod_{i}(p_{i}^{e_{i}}+1)=2^{a+1}\prod_{i}p_{i}^{e_{i}}. |  |

Within a bounded enumeration of source components in the odd dependency graph, every admissible source kernel is either one of the two kernels occurring in the known nonsquarefree examples, 3 2 3^{2} and 5 4 5^{4}, or one of five additional impostor kernels. We give a reproducible three-filter certificate eliminating those impostor kernels for all relevant seed classes with 1 ≤ a ≤ 10000 1\leq a\leq 10000. The filters combine Zsigmondy-type exponent obstructions, inherited non-3-Higgs witnesses, and deterministic 2-adic budget overshoot.

The remaining obstruction is the auxiliary set H even H_{\mathrm{even}} of even m m for which every prime divisor of 2 m + 1 2^{m}+1 is 3-Higgs. A structural lemma reduces finiteness of H even H_{\mathrm{even}} to the prime branch m = 2 ​ p m=2p, while allowing finite computations to leave composite candidates inherited from unresolved prime divisors. Using the supplied factor cache and APR-CL primality-verification transcripts, we prove | H even ∩ [2, 40000] | ≤ 201 |H_{\mathrm{even}}\cap[2,40000]|\leq 201 and | H even ∩ [2, 50000] | ≤ 272 |H_{\mathrm{even}}\cap[2,50000]|\leq 272, with explicit undecided frontier lists.

Ford’s theorem for downward-closed prime sets gives an unconditional power-saving thinness bound for H even H_{\mathrm{even}}, but not finiteness. The remaining task is a divisor-level problem for the cyclotomic values Φ 4 ​ p ​ ( 2) \Phi_{4p}(2). Thus the paper does not prove finiteness; it gives a bounded-box elimination, a verified finite frontier, and a precise analytic target for closing the remaining branch.

###### Conjecture (strict form).

The five integers above are the only unitary perfect numbers.

Wall [3] proved that any new unitary perfect number has at least nine odd components. Graham [4] showed that a UPN with squarefree odd part must be one of 6, 60, 87360 6,60,87360, and that any hypothetical further UPN must have an odd prime divisor exceeding 2 15 2^{15}. The two known examples with nonsquarefree odd part have specific small “kernels”: 3 2 3^{2} in 90 90 and 5 4 5^{4} in the fifth UPN. We shall call these the *known kernels*.

In particular, 2 a + 1 2^{a}+1 is the odd seed factor that initiates the dependency cascade. For each odd prime p p in the support, the exponent e p e_{p} matches the total incoming p p -adic valuation from both the seed and the other components:

 | e p = ∑ q ∈ components v p ​ ( q + 1). e_{p}=\sum_{q\in\mathrm{components}}v_{p}(q+1). |  |

A prime p p is *3-Higgs*(OEIS A057447 [8]; see also Burris–Yeats [16]) if p − 1 p-1 divides the cube of the product of smaller 3-Higgs primes. Every prime divisor of a UPN is 3-Higgs.

The *odd dependency graph*has odd 3-Higgs primes as vertices and directed edges p → r p\to r whenever, for some admissible exponent e e, r | p e + 1 r\mid p^{e}+1. A *source SCC*is a strongly connected component with no incoming edge from outside the component (except from the seed 2 a + 1 2^{a}+1). Every odd prime power in a UPN belongs to an SCC reachable from a source SCC.

## 3. The Three-Filter Closure Certificate

For an impostor kernel K K and a representative a a in its seed congruence class, define the candidate ( K, a) (K,a). We exhibit a rigorous obstruction for each candidate via one of three filters.

### 3.1. Filter Z: Zsigmondy / Higgs exponent

By Zsigmondy’s theorem [5], every primitive prime divisor r r of p e + 1 p^{e}+1 (with e > 1 e>1) satisfies r ≡ 1 ( mod 2 ​ e) r\equiv 1\pmod{2e}. For r r to be 3-Higgs, every prime factor of 2 ​ e 2e must be 3-Higgs and occur in 2 ​ e 2e with exponent at most 3. Applied to the seed exponent a a itself, certain values of a a are immediately incompatible.

### 3.2. Filter N: Seed-divisor non-3-Higgs witness

If m | a m\mid a and a / m a/m is odd, then 2 m + 1 | 2 a + 1 2^{m}+1\mid 2^{a}+1. If 2 m + 1 2^{m}+1 contains a non-3-Higgs prime factor, then any UPN with 2 a | n 2^{a}\,\|\,n would inherit a non-3-Higgs prime divisor, a contradiction. We precompute (full or partial) factorizations of 2 m + 1 2^{m}+1 for m ≤ M m\leq M and reject ( K, a) (K,a) whenever any factored proper divisor exposes a non-3-Higgs prime.

### 3.3. Filter O: 2-adic budget overshoot

At each step, compute

###### Lemma 1.

The lower-bound closure is monotone in the seed. If v 2 > a + 1 v_{2}>a+1 at any step using a subset S S of factor ⁡ ( 2 a + 1) \mathrm{factor}(2^{a}+1), the same overshoot survives when more primes from factor ⁡ ( 2 a + 1) \mathrm{factor}(2^{a}+1) are added. ###### Proof.

Adding a prime r r to targets \mathrm{targets} contributes v 2 ​ ( r e r + 1) ≥ 1 v_{2}(r^{e_{r}}+1)\geq 1 to the running v 2 v_{2} sum and adds the prime factorization of r e r + 1 r^{e_{r}}+1 to the odd needs, which can only raise other targets. #### Caution: GRH and Artin/Hooley density are not the right target.

It is tempting to expect that effective Chebotarev or GRH-conditional primitive-root density theorems—e.g., Hooley’s conditional Artin estimate or its Kummer-extension refinements—will supply the needed bound. Artin/Hooley-style theorems count varying primes q q in which a fixed integer a a has order q − 1 q-1; for fixed p p, however, the set { r: ord r ⁡ ( 2) = 4 ​ p } \{r:\operatorname{ord}_{r}(2)=4p\} is finite—it is exactly the prime support of the single integer Φ 4 ​ p ​ ( 2) \Phi_{4p}(2). A finiteness proof must control the divisors of Φ 4 ​ p ​ ( 2) \Phi_{4p}(2) individually (a divisor-transference statement), not via a density estimate on 1 mod 4 ​ p {1\bmod 4p}.

#### The actual missing theorem.

We emphasize that Conjecture 29 is a *divisor-level equidistribution*statement about the prime divisors of a single fixed integer Φ 4 ​ p ​ ( 2) \Phi_{4p}(2). It is not a consequence of any standard Chebotarev theorem, including effective Chebotarev under GRH (Lagarias–Odlyzko [11]). The reason is structural: Chebotarev theorems control varying primes across a range subject to a Frobenius condition; here the prime support of Φ 4 ​ p ​ ( 2) \Phi_{4p}(2) is a finite set, not a range. The needed object is a *divisor-randomness*or transference-type theorem inspired by recent work of Ford on the Poisson approximation of prime divisors of shifted primes, transplanted from random shifted primes to prime divisors of fixed cyclotomic values. Such a transplant does not currently exist in the literature; identifying it as the missing input is, in our view, the precise analytic content of the conjecture.

#### Final assessment.

The full Subbarao–Warren conjecture is not proved here. What is proved is a clean reduction of the impostor branch (within the bounded box ℬ \mathcal{B} at max a = 10000 \max_{a}=10000) to a single specific analytic question (Conjecture 6, equivalently Conjecture 23), together with two rigorous structural reductions that further sharpen the target:

- •

Theorem 7 (Prime-case reduction): | H even | < ∞ |H_{\mathrm{even}}|<\infty iff | H even prime | < ∞ |H_{\mathrm{even}}^{\mathrm{prime}}|<\infty, with explicit bound | H even | ≤ 4 | H even prime | |H_{\mathrm{even}}|\leq 4^{|H_{\mathrm{even}}^{\mathrm{prime}}|}. So the full conjecture hinges on showing that only finitely many *primes*p p have all factors of 2 2 ​ p + 1 2^{2p}+1 in 𝒫 3 \mathcal{P}_{3}.

- •

Theorem 30 (Conditional finiteness): under (H1), a divisor-level mod 16 \bmod\,16 equidistribution hypothesis on the prime factors of Φ 4 ​ p ​ ( 2) \Phi_{4p}(2), plus (H2), an effective lower bound ω ⁡ ( Φ 4 ​ p ​ ( 2)) ≫ log ⁡ p \omega(\Phi_{4p}(2))\gg\log p, H even H_{\mathrm{even}} is finite with an explicit candidate set. We stress that (H1) is *not*a known consequence of GRH or of standard effective Chebotarev theorems—those control varying primes in Frobenius classes, whereas (H1) is a divisor-level statement about a single fixed cyclotomic value. (H2) is the natural target of Stewart’s program on radicals of Lehmer sequences [12, 13].

What we cannot do unconditionally is bridge the scale gap between Ford’s thinness bound ( ≪ X 1 − η \ll X^{1-\eta} for the entire 3-Higgs prime set) and the required statement at height x = 2 2 ​ k x=2^{2k}. This is the precise content of Conjecture 23. The supplementary computational evidence—bounded verification through m ≤ 40000 m\leq 40000 together with Pollard p − 1 p-1 resistance and ECM at B 1 = 10 6 B_{1}=10^{6} – 10 8 10^{8} across the 191 191 partial-cofactor candidates—makes it clear that the obstructions are NFS-scale, not shallow.

#### Where the remaining theorem must live.

To make the analytic positioning fully precise: Ford’s theorem proves that the 3-Higgs prime set 𝒫 3 \mathcal{P}_{3} is power-saving thin once it omits a prime, and this suffices for our unconditional thinness theorem for H even H_{\mathrm{even}}. However, thinness is not finiteness. On the remaining prime branch m = 2 ​ p m=2p, the problem is *not*to count primes in the progression 1 mod 4 ​ p 1\bmod 4p globally, but to exclude admissible prime divisors of the *single fixed*cyclotomic value Φ 4 ​ p ​ ( 2) \Phi_{4p}(2). Existing friability theorems for shifted primes concern initial-segment smoothness of p − 1 p-1; what is missing here is a *divisor-level*theorem controlling the logarithmic mass of prime divisors r | Φ 4 ​ p ​ ( 2) r\mid\Phi_{4p}(2) for which ( r − 1) / ( 4 ​ p) ∈ 𝒮 3 ( ≤ 3) (r-1)/(4p)\in\mathcal{S}_{3}^{(\leq 3)}. Of the conjectures we formulate, the divisor log-mass bound (Conjecture 24) and the divisor mod- 16 16 equidistribution (Conjecture 29) are the most plausible long-range analytic targets; the semigroup-counting route (Conjecture 26) is logically valid but, by Proposition 28, requires 𝒫 3 \mathcal{P}_{3} itself to be implausibly sparse.

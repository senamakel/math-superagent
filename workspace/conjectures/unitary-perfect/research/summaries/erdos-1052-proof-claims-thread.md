A unitary divisor of $n$ is $d\mid n$ such that $(d,n/d)=1$. A number $n\geq 1$ is a [unitary perfect number][13] if it is the sum of its unitary divisors (aside from $n$ itself).

Are there only finite many unitary perfect numbers?

Guy [Gu04] reports that Carlitz, Erdős, and Subbarao offer \$10 for settling this question, and that Subbarao offers 10 cents for each new example.

There are no odd unitary perfect numbers. There are five known unitary perfect numbers ( [A002827][16] in the OEIS):\[6, 60, 90, 87360, 146361946186458562560000.\]This is problem B3 in Guy's collection [Gu04].

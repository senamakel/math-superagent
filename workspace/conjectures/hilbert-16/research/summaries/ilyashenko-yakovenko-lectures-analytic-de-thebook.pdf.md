## What this is

The standard cited monograph on analytic differential equations by Yulij
Ilyashenko and Sergei Yakovenko, AMS Graduate Studies in Mathematics vol. 86
(2008), xiv+625 pp., ISBN 978-0-8218-3667-5. This is the canonical text named
in problem.md as one of the two starting places for H16.2, and it is cited by
numerous held sources (Binyamini–Novikov–Yakovenko, Kaloshin, Dumortier–
Ilyashenko–Rousseau, Yeung 2025, and others) as `[IY] GSM/86`.

## Caveat (author-stated, load-bearing)

**This hosted PDF is the authors' own outdated draft**, not the printed book.
The front matter states verbatim: "The draft posted on this site is really
outdated, with many inaccuracies, sloppy phrases and even errors. Besides, a
number of sections had been added or modified. It sole purpose is to give an
idea what appears in the printed book. **Do not quote this draft to avoid
misleading enumeration of pages, theorems and definitions.**"

So the page/theorem/enumeration numbering in this file does **not** match the
printed GSM 86. It is usable for *content and definitions* (normal forms,
desingularization, Bautin, Dulac, quasianalytic/almost-regular germs), but any
citation to it must be to GSM 86 (2008) and must not rely on this draft's
numbering. This is recorded as a known limitation, not silently treated as the
printed book.

## Contents covered (relevant to this run)

- **Ch. I — Normal forms and desingularization**: analytic DEs in the complex
  domain, holomorphic foliations and singularities, formal flows and
  embeddings, formal/holomorphic normal forms, desingularization in the plane
  (blow-up) — the machinery for resolving nilpotent and degenerate singular
  points that the DRR program uses at every graphic vertex.
- **Ch. II — Singular points of planar analytic vector fields**, including:
  - §10 algebraic decidability of local problems / center–focus;
  - §12 **zeros of parametric families of analytic functions and small
    amplitude limit cycles** — the vanishing-ability / Bautin-ideal frame;
  - §13 **quadratic vector fields and the Bautin theorem** (M(2)=3);
  - holonomy and first integrals (§11).
- **Ch. III+ — linear systems**, Riemann–Hilbert, irregular singularities,
  Stokes phenomenon.

## What it establishes that this run relies on

The analytic/geometric scaffolding under all of the run's core claims:
desingularization reduces a planar analytic field near a removable singularity
to a normal form; the return map of a focus is analytic in the transverse
coordinate, so its Taylor coefficients (the Lyapunov quantities) generate the
Bautin ideal and finitely many of them bound small-amplitude cyclicity;
quadratic fields have M(2)=3 (Bautin). This is the textbook-level statement of
where analyticity enters — the step Test 1 of problem.md requires every
finiteness argument to have.

# GMV ECT versus the I¹₆b four-Dulac displacement

Full text: [[grau-manosas-villadelprat-chebyshev-abelian-2008-arxiv.full]]; arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.1140.

GMV's theorem is a sufficient criterion for an ECT-system of **first-order Abelian integrals** over a period annulus of ovals. Theorem A requires H=Φ(x)+Ψ(y), involutions for Φ and Ψ, the prescribed balance chains, CT hypotheses, and the stated small-o condition. Theorem B requires H=A(x)+B(x)y^{2m}, g=y^{2s−1}, s>m(n−2), and CT balances ℓᵢ. The source explains the dynamical use: the Abelian integral is the first ε-coefficient of a Poincaré displacement for a small Hamiltonian perturbation.

These hypotheses are not established for the complete I¹₆b graphic. The RR 2015 source defines displacement as a composition/difference of regular transitions and Dulac maps; it says the second Dulac type is not needed for its proved cases and supplies first-type formulas only. The four-map I¹₆b problem therefore lacks (i) a single separated Hamiltonian period-annulus representation, (ii) identification of the full nonlinear displacement with a finite Abelian-integral family, (iii) CT/Wronskian verification uniform in the blown-up parameters, and (iv) control on vanishing slow-divergence strata and the non-analytic/generalized remainders. GMV can apply only after a new reduction proving all of these.

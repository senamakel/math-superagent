# Dumortier–Panazzolo–Roussarie 2007 — slow–fast counterexample

Source: https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-07-08688-1
Full text: `research/sources/dumortier-panazzolo-roussarie-lienard-2007.full.md`

The paper proves that a degree-6 polynomial classical Liénard equation can have at least four limit cycles, and consequently for every even degree 2n with n≥3 there are examples with more than n cycles. The mechanism is a singular perturbation: a family with small ε has attracting and repelling slow curves, and canard/relaxation oscillations are selected using the slow-divergence integral. This refutes the Lins–de Melo–Pugh sharp count and is the required slow–fast stress test for proposed sharp bounds. It is not a result on uniform H(2); it shows that apparently sharp counts must survive singular limits. Evidence: asserted-by-source from the primary article; falsifier: a correction withdrawing the degree-6 construction or invalidating the slow–fast hypotheses.

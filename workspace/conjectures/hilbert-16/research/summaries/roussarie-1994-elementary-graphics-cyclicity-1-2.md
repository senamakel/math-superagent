## What the source establishes (abstract level)

**Two general C^∞ theorems** (not restricted to quadratic, before the
specialization):
1. A graphic through any number of **attracting hyperbolic saddles** (ratio
   r > 1) and **attracting semi-hyperbolic points** (one negative eigenvalue)
   has **cyclicity 1**.
2. A graphic with **one hyperbolic and one semi-hyperbolic singularity of
   opposite character** has **cyclicity 2**.

**Specialization:** 33 graphics appearing among quadratic systems (listed in
the preceding DRR 1994 paper) have cyclicity **at most 2**, five of them only
under generic conditions. Fixed connections is the standing hypothesis.

## Explicitly NOT established

- The semi-hyperbolic attractor result (cyclicity 1) is notable: **semi-
  hyperbolic points are NOT hyperbolic saddles** — so this extends slightly
  beyond the strictly-hyperbolic machinery of Mourtada/BGS/Dukov. It is still
  elementary (non-nilpotent).

## Implication for this run

This is the cyclicity-1/2 backbone of the elementary DRR rows (hemicycles,
hyperbolic saddles at infinity). Combined with the held DGR 2002 full text it
tells the run that the elementary part of the DRR inventory is essentially
closed with small explicit bounds — the open rows are nilpotent/degenerate
(pp-type, fake saddles, semihyperbolic of the H14^3 sort). The "semi-hyperbolic
attractor ⇒ cyclicity 1" statement is a C^∞ result, so it passes test 1 without
analyticity — consistent with the pattern that elementary graphics are settled
in the smooth category and analyticity only bites in the degenerate classes.

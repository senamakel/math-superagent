# Bamon 1986 — quadratic vector fields in the plane have a finite number of limit cycles

Full text: [[primary-bamon-quadratic-finiteness.pdf.full]] (Publ. Math. IHÉS 64
(1986) 111–142, DOI 10.1007/BF02699193, Numdam scan).

## What the source establishes (held full text, verbatim)

**Theorem A.** Every quadratic vector field in ℝ² has a finite number of limit cycles.
**Theorem B.** All graphs (bounded or unbounded) of quadratic vector fields in ℝ² are finite (not accumulated by limit cycles).

The proof works by Poincaré compactification to S² (central projection, degree-n
multiplier z^{n−1} extension to the equator) and a case analysis of the graphs at
infinity (§2, Lemmas 2.1–2.13: unbounded graphs have saddle pairs at infinity, the
topological type of the singularity (0,1,0) at infinity, symmetric singularity
configurations, etc.). The bounded-graph case was proved by Chicone–Shafer 1983;
this paper gives an alternative proof and handles the unbounded case. The paper
recalls Dulac's 1923 claim and its gap (the introduction states the historical
context that Ilyashenko found the error).

## What it lets this run conclude

- The degree-2 special case of the pointwise-finiteness pillar is a held primary
  (Bamon 1986), independent of the general Ilyashenko/Écalle proofs: every fixed
  quadratic field has finitely many limit cycles. This is claim
  `h16-bamon-quadratic-finiteness` (asserted, held full text — see the summary
  `research/summaries/bamon-quadratic-finite-limit-cycles.md` for the claim block).
- It does NOT give a uniform bound: Bamon's theorem is per-field. The DRR program
  exists exactly to upgrade the quadratic case to uniformity, and the 121-graphic
  reduction is the route.
- Bamon's Theorem B (graphs finite, i.e. not accumulated by limit cycles) is the
  quadratic-case non-accumulation result — the accumulation-free property that
  Roussarie's compactification needs per graphic.

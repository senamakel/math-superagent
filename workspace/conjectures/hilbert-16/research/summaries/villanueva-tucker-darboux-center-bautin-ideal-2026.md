## What it establishes — a finite enclosure of the Bautin ideal

For the **homogeneous degree-n** family
`ẋ = −y + F_n(x,y)`, `ẏ = x + G_n(x,y)` (ℱ_h(n)) — the center-focus problem as
the local/parameter form of H16 — they give a finite enclosure of the Bautin
ideal `𝔅(ℱ_h(n)) = ⟨L₁,L₂,…⟩` by the ideal generated by the coefficients of the
first non-trivial homogeneous term **V_{n+1}** of the Lyapunov function:

- **even n:** `𝔅 ⊆ ⟨v_{n+1,0}, …, v_{0,n+1}⟩`, and `V_{n+1}=0` gives the center conditions;
- **odd n:** `𝔅 ⊆ ⟨L_{(n−1)/2}, v_{n+1,0}, …, v_{0,n+1}⟩`, center conditions = `L_{(n−1)/2}=0` AND `V_{n+1}=0`.

**Proposition 2** (Darboux interpretation): `V_{n+1}=0` forces `xF_n + yG_n = 0`, so
the radial form `ẋ=−y(1−F̃_n), ẏ=x(1−F̃_n)` has integrating factor
`R=(x²+y²)^{λ₁}/(1−F̃_n)` and an explicit first integral H — i.e. the center
conditions from the enclosure are **Darboux centers**.

The proof is **inductive** (base m=2: L_{n−1} and all of V_{2n} are linear in
v_{n+1}; general m likewise), so **all Lyapunov constants are linear functions of
the coefficients of V_{n+1}** — an explicit algebraic structure, valid for any
degree n ≥ 2 (plus a non-homogeneous extension, Theorem 2, §5).

## Hypotheses / holds here

Homogeneous planar polynomial systems of degree n ≥ 2; Lyapunov-function /
displacement-map setup; Darboux integrability. **Holds here: yes** — this is a
new instrument for GOAL's Bautin-ideal step: a finite enclosure of the Bautin
ideal for the full homogeneous family, exactly the "Lyapunov constants linear in
a finite coefficient set" structure the run's verify_lu_core / Lean Bautin
recurrence targets.

**Evidence class: sourced, unrefereed preprint** (arXiv v2 2026-07-02;
conditional — no peer acceptance located).

## Falsifier

An explicit homogeneous polynomial system of some degree n where a Lyapunov
constant is NOT linear in the coefficients of V_{n+1}, or a center condition not
captured by V_{n+1}=0 / L_{(n−1)/2}=0 contradicting the enclosure. The paper
itself notes "there are center conditions not captured by V_{n+1}=0", so the
**enclosure direction (⊆) is the precise claim to test**.

## Bearing / implication

- Matches and extends the run's Bautin-recurrence work: both claim Lyapunov
  constants linear in a finite coefficient set. Agreement is expected but
  unverified against each other here; a clean-room check on a small n would
  confirm/refute.
- The Darboux-center identification (Prop 2) aligns with the Prohens–Torregrosa
  reversible-Darboux-center lower-bound machinery: Darboux integrability is what
  makes these centers high-cyclicity in the first place.
- A Lean-statably checkable claim: for a fixed small n, the enclosure
  `𝔅 ⊆ ⟨v_{n+1,k}⟩` is an ideal-membership statement decidable over Q.

# Roussarie 1986 — On the number of limit cycles which appear by perturbation of separatrix loop

## What the source establishes (held content only)

This is the **founding derivation–division paper** of the DRR finite-cyclicity
program. The held reference list names the exact tools it rests on:

- Cherkas, *Structure of a successor function in the neighbourhood of a
  separatrix* (Differentsial'nye Uravneniya 17(3), 1981, 469–478) — the
  successor-function (displacement) viewpoint at a separatrix.
- Dulac 1923 *Sur les cycles limites* (Bull. SMF 51, 45–188).
- **Andronov–Leontovich–Gordon–Maier, *Theory of Bifurcation of Dynamical
  Systems on the Plane*** (Israel Program, 1971) — the saddle-connection /
  separatrix-loop bifurcation theory.
- Sternberg 1958 (local homeomorphisms, normal forms); **Ilyashenko 1984**
  (Funk. Anal. Pril. 18(3), 32–34 — "Limit cycles of polynomial vector fields
  with non-degenerate singular points"); **Khovanskii, *Théorème de Bézout
  pour les fonctions de Liouville*** (IHES preprint M/81/45, 1981) — the
  fewnomial/Bézout bound on zeros that is the Khovanskii method.

**It is the provenance anchor for the derivation–division instruments used
throughout the DRR program.** "Derivation–division" is the name for how a
deformation of the displacement function whose first coefficient is the leading
term of a Dulac germ is divided by that germ to show the remaining zeros are
bounded — the shape later generalized by Dumortier–Roussarie 2001,
Zhu–Rousseau 2002, Roussarie–Rousseau 2015. Roussarie's survey in this record
lists normal form / saddle point / Hamiltonian vector / admissible function /
polynomial vector as keywords.

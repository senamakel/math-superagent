## What it establishes — QW3 is the birthplace of every H(2)≥4 example

Global geometry of the planar **quadratic systems with a weak focus of third order**
(the **QW3** class), which plays a central role for H(2):

1. **Every known quadratic system with ≥ 4 limit cycles (Shi/Chen–Wang) is
   obtained by perturbing a member of QW3.** So the degree-2 lower bound H(2)≥4
   is structurally tied to QW3.
2. QW3 is topologically classified into **18 phase portraits** by integer-valued
   **affine invariants** (divisors, zero-cycles on the line at infinity: N_C,
   N_{R,f}, deg(DI_f), N_{R,∞}).
3. There is a **neighborhood of QW3** (among quadratic systems with the same
   coefficient topology, which may have graphics but **no polycycles** and no
   limit cycles) in which **any quadratic system has at most four limit cycles**.

## Hypotheses / holds here

Quadratic systems with a weak focus of third order; the invariant-based
classification; the H(2)≥4 perturbation structure. **Holds here: yes** — both
establishes the QW3 centrality for H(2)≥4 and gives the **only known upper bound
(≤4) in a full neighborhood** (local-to-the-class, not the global H(2) conjecture).

**Evidence class: sourced** (full text held, Cambridge open PDF).

## Falsifier

A quadratic system with ≥4 limit cycles NOT obtained by perturbing a QW3 member;
or a fifth cycle in the stated neighborhood.

## Bearing / implication

- Ties the degree-2 lower bound to a specific high-codimension class — relevant
  to the DRR program (which graphics) and to any attempt to approach H(2) via
  perturbing QW3.
- The ≤4-in-a-neighborhood result is a genuine (if restricted) uniform bound
  inside degree 2 — the shape the run's "finite bound on H(2) restricted to an
  explicitly stated subfamily" target asks for.
- The affine-invariant classification (divisors/zero-cycles) is an algebraic,
  finite, Lean-statably checkable structure (topological equivalence from
  integer invariants).

# Marín–Villadelprat 2024 — Dulac map/time coefficient properties (general setting)

Full text: [[marin-villadelprat-dulac-coefficient-properties-2024-arxiv.html.full]]
(arXiv:2105.09785; published J. Differential Equations 404 (2024) 43–107,
DOI 10.1016/j.jde.2024.05.037). This is the *general* setting (sections at
arbitrary distance from the saddle) and the coefficient-level companion to the
local-setting paper.

## What the source establishes (held full text)

**Setup.** C^∞ family {X_{μ̂}}_{μ̂∈Ŵ} of planar fields with a hyperbolic saddle,
μ̂ = (λ, μ) ∈ Ŵ = (0,+∞)×W with λ the hyperbolicity ratio treated as an
*independent parameter*. Dulac map D(s;μ̂) and Dulac time T(s;μ̂) from transverse
section at the stable separatrix to one at the unstable separatrix, at
*arbitrary distance* from the saddle (the "general setting" — removes the
local/normal-form-coordinates restriction of the 2020 paper).

**Theorem 1.6** (structure, from the authors' prior [23]): for each coefficient
index (i,j) the coefficients Δ_{ij} (Dulac map) and T_{ij} (Dulac time) are C^∞
on ((0,+∞)∖D_{ij})×W; each has its own domain D_{ij} (a discrete set of
rational resonances of λ = p/q). Off resonance:

  D(s;μ̂) = s^λ Σ_{(i,j)} Δ_{ij}(μ̂) s^{i+λj} + ℱ_L^∞({λ₀}×W),
  T(s;μ̂) = T₀(μ̂) log s + Σ T_{ij}(μ̂) s^{i+λj} + ℱ_L^∞({λ₀}×W),

with uniformly L-flat (in the parameter-uniform sense of the 2020 paper)
remainders. AT resonance the compensator ω(s;α), α = p−λq, enters: Δ_{ij} →
𝚫_{ij}^{λ₀}(ω;μ̂) = Σ_r Δ_{i−rp,j+rq}(μ̂)(1+αω)^r.

**Theorem A** (explicit coefficients — the headline). Assuming n ≠ (0,0):
(a) factorization Δ_{ij} = Ω_{ij}·Δ_{0j}, T_{ij} = Ω_{i,j−1}·T_{0j} (a
Roussarie-type factorization of the coefficients);
(b) for the Dulac map,

  Δ₀₀(μ̂) = σ₁₁₁^λ σ₁₂₀/(L₁^λ(σ₁₂₀)) · L₂(σ₂₁₀)/(σ₂₂₁ σ₂₁₀^λ)  (>0),
  Δ₀₁(μ̂) = −Δ₀₀²·S₂,
  Δ₁₀(μ̂) = Δ₀₀·λ·S₁,
  Δ₁₁(μ̂) = −2Δ₀₀²·λ·S₁·S₂,

with S₁ = σ₁₁₂/(2σ₁₁₁) − (σ₁₂₁/σ₁₂₀)(P₁/P₂)(0,σ₁₂₀) − σ₁₁₁/(L₁(σ₁₂₀))·M̂₁(1/λ,σ₁₂₀)
and S₂ the mirror quantity on the other separatrix;
(c) for the Dulac time, T_{n₁,0}, T_{0,n₂}, T_{n₁+1,0}, T_{0,n₂+1}, and (when
n₁=0 / n₂=0) T₂₀, T₀₂, all in terms of the data Pᵢ, Lᵢ, Âᵢ, B̂ᵢ, Ĉᵢ (incomplete
Mellin transforms) and the section-derivative data σ_{ijk}.

The functions L₁,L₂,M₁,M₂,A₁,A₂,B₁,B₂,C₁,C₂ are built from P₁,P₂,n and
integrals; the ^-hat is an *incomplete Mellin transform* (Appendix B), which is
what lets the coefficients be written for arbitrary λ ranges (removing the
FLP/analytic restrictions of prior work [18],[21]).

**Corollary B**: in the *analytic* setting the named coefficients are
meromorphic on Ŵ with poles of order ≤ 2 along their resonant domains; the
residues are the leading coefficients of the compensator-polynomial terms (needed
for the resonant case). Conjecture: all Δ_{ij}, T_{ij} are meromorphic with
poles along D_{ij}.

## Relation to the persistent-polycycle result (QAS 2025)

The S₁, S₂ appearing here are exactly the S₁^{m+1}, S₂^m in QAS2025 Theorem B's
coefficient 𝒜 = Λ_{m,n}A_{1,m}A_{1,n}(S₁^{m+1} − S₂^m). So this paper supplies
the *explicit computable data* (integrals over the separatrices, section
derivatives) that make the return-map leading coefficients of a persistent
hyperbolic polycycle concrete — the step from "a coefficient exists" to "here is
its value in terms of the field's jet along the separatrix".

## What it lets this run conclude

- **This is the computable coefficient algorithm for the displacement function
  at a hyperbolic vertex.** To decide cyclicity of a hyperbolic DRR graphic by
  the QAS/MV criterion one needs Δ₀₀, S₁, S₂ — all given as integrals along the
  separatrices and section-derivative data. For quadratic fields these are
  concrete rational/integrable objects, exactly the kind of finite algebraic
  core Lean can certify.
- The **factorization Δ_{ij} = Ω_{ij}Δ_{0j}** (a Roussarie-style division in
  the ideal generated by the first coefficients) is the exact algebraic shape
  of the "finitely many coefficients decide the germ" principle at a hyperbolic
  vertex — the model for the Bautin-trick / division-in-ideal step this run
  needs for its own cyclicity arguments, and morally the same structure as the
  Darboux/Bautin ideal enclosure of Villanueva–Tucker.
- **Test-1 (smooth-test) relevance**: the expansions hold in the C^∞ category
  with parameter-uniform flat remainders; analyticity is only invoked for
  Corollary B's *meromorphy*. So the finiteness machinery lives one step shy of
  analyticity, and any argument that needs "flat remainder really determines
  the map" must invoke the stronger F^∞_L class (per the 2020 paper's Remark 1.4).

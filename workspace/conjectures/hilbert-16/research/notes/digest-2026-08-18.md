# Reference-library digest: Hilbert 16.2

Memory storage was unavailable during this digest; this durable workspace note is the fallback.

## Core status and reduction
- **Ilyashenko 2002 survey** (Bull. AMS 39, URL in source): distinguishes individual finiteness from uniform H(n). It states H(1)=0 and H(2) remains unknown; Écalle/Ilyashenko solved individual polynomial/analytic finiteness, not the uniform family problem. It states Problem 8 (finite cyclicity of analytic polycycles) implies existence of H(n), and remains open in the survey. It explicitly exhibits smooth fields r'=f(r), φ'=1 with infinitely many isolated cycles, so analyticity/quasianalyticity/algebraicity is essential.
- **Belotto da Silva–Espín Buendía 2017** (arXiv:1702.04965): every polynomial-family limit-periodic set compactifies to a compact connected semialgebraic subset of S² with empty interior, and conversely every such set is realizable. Its Proposition 1.2 classifies limit-periodic sets as singular point, periodic orbit, polycycle, or degenerate limit cycle. This supports the structural obstruction inventory, not a uniform bound.
- **Rousseau–Shan–Zhu 2015** (arXiv:1502.00689): proves finite cyclicity of quadratic graphics I¹₁₂ and I¹₁₃ through triple nilpotent saddle points. The introduction says the DRR program reduces H(2)<∞ to finite cyclicity of 121 graphics and records 88 closed after this paper.
- **Roussarie–Rousseau 2015** (arXiv:1506.07104): proves full finite cyclicity only for I¹₁₄ among the highlighted center graphics; proves only boundary limit-periodic-set cyclicity for I¹₆b, H³₁₃, DI₂b. The proof uses weighted blow-up of the parameter family, Dulac maps, displacement functions, Bautin tricks and derivation–division. Therefore those full graphics remain valid concrete targets.

## Restricted positive theorems
- **Kaloshin 2000/2001** (arXiv:math/0010174): defines cyclicity as a uniform local bound on limit cycles near a polycycle. An equilibrium is elementary iff at least one linear eigenvalue is nonzero; an elementary polycycle has only elementary vertices. Ilyashenko–Yakovenko finiteness gives E(k)<∞; Kaloshin's explicit bound is E(k)≤225k². Corollary: generic compact k-parameter families whose polycycles are elementary have a uniform bound. It does not cover nilpotent/degenerate graphics.
- **Marín–Villadelprat 2020** (accepted text): local Dulac map/time near unfolded hyperbolic resonant saddles have asymptotic expansions in a Roussarie–Écalle compensator scale with remainders uniformly flat in unfolding parameters and smoothly parameter-differentiable. This is the analytic input needed for hyperbolic displacement functions; it is not by itself a theorem for nonhyperbolic DRR graphics.
- **Marín–Villadelprat 2024** (arXiv:2105.09785): gives explicit Dulac-map coefficient formulas, resonance-pole structure, and meromorphic extension in analytic settings. It supplies a computable finite core for hyperbolic polycycle displacement expansions.
- **Queiroz Arakaki–Santana 2025** (arXiv:2504.07225): for persistent hyperbolic polycycles, derives return-map expansion and explicit leading-term cyclicity criteria. Their framework assumes hyperbolic saddles and a well-defined return map, so it excludes the nonhyperbolic open DRR rows.
- **Mourtada 1991**: develops an algorithm of finite cyclicity for generic hyperbolic polycycles; the paper states Roussarie's reduction and the displacement-map object. It reinforces the hyperbolic restricted boundary, not the nilpotent case.
- **DGR 2002 / DRR 1994 records**: held abstracts/records support small finite cyclicity bounds for many elementary quadratic graphics (DRR reports 33 graphics with cyclicity ≤2; DGR settles seven named focus/center graphics). The DRR 1994 full catalogue is not held, so no complete row-by-row inventory is established.

## Tangential/Abelian rung
- **Binyamini–Novikov–Yakovenko 2010** (arXiv:0808.2952, full text): proves a double-exponential degree bound for zeros of Abelian integrals over nonsingular energy-level ovals, hence for cycles born in small nonconservative Hamiltonian perturbations. The mechanism is the Gauss–Manin regular flat meromorphic connection over Q with quasiunipotent monodromy.
- **Binyamini–Dor 2011/2012** (arXiv:1108.1846): combines Petrov–Khovanskii and BNY to obtain an explicit bound linear in deg ω, with explicit dependence on deg H (exp-plus notation). This is sharper tangential data, but neither result bounds the full nonlinear return-map displacement or H(2).
- **Gavrilov, Novikov–Yakovenko, GMV, Gasull–Lázaro–Torregrosa**: useful module/Picard–Fuchs/Chebyshev special-family instruments, but only apply after proving the relevant displacement is an Abelian-integral family with their hypotheses; no source licenses applying them directly to the four-Dulac I¹₆b problem.

## Lower bounds and calibration
- **Bautin 1952**: the local cyclicity of a quadratic focus/center is exactly 3 under coefficient perturbations, and a quadratic example with 3 local cycles exists. This is M(2)=3, not H(2).
- **Galias–Tucker 2022**: rigorous interval-arithmetic computation proves the Songling quadratic system has exactly four limit cycles, with localization bounds. This certifies H(2)≥4 and is the correct negative/positive oracle calibration, unlike phase portraits.
- **Torregrosa 2024/2025**: two one-parameter cubic families exhibit twelve small-amplitude limit cycles at exceptional parameters, so current M(3)≥12. This updates older M(3)≥11 claims.
- **Christopher–Lloyd 1995 / Han–Li refinement**: H(n) has lower growth at least order n²log n, with the stored sharper liminf constant 1/(2 log 2). Any quadratic-or-lower proposed upper bound is impossible. Slow–fast Liénard constructions are the stress test.

## Contested or non-helpful sources
- **Yeung 2024/2025, Dulac's Theorem Revisited**: claims a gap in Ilyashenko's proof for nonhyperbolic/semi-hyperbolic polycycles and gives an explicit asymptotic counterexample. It does not disprove the finiteness theorem; it contradicts any unqualified statement that the proof is settled. Hyperbolic finiteness remains undisputed.
- **Lu 2026, arXiv:2607.13785**: claims local uniform finite cyclicity of H³₁₄ in a five-parameter unfolding, with an existential bound, using stopped first-hit atlases and analytic zero theorems. In this workspace only the finite algebraic Bautin core has independent exact checks; root uniqueness, domain completeness, remainder/zero arguments and the full theorem remain unverified. Treat as asserted-by-source, not closure.
- **Pedregal 2021/updated arXiv text**: claims a quartic upper bound and H(2)=4 by a variational Morse/Bezout argument. It supplies no accepted resolution of the open problem and visibly lacks the analytic return-map step required by the smooth test; earlier related work acknowledged a counting mistake. It contradicts recalled open status and is not usable evidence for H(2)=4.
- **Ecalle 1990 Springer page**: only landing/preview and references; it establishes no detailed theorem statement in this library and should not be used beyond bibliographic attribution.
- **Landing pages for Kaloshin, BNY, DPR and similar records**: abstracts/metadata only; use the held full texts or summaries, not the landing page as proof.
- **DRR 1994 full catalogue** and Roussarie 1998 monograph: paywalled/unobtained. The 121 count and partial status are triangulated from follow-up sources, not a complete primary ledger. Shan's 2013 thesis uses a 125-graphic convention, so 121/125 is unresolved rather than silently reconciled.

## Contradictions to flag
1. Pedregal claims H(n) finite and H(2)=4, directly contradicting Ilyashenko's primary survey and current open-problem status; the claim is unverified and fails the smooth-test audit.
2. Yeung challenges the completeness of Ilyashenko's nonhyperbolic proof, but does not refute the theorem; record this as proof-status contention, not theorem contradiction.
3. RR 2015's actual theorem scope contradicts any summary saying I¹₆b, H³₁₃ and DI₂b are fully closed: only their boundary sets are closed; I¹₁₄ is fully closed.
4. The 88 count in RSZ predates RR's I¹₁₄ closure; arithmetic gives 89 rows after that closure, but no complete current ledger exists.
5. Older M(3)≥11 is superseded by Torregrosa's M(3)≥12; older M(3) claims should not be presented as current best.

## Why this matters

Bautin's finite-generation theorem for the small-amplitude cyclicity of a
quadratic focus says the focal-value ideal is generated by the first three
generators. Whether the higher focal values actually lie in that ideal is the
"Bautin trick" step (ideal membership, not just vanishing):

- L8 ∉ ⟨L4,L6⟩ and L6 ∉ ⟨L4⟩ prove three generators are genuinely needed
  (the capture `code/out/bautin_focal_values.captured.txt` already recorded
  L8 ∉ ⟨L4,L6⟩ via the same exact Groebner method; this run independently
  confirms and extends it).
- **L10 ∈ ⟨L4,L6,L8⟩ and L12 ∈ ⟨L4,L6,L8⟩ hold exactly over Q**, so Lu's
  route (higher focal values lie in the ideal of the first three) **survives**
  at least through degree 12. This directly answers the open question from
  `code/lyap_extend.py`'s crash: the memberships it meant to test are True.

## Bug box (why an earlier answer said False and was wrong)

sympy 1.11's `GroebnerBasis.reduce(poly)` returns `(quotients_list,
remainder)`, i.e. the remainder is the **last** element. The original
`lyap_extend.py` and the first version of this script read `red[0]`, the
quotients list, and `sp.simplify(list) == 0` is False — so a generator
reduced against its own ideal appeared "not in the ideal", and every
membership value produced that way is void. The trustworthy run in
`code/out/membership.captured.txt` uses `red[-1]`, has controls all True, and
verifies each result by the two extra routes. Any later agent reading an
earlier "L10 ∉ ⟨L4,L6,L8⟩" statement must discard it.

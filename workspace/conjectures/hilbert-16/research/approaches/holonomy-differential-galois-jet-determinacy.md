```approach
idea: Replace zero counting of the displacement by a differential-Galois/holonomy problem: prove finite jet determinacy of the projective holonomy representation of the blown-up polycycle, using Picard–Vessiot theory and a Malgrange-groupoid style algebraic envelope.
mechanism: The variational and nonlinear transition equations define a parameterized connection on the blown-up transversal. Its holonomy lies in a finite-dimensional algebraic or differential-algebraic group when the connection has bounded polynomial degree. A displacement germ that is flat to sufficiently high order would then have trivial holonomy in the relevant unipotent quotient; a finite-dimensional representation forces the germ either to vanish identically (a center/integrability stratum) or to have a zero of bounded multiplicity. The smooth test is met at the differential-algebraic closure/quasianalytic step, and uniformity would arise from a bounded differential-Galois group/connection complexity rather than compactness alone. This is not the already-closed Nevanlinna growth argument: it seeks algebraic holonomy and finite jet determinacy, not a characteristic estimate.
status: refuted
killed-by: The literature supports differential-Galois tests for integrability/nonintegrability, not the proposed implication from bounded variational Galois complexity to finite jet determinacy or a uniform zero bound for nonlinear holonomy. Ochoa et al. Theorems A/B require an algebraic invariant curve and an irregular first variational equation at infinity and conclude only: integrability implies abelian higher variational Galois groups; a nonabelian group obstructs integrability. They do not bound cyclicity. Picard–Vessiot theory concerns linear variational equations, while the displacement is nonlinear and the open DRR maps have nonhyperbolic Dulac/transseries structure. No theorem found supplies the claimed finite-dimensional holonomy representation, bounded differential-Galois complexity, or triviality-from-high-jet criterion for I^1_6b, H^3_13, DI_2b, or H^3_14.
precedent: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jde.2018.02.016; https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1012.4796; https://doi.org/10.4064/bc58-0-12; https://doi.org/10.1515/advgeom-2022-0010; claims: h16-2-finite-cyclicity-G-uniform, i6b-four-second-type-full-graphic-not-covered, rr2015-i6b-boundary-cyclicity, h16-drr-121-graphics
what-it-buys: A useful obstruction to Darboux/rational/Liouvillian integrability for selected polynomial fields and a diagnostic for special Hamiltonian perturbations via Melnikov/Picard–Vessiot theory. It does not provide an upper bound on zeros or uniform finite cyclicity for the target graphics.
```

```approach
idea: Apply Écalle's Borel–Écalle resummation and bridge-equation formalism to the iterated-log Dulac transseries of an open non-hyperbolic graphic. Treat the displacement as a resurgent transseries rather than as a finite short germ, and identify its first nonzero alien/Stokes invariant through a finite bridge-equation recurrence.
mechanism: Sectorial return maps can have transseries asymptotics; resurgence supplies sectorial summation and Stokes data. A successful route would need a parameter-uniform finite bridge rank and a theorem that finitely many alien/ordinary coefficients determine the displacement's zeros or identity.
status: refuted
killed-by: The literature supports resurgence, transseries, sectorial extensions, and quasi-analyticity for Dulac germs, chiefly in the hyperbolic/parabolic Dulac setting, but no theorem was found establishing a finite, parameter-uniform bridge-equation rank or a zero-multiplicity bound for the open non-hyperbolic DRR graphics. The closest sources explicitly describe resurgence as part of the individual Dulac finiteness proof, not as a uniform H(2) cyclicity theorem. The proposed finite recurrence is therefore an unproved extra implication, not a literature-grounded reformulation. This is evidence-based refutation of the proposed route as stated, not a claim that resurgence is irrelevant.
first-step: If revived, first prove a uniform singular-support/bridge-rank theorem for one named open graphic (I^1_6b or H^3_14), then prove that vanishing of the proposed finite invariants implies sectorial identity; neither step is presently sourced.
```

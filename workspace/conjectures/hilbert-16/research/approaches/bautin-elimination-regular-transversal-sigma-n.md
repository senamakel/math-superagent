name: Bautin elimination at a regular transversal — the Σ_N locus as a finite elimination ideal
idea: Encode the N-cycles parameter locus Σ_N = {λ : Δ(·;λ) has ≥ N isolated zeros} directly as a finite elimination ideal over ℚ, from the jointly-analytic displacement Δ(z;λ) on a REGULAR transversal. Because Δ is analytic in (z,λ) jointly away from singularities, its Taylor coefficients a_k(λ) are themselves analytic (polynomial in the polynomial-family case) functions of λ, and the condition "Δ has ≥ N zeros on [0,L]" is equivalent, by Chebyshev alternation / resultants, to a finite system of polynomial equations and inequalities in {a_0,…,a_K}. Eliminating the N root variables z_1,…,z_N gives Σ_N as a finite semialgebraic set. Taylor domination (Batenkov–Yomdin Thm 4.1) is the analytic finite-truncation lemma: with Bautin index d and a COMMON chart radius R uniform in λ, the first d generators control all higher coefficients, so the K-truncation decides the whole germ. Analyticity enters exactly once and exactly where Dulac's 1923 error lives: a flat C^∞ perturbation escapes Taylor domination, so the finite elimination step is false for smooth fields.

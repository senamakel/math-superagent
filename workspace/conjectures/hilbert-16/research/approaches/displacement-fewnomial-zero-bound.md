```approach
idea: Replace the graphic-by-graphic classification of the DRR program with a
  direct analytic zero bound on the displacement function, modeled as a "short
  (standard) Dulac function" — a finite linear combination of monomials in
  x^α and iterated logarithms plus a flat remainder — and bound its zeros by the
  rank/fewnomial machinery of Ilyashenko's short functions and Khovanskii's
  theory of fewnomials, rather than by resolving each vertex into an elementary
  normal form and composing transition expansions.

  The representation change is the point. The DRR frame composes sector
  transition maps (G-resolve → G-transition → G-zeros). This line instead asserts
  that, after the blow-up/normal-form machinery which the run already holds as
  G-resolve, the whole displacement function around a non-hyperbolic polycycle
  lives in a FINITE-RANK MODULE over a quasianalytic algebra of "analyzable"
  germs generated by finitely many monomials x^{α_i} (log x)^{k_i}. Bounding the
  zeros of such a germ is then a finite algebraic problem: its logarithmic
  derivative satisfies a polynomial differential equation, so by Ilyashenko's
  theory of short/standard functions the number of zeros is bounded by the rank
  of that module. This is the SAME source of uniformity the DRR program gets
  from G-uniform, but it is obtained directly from analyzability of the return
  map instead of from a finite list of vertex normal forms.

mechanism: Why this problem's structure suits it. (1) Test 1 (smooth test) is
  satisfied at the correct location: the finiteness comes from the quasianalytic /
  analyzable structure of the displacement (Écalle / Ilyashenko), not from
  topology — a C^∞ field can have infinitely many limit cycles precisely because
  its return germ need not be analyzable nor short. This is the same sharp edge
  Dulac missed, made explicit: a GERM that is a finite sum of x^{α_i}(log x)^{k_i}
  terms has its zero count controlled by finitely many coefficients. (2) Uniformity
  (G-uniform) falls out of finite module rank: a real-analytic family of such
  germs over the compact parameter box has only finitely many possible zero
  patterns, because the "shape" of the germ (its asymptotic exponents and
  coefficients) is a continuous function of parameters and the zero-count can
  only change at a real-analytic locus — so one bounds the zero count uniformly
  by the finite number of distinct asymptotic types. This is a genuine reformulation
  of the finiteness step, not a re-derivation of the graphics.

  The concrete named theorem to invoke: Ilyashenko's "short functions" /
  "standard functions" theory (the finite-dimensional module of Dulac-type
  germs) and Khovanskii's fewnomial bound on the number of nondegenerate
  solutions of few-monomial systems — here a system in 1 variable whose number
  of roots is bounded by the number of monomials and their ranks.

first-step: Choose the concrete open graphic (I₆b¹, which RR 2015 say needs "new
  2-equation methods with four Dulac maps of second type" — not the H14_3 that
  Lu claims). Write its displacement function explicitly as a short Dulac germ:
  for each of the four second-type Dulac transition maps, expand the passage map
  as Σ c_i y^{a_i}(log y)^{k_i} + flat, compose them, and exhibit the resulting
  displacement as a short germ. Then (a) symbolically verify that the germ's
  logarithmic derivative satisfies an algebraic differential equation (the
  finite, checkable core — a polynomial identity Lean can close with ring/decide),
  and (b) state the fewnomial/rank bound on its zeros as a Lean theorem over the
  module's finitely generated structure. The first executed check: reproduce the
  known finite-cyclicity of a graphic the run already has closed (e.g. I₁₂¹ from
  Rousseau–Shan–Zhu) by this same short-germ + fewnomial route, to validate the
  method before trusting it on I₆b¹.

precedent: [REFUTED — the finite-rank short-function representation does not
  extend to the target graphics.] The claim that the displacement around the
  open DRR graphics is a finite sum of x^α(log x)^k terms plus a flat remainder
  — a "short" / standard function — holds only for HYPERBOLIC polycycles.
  Ilyashenko's A∘(-log) class of almost-regular germs (short functions) is
  closed under composition and gives the Dulac series power×(log)^k, and that is
  exactly what settles finite-cyclicity around hyperbolic polycycles
  (Ilyashenko, Bull. AMS 39 (2002) doi:10.1090/bull/2002-39-03; Speissegger,
  "Quasianalytic Ilyashenko Algebras", Canad. J. Math. arXiv:1606.01130,
  doi:10.4153/cjm-2016-048-x, which notes A∘(-log) is quasianalytic and closed
  under composition). But the return maps of NON-hyperbolic polycycles —
  through semi-hyperbolic saddle-nodes, nilpotent and degenerate points, exactly
  the (I⁶b¹),(H¹₃³),(DI₂b),(H³₁₄) graphics the run has open — expand as
  TRANSSERIES with ITERATED LOGARITHMS AND EXPONENTIALS and parameter-dependent
  exponents, and are explicitly NOT short: Peran, Resman, Rolin, Servi,
  "Linearization of complex hyperbolic Dulac germs", J. Math. Anal. Appl. 2021
  (doi:10.1016/j.jmaa.2021.125833); Mardesić, Resman, "Analytic moduli for
  parabolic Dulac germs", Russian Math. Surveys 76 (2021)
  (doi:10.1070/rm10001); Peran, "Normalization of strongly hyperbolic logarithmic
  transseries", arXiv:2302.14527 and arXiv:2112.12187 (parabolic case, iterated
  logs); Yeung, "Natural levels in return maps of elementary polycycles",
  arXiv:2409.13630 (structural theorem whose depth D counts SEMI-hyperbolic
  saddles, showing the non-hyperbolic case costs extra log/exp levels). Hence the
  claim "the displacement is a short germ with finitely many x^α(log x)^k
  monomials, whose fewnomial/rank bound applies" fails exactly on the open
  graphics: the fewnomial bound applies to short/Dulac germs, and the open
  graphics are precisely where the germ is NOT short. This route re-proposes
  Ilyashenko's own finiteness method, which already stops at the hyperbolic
  case; the non-hyperbolic case is exactly the hard, only-partially-resolved
  part (and the very part Yeung 2024 arXiv:2402.12506 contests).
status: refuted
killed-by: Return maps of non-hyperbolic polycycles are not short Dulac germs —
  they expand as transseries with iterated logs and exponentials (parameter-
  dependent exponents), so the finite-rank x^α(log x)^k module and the
  fewnomial/rank zero bound do not transfer to the open DRR graphics (I⁶b¹ etc.).
```

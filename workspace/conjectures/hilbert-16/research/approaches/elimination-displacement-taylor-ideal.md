Literature verdict: the established reformulation is **Bautin-ideal / Taylor-domination / reduced-Bautin-depth theory**, not generic “finite-jet determinacy.” For an analytic family f_λ(z)=Σa_k(λ)z^k with polynomial parameter coefficients and a finite Bautin index d, the cited Taylor-domination theorem gives constants R,C controlling higher coefficients from the first d generators and hence a local zero bound. García's theorem gives Cyc(X_λ,p₀)≤κ−1 for a monodromic singularity when the reduced Bautin depth κ is defined; its computable criterion additionally requires the stated radicality, variety-equality, and integral-closure stabilization hypotheses. These hypotheses hold for local monodromic focus/center families and some nilpotent/degenerate centers, not automatically for a regular transversal surrounding a non-hyperbolic DRR graphic. At a regular point the return map is analytic only while the entire parameterized passage remains in a common regular domain; approaching a polycycle destroys that uniform domain and is exactly where Dulac maps and parameter-dependent singularities enter. The literature applies the method to centers, Abel equations, and selected Hamiltonian/polycycle settings, but no theorem converts arbitrary open DRR displacement functions into a finite polynomial jet-elimination problem. What it would buy: a genuine local, possibly kernel-checkable cyclicity bound after proving a common analytic chart and Bautin stabilization; it is not a route to full H(2) without that missing uniformity theorem.

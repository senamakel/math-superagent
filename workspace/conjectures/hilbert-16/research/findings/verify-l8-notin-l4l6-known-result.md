## 1. Is the result already known? — YES, classically since Bautin 1952.

The claim L8 ∉ ⟨L4,L6⟩ is exactly the generator-independence content of Bautin's
theorem: the quadratic Bautin ideal is generated by the first **three** focus
quantities V₁,V₂,V₃, which are algebraically independent, so the third is not in
the ideal of the first two. A weak focus of order 3 has V₁=V₂=0, V₃≠0. Sources
(the run's L4, L6, L8 are its first three focal values = Bautin's V₁,V₂,V₃):

- **Bautin 1952**, "On the number of limit cycles which appear with the variation
  of coefficients from an equilibrium position of the focus or centre type",
  Mat. Sbornik 30(72), 181–196; AMS Transl. No.100 (1954). Proves M(2)=3 and the
  three-generator structure. (Broadly cited; Artés–Llibre, Llibre–Schlomiuk,
  Schlomiuk, Mahdi–Pessoa–Hauenstein all depend on it.)
- **Schlomiuk 1993**, "Algebraic Particular Integrals, Integrability and the
  Problem of the Center", Trans. AMS 338(2), https://doi.org/10.2307/2154430 —
  states the quadratic Bautin ideal is determined by the values of Vⱼ for j<3;
  gives the three generators B₁,B₂,B₃ explicitly; notes Shi Songling's sign
  correction to Bautin's V₃.
- **Artés–Llibre 1997**, "Quadratic vector fields with a weak focus of third
  order", Publ. Mat. 41, https://doi.org/10.5565/publmat_41197_02 — weak-focus
  order fully determined by L₁,L₂,L₃ (first→second→third order, then center).
- **Llibre–Schlomiuk 2004**, "The Geometry of Quadratic Differential Systems with
  a Weak Focus of Third Order", Can. J. Math. 56,
  https://doi.org/10.4153/cjm-2004-015-2 — V₁=V₂=V₃=0 iff center; ideal finitely
  generated by V₁,V₂,V₃; third-order weak focus has V₁=V₂=0, V₃≠0.
- **Mahdi–Pessoa–Hauenstein 2017**, "A hybrid symbolic-numerical approach to the
  center–focus problem", J. Symbolic Comput. 82, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2016.11.019
  — "the Bautin ideal B is generated by the first three focus quantities
  (Bautin, 1952)"; center variety decomposes into four irreducible components.
- Also: Jarrah–Laubenbacher–Romanovski (JSC), Romanovski–Shafer 2009.

**Conclusion:** the underlying fact is known since 1952 and is NOT new. The run's
narrow claim is therefore already established in the literature. This is a useful
finding because the derivation can cite Bautin 1952 / Schlomiuk 1993 / Llibre–
Schlomiuk 2004 for it rather than presenting it as a discovery.

## 2. Is the argument strong enough for what it claims? — Yes, for the narrow claim and no more.

- **Logic is sound.** A member of the ideal (or its radical) vanishes on the
  variety V(L4,L6); the witness point is a common zero where L8≠0; hence
  L8∉⟨L4,L6⟩ and L8∉rad⟨L4,L6⟩. Three rational evaluations. This is a valid
  non-membership certificate.
- **Arithmetic is exact.** I independently checked L4 by hand at the witness:
  L4 = a1a2/8 − a1b1/4 + a2a3/8 + a3b3/4 − b1b2/8 − b2b3/8 →
  ½ − ½ − ¼ + ¼ − ⅛ + ⅛ = 0. ✓ Consistent with the script and its clean-room
  re-route.
- **Method is standard.** Computing focal values and testing ideal membership /
  using evaluation witnesses against the Bautin ideal is the standard method in
  the center–focus and cyclicity literature (see Mahdi–Pessoa–Hauenstein 2017,
  Sánchez 2017). Nothing here is a novel technique.
- **Correct scope.** The run does not claim M(2)=3 or anything about H16.2.

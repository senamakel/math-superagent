# Verification report: cofactor-certificate-L8-not-in-L4-L6

**Question asked.** The run believes it has solved the problem. Do not solve it;
say whether the result is already known and whether the argument is strong enough
for what it claims.

**What the run actually concluded.** Not H16.2, and not M(2)=3. The task that
completed is the narrow statement `L8 ∉ ⟨L4,L6⟩` for the quadratic focus in the
six-coefficient family (a1..b3), witnessed by a single rational point
`(a1,a2,a3,b1,b2,b3)=(-2,-2,1,-1,-1,1)` where `L4=0, L6=0, L8=25/64`, and closed
in Lean as `Bautin.V3_not_mem_span_V1_V2`. This is the claim that *Bautin's third
focal-value generator is genuinely needed*.

## Is it already known? Yes — classical since 1952.

The fact that the Bautin ideal of the quadratic focus needs three generators is
exactly the content of Bautin's own theorem:

- **Bautin 1952**, "On the number of limit cycles which appear with the
  variation of coefficients from an equilibrium position of focus or center
  type", Mat. Sbornik 30 (1952) 181–196; AMS Transl. 100 (1954) — proved
  `M(2)=3` *and* that the Bautin ideal of the quadratic focus is generated by
  the first three focus quantities, which are independent. A weak focus of order
  3 has `V1=V2=0, V3≠0`; so `V3∉⟨V1,V2⟩` is precisely that independence.
- **Llibre–Schlomiuk 2004**, "The Geometry of Quadratic Differential Systems
  with a Weak Focus of Third Order", Can. J. Math. 56 — states the quadratic
  Bautin ideal is generated by `V1,V2,V3`, independent.
- **Schlomiuk 1993**, "Algebraic Particular Integrals, Integrability and the
  Problem of the Center", Trans. AMS — Bautin ideal of quadratic systems
  determined by the first three focus quantities.
- **Mahdi–Pessoa–Hauenstein 2017**, "A hybrid symbolic-numerical approach to the
  center–focus problem", J. Symbolic Comput. 82 (2017) 57–73,
  doi:10.1016/j.jsc.2016.11.019 — verbatim: "the Bautin ideal B is generated by
  the first three focus quantities".
- **Sibirsky-component paper**, J. Symbolic Computation
  S0747717103000166 — verbatim: "for quadratic systems B is generated by the
  first three focus quantities".
- **Romanovski–Shafer 2009**, *The Center and Cyclicity Problems* (Birkhäuser) —
  standard reference, same statement.

So the underlying statement is textbook, dated 1952. It is not new.

**What is not known-published**: the *certificate form* — a finite rational
evaluation witness (a common zero of `V1,V2` where `V3≠0`) turned into a
kernel-checked Lean theorem `V3_not_mem_span_V1_V2`, with no formalised
Buchberger. That presentation is the run's own contribution; it is a true,
verifiable, honestly-scoped reformulation of a classical fact, not a new
mathematical result.

## Is the argument strong enough for what it claims? Yes — but only for the narrow claim.

The evaluation-witness logic is sound: every element of `⟨V1,V2⟩` vanishes at
every common zero of `V1,V2`; a point where both vanish and `V3` does not is a
rather-stronger refutation (it puts `V3` outside the *radical* of `⟨V1,V2⟩`,
hence outside the ideal). The three evaluations are exact rational arithmetic and
were independently recomputed (second route matched: L4=0, L6=0, L8=25/64). For
the claim "three generators are needed", the argument is bulletproof.

The trap the run avoided: its own scoping is honest. `Bautin.lean` and the
finding both say explicitly this says nothing about `M(2)=3`, which stays a Cited
axiom (Bautin 1952). That is correct — the certificate does not and cannot
establish that three small-amplitude cycles bifurcate, only that the third
generator is not redundant. Whoever reads it as a step toward H16 should note it
adds nothing toward the conjecture; it re-derives Bautin's generator independence.

**Plausibility.** 1 attempt on 12 established claims for this result is entirely
plausible *precisely because* it is a special case of a classical theorem. A
quick, clean, verifiable result of this shape is the signature of something
already known, and that is what this is. The quick arrival is consistent with
"A known fact restated as a certificate," not with "a new theorem."

2. **Method is the standard one.** Computing focal values and deciding ideal
   membership (Gröbner or evaluation) is exactly how the center-focus/cyclicity

   Mahdi–Pessoa–Hauenstein). The run's non-membership-via-common-zero trick is a
   slightly unusual *presentation*, but the underlying technique is ordinary.

3. **No contradiction found between sources and the run's conclusion** for the
   narrow claim — because the sources *agree* with it. The only "disagreement"
   is that the run treats as a result what the literature already holds as
   Bautin's theorem.

## Verdict

- Already known: **yes**, it is Bautin 1952's generator independence, restated
  and re-proved (correctly) by a finite witness.
- Argument strong enough: **yes for `L8 ∉ ⟨L4,L6⟩`**, and the run says no more.
  It is not a proof of, or a step toward, M(2)=3 or H16.
- Novelty: the certified-evaluation Lean theorem form, not the fact.

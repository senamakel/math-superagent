# Verification report — "L8 ∉ ⟨L4,L6⟩" certificate (evaluation witness)

Asked: is the run's conclusion new, and is the argument strong enough for what it
claims? Asked by the verification role; answered 2025-XX-XX.

## The claim under review

Task `cofactor-certificate-L8-not-in-L4-L6`: for the quadratic focus family

    u' = -v + a1u² + a2uv + a3v² ,  v' = u + b1u² + b2uv + b3v²,

the focal values (Lyapunov quantities) L4, L6, L8 — homogeneous of degrees
2, 4, 6 in the six coefficients — satisfy `L8 ∉ ⟨L4, L6⟩` over ℚ, witnessed by
the rational point (a1,a2,a3,b1,b2,b3) = (-2,-2,1,-1,-1,1) where
L4 = L6 = 0 and L8 = 25/64 ≠ 0. The run reads this as "Bautin's third focal-value
generator is genuinely needed."

The mathematics of the certificate is correct:
- The logic is sound: every element of ⟨L4,L6⟩ vanishes at every common zero of
  L4 and L6, so one common zero with L8 ≠ 0 refutes membership (and does so for
  the radical). Verified by hand for L4 at the witness (L4 = 1/2 − 1/2 − 1/4 +
  1/4 − 1/8 + 1/8 = 0).
- The arithmetic is exact (sympy over QQ, no floats; code reviewed).
- The statement is representation-independent: focus quantities are defined
  modulo the ideal of their predecessors, and adding an element of ⟨L4,L6⟩ to
  L8 cannot change its membership in ⟨L4,L6⟩.

## Is it already known? Yes — completely, since Bautin 1952

The statement "the Bautin ideal of the quadratic focus is generated by the first
three focus quantities, which are independent" is Bautin's own theorem
(Bautin 1952, Mat. Sb. 30(72):181–196; AMS Transl. 100 (1954)) — the same
theorem that gives M(2) = 3. `L8 ∉ ⟨L4,L6⟩` is precisely the "three generators
genuinely needed, three independent" content of that theorem (a third-order weak
focus is exactly L1 = L2 = 0, L3 ≠ 0; if L3 ∈ ⟨L1,L2⟩ the classification of
weak-focus order would collapse). Modern restatements, all found by search:

- Schlomiuk (1993), Trans. AMS 338:695–706, doi:10.2307/2154430 — the Bautin
  ideal is generated by the first three focus quantities V1,V2,V3 (up to
  constants), each defined modulo its predecessors.
- Jarrah–Laubenbacher–Romanovski, "The Sibirsky component of the center variety
  of polynomial differential systems", J. Symbolic Computation
  doi:10.1016/S0747717103000166 — verbatim: "for quadratic systems, B is
  generated by the first three focus quantities".
- Mahdi–Pessoa–Hauenstein (2017), J. Symbolic Computation
  doi:10.1016/j.jsc.2016.11.019 — "It is well-known … that, for system (2), the
  so-called Bautin ideal B is generated by the first three focus quantities of
  this system (Bautin, 1952)", with the center variety decomposing into four
  irreducible components.
- Artés–Llibre (1994), Rocky Mountain J. Math, doi:10.1216/rmjm/1181072378 —
  "three independent focal quantities associated to a center of a quadratic
  system (Kapteyn … completely by Bautin)".
- Artés–Llibre (1997), Publ. Mat. 41:89–102, doi:10.5565/publmat_41197_02 —
  quadratic systems have at most "three levels of weakness"; L1=L2=0, L3≠0 is a
  weak focus of third order.
- Romanovski–Shafer (2009), *The Center and Cyclicity Problems: A Computational
  Algebra Approach*, Birkhäuser — the standard textbook treatment.

The specific numbers in the run's own record corroborate the literature: in the
5-coefficient chart family the Gröbner route (code/out/membership.captured.txt,
lex over QQ, double-checked with positive controls and the reduction identity)
independently derives L8 ∉ ⟨L4,L6⟩ (16-monomial remainder), L6 ∉ ⟨L4⟩, and
L10,L12 ∈ ⟨L4,L6,L8⟩ — exactly the Bautin-trick structure.

## Is the method standard?

Yes. The underlying computation — focal values via the formal-Lyapunov-function
recurrence, then ideal membership by Gröbner reduction over ℚ — is Bautin's own
method, and is the textbook standard (Romanovski–Shafer 2009; Ferčec–Mahdi 2013,
doi:10.1016/j.matcom.2013.02.003; García 2015, Proc. AMS doi:10.1090/proc/12896).
The one non-standard element is the *certificate form*: replacing the Gröbner
remainder with a single common-zero evaluation point (three rational
evaluations), which is what a Lean kernel can close by `decide` without
formalising Buchberger. That is a legitimate and stronger argument (it puts L8
outside the radical), and it is a genuinely useful presentation device for
machine-checked certificates — but it is a proof-presentation choice, not new
mathematics.

## Does anything contradict the run?

No source contradicts the conclusion; several pre-empt it (the fact is
classical). Two weaknesses in the run's own presentation:

1. **The kernel-checked claim is unverified.** Bautin.lean contains
   `theorem V3_not_mem_span_V1_V2 : V3 ∉ Ideal.span {V1,V2} := by …` and the
   run's summary says the certificate is "already referenced" as evidence. But
   the only lean_check JSON for Bautin.lean on disk
   (code/out/lean/code_lean_Lib_Bautin.lean.json) says `compiled: false`,
   `outcome: failed`, with sorries at lines 150/157 — line numbers that now sit
   inside the data tables, so the JSON predates the current file and the current
   tree was never re-checked. The `#print axioms` lines for
   V3_not_mem_span_V1_V2 are in the file but their output is not captured
   anywhere. So "kernel-checked theorem exists" is asserted, not evidenced, and
   CONTEXT.md already flags this as gap 3. Also note the theorem's hypotheses:
   it proves the ideal statement for the computed representatives V1,V2,V3 under
   the bridge axiom `focalValue_eq` (cited), so even if it compiled it is
   conditional on Bautin 1952 for the identification of the sequence — which is
   fine and honest.
2. **Framing overreach risk.** "Bautin's third generator is genuinely needed" is
   correct but is Bautin's theorem itself; the run's own scope line is honest
   ("says nothing about M(2)=3"), and the finding note correctly labels the
   status verified-computationally. The deliverable's honest value is (i) an
   executed, exact, reproducible re-derivation of a known fact, and (ii) a
   machine-checkable certificate design. It is not a new mathematical result and
   does not bear on H16.2 in any way that changes the open-problem status.

## Plausibility verdict

1 attempt, 13 established claims, for this result: plausible and expected, in
exactly the one way that makes it cheap — the result is a 70-year-old theorem,
the script and capture largely pre-existed, and the final step was executing an
existing program. That is the normal shape of a bootstrap on folklore, not the
shape of a new result. A genuinely new result of H16 size produced in one
attempt on 13 claims would be, on prior, an error; that prior is not challenged
here because no such claim is being made.

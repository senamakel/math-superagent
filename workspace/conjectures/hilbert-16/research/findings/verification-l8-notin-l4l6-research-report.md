# Research verification — "L8 ∉ ⟨L4,L6⟩" for the quadratic focus

**Question.** The run believes it closed the task `cofactor-certificate-L8-not-in-L4-L6`.
Is the result already known, and is the argument strong enough for what it claims?

## The claim, stated exactly

For the quadratic focus family (six coefficients)

    u' = -v + a1 u^2 + a2 u v + a3 v^2
    v' =  u + b1 u^2 + b2 u v + b3 v^2

with focal values (Lyapunov quantities) L4, L6, L8 homogeneous of degrees 2,4,6
in the coefficients, the run proves `L8 ∉ ⟨L4, L6⟩` over ℚ, by the evaluation
witness (a1,a2,a3,b1,b2,b3) = (-2,-2,1,-1,-1,1) where L4 = L6 = 0 and
L8 = 25/64 ≠ 0. The run reads this as "Bautin's third focal-value generator is
genuinely needed", and closes it in Lean as `Bautin.V3_not_mem_span_V1_V2`
(code/lean/Lib/Bautin.lean lines 217-272).

**The certificate logic is sound**, verified by tracing: every element of
⟨L4,L6⟩ vanishes at every common zero of L4 and L6, so one common zero where
L8 ≠ 0 refutes membership — and in fact puts L8 outside the *radical* of
⟨L4,L6⟩, a stronger statement. The three evaluations are exact rational
arithmetic (sympy over ℚ, no floats, in code/bautin/cofactor_certificate.py and
its capture). The witness arithmetic (0, 0, 25/64) is consistent with the
cleared-integer form (V1num=0/den8, V2num=0/den192, V3num=7200/den18432) that
the Lean kernel is asked to check. The focal-value recurrence matches the
standard Lyapunov-function route.

## Is it already known? Yes — the fact is Bautin's theorem (1952).

The statement "the Bautin ideal of the quadratic focus is generated by the first
three focus quantities V1,V2,V3, which are independent" is exactly the content
of Bautin's own theorem that gives M(2)=3. A weak focus of order 3 is defined by
V1=V2=0, V3≠0; if V3 were in ⟨V1,V2⟩ the third-order classification would be
vacuous. So `V3 ∉ ⟨V1,V2⟩` is precisely that proven independence, known since
1952. Independent modern restatements, all located by search:

- **Bautin 1952**, "On the number of limit cycles appearing with the variation
  of coefficients from an equilibrium state of the type of a focus or a center",
  Mat. Sb. (N.S.) 30(72) (1952) 181–196; AMS Transl. 100 (1954). Primary source
  (held full: research/sources/bautin-1952-full.pdf.full.md).
- **Schlomiuk 1993**, "Algebraic Particular Integrals, Integrability and the
  Problem of the Center", Trans. AMS 338:695–706, doi:10.2307/2154430 — the
  Bautin ideal of the quadratic system is determined by the first three focus
  quantities V1,V2,V3, each defined modulo the ideal of its predecessors.
  (doi:10.2307/2154430)
- **Llibre & Schlomiuk 2004**, "The Geometry of Quadratic Differential Systems
  with a Weak Focus of Third Order", Canad. J. Math. 56(2):310–343 — third-order
  weak focus family, L1=L2=0, L3≠0; all H(2)≥4 examples perturb this family.
  (doi:10.4153/cjm-2004-015-2; also Artés–Llibre 1997, Publ. Mat. 41:89–102,
  doi:10.5565/publmat_41197_02 — "three levels of weakness".)
- **Jarrah–Laubenbacher–Romanovski**, "The Sibirsky component of the center
  variety of polynomial differential systems", J. Symbolic Computation,
  doi:10.1016/S0747717103000166 — verbatim "for quadratic systems, B is
  generated by the first three focus quantities".
- **Mahdi–Pessoa–Hauenstein 2017**, "A hybrid symbolic-numerical approach to the
  center–focus problem", J. Symbolic Comput. 82:57–73,
  doi:10.1016/j.jsc.2016.11.019 — verbatim "the Bautin ideal B is generated by
  the first three focus quantities".
- **Romanovski–Shafer 2009**, *The Center and Cyclicity Problems: A
  Computational Algebra Approach*, Birkhäuser — standard textbook treatment.

The run's own record independently corroborates this: the Gröbner route
(code/out/membership.captured.txt, exact lex over ℚ) gives L8 ∉ ⟨L4,L6⟩,
L6 ∉ ⟨L4⟩, L10,L12 ∈ ⟨L4,L6,L8⟩ — precisely the Bautin-trick structure (first
three independent, all later generators in the ideal of them).

## Is the method standard? Yes.

Computing focal values via the formal-Lyapunov-function recurrence and deciding
ideal membership by Gröbner reduction over ℚ (or, in the certificate form, by a
common-zero evaluation) is the textbook method of the center-focus/cyclicity
literature (Bautin; Romanovski–Shafer 2009; Ferčec–Mahdi 2013,
doi:10.1016/j.matcom.2013.02.003; Mahdi–Pessoa–Hauenstein 2017). The one
non-standard element is the *certificate form* — replacing a Gröbner remainder
with a single common-zero rational evaluation, which is what a Lean kernel can
close by `decide` without formalising Buchberger. That is a genuinely useful
presentation device for machine-checked certificates, and a *stronger* statement
(radical non-membership), but it is a proof-presentation choice, not new
mathematics.

## What would contradict the run? Nothing — the run over-scopes only mildly.

Every source agrees with the mathematical content; several pre-empt it (the fact
is classical). Three cautions for a later reader:

1. **The Lean closure is not verifiable from this environment.** The only on-disk
   compile record for Bautin.lean (code/out/lean/code_lean_Lib_Bautin.lean.json)
   says `compiled:false, outcome:"failed"` with `sorry` warnings at lines
   150/157 — but that artifact is STALE: it lists declarations
   (`m_two_eq_three`, `bautin_ideal_proper`, `bautin_finitely_generated`) that
   do not exist in the current 356-line file, and the current file (grep) shows
   no `sorry` in `V3_not_mem_span_V1_V2` or its three evaluation lemmas. So the
   JSON proves nothing about the current tree either way; a fresh lean_check on
   the current Bautin.lean is required before "kernel-checked" is claimed.
   Moreover, even if it compiles, `V3_not_mem_span_V1_V2` is *conditional*: the
   identification of V1,V2,V3 with the true focal-value sequence rests on a
   `Cited` axiom `focalValue_eq` (Bautin 1952), so the theorem is honest
   `conditional`, not `formalised` — which the file itself states.
2. **Framing.** "Bautin's third generator is genuinely needed" is correct but is
   Bautin's theorem; the run's scope line is honest ("says nothing about
   M(2)=3"). The deliverable's real value is (i) an executed, exact, reproducible
   re-derivation of a known fact, and (ii) a machine-checkable certificate
   design. It does not bear on H16.2 or even on M(2)=3.
3. **Coset convention.** Focus quantities are defined modulo the ideal of their
   predecessors (Schlomiuk 1993); the run uses one correct representative of L8.
   Its non-membership is representation-independent, but a citer should note the
   convention.

## Plausibility of 1 attempt on ~13 established claims

Plausible and expected — but only in the direction that makes it cheap: the
result is a 70-year-old theorem, the script and capture largely pre-existed, and
the final step was executing an existing program and reading its output. That is
the normal shape of bootstrapping folklore, not of a new result. A genuinely new
result of Hilbert-16 size produced in one attempt on 13 claims would, on prior,
be an error; that prior is not challenged here because no such claim is made.

## Verdict

- **Already known: yes** — Bautin 1952 (generator independence of the quadratic
  Bautin ideal), restated correctly as a finite witness.
- **Argument strong enough: yes for `L8 ∉ ⟨L4,L6⟩`**, and the run claims no more.
- **Method: standard** (Lyapunov recurrence + ideal membership), with a
  non-standard, useful certificate presentation.
- **Novelty:** the certified-evaluation Lean theorem form, not the fact.
- **Action:** cite Bautin 1952 (optionally Schlomiuk 1993 or
  Mahdi–Pessoa–Hauenstein 2017) wherever the certificate is presented; re-run
  lean_check on the current Bautin.lean and capture `#print axioms` before

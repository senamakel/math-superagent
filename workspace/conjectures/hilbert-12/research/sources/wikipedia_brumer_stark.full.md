The **Brumer–Stark conjecture**is a [conjecture][1] in [algebraic number theory][2] giving a rough generalization of both the [analytic class number formula][3] for [Dedekind zeta functions][4], and also of [Stickelberger's theorem][5] about the [factorization][6] of [Gauss sums][7]. It is named after [Armand Brumer][8] and [Harold Stark][9].

It arises as a special case (abelian and first-order) of [Stark's conjecture][10], when the [place][11] that [splits completely][12] in the extension is finite. There are very few cases where the conjecture is known to be valid. Its importance arises, for instance, from its connection with [Hilbert's twelfth problem][13].

## Statement of the conjecture

[[edit][14]]

Let *K*/*k*be an [abelian extension][15] of [global fields][16], and let *S*be a set of places of *k*containing the [Archimedean places][17] and the [prime ideals][18] that [ramify][19] in *K*/*k*. The *S*-imprimitive [equivariant Artin L-function][20]*θ*(*s*) is obtained from the usual equivariant Artin L-function by removing the [Euler factors][21] corresponding to the primes in *S*from the [Artin L-functions][22] from which the equivariant function is built. It is a function on the [complex numbers][23] taking values in the complex [group ring][24]**C**[*G*] where *G*is the [Galois group][25] of *K*/*k*. It is analytic on the entire plane, excepting a lone simple pole at *s*= 1.

Let K</sub>''"}},"i":0}}]}'>*μ K*be the group of [roots of unity][26] in *K*. The group *G*acts on K</sub>''"}},"i":0}}]}'>*μ K*; let *A*be the [annihilator][27] of K</sub>''"}},"i":0}}]}'>*μ K*as a **Z**[*G*] - [module][28]. An important theorem, first proved by [C. L. Siegel][29] and later independently by [Takuro Shintani][30], states that *θ*(0) is actually in **Q**[*G*]. A deeper theorem, proved independently by [Pierre Deligne][31] and [Ken Ribet][32], [Daniel Barsky][33], and [Pierrette Cassou-Noguès][34], states that *Aθ*(0) is in **Z**[*G*]. In particular, *Wθ*(0) is in **Z**[*G*], where *W*is the cardinality of K</sub>''"}},"i":0}}]}'>*μ K*.

The [ideal class group][35] of *K*is a **[G -module][36]. From the above discussion, we can let *Wθ*(0) act on it. The Brumer–Stark conjecture says the following: [1]

**Brumer–Stark Conjecture.**For each nonzero [fractional ideal][37] a {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {a}}}[image: {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {a}}}] of *K*, there is an "anti-unit" *ε*such that

1. a W θ ( 0) = ( ε). {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {a}}^{W\theta (0)}=(\varepsilon ).}[image: {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {a}}^{W\theta (0)}=(\varepsilon ).}]
2. The extension K ( ε 1 W) / k {\displaystyle K\left(\varepsilon ^{\frac {1}{W}}\right)/k}[image: {\displaystyle K\left(\varepsilon ^{\frac {1}{W}}\right)/k}] is abelian.

The first part of this conjecture is due to Armand Brumer, and Harold Stark originally suggested that the second condition might hold. The conjecture was first stated in published form by [John Tate][38]. [2]

The term "anti-unit" refers to the condition that ''ν''</sub>"}},"i":0}}]}'>|*ε*|*ν*is required to be 1 for each Archimedean place *ν*. [1]

## Progress

[[edit][39]]

The Brumer Stark conjecture is known to be true for extensions *K*/*k*where

- *K*/**Q**is cyclotomic: this follows from [Stickelberger's theorem][5] [1]
- *K*is abelian over **Q**[3]
- *K*/*k*is a [quadratic extension][40] [2]
- *K*/*k*is a [biquadratic extension][41] [4]

In 2020, [5] [Dasgupta][42] and [Kakde][43] proved the Brumer–Stark conjecture away from the prime 2. [6] In 2023, a full proof of the conjecture over **Z**has been announced. [7]

## Function field analogue

[[edit][44]]

The analogous statement in the [function field case][45] is known to be true, having been proved by [John Tate][38] and [Pierre Deligne][31] in 1984, [8] with a different proof by David Hayes in 1985. [9] [10]

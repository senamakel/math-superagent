[2] Two drawings of the Berlekamp–Van Lint–Seidel graph

In [graph theory][3], the **Berlekamp–Van Lint–Seidel graph**is a [locally linear][4] [strongly regular graph][5] with parameters ( 243, 22, 1, 2) {\displaystyle (243,22,1,2)}[image: {\displaystyle (243,22,1,2)}]. This means that it has 243 vertices, 22 edges per vertex (for a total of 2673 edges), exactly one shared neighbor per pair of adjacent vertices, and exactly two shared neighbors per pair of non-adjacent vertices. It was constructed by [Elwyn Berlekamp][6], [J. H. van Lint][7], and [Johan Jacob Seidel][8] [[de][9]] as the [coset graph][10] of the [ternary Golay code][11]. [1]

This graph is the [Cayley graph][12] of an [abelian group][13]. Among abelian Cayley graphs that are strongly regular and have the last two parameters differing by one, it is the only graph that is not a [Paley graph][14]. [2] It is also an [integral graph][15], meaning that the [eigenvalues][16] of its [adjacency matrix][17] are integers. [3] Like the 9 × 9 {\displaystyle 9\times 9}[image: {\displaystyle 9\times 9}] [Sudoku graph][18] it is an integral abelian Cayley graph whose group elements all have order 3, one of a small number of possibilities for the orders in such a graph. [4]

There are five possible combinations of parameters for strongly regular graphs that have one shared neighbor per pair of adjacent vertices and exactly two shared neighbors per pair of non-adjacent vertices. Of these, two are known to exist: the Berlekamp–Van Lint–Seidel graph and the 9-vertex Paley graph with parameters ( 9, 4, 1, 2) {\displaystyle (9,4,1,2)}[image: {\displaystyle (9,4,1,2)}]. [5] [Conway's 99-graph problem][19] concerns the existence of another of these graphs, the one with parameters ( 99, 14, 1, 2) {\displaystyle (99,14,1,2)}[image: {\displaystyle (99,14,1,2)}]. [6]

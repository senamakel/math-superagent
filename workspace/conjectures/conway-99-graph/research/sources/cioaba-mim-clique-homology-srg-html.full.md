###### Abstract

## 1 Introduction

Let G G be a graph and Cl ​ ( G) \text{Cl}(G) be its clique complex. If R R is a ring, the vanishing of the first clique homology H 1 ​ ( Cl ​ ( G), R) H_{1}(\text{Cl}(G),R) over R R phenomenon has been studied in several contexts, particularly for ℤ \mathbb{Z}, 𝔽 2, {\mathbb{F}}_{2}, the field with two elements, and ℝ {\mathbb{R}}, see [7, 12, 20]. The assertion H 1 ​ ( Cl ​ ( G), R) = 0 H_{1}(\text{Cl}(G),R)=0 is equivalent to the cycle space of the graph G G being spanned by the signed boundaries of the triangles in G G.

While they did not explicitly refer to clique complexes, homology, or cohomology, Duchet, Las Vergnas, and Meyniel [7] obtained a characterization of planar graphs G G with H 1 ​ ( Cl ​ ( G), 𝔽 2) = 0 H_{1}(\text{Cl}(G),{\mathbb{F}}_{2})=0 and called such graphs nullhomotopic. Meshulam [20] proved that any graph G G with the property that every four vertices have a common neighbor satisfies H 1 ​ ( Cl ​ ( G), 𝔽) = 0 H_{1}(\text{Cl}(G),{\mathbb{F}})=0 for every field 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}}. Linial and Meshulam [17] showed that in a random 2 2 -complex Y Y, H 1 ​ ( Y, 𝔽 2) = 0 H_{1}(Y,{\mathbb{F}}_{2})=0 has a sharp threshold p ∼ 2 ​ log ⁡ ( n) n p\sim\frac{2\log(n)}{n}. In [6], we strengthened Meshulam’s result over ℝ {\mathbb{R}} and proved that any graph G G with the property that every induced cycle has four consecutive vertices with a common neighbor satisfies H 1 ​ ( Cl ​ ( G), 𝔽) = 0 H_{1}(\text{Cl}(G),{\mathbb{F}})=0. We also showed that if G G is a strongly regular graph with parameters ( v, k, λ, μ), (v,k,\lambda,\mu), and 2 ​ λ + μ + 2 > 2 ​ k, 2\lambda+\mu+2>2k, then H 1 ​ ( Cl ​ ( G), ℝ) = 0 H_{1}(\text{Cl}(G),{\mathbb{R}})=0 and observed that the Paley graph P ⁡ ( q) P(q) on q q vertices satisfies H 1 ​ ( Cl ​ ( P ⁡ ( q)), ℝ) = 0 H_{1}(\text{Cl}(P(q)),{\mathbb{R}})=0 for q ≥ 4097 q\geq 4097.

In this paper, we study the vanishing H 1 ​ ( Cl ​ ( G), 𝔽) = 0 H_{1}(\text{Cl}(G),{\mathbb{F}})=0 phenomenon for strongly regular graphs G G and an arbitrary field 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}}. We combine our vanishing H 1 H_{1} results with Neumaier’s classification of strongly regular graphs to isolate the strongly regular graphs G G for which H 1 ​ ( Cl ​ ( G), 𝔽) H_{1}(\text{Cl}(G),{\mathbb{F}}) may be nonzero for some 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}}. In particular, we prove that lattice graphs and complete bipartite graphs have non-vanishing H 1 H_{1}. Furthermore, outside of these two families and a finite exceptional family, any strongly regular G G with H 1 ​ ( Cl ​ ( G), 𝔽) ≠ 0 H_{1}(\text{Cl}(G),{\mathbb{F}})\neq 0 must have integral adjacency eigenvalues, and for every fixed m ≥ 3, m\geq 3, there are only a finite number of such graphs G G with smallest eigenvalue − m -m.

Our proofs are combinatorial and are based on local surgery of cycle lemmas. In particular, our techniques do not require an inner product structure of the ambient field 𝔽, {\mathbb{F}}, and are valid over every field. Our starting point is a collection of cycle-surgery lemmas. They replace cycles by shorter cycles after adding signed triangle boundaries. The arguments are algebraic over the coefficient ring: they do not use an inner product and remain valid over arbitrary characteristic. One consequence is the following vanishing criterion: if every induced cycle of a graph has four consecutive vertices with a common neighbor, then its cycle space is triangle-boundary generated over every field. For strongly regular graphs, it is enough to verify an analogous local condition only for induced cycles of lengths four and five (see Theorems 2.10 and 2.11). ## 9 Conclusion

In this paper, we study the first homology groups of strongly regular and determined several sufficient conditions for these groups to be non-trivial. It would be interesting to study which graphs in the exceptional families E m E_{m} for m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 in Neumaier’s classification of strongly regular graphs have nontrivial first homology group.

In the course of our investigation, we encountered the question of determining, for a strongly regular graph G G and non-adjacent vertices x x and y y, if G x, y G_{x,y}, the subgraph induced by the common neighbors of x x and y y, is connected or not. We studied this problem for Paley graphs and conference graphs in general, and we hope to extend our results in the future.

## Why the falsifier was NOT found (so the route is grounded, not dead)

The most dangerous thing that could refute this approach is a theorem that the irreducible T(x)-module dimensions of an SRG are fixed by the parameters. The searches returned no such theorem. On the contrary: the Tomiyama-Yamazaki dimension formula depends explicitly on the *spectra of the first and second subconstituents*, and Levstein-Maldonado show the whole Terwilliger algebra of a GQ point graph takes only two isomorphism types (s^2=t or not) -- a classification that is *coarser* than the parameters and depends on local geometry. So the T(x)-module structure of an SRG is genuinely local and can in principle differ at 99 from both controls. This is what makes the approach admissible: it is NOT a parameter-determined invariant that survives on 9 and 243 unchanged.

## What it would buy, and the honest boundary

If the irreducible T(x)-module dimensions at (99,14,1,2) fail integrality while (9,4,1,2) and (243,22,1,2) pass, that is a genuine 99-specific nonexistence step the eigenvalue-only routes never see. But that failure is NOT established by the sources -- the sources only establish that the decomposition is local and hence a legitimate candidate differentiator. The computation must be done. The route's own speculativeness (flagged by the candidate and confirmed here) is that the algebra of a general non-distance-regular SRG need not be rigid enough to force the obstruction; that is exactly what the first-step computation decides.

## Status

grounded (premise: local, admissible, not parameter-determined -- supported by Tomiyama-Yamazaki as reported and Levstein-Maldonado); speculative (positive obstruction: unknown, must be computed). Not refuted, not adopted.

## What it establishes (from abstract + as-restated in [6])

**[6]-Thm 5.4 / Thm 2.10 of the successor]** If G is a graph in which every
induced cycle has four consecutive vertices with a common neighbor, then
H₁(Cl(G), ℝ) = 0 — the cycle space is generated by the triangle boundaries
(stated over ℝ in [6]; the successor lifts it to every field). For SRGs the
tightening is that checking induced cycles of length 4 and 5 suffices (the
μ>0 common-neighbor surgery).

**[6]-Prop 5.2]** For a connected conference/Paley-type graph the common
neighbourhood N(x) is connected (used by the successor at v > 9).

**[6]-Thm 5.19 / Lemma 6.5** of the successor: the local surgery lemmas for
re-expressing a 4-cycle's circuit as a combination of triangle boundaries now
stated over every field.

**Spectra of higher Laplacians / triangle complexes.** The paper computes
spectra of the triangle complex Laplacians (higher-order Hodge Laplacians) for
several SRG families — Hamming graphs and Triangular graphs (Latin-square and
triangular families) — and shows the spectrum of the triangle complex
*distinguishes* non-isomorphic cospectral SRGs in many cases (every SRG with
the same parameters is automatically cospectral, so the interest is in a
refinement that can tell them apart), but exhibits examples where it does not.

## Bearing on the run

- This is the **spectral/ℝ-cohomology antecedent** of the clique-complex line.
  Its ℝ-version of the vanishing criterion (every induced cycle has four
  consecutive vertices with a common neighbor ⟹ H₁ = 0) is the mechanism the
  classification paper generalizes to arbitrary fields.

  H₁ by the computed gate, so neither the spectral paper nor its successor

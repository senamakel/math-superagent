# Approach — degree-3-critical classification + Ω(log n) cycle lengths

```approach
idea: Exploit that a vertex-minimal counterexample is an *induced degree-3-critical graph* (2n−2 edges, no proper induced subgraph of min degree ≥ 3), and attack the conjecture through the Erdős–Faudree–Gyárfás–Schelp / DCG theory of such graphs rather than through arbitrary δ≥3 graphs.
mechanism: Degree-3-critical graphs are the extremal object at the exact edge density where the "many cycle lengths" theorems are sharp: the held DCG claim gives Ω(log n) *distinct* cycle lengths in any n-vertex degree-3-critical graph, and EFGS give a cycle of length ≥ log n plus C3/C4. Ω(log n) distinct lengths alone does not force a power of two (e.g. 3,5,7,…), so the missing ingredient is a *second* structural fact about degree-3-critical graphs — pancyclicity in the non-induced case (NPS: only wheels, hence pancyclic), or the short-cycle forcing in the induced case — that constrains the cycle-length set enough to intersect {4,8,16,…}. Concretely: show a minimal counterexample's cycle-length set either is an interval (contradiction, since intervals contain a 2-power iff they are long enough) or has a congruence/structure defect that a δ≥3, 2n−2-edge graph cannot have.
status: refuted
killed-by: A vertex-minimal counterexample is NOT in general degree-3-critical (2n−2 edges), so the EFGS/NPS/DCG classification results that fuel this approach do not apply. Degree-3-critical is defined (NPS, Di Braccio et al.) as n-vertex, 2n−2 edges, no proper induced subgraph of minimum degree ≥3. A vertex-minimal counterexample to E–G satisfies only the "no proper induced subgraph of min degree ≥3" condition; it is not pinned to 2n−2 edges. Indeed Carr/Markström (arXiv:2605.22844, held) show a *cubic* counterexample would have 3n/2 edges — not 2n−2 unless n=4 — so the edge-count hypothesis of the entire degree-3-critical cycle-length machinery fails in the very regime (cubic, which regular counterexamples must be) the approach most wants. Because of this the three pillars named in the idea are inert: (a) "Ω(log n) distinct cycle lengths" (DCG Thm 1, arXiv/Combinatorica 2026) is for degree-3-critical (2n−2-edge) graphs; (b) "C3/C4 and longest cycle Ω(log n)" (EFGS 1988) is for G*(n,2n−2); (c) "non-induced ⇒ pancyclic, only wheels" (NPS Thm 1.4) is again for 2n−2-edge graphs with no proper non-induced subgraph — a condition a minimal E–G counterexample does not meet. NPS Thm 1.2 (degree-3-critical graphs with no 23-cycle at arbitrary size) is a strict caution even where the hypotheses DO hold: 3-criticality alone does not force a prescribed sparse cycle length, so the "2n−2 edges + Ω(log n) lengths" leap to "a 2-power" has no solid base.
precedent:
  - Di Braccio–Katsamaktsis–Ma–Malekshahian–Zhao, "Leaf-to-leaf paths and cycles in degree-critical graphs", Combinatorica 2026, doi:10.1007/s00493-026-00205-2 — degree-3-critical defined with 2n−2 edges; Ω(log n) distinct cycle lengths. Held: research/sources/degree-3-critical-leaf-to-leaf-cycles.combinatorica.full.md.
  - Narins–Pokrovskiy–Szabó, arXiv:1408.5289 — definition (2n−2 edges), Thm 1.2 (no-23-cycle), Thm 1.4 (non-induced pancyclic/wheels). Held: research/sources/narins-szabo-degree3-critical-short-cycles.full.md.
  - Erdős–Faudree–Gyárfás–Schelp, Ars Combin. 25B (1988) 195–201 (Rényi PDF held) — G*(n,2n−2) degree-3-critical baseline, C3/C4, Ω(log n) longest cycle.
  - Carr, "Every Minimal Counterexample to the Erdős–Gyárfás Conjecture is Predominantly Cubic", arXiv:2605.22844 — cubic counterexample has 3n/2 edges; regular minimal counterexamples are cubic. Held: research/sources/carr-predominantly-cubic.full.md.
  - claims on disk: nps-degree3-critical-no-23-cycle, nps-noninduced-degree3critical-pancyclic, dcg-degree3-critical-omn-log-n-cycle-lengths, efg-degree-3-critical-longest-cycle-omega-logn, regular-ce-is-cubic.
first-step (if revisited): not grounded — before any use of the classification, one would have to prove a minimal counterexample is 2n−2-edge, which is false for cubic counterexamples; the degree-3-critical lens is the wrong extremal frame for this problem.
```

## Why this is different

The backward skeletons use the minimal counterexample's longest path; the open thread uses degree counts/discharging. This approach uses the *edge count* (2n−2) and the degree-3-critical classification/pancyclic machinery that the run holds but has never aimed at the 2-power question — it is an extremal/classification line, distinct from both the algebraic (cycle-space) and combinatorial-transfer (Kraft) lines.

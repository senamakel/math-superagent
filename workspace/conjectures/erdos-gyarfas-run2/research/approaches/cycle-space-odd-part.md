# Approach — 2-adic odd-part invariant on the cycle space

```approach
idea: Reframe "cycle of length 2^k" as "a cycle whose length has odd part 1". Write every cycle length as ℓ = 2^{v_2(ℓ)} · m with m odd, and study the graph invariant Θ(G) = { odd part of ℓ : ℓ a cycle length of G }. The conjecture is exactly 1 ∈ Θ(G). This turns a sparse prescribed-length problem into an invariant on the GF(2)-cycle space, where the target is the *minimum* element of the divisibility order.
mechanism: The cycle space over GF(2) has dimension β(G) = m − n + 1 ≥ n/2 for a connected δ≥3 graph. Its circuits are the cycles, and two cycles C1, C2 sharing exactly a path of length p recombine (symmetric difference) into a cycle of length ℓ1 + ℓ2 − 2p — an elementary "length-recombination" law on S(G). Tracking v_2 under this law is a *binary carry* arithmetic: ℓ1+ℓ2−2p is even, and the 2-adic valuation of the sum is governed by where the binary expansions of ℓ1, ℓ2 and 2p cancel (Lucas' theorem / carries). The conjecture becomes a closure problem: does the recombination law force the odd part down to 1? This is the cycle-space analogue of binary GCD, and it is where the powers of two are native: the minimal element of Θ(G) under divisibility is the power-of-2 core of every cycle length.
status: refuted
killed-by: The recombination law is real but only for a special pair geometry, and no global closure follows. "Two cycles sharing exactly a path of length p recombine to a cycle of length ℓ1+ℓ2−2p" holds (via symmetric difference) precisely when the two cycles meet in a single maximal path P (a theta-like configuration) and are otherwise vertex-disjoint. For arbitrary two cycles in a graph, C1⊕C2 is only guaranteed to be an even subgraph decomposing into a *disjoint union of several cycles* — no single cycle of the prescribed length ℓ1+ℓ2−2p (and hence no determined v2 value) is forced. So the set Θ(G) of odd parts is NOT closed under any recombination the law dictates for arbitrary pairs, and there is no forcing to drive the odd part down to 1. No source in the literature applies a 2-adic-valuation / odd-part invariant to the cycle space for the Erdős–Gyárfás problem; searches for "2-adic valuation odd part cycle length", "odd part cycle length graph", "cycle space recombination v2" return only cycle-space linear algebra over GF(2) (Bondy–Murty, Wikipedia, matroid papers) with the standard result that symmetric difference preserves even degree but not cycle-length arithmetic. The GF(2) cycle-space structure is standard (dimension m−n+c, symmetric-difference addition) and provides no length-obstruction information of the kind the approach needs.
precedent:
  - Bondy–Murty, *Graph Theory* GTM 244 (2008), Ch. 14 — cycle space over GF(2), dimension m−n+c, symmetric difference. Notes held (faculty.etsu.edu notes); the GF(2) linear-algebra content is standard and confirms only the vector-space identity, not length closure.
  - Khanna–Putterman–Song, "Optimal Parallel Basis Finding in Graphic and Related Matroids" (ICALP 2026) — Claim 10 / Lemma 11: symmetric difference of two distinct cycles contains a cycle (decomposes into cycles), confirming the recombination does not generally give one cycle of length ℓ1+ℓ2−2p. This is the mechanism-level refutation.
  - searches for a 2-adic/odd-part invariant applied to E–G cycle lengths: no applicable source found.
first-step (if revisited): none grounded — the closure claim that makes the approach work fails at the recombination step itself; the literature's only relevant statement (symmetric difference decomposes into several cycles) actively competes with the single-cycle closure the idea needs.
```

## Why this is different

The open thread and the backward skeletons argue over *degree structure* and *longest paths* in the graph. This is an arithmetic/algebraic reformulation on the cycle space (a GF(2)-vector space), with the powers of two entering through binary carries and 2-adic valuation rather than through "length happens to lie in an interval".

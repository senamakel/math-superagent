# Approach — Kraft / leaf-to-leaf-path transfer

```approach
idea: Transfer the conjecture from "cycle of length 2^k in a δ≥3 graph" to "leaf-to-leaf path of length 2^k in a (even) 1–3 tree", using Narins–Pokrovskiy–Szabó's cycle↔leaf-path dictionary and Rautenbach–Sereni–(et al.)'s Kraft inequality Σ 2^{-w} ≤ (ℓ−1)/4 over leaf-to-leaf path lengths. Powers of two become the *natural base* of the object (binary trees), so the sparseness obstruction is turned from a liability into the arithmetic the proof runs on.
mechanism: A vertex-minimal counterexample is induced-degree-3-critical (no proper induced subgraph of min degree ≥ 3, else it is a smaller counterexample). NPS build degree-3-critical graphs G(T) from even 1–3 trees T with a dictionary: cycles of G(T) ↔ leaf-to-leaf paths of T (their Lemma 2.1). So forcing a 2^k-cycle reduces to forcing a leaf-to-leaf path of length 2^k in a full/even binary tree. RSW already prove a Kraft-type bound on exactly these path lengths — the inequality 2^{-w} summing to ≤ (ℓ−1)/4 is a *binary* (base-2) statement, which is the one place in the entire library where the base 2 is native rather than imposed. The route: (a) nail down which induced-degree-3-critical graphs are covered by NPS's G(T) (is every minimal counterexample a G(T), or does it merely contain one?); (b) prove the tree statement "every even 1–3 tree of order ≥ N has a leaf-to-leaf path of length a power of two" — or, failing that, classify the trees that avoid it and show they cannot arise from a δ≥3 counterexample.
status: refuted
killed-by: The NPS dictionary is real but points the wrong lengths at the tree, and the Kraft bound cannot force the sparse target. NPS Lemma 2.1 (verified in full text, arXiv:1408.5289): a 2^k-cycle in G(T) ⟺ T has a leaf-leaf path of length 2^k−2, OR two vertex-disjoint leaf-leaf paths with e(P1)+e(P2)=2^k−4. So the tree statement this approach picks up is "leaf-leaf path of length 2^k−2", not "of length 2^k" as the idea wrote. RSW's Kraft inequality Σ_{w∈W} 2^{−w} ≤ (ℓ−1)/4 (arXiv:2507.10351, "final observation", full text held) is a *density* bound on the multiset of leaf-leaf lengths — it constrains how many leaves sit at small depths, but forces nothing about a member of the sparse set {2^k−2}. Di Braccio et al. (Combinatorica 2026, doi:10.1007/s00493-026-00205-2) show there are arbitrarily large 1–3 trees with only O(N^{0.91}) distinct leaf-leaf path lengths < N, and every 1–3 tree on ≥2^N vertices has only Ω(N^{2/3}) such lengths — i.e. the leaf-leaf spectrum can be badly sub-polynomial, so no density/richness argument forces a specific (power-of-two-minus-two) length. The transfer hypothesis is also not grounded: NPS's G(T) is a construction of induced-degree-3-critical graphs, not a characterisation of all of them, so "minimal counterexample is a G(T)" is unproven and unsubstantiated in the literature.
precedent:
  - Narins–Pokrovskiy–Szabó, "Graphs without proper subgraphs of minimum degree 3 and short cycles", arXiv:1408.5289 / Combinatorica 37 (2017) 495–519 — Lemma 2.1 dictionary, G(T) construction. Held: research/sources/narins-szabo-degree3-critical-short-cycles.full.md.
  - Rautenbach–Scherer–Werner, "Leaf to leaf path lengths in trees of given degree sequence", arXiv:2507.10351 — Kraft inequality. Held: research/sources/rautenbach-leaf-to-leaf-paths-trees.full.md.
  - Di Braccio–Katsamaktsis–Ma–Malekshahian–Zhao, "Leaf-to-leaf paths and cycles in degree-critical graphs", Combinatorica 2026, doi:10.1007/s00493-026-00205-2 — sparse leaf-leaf spectra. Held: research/sources/degree-3-critical-leaf-to-leaf-cycles.combinatorica.full.md.
  - claims on disk: nps-degree3-critical-no-23-cycle, nps-even-13-tree-leaf-lengths, rsw-kraft-full-binary-leaf-lengths, rsw-lp-tree-radius-bound, dcg-degree3-critical-omn-log-n-cycle-lengths.
first-step (if revisited): none grounded — the transfer is not surjective onto counterexamples and the Kraft density bound does not reach the sparse 2-power-minus-two target; the near-miss theorem (NPS Thm 1.3: every large even 1–3 tree has leaf-leaf lengths 0,2,…,18 but misses 20) shows the exact obstruction: infinitely many leaf lengths are missing, and 20 is one of the length-minus-two values that a 2-power cycle needs.
```

## Why this is different from the open thread

The near-cubic thread uses the *degree counts* of a minimal counterexample and discharging. This approach changes the object entirely — from the graph to its 1–3-tree skeleton and from degree counts to binary words/Kraft sums — and it is the only place in the library where the base 2 is built into the algebra rather than bolted on at the end.

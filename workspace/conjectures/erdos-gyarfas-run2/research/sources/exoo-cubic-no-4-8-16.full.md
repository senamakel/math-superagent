# Graphs Without Cycles of Specified Lengths

The following graphs are motivated by the Erdos/Gyarfas conjecture which asserts
(more or less) that every graph with minimum degree at least 3 has a cycle whose
length is a power of 2.

[1] A smallest trivalent graph with no 4, 6, or 10 cycles.
[2] A trivalent graph of order 32 with no 4, 8 or 32 cycles.
[3] A smallest trivalent graph with no 4 or 8 cycles (there are 3 others).
[4] Another example of a graph with no cycles of lengths 4 and 8.
[5] The (unique) smallest trivalent graph with no 4 or 6 cycles.
[6] A smallest trivalent graph with no 4, 6 or 8 cycles.

The smallest graph I know of with no 4, 8 or 16 cycles has 78 vertices. The
smallest with no 4, 8, 16 or 32 cycles has 540 vertices. I'd be interested to
hear of any smaller examples.

(Geoffrey Exoo, "Graphs Without Cycles of Specified Lengths", maintained at
http://isu.indstate.edu/ge/COMBIN/CYCLES/index.html — catalogue of extremal
trivalent graphs avoiding specified cycle lengths, motivated by the
Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture. These are "smallest known", not proven exhaustive
beyond Markström's complete search up to N≤52 for no-{4,8,16} cubic graphs.)

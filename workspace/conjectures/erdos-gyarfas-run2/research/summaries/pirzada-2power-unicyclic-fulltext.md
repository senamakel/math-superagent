## What the paper establishes

- Defines **2-power unicyclic cubic graphs**: cubic graphs with exactly one cycle
  whose length is a power of two.
- **Theorem 2.1**: an infinite family G_i of such graphs exists. |G_1|=94 (only C32
  as a 2-power cycle), |G_2|=222 (only C64), |G_3|=478 (only C128), builder
  |G_i| = |G_{i-1}| + 2^{i+4}. Two copies of a gadget X_i are joined by a bridge
  edge, bounding every cycle within one half, and the shortest cycle excludes all
  smaller powers — forcing a single 2-power length.
- These are genuine near-counterexamples (arbitrarily large cubic graphs whose only
  2-power cycle is one prescribed 2^k), alongside Bensmail's construction.

## What is NOT established

The paper's Conclusion (and abstract) assert that no counterexample to the
Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture exists, with a closing step that is circular: it invokes
"the observation that each cubic graph has a 2-power cycle," which is exactly the
conjecture under attack. The construction is solid; the conjecture is *not* proved
here. Cite this paper for the 2-power unicyclic construction only.

## What closed, and why

Do not propose these again. A reason stated precisely is what makes that possible; one left blank makes this row worthless.

- [[bombieri-pila-determinant]] (refuted): The bound it actually produces is FAR WEAKER than the trivial bound it is meant to beat, and is not O(1). Exact Bombieri–Pila statement (Pila's refinement, Bloom–Lichtman 2025, Enumer. Theory 4, 327-358, Theorem 1): for irreducible F ∈ R[x,y] of degree d ≥ 2 and N large depending only on d, #{ (x,y) ∈ [1,N]² : F(x,y) = 0 } ≤ (log N)^{O(d)} · N^{1/d}. Applied to the family C(x,k1)=C(y,k2) of degree d = max(k1,k2) with box [1,a]²: per curve, ≤ (log a)^{O(d)} a^{1/d}. Three independent failures. (1) WORSE THAN TRIVIAL: the trivial bound for the same quantity is far stronger. Every representation with a fixed k satisfies the degree-k polynomial equation C(n,k)=a, which has at most k integer roots in n; summing k over k ≤ log₂ a gives the exact trivial bound N(a) ≤ Σ_{k≤⌊log₂a⌋} k + 2 = (⌊log₂a⌋(⌊log₂a⌋+1))/2 + 2 = O((log a)²) (T(3003)=68, T(10^6)=192, T(10^12)=782, T(10^30)=4952 under the both-halves+trivial convention). The Bombieri–Pila route gives, already at the smallest relevant degree d=3 (the (2,3) curve C(x,2)=C(y,3)), ~ (log a)^{O(3)}·a^{1/3} per curve — an UNBOUNDED function of a that exceeds the trivial bound for every a ≳ 10^4 and grows without bound (a^{1/3} ≫ (log₂a)²/2 for large a). The sum over ~(log a)² pairs never reduces to O(1): Σ_{d≥3} (log a)^{O(d)} a^{1/d} is dominated by the d=3 term, which diverges; the d ≥ 3 aggregate is ~(log a)²·(log a)^{O(3)}·a^{1/3} → ∞. (2) HYPOTHESIS NOT MET FOR THE SMALL-DEGREE CURVES THAT CARRY THE MULTIPLICITY: the degree-3 curves include (k1,k2)=(2,3),(3,2),(3,3-diagonal-degenerate),(3,4),(4,3); the (2,3) triangular=tetrahedral curve C(x,2)=C(y,3) is degree 3 and its Bombieri–Pila bound is ~a^{1/3}, while the actual X(Z) is known (Mordell 1963, MacLeod–Barrodale 1970; claim sdw-elliptic-logarithms-eight-pairs) to be FINITE and effectively solvable — the determinant bound is not what settles it. (3) NO PROGRESS ON THE BOUNDARY: every known witness lives in columns k∈{2,5,6} plus the infinite Pell family in k∈{2,3} — exactly the k=1,2 columns the mechanism explicitly EXCLUDES from its "controlled" regime; the approach's own target (control k1,k2 ≥ 3, leave k=2 to a separate column problem) abandons the columns where all multiplicity resides, and the reduced problem C(x,2)=C(y,k) is precisely the per-pair effective-wall (avanesov/de weger/bmsst/stroeker-deweger for finitely many k; BST 1999 ineffective for the rest) already in the library. No source applies Bombieri–Pila to Singmaster; the only overlapping issues in the literature are method-level (Bloom–Lichtman, Marmon, Mendes da Costa on elliptic improvements), none addressing equal binomial coefficients. The single bound in the whole problem that the determinant method genuinely improves — O(N^{1/d}) for irreducible curves — is already dwarfed by the trivial degree-k root count in the variable n, which is the correct cheap oracle for this problem.
- [[chabauty-coleman-uniform]] (refuted): Four independent obstructions. (1) The `effective-methods-wall` (grounded, held primaries) establishes that no effective integral-point method exists for genus ≥ 3 — Chabauty–Coleman is a RATIONAL-point method (it bounds #X(Q), not #X(Z)), and the binomial problem needs INTEGER points; a rational-point bound doesn't give an integer-point bound because the curve embeds differently in the integer lattice. (2) Even if Chabauty–Coleman applied to integer points, the method requires explicit p-adic integration on Jacobians, which is only implemented for genus 1 (elliptic logarithms) and genus 2 (Stoll's refinements of Coleman); the family leaves genus 2 immediately — `genus{2,n}=floor((n-1)/2)` and `{3,4}` is already genus 3 — so the practical computation doesn't exist beyond a tiny initial segment. (3) The uniform-rank conjecture is unproven and the proposed "Jacobian decomposes in a structured way" claim is speculative — Prym varieties of fiber products are not generally simpler than the original Jacobian, and there is no theorem bounding ranks of such families. (4) The Chabauty–Coleman bound `#X(Q) ≤ #X(F_p) + 2g - 2` (when it applies) GROWS with g, so even if the method worked uniformly it would give `#X(Q) ≤ O(g) = O(k1·k2)`, which is a function of the column indices — not a constant B, and not uniform in (k1,k2). The approach asks to solve a harder open problem (uniform MW rank bound for a high-genus family) to attack Singmaster, with the wrong direction of dependence — each barrier is independently fatal.
- [[consecutive-block-merge]] (refuted): identical in mechanism to the Saradha-Shorey-Tijdeman (SST 1995) and Beukers-Shorey-Tijdeman (BST 1999) equal-products classification. SST 1995 already classifies exceptional infinite families (Theorem 2: gcd(L,M)>1 gives effective bounds; only d1=1,d2=4,L=M infinite family survives). BST 1999 Theorem 2.2 gives the full genus classification. The block-merge analysis for the (5,6) case of 3003 collapses trivially (15·6 = 10·9) and does not generalize — the SST proof structure (shifted-power reduction, resultant factorization, Chebyshev/rolle contradiction) is the correct general engine, already in the library. The block-merge metaphor adds no new mathematics beyond what the held papers already establish.
- [[erdos-selfridge-structural]] (refuted): The mechanism's engine is the claim "for every k ≥ 2 there is a prime p > k whose exponent in (n+1)...(n+k) is exactly 1." The held primary (Erdős–Selfridge, Illinois J. Math. 19 (1975) 292–301, Theorem 2 — full text at research/sources/erdos-selfridge-product-not-power-1975.full.md) proves only: for k ≥ 3, l ≥ 2, n+k ≥ p(k) (p(k) the least prime ≥ k), there is a prime p ≥ k whose exponent in (n+1)...(n+k) is NOT a multiple of l. The stronger statement "there is a prime p > k dividing the product to the FIRST power" is stated in the same paper to be a CONJECTURE: "One could conjecture the following strengthening of Theorem 2: if k ≥ 4 and n+k ≥ p(k), then there is at least one prime greater than k which divides (n+1)...(n+k) to the first power. This conjecture, if true, seems very deep." It remains open (Erdős, Rocky Mountain J. Math. 15 (1985) 353–363, restates it as a hypothesis, not a theorem). Numerical falsification of the premise: for k=2, n=7, the product 8·9 = 72 = 2³·3² has NO prime > 2 to exponent 1 (3 appears with exponent 2). The theorem itself only yields an exponent not divisible by l — it is the Sylvester–Schur occurrence of a prime p > k (Sylvester 1892, Schur 1929), one prime with some exponent, not a first-power prime. So the approach does not rest on a provable Erdős–Selfridge statement; the classification-by-prime-support lemma proposed ("if both blocks are large they must align") is unsupported: the witnesses show the opposite — C(15,5)=C(14,6)=3003 has the large primes 7,11,13 — all exceeding max(5,6) — appearing inside BOTH blocks (360360 = 2³·3²·5·7·11·13; 2162160 = 2⁴·3³·5·7·11·13), i.e. the "exponent-1 alignment" never forces contradiction. Moreover the equal-products machinery that CAN say something here is already held: Saradha–Shorey–Tijdeman 1995 (effective when gcd(L,M)>1, claim sst-effective-shared-factor) and Beukers–Shorey–Tijdeman 1999 (ineffective finiteness, claim bst-fixed-kl-ineffective-primary) — the same exhaustion of the block structure that closed sylvester-prime-machine. No source applies the exponent-1 prime guarantee to Singmaster; no literature supports an alignment classification.
- [[frey-curve-modular]] (refuted): The modular method requires the Diophantine equation to be transformable into a generalized Fermat form A·x^p + B·y^q = C·z^r (three-term additive), to which a Frey curve E and a Galois representation ρ on E[p] can be attached. The equal-products equation is MULTIPLICATIVE: x(x-1)...(x-k1+1)·k2! = y(y-1)...(y-k2+1)·k1! — a product equality, not a sum. No known transformation converts this into a three-term additive equation for general k1,k2. Bennett's successful modular-method results (2001, 2013) handle equations of the form (x+1)...(x+k) = y^n where one side is a PURE POWER — the pure power y^n is essential for the Frey curve construction (it provides the p in E[p]). The binomial equal-products equation has BOTH sides as products of consecutive integers; neither side is a pure power unless one of k1,k2 is 1 (trivial column) or both sides collapse to a single factor. Even for the triangular column k1=2: x(x-1)·k! = 2·y(y-1)...(y-k+1). The RHS is a product of k consecutive integers, not a pure power, so no prime exponent p can be extracted uniformly. BMSST 2008 solved (2,5) using elliptic logarithms (Baker's method), NOT the modular method. The full text of BMSST (held, research/sources/bugeaud-hyperelliptic-2008.full.md) confirms this: their method is Baker/Matveev linear forms in elliptic logarithms, combined with Mordell-Weil sieving — zero modularity. No source applies the Darmon–Granville modular machinery to the equal-products equation; the search surfaces only Bennett's pure-power results and the general modular-method literature (Darmon–Granville 1995, Ribet 1990), none of which address equal consecutive-integer blocks. The approach describes a template that does not exist for this equation shape.
- [[height-gap-adelic]] (refuted): Four obstructions. (1) Vojta's inequality is a RATIONAL-point statement (it bounds #X(K) for a number field K), not an INTEGER-point statement; the curve X_k(a): C(y,k) = a for fixed a has rational points y but the integer condition n = y is an integrality constraint that Vojta does not control — this is the same rational-vs-integral gap that sank chabauty-coleman-uniform. (2) Vojta's constant is INEFFECTIVE: the "2 + ε" has no computable ε from the proof; Rémond's explicit version (2000, Compositio 117) requires the heights of the Jacobians and the Northcott number of the field, which grow with k and a. The claimed "effective" Rémond constants exist in theory but have never been computed for any nontrivial family, and the dependence on the genus (which grows with k) makes them astronomical. (3) The approach conflates the PARAMETER a with the COORDINATE: X_k(a) is a DIFFERENT curve for each a — it's not a fixed curve with varying a. Vojta applies to a FIXED variety, not to a family parameterized by a. Each different a gives a completely different set of curves with no shared geometry. (4) Even if a height inequality forced k ≤ K for large N(a), it would not give an effective K — the dependence on the height constants is implicit and Vojta's method does not produce computable constants without solving the effective Mordell conjecture, which is open. The known witnesses (3003 with k=5,6; the N=6 values with k=2,3) are consistent with "k is small" but provide no evidence that a height-based proof exists.
- [[hypergeometric-wz-bijection]] (refuted): The premise "C(n,k) can be written as a terminating hypergeometric series ... and the WZ algorithm can classify ALL terminating hypergeometric identities of this form" describes a different class of objects than the one this problem needs. What actually holds: (a) C(n,k) = (n)̄_k/k! is a POLYNOMIAL in n of degree k — a single hypergeometric TERM, not a hypergeometric SUMMATION identity; the WZ/Gosper/Zeilberger machinery classifies SUMMATION identities — Σ_k F(n,k) = f(n) with a certificate R(n,k) — and certifies that such an identity holds for ALL n, whereas C(x,k1)=C(y,k2) is an equality of two single polynomial values at SPECIFIC integer arguments (x,y). It is not a hypergeometric summation identity at all; there is no free summation index, so there is no telescoping certificate to search over. (b) The classification that genuinely exists for hypergeometric coincidences is the Beukers–Heckman interlacing criterion / algebraicity classification (Invent. Math. 97 (1989) 325–354; Fürnsinn–Yurkevich arXiv:2308.12855 gives the general algorithm) and the Vidūnas classification of algebraic transformations of Gauss hypergeometric functions/Belyi pull-backs (Funkcial. Ekvac. 52 (2009); van Hoeij–Vidūnas, J. Algebra 2015). These classify when ₂F₁(a,b;c;z) is ALGEBRAIC in z, or when two hypergeometric FUNCTIONS are related by a pull-back φ — i.e. identities of functions of a variable, holding for all z in a region. The binomial-coincidence equation C(x,k1)=C(y,k2) is NOT of that shape: the parameters and arguments are linked, not two evaluations of the same function at two values of one variable. No published source applies WZ/Gosper/Zeilberger, or Beukers–Heckman, or the Belyi-pull-back classification to Singmaster's problem or to C(x,k1)=C(y,k2); the searches surface only the general algorithmic theory (Wilf–Zeilberger 1992, Gosper 1978, Koepf 1994) and the general hypergeometric-algebraicity classifications, none of which mention equal binomial coefficients. (c) The realistic content the proposal itself concedes — "reduce to a finite set of Thue equations via hypergeometric-to-polynomial reduction, which is essentially what Stroeker–de Weger already did for the elliptic pairs" — is exactly the per-pair effective integral-point wall already in the library (sdw-elliptic-logarithms-eight-pairs; deweger-smallk-effective; bst-fixed-kl-ineffective-primary). WZ adds no new theorem over the polynomial root-count N(a) ≤ O((log a)²), the trivial oracle this problem already uses. (d) The claim "the known infinite family corresponds to one WZ certificate" is a category error: the Pell family is a parametrized FAMILY of solutions of a single polynomial equation, not a hypergeometric identity provable by telescoping; WZ certificates prove identities, they do not enumerate Diophantine solutions.
- [[kummer-lucas-p-adic]] (refuted): kummer-lucas-class-not-logarithmic — the proposed core lemma is unconditionally false. For p=2, every entry of row n=2^m−1 is odd and ≡1 (mod 2) with v_2=0 (all m binary digits of 2^m−1 are 1; Lucas rowwise product C(1,k_i)≡1), so the single p-adic class (v_2=0, residue 1) contains all 2^m pairs (2^m−1,k), k=0..2^m−1 — exponential in the bit-length, not logarithmic. Same for generic p via row n=(p^a-1)/(p-1) congruence-to-1 results (Lucas's theorem / generalized Lucas; MDPI 2020 generalization: a row is all ≡1 (mod p^e) iff n=(p^e m -1)/(p^e-1)). Hence fixed p-adic classes are large/infinite and no finite intersection of prime-class constraints bounds N(a). Per-prime local information does not control how many globally-distinct (n,k) land on one integer — the same structural wall as Faltings/Siegel.
- [[legendre-digit-sum]] (refuted): This is an additive-combinatorial variant of the refuted `kummer-lucas-p-adic` approach. While the simultaneous-prime constraint IS genuinely stronger than the single-prime Lucas case (which was refuted because one p-adic class is exponentially large), the approach provides no mechanism to prove the intersection is O(1). The digit-sum functions s_p(n) for different primes p are NOT independent — they are coupled through the fact that they describe the same integer n — and there is no known additive-combinatorial theorem that bounds the size of the intersection of level sets of digit-sum functions across multiple bases. The claim "intersection cardinality drops exponentially with the number of prime factors" is an unproven heuristic, not a theorem. Moreover, the binary-lucas-submask approach (adopted) already captures the strongest single-prime case (p=2 gives the submask constraint k ⊆ n), and the Legendre approach's extension to all primes is a complexity increase without a new structural insight — more constraints of the same type don't change the fundamental difficulty. The approach is also essentially identical to the `skolem-strassman-p-adic` proposal (which remains unchecked against the literature): both seek to use p-adic constraints across multiple primes to bound the zero-set of the two-variable function C(n,k)-a. The refutation of kummer-lucas-p-adic showed that per-prime congruences are weak; the adelic version needs a proof that the intersection is small, and no such proof has been provided or exists in the digit-sum literature.
- [[mason-stothers-abc]] (refuted): (i) elementary: the direct binomial three-term identity makes Mason-Stothers hold with equality, hence vacuously, for every distinct pair — **captured run confirms this**: code/out/check_mason_stothers_bound.captured.txt shows degB'=0 for all 21 pairs with 2<=k2<k1<=8, slack >= 0 throughout (slack = (N0_ub-1) - maxdeg; slack>=0 means the inequality never binds). (nonzero rational constant). - [[skolem-strassman-p-adic]]: For fixed k, the function f_k(n) = C(n,k) = n(n-1)...(n-k+1)/k! is a polynomial in n of degree k. But more importantly, it can be expressed as a p-adic analytic function via the Mahler expansion: C(n,k) = ∑_{j=0}^k a_j(k) · C(n,j) where a_j(k) are the Stirling numbers (or more precisely, C(n,k) itself is already a binomial-coefficient polynomial). The equation C(n,k) = a for fixed k is just a polynomial root-finding problem (degree k), giving at most k integer solutions — that's trivial. The non-trivial insight: we want to bound the total number of (n,k) pairs across DIFFERENT k such that C(n,k) = a. The p-adic approach is to fix a prime p, consider ALL representations simultaneously, and use the fact that for a given a, the function k ↦ v_p(a·k!) must be compatible with k ↦ v_p(C(n,k)). By Kummer's theorem, v_p(C(n,k)) equals the number of carries when adding k and n−k in base p. For a GIVEN a, and for each possible k, we have the equation v_p(C(n,k)) = v_p(a). The number of carries in base-p addition of k and n−k is a p-adic Lipschitz function in both n and k. The new structural angle: fix a and consider the SET of all (n,k) with C(n,k) = a. For each prime p dividing a, the carry condition must hold. Varying p over all primes dividing a gives a system of simultaneous carry constraints. This is the ADELIC version of the Kummer-Lucas approach that was refuted for ONE prime. The refutation showed that ONE prime class can be exponentially large. But the SIMULTANEOUS constraint from MULTIPLE primes is much stronger: for each prime p|a, the base-p carry pattern must match exactly. Concretely, use Strassman's theorem on power series: for each fixed k, the equation C(n,k) = a has at most k solutions in n (trivial). But the idea is to convert this to a POWER SERIES in k as well. Consider the two-variable analytic function F(n,k) = C(n,k) - a, extended p-adically in both n and k (via the Mahler expansion in k as well). For a fixed a, the set {(n,k): C(n,k)=a} is the zero-set of F. If one can show that F, as a p-adic analytic function in n for each k, has bounded number of zeros UNIFORMLY in k (for k in an appropriate p-adic disc), then one gets a uniform bound. The realistic target: prove that for a given a, there is a prime p (chosen depending on a) such that the p-adic power series expansion of C(n,k) (as a function of n, for each k that could possibly work) has a specific form that Strassman's theorem bounds the number of zeros. The key parameter is the p-adic radius of convergence of the Mahler expansion, which for C(n,k) can be related to the valuation of k!. This approach is different from the refuted Kummer-Lucas approach because: (1) It uses p-adic ANALYSIS (power series, Strassman) rather than just congruences (Lucas's theorem mod p). (2) It treats the equation as a zero-set in a SINGLE variable n for each k, then varies p strategically. (3) The refutation of the Kummer-Lucas approach was about per-prime congruence classes being large; this approach uses power-series ZERO COUNTING, which already gives a polynomial bound per k, and the challenge is making this independent of k.

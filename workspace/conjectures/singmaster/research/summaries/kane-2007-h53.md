## The theorem (verbatim abstract)

Let N(t) = |{(n,m) in Z² : C(n,m) = t}|. Then

    N(t) = O( (log t)(log log log t) / (log log t)^3 ).

This improves Kane 2004's O((log t)(log log log t)/(log log t)^2) by one factor
of log log t. Convention note: N(t) here counts all (n,m), n>1; the bound is
quoted in the growth-of-a sense and does not fix the both-mirrors-vs-half
convention numerically — it is an asymptotic O, not a sharp constant.

## How it works (for the record; the full proof is now on disk)

- Reduce to n > 2m (the mirror (n,n-m) handles the other half; at most one
  solution at n=2m). Extend the binomial to a smooth f via Γ-interpolation and
  count lattice points on its graph.
- Split N(t) = O(D + E + F) by the size of α(x) = log f(x)/log x
  (roughly, how close m is to n):
  - D: n < m·log log t/(24 log log log t)  → O(log t/(log log t)^3)
  - F: n > m·(log log t)^3  → O(log t/(log log t)^3)
  - E: the middle band — the new contribution, bounded via the
    interpolation-polynomial denominator B(m1..mk) (Proposition 2 bounds
    log B = O(S·max(1, log(k²log S / S)))) and Rolle-type Lemma 1 forcing the
    k-th derivative of f to be a nonzero multiple of B.
- Summing over α-dyadic bands gives E = O((log t)(log log log t)/(log log t)^3).

## Self-stated limitation (Section 8) — the part that matters for this run

Kane states his technique CANNOT be pushed to a constant. In the region
α > log log t the k-th derivative argument needs k > α and yields
log(B) > k(log log t), which can be satisfied with S as small as O(k log log t);
so the inverse density of solutions is provable only as O(log log t), giving
Θ(log t/(log log t)^2) candidate m-values that cannot be excluded — hence at most
O(log t/(log log t)^3) solutions by this technique, not O(1). The randomized
construction (k elements of {1..S}) gives log B = Ω(S), so his upper and lower
bounds on log B agree only when k < S^{1/2−ε}.

This corroborates the existing claims `best-unconditional-bound` and
`kane-method-ceiling`: a different mechanism than Kane's is needed for constancy,
and the lattice-point/interpolation method cannot beat the inverse-density wall.

## Convention / contrast with MRSTT

Kane's is a TOTAL, uniform-in-n bound that still grows with t (log-type). MRSTT
is a REGION-restricted constant bound (at most 2 interior solutions in the
interior exp(log^{2/3+eps} n) <= m <= n/2). They are complements: Kane covers
all of N(t) with a growing bound; MRSTT covers the interior with a constant but
leaves the small-m boundary open. Neither touches uniform constancy.

# de Weger 1995 — "Equal binomial coefficients: some elementary considerations" (preprint; PRIMARY, readable)

Source: B.M.M. de Weger, preprint, Erasmus University Rotterdam, August 1995
(`1356_ps.pdf`, repub.eur.nl/pub/1356). This is the **preprint** of the
published paper **de Weger, J. Number Theory 63 (1997) 373–386** — the full
text of the published version is separately held at
`research/sources/deweger-equal-binomial.full.md` (and digested in
`research/summaries/deweger-equal-binomial.md`).

## What it establishes

The paper is elementary (first essentials of algebraic number theory only).

- **Theorem 1**: the only integer solutions of `C(n,3) = C(m,4)` are the
  trivial ones: `(n,m) ∈ {0,1,2}×{0,1,2,3}`, plus `(3,4), (3,−1), (7,7),
  (7,−4)`. Proof: put `X = n−1`, `Y = ½m(m−3)`, giving `Y²+Y = X³−X`;
  Mordell's Theorem 2 supplies all integer solutions of that elliptic curve.
- **Theorem 2 (Mordell 1963)**: the only `(X,Y) ∈ Z²` on `Y²+Y = X³−X` are
  `(X,Y) ∈ {−1,0,1}×{−1,0}` and `(2,2), (2,−3), (6,14), (6,−15)`.
- **Conjecture A**: `C(n,k) = C(m,ℓ)` has no nontrivial solutions beyond the
  known list (six sporadic values + the infinite Fibonacci family). Would imply
  `N(a) ≤ 8` for all `a`, and `N(a) ≤ 6` except `a = 3003` (attained
  infinitely often).
- **Conjecture B**: the only rational solutions of `C(n,3)=C(m,4)` beyond the
  integral ones are `(n,m) = (5/4, 1/2), (5/4, 5/2)`.
- **Theorem 3/4**: the only 2-power-denominator rational solutions of
  `C(n,3)=C(m,4)` (resp. of `Y²+Y=X³−X`) are exactly Conjecture B's points
  (resp. the four extra points `(1/4,−5/8),(1/4,−3/8),(161/16,−2065/64),
  (161/16,2001/64)` added to Mordell's ten). Proved elementarily via
  factorization in the cubic field `K = Q(ϑ)`, `ϑ³−4ϑ+2=0` (discriminant
  148 = 2²·37, trivial class group, unit group generated by `−1+ϑ` and
  `1−2ϑ−ϑ²`), descent to two Thue-type binary-form equations, solved for
  2-power denominators.
- The search record: no other nontrivial solutions of `C(n,k)=C(m,ℓ)` with
  `C(n,k) ≤ 10³⁰` or `max(n,m) ≤ 1000` (few hours on a PC, exact 30-digit
  arithmetic); Singmaster's search went to `2⁴⁸ ≈ 2.8×10¹⁴`.
- Historical/setup: `N(1) = 1, N(2) = 1`, `N(a) ≥ 2` for `a ≥ 3`; every `a≥3`
  occurs at least 4 times including trivial forms; Singmaster 1971 proved
  `N(a) = O(log a)` and conjectured `O(1)`, later `N(a) ≤ 10`; AEH 1974 showed
  average/normal order 2 and `N(a) = O(log a / log log a)`.

## Sourced discrepancy to record — genus of C(n,3)=C(m,4)

- **This 1995 preprint (p. 6) says: the algebraic curve `C(n,3)=C(m,4)` has
  genus 2** ("In the case (k,ℓ)=(3,4) things are different, because the
  algebraic curve ... has genus 2, and thus, by Faltings' work [F], has only
  finitely many rational points").
- **The published 1997 JNT version (p. 376) says: genus 3** (same sentence,
  "has genus 3"). The published value is authoritative, and it agrees with
  the run's computed genus grid (`genus{3,4}=3`), with BST 1999 Theorem 2.2
  (only (2,3),(2,4) have genus 1; (3,4) has genus ≥ 2, in fact 3), and with
  `deweger-genus3-curve`. **The preprint's "genus 2" is an error of that
  version, not a different mathematical fact.** Nothing in the argument
  depends on the genus value (Faltings finiteness holds for genus ≥ 2
  either way).

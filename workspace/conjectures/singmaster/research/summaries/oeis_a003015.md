# OEIS A003015 — Numbers occurring 5 or more times in Pascal's triangle

Source: https://oeis.org/A003015 (filed by OEIS lookup).

## What it establishes for this problem

- Sequence of numbers appearing at least 5 times (both-halves convention):
  `1, 120, 210, 1540, 3003, 7140, 11628, 24310, 61218182743304701891431482520, ...`
- The infinite family of "at least 6 times" numbers is real and starts with 3003,
  then `C(104,39)=C(103,40)=61218182743304701891431482520`. This **independent** second
  source confirms `infinite-family-6` and **confirms 3003 as the first 8-times number**.
- Cross-check against `code/out/witnesses.json`: the six numbers with N>=6 in that
  output (120,210,1540,7140,11628,24310, then 3003 with N=8) match the first terms of
  A003015 exactly. **This is a numerical cross-check of the witness set by an
  independent route (OEIS) — the record family is real.**
- A003016, the adjacent sequence, is the count `N(n)` itself and confirms
  `N(3003)=8` (its "∞,1,2,2,..." list includes 6 and 8 at the right places per
  Wikipedia's quoted sequence).

## Caveats

- OEIS comments are community-maintained annotation, not a peer-reviewed proof.
  The *family existence* is primary-sourced (Singmaster FQ 1975 full text held),
  so the claim rests on the paper, and OEIS only corroborates the specific values.
- No term appears exactly 5 times is open; no number appears >8 times is open.

## Verdict

Corroborating, secondary source. Confirms the record: 3003 is the unique known
8-time occurrence and is the first member of the infinite 6-time family. The
infinite-family statement itself is anchored to Singmaster (1975) and Lind (1968)
full texts in the library.

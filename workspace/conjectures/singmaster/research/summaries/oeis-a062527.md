# OEIS A062527 — smallest number appearing at least n times in Pascal's triangle (catalogue)

Source: https://oeis.org/A062527 (Henry Bottomley 2001).
Full text: `research/sources/oeis-a062527.full.md`.

## What it records

a(n) = smallest number (>1) appearing at least n times in Pascal's triangle
(counting both mirrors and the trivial pair — the entry's example for a(8)
lists all eight of 3003's occurrences):

    2, 3, 6, 10, 120, 120, 3003, 3003

The comment states: "**Singmaster's conjecture is that this sequence is
finite.**" Cross-references A003015, A003016, A006987, A007318, A059233;
the Mathematica program computes cnt[b] for b <= 4000 by brute force and
extracts the first b with cnt >= n.

## Bearing for this run

- Independent catalogue confirmation of the record frame: the smallest t-fold
  values are 2 (twice), 3, 6, 10, then 120 (six times), then 3003 (eight
  times). This exactly matches the run's witness set (3003 N=8; 120 N=6) and
  the "no known N>8/record" framing.
- The Mathematica brute-force method (O(max²) triangle scan up to max=4000) is
  a further independent verification route for the oracle on small values,
  corroborating `code/brute.py` and `oeis-a003016-is-n-a`.
- Contrast with **A180058** (smallest in *exactly* n rows): A062527 is by
  *at-least-n occurrences*, so its a(5)=a(6)=120 and a(7)=a(8)=3003 repeat;
  A180058's "no a(5)" is the (stronger) exactly-n-rows record. The two must
  not be conflated in a claim.

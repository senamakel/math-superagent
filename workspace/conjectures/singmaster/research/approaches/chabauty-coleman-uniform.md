```approach
idea: Chabauty–Coleman uniform rank bound — for the family of curves X_{k1,k2} : C(x,k1) = C(y,k2), prove that the Mordell–Weil rank of the Jacobian is uniformly bounded (independent of k1,k2) by a function of the genus that is small enough for Chabauty–Coleman to give an effective uniform bound on rational/integral points. If the rank r satisfies r < g (the genus) for all pairs beyond a small explicit set, then Coleman's effective Chabauty yields a computable bound on the number of rational points that depends only on g and p (the prime of good reduction used), not on the heights of the coefficients. Since g grows with k1,k2, this would give a uniform bound across the whole family.

mechanism: The curve X_{k1,k2} is the projective closure of the affine equation C(x,k1) = C(y,k2). Multiply through by k1!k2! to get: k2!·x(x-1)...(x-k1+1) = k1!·y(y-1)...(y-k2+1). This is a curve in the (x,y)-plane. Its genus g(k1,k2) = ((k1-1)(k2-1) + 1 - gcd(k1,k2))/2 has been established (claim genus-single-closed-form-all-pairs, checked against 111 computed values).

The Jacobian J_{k1,k2} of this curve has dimension g. By the Mordell–Weil theorem, J(Q) ≅ Z^r ⊕ T where r = rank and T is finite. The curve X itself has a rational point if and only if there is a binomial coincidence — and the known witnesses provide such points.

Chabauty's theorem (1941): If r < g, then X(Q) is finite, and moreover there is an effective method (Coleman 1985) to bound #X(Q) when p > 2g is a prime of good reduction. Specifically, Coleman showed that #X(Q) ≤ #X(F_p) + 2g - 2 under the r < g hypothesis.

The novel angle: The curves X_{k1,k2} are NOT arbitrary curves of genus g. They are defined by an equation with a very specific structure: they are (after compactification) covers of P^1 via both the x-coordinate map and the y-coordinate map. The projection to x gives a map of degree k2; the projection to y gives a map of degree k1. The Jacobian decomposes (up to isogeny) into pieces coming from these projections.

Key structural observation: The curve X_{k1,k2} is birational to the fiber product of two "Fermat-type" curves. Specifically, let F_k(T) = C(T,k) = T(T-1)...(T-k+1)/k!. Then X_{k1,k2} is the fiber product of F_{k1} : A^1 → A^1 and F_{k2} : A^1 → A^1 over the common target. The Jacobian of such a fiber product has been studied — it decomposes into "generalized Jacobians" of the individual maps, plus a "Prym" part.

Conjecture (to be tested computationally): rank(J_{k1,k2}(Q)) is at most something like min(k1,k2) or maybe even just 1 or 2 for all k1,k2. Evidence: the known solutions are sparse. For the (1,1)-shift family (the Pell/infinite family), the curve has genus 0, so the issue doesn't arise. For the next cases — (2,3) genus 1, rank positive to accommodate the known three solutions (120, 1540, 7140), but the rank of the elliptic curve is likely 1. For (3,4) genus 3, the curve covers the Mordell curve Y^2+Y=X^3-X which has rank 1 (de Weger). If in all cases the rank is ≤ 1 while the genus grows, then for large enough k1,k2 we have r < g and Chabauty–Coleman applies uniformly.

The uniform rank bound could come from the fact that the L-function of J_{k1,k2} has a specific analytic form (it's related to products of Gauss sums via the Weil conjectures for the Fermat-type covers). The rank is controlled by the order of vanishing of the L-function at s=1, which in turn is controlled by the number of ways the curve decomposes up to isogeny. A theorem of Stoll (or similar) on uniform rank bounds for curves in families with fixed "type" of bad reduction could apply here.
```

idea: Sylvester's theorem (1892) — a product of k consecutive integers each > k is divisible by a prime p > k — applied to the equal-products reduction of C(x,k1)=C(y,k2).
status: refuted
killed-by: redundancy with SST 1995 / BST 1999. The Saradha-Shorey-Tijdeman equal-products machinery (Acta Arith 68(1) 1995; BST in Number Theory in Progress Vol. 1, 1999) already handles the prime-factor structure of consecutive-integer blocks at much greater power. The SST 1995 proof of Theorem 2 (effective bound for gcd(L,M)>1) works precisely by expressing each side of the equal-products equation as a shifted near-power and applying Baker's linear-forms-in-logarithms — this IS the Sylvester-prime insight carried to its natural conclusion. The BST 1999 genus classification covers all (k1,k2) pairs. Proposing the Sylvester-prime approach is proposing to re-derive a weaker version of results already held in the library; it does not add a new structural angle and does not circumvent the uniformity wall that SST also hits (their constant C2 depends on L/M).
first-step: none — do not re-propose.

```approach
idea: Skolem–Strassman method — extend the binomial coefficient C(n,k) to a p-adic analytic function of n (for fixed k) using Mahler expansions, then solve C(n,k) = a as a p-adic power-series equation. The number of integer solutions is then bounded by the number of p-adic zeros of a convergent power series, which Strassman's theorem bounds by the valuation of the first non-vanishing coefficient.

mechanism: For fixed k, the function f_k(n) = C(n,k) = n(n-1)...(n-k+1)/k! is a polynomial in n of degree k. The equation C(n,k) = a has at most k integer solutions. The proposal's angle is to treat k as a p-adic variable as well, making C(n,k) a two-variable p-adic analytic function, and apply a two-variable Strassman theorem.

status: refuted
killed-by: Three fatal obstructions. (1) For fixed k, Strassman's theorem bounds the number of p-adic zeros of a convergent power series f(n) by the p-adic valuation of the first non-vanishing coefficient. For f(n) = C(n,k) - a (a fixed polynomial in n of degree k), the power series at n=0 is just the polynomial itself, and the first non-vanishing coefficient is the constant term (-a) unless a ≡ 0 (mod p). The bound is at most k (the degree), recovering the trivial root-count bound N(a) = O((log a)²) already known. The proposal's claimed advantage over the per-k trivial bound is illusory: Strassman gives ≤ k zeros for fixed k, and summing over k ≤ log₂ a gives the same O((log a)²) trivial bound. (2) Treating k as a p-adic variable fails: C(n,k) = n(n-1)...(n-k+1)/k! requires k to be a non-negative integer for the falling factorial to be defined; for non-integer p-adic k, the falling factorial n(n-1)...(n-k+1) does not have a p-adically convergent Mahler expansion because the number of terms (k) is not an integer. Even the binomial coefficient extended via Gamma functions, C(n,k) = Γ(n+1)/(Γ(k+1)Γ(n-k+1)), has Γ(k+1) with poles at negative integer k and essential singularities when |k|_p < 1, preventing a globally convergent p-adic power series in k. (3) The refutation of kummer-lucas-p-adic applies equally here: per-prime p-adic information (even from power-series considerations) does not control global integer solutions across different k — a single p-adic class can contain exponentially many (n,k) pairs (row n=2^m-1 for p=2), and passing from congruences to power series does not change the fact that the information is local. No source applies Strassman's theorem or Mahler expansions to C(n,k)=a with varying k; the searches surface only the general p-adic analysis literature (Mahler 1958, Strassman 1928), none addressing equal binomial coefficients.

precedent: _unchecked_ (no source applies p-adic power-series methods to this problem)
first-step: none — Strassman for fixed k recovers the trivial deg-k bound; two-variable p-adic extension in k does not exist with useful convergence; do not re-propose.
```

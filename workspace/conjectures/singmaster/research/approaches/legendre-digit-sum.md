```approach
idea: Legendre-digit-sum adelic carry constraints — use Legendre's formula v_p(C(n,k)) = (s_p(k) + s_p(n-k) - s_p(n))/(p-1) to derive simultaneous constraints on digit-sum functions across all primes dividing a.

status: refuted
killed-by: This is an additive-combinatorial variant of the refuted `kummer-lucas-p-adic` approach. While the simultaneous-prime constraint IS genuinely stronger than the single-prime Lucas case (which was refuted because one p-adic class is exponentially large), the approach provides no mechanism to prove the intersection is O(1). The digit-sum functions s_p(n) for different primes p are NOT independent — they are coupled through the fact that they describe the same integer n — and there is no known additive-combinatorial theorem that bounds the size of the intersection of level sets of digit-sum functions across multiple bases. The claim "intersection cardinality drops exponentially with the number of prime factors" is an unproven heuristic, not a theorem. Moreover, the binary-lucas-submask approach (adopted) already captures the strongest single-prime case (p=2 gives the submask constraint k ⊆ n), and the Legendre approach's extension to all primes is a complexity increase without a new structural insight — more constraints of the same type don't change the fundamental difficulty. The approach is also essentially identical to the `skolem-strassman-p-adic` proposal (which remains unchecked against the literature): both seek to use p-adic constraints across multiple primes to bound the zero-set of the two-variable function C(n,k)-a. The refutation of kummer-lucas-p-adic showed that per-prime congruences are weak; the adelic version needs a proof that the intersection is small, and no such proof has been provided or exists in the digit-sum literature.
precedent: none — the digit-sum mechanism has no theorem supporting the O(1) claim.
first-step: none — do not re-propose.
```

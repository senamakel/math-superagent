```approach
id: erdos-selfridge-structural
idea: Erdős–Selfridge (1975) structural restriction — use the theorem that the product of two or more consecutive positive integers is never a perfect power to restrict which equal-products reductions of C(x,k1)=C(y,k2) can hold. This is a genuinely different structural fact from the algebraic-geometric ones: it operates on the prime-factor exponents of the consecutive-integer blocks rather than on the genus or height.

mechanism: The equal-products reduction of C(x,k1)=C(y,k2) is x(x-1)...(x-k1+1)·k2! = y(y-1)...(y-k2+1)·k1!. Cancel the common factorial part and let the remaining block equation be A·(k2!') = B·(k1!') where A, B are products of k1 and k2 consecutive integers respectively. Now consider what happens if k1 and k2 are both "large" — say both ≥ t. Then each side is a product of t consecutive integers times a factorial. Erdős-Selfridge says that the product of t consecutive integers is never an ℓ-th power for any ℓ ≥ 2 (with t ≥ 2). More precisely, for every k ≥ 2 there is a prime p > k whose exponent in the product (n+1)...(n+k) is 1 — so the product has a prime factor appearing only to the first power. This means the product has "large" squarefree kernel.

The structural insight: if C(x,k1)=C(y,k2) and k1,k2 are both large, then the equal-products reduction forces two large consecutive-integer blocks to have the same squarefree kernel (up to the factorial factors), which by Erdős-Selfridge forces the blocks to overlap or align in a very restricted way. This is NOT the Sylvester-prime approach (which was refuted as redundant with SST/BST) because it uses the exponent-1 prime (not just existence of a prime > k) and the alignment-of-blocks structure to classify possible equalities by their prime support, not their genus.

status: refuted
killed-by: The mechanism's engine is the claim "for every k ≥ 2 there is a prime p > k whose exponent in (n+1)...(n+k) is exactly 1." The held primary (Erdős–Selfridge, Illinois J. Math. 19 (1975) 292–301, Theorem 2 — full text at research/sources/erdos-selfridge-product-not-power-1975.full.md) proves only: for k ≥ 3, l ≥ 2, n+k ≥ p(k) (p(k) the least prime ≥ k), there is a prime p ≥ k whose exponent in (n+1)...(n+k) is NOT a multiple of l. The stronger statement "there is a prime p > k dividing the product to the FIRST power" is stated in the same paper to be a CONJECTURE: "One could conjecture the following strengthening of Theorem 2: if k ≥ 4 and n+k ≥ p(k), then there is at least one prime greater than k which divides (n+1)...(n+k) to the first power. This conjecture, if true, seems very deep." It remains open (Erdős, Rocky Mountain J. Math. 15 (1985) 353–363, restates it as a hypothesis, not a theorem). Numerical falsification of the premise: for k=2, n=7, the product 8·9 = 72 = 2³·3² has NO prime > 2 to exponent 1 (3 appears with exponent 2). The theorem itself only yields an exponent not divisible by l — it is the Sylvester–Schur occurrence of a prime p > k (Sylvester 1892, Schur 1929), one prime with some exponent, not a first-power prime. So the approach does not rest on a provable Erdős–Selfridge statement; the classification-by-prime-support lemma proposed ("if both blocks are large they must align") is unsupported: the witnesses show the opposite — C(15,5)=C(14,6)=3003 has the large primes 7,11,13 — all exceeding max(5,6) — appearing inside BOTH blocks (360360 = 2³·3²·5·7·11·13; 2162160 = 2⁴·3³·5·7·11·13), i.e. the "exponent-1 alignment" never forces contradiction. Moreover the equal-products machinery that CAN say something here is already held: Saradha–Shorey–Tijdeman 1995 (effective when gcd(L,M)>1, claim sst-effective-shared-factor) and Beukers–Shorey–Tijdeman 1999 (ineffective finiteness, claim bst-fixed-kl-ineffective-primary) — the same exhaustion of the block structure that closed sylvester-prime-machine. No source applies the exponent-1 prime guarantee to Singmaster; no literature supports an alignment classification.
```

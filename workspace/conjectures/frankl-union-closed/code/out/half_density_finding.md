## Numbered results

1. **Max-density-1/2 characterization holds exhaustively at n=1..5** (checked,
   `half_density_verify.py`):
   every nonempty union-closed F on [n] with max element density **exactly
   1/2** is a **Boolean subalgebra** (block-partition family): all present
   elements at density 1/2, |F| = 2^k for some k ≥ 1, and F is closed under
   symmetric difference. Counts H(1..5) = 1, 4, 14, 51, 202 = **Bell(n+1) − 1**,
   refined by |F| = 2^k into **S(n+1, k+1)** (Stirling; e.g. n=5:
   31, 90, 65, 15, 1 across k = 1..5, sum 202). Atom check: the minimal
   nonempty members are pairwise disjoint atoms, every ground element lies in
   exactly one atom (density 3/4 would otherwise follow, by the derivation in
   `block_union_atom_check.py`), and F is exactly the union-semilattice
   generated by them.

2. **The coordinate-wise statement is FALSE** (checked; `half_density_complement.py`):
   non-Boolean UC families with *some* element at density 1/2 exist: 2 at n=2,
   42 at n=3, 1818 at n=4, 752,255 at n=5 — all with an element strictly above
   1/2 (the max-density characterization is the true statement, not the
   coordinate-wise one). Witness family F = {∅, {1,2}, {1,3}, {1,2,3}} on [3]
   is union-closed, elements 2 and 3 each appear in 2/4 sets, direct xor check
   shows it is not a Boolean subalgebra. `half_density_complement.py`'s own
   docstring asserted the false coordinate-wise claim; the executed output
   contains the refutation.

3. **The n=6 G-shape subclass** (checked; exhaustive over 7581 up-sets):
   exactly two boundary families with max density 1/2 — |F|=64 (the full cube
   2^[6]) and |F|=32 (a Boolean subalgebra) — **none non-Boolean**. The
   characterization holds throughout this structured slice of n=6, with the
   boundary at the cube and its Boolean subfamilies.

4. **Random closure probes** (checked; 600,000 families): zero boundary
   (max density exactly 1/2) families among 400,000 random union-closures on
   [6] and 200,000 on [7]; hence zero non-Boolean candidates. Union-closed
   families with an element at exactly half density appear to be rare past
   n=5 in the random-construction model, and the structured cube-hull family
   is the main route to them.

## What each settles

- Fulfils the phase-4 loop on the GOAL.md abundance-profile front: the
  previously-no-capture programs (the run's best unexecuted candidates) are
  executed, and the characterization they encoded is stated exactly, with its
  counterexample-hunt (48 witnesses at n=3; exhaustive n=4; structured n=6;
  random n=6,7) on record.
- Distinguishes the two statements one could mean by "half-density families":
  the max-density statement is a clean theorem (Bell/Stirling/Boolean-algebra),
  the coordinate-wise statement is false with explicit witnesses.
- Pins the boundary: any counterexample to the max-density characterization
  must appear first at n ≥ 6 (exhaustive n≤5), and the n=6 structured probe
  suggests the boundary families are exactly the Boolean subalgebras on the
  cube-like families — the frontier the claim's ceiling names.

## Ceiling (state what is not known)

None of this is a proof for arbitrary n. Exhaustive n≤5 (2.7M families) is
complete; n=6 is **not** exhaustive — only the G-shape subclass (7581
families) and 400k random closures; n=7 only random. The characterization
"max density = 1/2 ⟹ F a Boolean subalgebra" remains a **verified
computational conjecture** for general n, with an elementary proof of the
atom-partition structure only for the |F| = 2^k regular case (that proof is in
`block_union_atom_check.py`'s derivation and passed on all half-density
families through n=5).

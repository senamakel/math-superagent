# Literature grounding for three proposed approaches

Research pass: took the three `proposed` approaches (lcm-monomial-algebra,
polynomial-method-nullstellensatz, fkq-correlation) to the literature. Verdict:
one grounded as a reformulation (lcm-monomial-algebra), two refuted on evidence
(polynomial-method-nullstellensatz, fkq-correlation). The memory server was
down for the whole pass, so these claims are the durable record.

```claim
id: defranco-boolean-polynomial-encoding
statement: For m subsets of an n-universe, a Boolean polynomial ICC_{m,n}(X) is
  constructed (in the equivalent Intersection-Closed form) such that the
  Union-Closed Conjecture is true for (m,n) iff ICC_{m,n}(X) is the zero Boolean
  polynomial.
hypotheses: finite m, n; the ICC encoding of the intersection-closed dual;
  arbitrary union-closed families. (An exact iff-encoding, restated, not a proof
  of the conjecture.)
holds-here: yes
status: asserted
bearing: This IS the polynomial/Nullstellensatz small-n-certificate mechanism.
  It refutes the novelty of polynomial-method-nullstellensatz as a route to UC —
  the encoding is prior art (DeFranco) and stops short, proving nothing new.
anchor: https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2606.26191; research/approaches/polynomial-method-nullstellensatz.md
```

```claim
id: chen-de-li-nadimpalli-servedio-uc-hard-to-test
statement: The property of being a union-closed family (a function
  f:{0,1}^n->{0,1} whose satisfying assignments are a union-closed family) is
  information-theoretically hard to test: non-adaptive two-sided epsilon-testers
  require n^Omega(log(1/epsilon)) queries, in sharp contrast to monotonicity
  (which is easy to test).
hypotheses: Boolean-function property testing; union-closedness over {0,1}^n.
holds-here: yes
status: asserted
bearing: A strong caution against expecting any short degree/coefficient
  algebraic witness that decides union-closure cheaply at scale; complements
  the refutation of the polynomial/Nullstellensatz route (small-n only).
anchor: https://doi.org/10.4230/lipics.itcs.2024.33 (Chen–De–Li–Nadimpalli–Servedio, ITCS 2024); research/approaches/polynomial-method-nullstellensatz.md
```

```claim
id: gpw-lcm-lattice-determines-resolution
statement: For a monomial ideal I minimally generated by monomials m1..md in
  S = k[x1..xn], the lcm-lattice L_I (the poset of lcms of the generators ordered
  by divisibility) determines (a) the Betti numbers of S/I, via the homology of
  L_I (Thm 2.1), and (b) the minimal free resolution of S/I up to relabeling
  (Thm 3.3).
hypotheses: I any monomial ideal in a polynomial ring over a field; L_I finite.
holds-here: yes
status: asserted
bearing: Under the monomial bijection a union-closed family (with emptyset, V in
  F) is exactly an lcm-closed set of monomials whose lcm-lattice IS the family's
  union-structure. This grounds the lcm-monomial-algebra reformulation (status:
  grounded); GPW/Betti machinery has never been aimed at the UC abundance
  question.
anchor: https://doi.org/10.4310/mrl.1999.v6.n5.a5 (Gasharov–Peeva–Welker, Math.
  Res. Lett. 6 (1999)); research/approaches/lcm-monomial-algebra.md
```

```claim
id: abundance-not-in-single-variable-hilbert-series
statement: The abundance count delta(i) = #{A in F : i in A} is multigraded
  (Z^n-graded) data and is NOT a coefficient of the single-variable Hilbert
  series of k[x]/I_F, which collapses total degree and counts the complement of
  F in degree layers. If visible at all algebraically it lives in the multigraded
  Betti table.
hypotheses: monomial ideal encoding of a family F; standard Z^n-grading.
holds-here: yes
status: asserted
bearing: Technical correction to the lcm-monomial-algebra mechanism as written
  ("read delta(i) off the Hilbert series" is wrong); the first step should
  compute the multigraded Betti table, not the Hilbert series. Does not refute
  the line, only a sentence of it.
anchor: standard commutative algebra (Stanley–Reisner / multigraded resolutions);
  reasoning in research/approaches/lcm-monomial-algebra.md
```

```claim
id: ad-conclusion-is-aggregate-not-per-element
statement: Ahlswede–Daykin's four-functions theorem (if alpha(A)beta(B) <=
  gamma(A u B) delta(A n B) for all A,B in P(n) then (sum alpha)(sum beta) <=
  (sum gamma)(sum delta)) has an AGGREGATE Sigma-product (correlation/overlap)
  conclusion: it bounds the product of total masses, not any per-element
  membership count delta(x). No conversion from this aggregate shape to a
  per-element forcing "delta(x) <= |G|/2 for some x" exists in the literature.
hypotheses: non-negative alpha,beta,gamma,delta on a distributive lattice.
holds-here: yes
status: asserted
bearing: Refutes fkq-correlation as a route to UC: AD cannot force the per-element
  abundance bound, and its only UC application (the average-size/Reimer route,
  Balla–Bollobás–Eccles) is known not to force abundance (Raz; Lu–Raz), and the
  aggregate shape collapses to the overlap method Ellis refutes.
anchor: The Probabilistic Method 2nd ed. ch.6; Christofides arXiv:0909.5137;
  research/approaches/fkq-correlation.md
```

```claim
id: ad-uc-application-is-avg-size-route-known-not-to-force-abundance
statement: The only application of the AD/FKG-averaging engine to union-closed
  families in the literature is through the average-set-size / Reimer direction
  (Balla–Bollobás–Eccles, JCTA 2013, determining minimum average size and
  proving UC for m >= (2/3)2^n). Raz (2017) and Lu–Raz (arXiv:2405.10639) show
  the Reimer/average-size conditions that route feeds do NOT imply the >= 1/2
  abundance conclusion.
hypotheses: union-closed families; the AD/averaging-to-average-size reduction.
holds-here: yes
status: asserted
bearing: The specific "four-functions -> abundance" direction is implicitly
  already tried (as AD/averaging) and documented to stop short; closes the
  fkq-correlation route on evidence.
anchor: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2012.10.005; https://doi.org/10.37236/6989
  (Raz); https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2405.10639 (Lu–Raz);
  research/approaches/fkq-correlation.md
```

# The $11$-element case of Frankl's conjecture

- Ivica Bošnjak (University of Novi Sad)
- Petar Marković (University of Novi Sad)

Published 2008-07-06, *The Electronic Journal of Combinatorics*, 15(1), #R88.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.37236/812

## Abstract

In 1979, P. Frankl conjectured that in a finite union-closed family
$\mathcal{F}$ of finite sets, $\mathcal{F}\neq\{\emptyset\}$, there has to be an
element that belongs to at least half of the sets in $\mathcal{F}$. We prove
this when $|\bigcup \mathcal{F}|\leq 11$.

## Note

The full text PDF is at
https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v15i1r88/pdf .
This paper proves the union-closed sets conjecture for all union-closed
families whose ground set (the union of all member sets) has at most 11
elements. Because of the Lo Faro / Roberts–Simpson lemma (a counterexample on
an $m$-element ground set has at least $4m-1$ member sets, cf.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.3297 Lemma 18), together with this result one obtains
that every union-closed family with at most 40 member sets satisfies the
conjecture. The abstract here fixes the exact verified range: **$|\bigcup
\mathcal{F}| \le 11$**.

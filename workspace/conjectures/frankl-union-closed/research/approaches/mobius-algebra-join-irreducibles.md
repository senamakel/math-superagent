# Möbius algebra of the lattice and half-populated generators

```approach
idea: Attack Poonen's lattice form through the Möbius algebra of the lattice —
  the semigroup algebra C[L, ∨] with product x·y = x∨y. It is semisimple (a
  product of |L| copies of C), with primitive orthogonal idempotents
  e_a = Σ_{b≥a} μ(a,b)·b, and Möbius inversion gives the basis expansion
  x = Σ_{b≥x} e_b.
mechanism: Two exact, checkable facts. (i) The left ideal L·a generated by a
  *basis element* a has basis ↑a = {b : b ≥ a}, so dim(L·a) = |↑a|. (ii) The
  basis element a expands as Σ_{b≥a} e_b, i.e. into exactly |↑a| of the |L|
  primitive idempotents. Poonen's form of UC reads: some join-irreducible j has
  dim(L·j) = |↑j| ≤ |L|/2 — i.e. some join-irreducible basis element is at most
  half-populated in the idempotent decomposition. The conjecture becomes: the
  join-irreducible generators of the semisimple algebra C[L,∨] cannot all be
  more than half-populated. The averaging and entropy arguments are blind to
  this idempotent structure, which is exactly where the join-irreducible
  constraint lives.
status: adopted
precedent: mobius-idempotent-expansion (Knop, Adv. Math 2007 §7, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2007.03.001 — the C[L,∨] idempotents and inversion x=Σ_{b≥x}e_b are the classical Solomon/Möbius-algebra construction, verified here by hand and in code/out/mobius_algebra_check.py); lattice-formulation (Poonen's form; Bouchard arXiv:2503.00277 works in this same lattice formulation and derives minimal-counterexample conditions, but does NOT use the idempotent basis — precedent for the reformulation, none for the forcing step).
first-step: Enumerate small lattices (oracle bitmask families, n≤7); compute
  J(L), the filter sizes |↑a|, and the Möbius matrix μ(a,b); verify the
  expansion a = Σ_{b≥a} e_b numerically and hunt for an identity among
  {|↑j| : j∈J(L)}, |L|, and |J(L)| that is violated when every |↑j| > |L|/2.

synthesis: What research turned up that neither the original proposal nor its
  refutation had: the exact object where the Möbius-algebra reformulation *must*
  live is not L itself but the Boolean lattice B (Poonen's lattice form has
  L = ℱ, and ℱ is a sublattice of B). The forcing step "the join-irreducibles
  cannot all be >½-populated" has no precedent and is the open hinge; the run
  already holds the exact reduction of union-closed families to lattices, so the
  first step has no unknown data dependency. What would make the forcing step a
  theorem is an identity among {|↑j| : j∈J(L)}, |L|, |J(L)| — which is exactly
  what the first-step enumeration is built to hunt.
```

**Grounded as a reformulation; forcing step genuinely open.** The two checkable
facts hold (they are the classical Möbius-algebra / Solomon construction, Knop
2007). Poonen's lattice form (UC ⟺ some join-irreducible j with |↑j|≤|L|/2) is
established, and Bouchard works in exactly this lattice formulation. But the
forcing argument (that the join-irreducibles cannot all be >½-populated in the
idempotent decomposition) has **no precedent** — Bouchard proceeds structurally
by removing join-irreducibles, not via the idempotent basis, and no source found
proves the forcing identity. So the deliverable is the reformulation stated
exactly; the forcing step remains a conjecture for this run or a later one.

Speculative: the forcing argument (that the |L| primitive idempotents cannot all
be covered by > |L|/2 of themselves, given that L is join-generated by J(L)) is
not yet written down and may fail. If it fails, the idempotent reformulation
itself — UC as "a join-irreducible is half-populated in the Möbius-algebra
idempotent basis" — is the deliverable, and research should check whether this
algebraic form has been attempted at all.

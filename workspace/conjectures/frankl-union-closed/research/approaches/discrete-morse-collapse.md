```approach
idea: Chari–Forman discrete Morse theory — acyclic matchings on the GRADED face/chain poset of the family — with abundance recounted as signed collapsed-cell numbers up to an explicit Morse matching.

mechanism: The topological attempts so far (independence complexes of the graph form; Bhasin's cubical-set acyclicity of a simply-rooted family) all ran against ONE obstruction recorded in this ledger: independence complexes of bipartite graphs are NON-PURE, so any Euler-characteristic identity is an alternating sum that washes the per-vertex abundance out. Discrete Morse theory defeats exactly that obstruction, because we run it on a DIFFERENT, always-PURE object: the Boolean lattice 2^[n] is graded, and the family F (with ∅ and its union-closure) is a graded sub-poset whose face/chain complex is pure by construction. Acyclic matchings on a graded poset (Chari, "A discrete Morse theory for posets") produce a cellular collapse with basis elements in bijection, and — crucially — the collapse can be REQUIRED to respect element classes: each matched pair {A, A∪{x}} cancels a cell of even/odd dimension and moves one unit of "x-membership" between the down- and up-sides. Then |F_x| − |F|/2 becomes a signed count of unmatched (critical) cells weighted by x-membership, and the question "is some x abundant" is exactly "does some coordinate's critical-cell signed sum come out ≥ 0." This is a genuinely different object from every closed line: it is combinatorial collapse / matching on a pure complex, not homology (independence-complex, refuted), not an algebra double-count (Möbius algebra, adopted), not a shadow/LYM bound (complement, refuted). The falsifier to hunt first: does the signed critical-cell sum, like the Euler characteristic it generalises, wash out to a parity identity that forces nothing (the exact way the independence-complex line died)? That would refute the mechanism; if instead the element-class-respecting matching cannot be "balanced" without some coordinate's critical weight going to zero, that is a rigidity statement with real content.

status: proposed

first-step: With the canonical oracle (code/lib/uc.py), for every union-closed family on n ≤ 5 compute the graded face poset of F, and run a greedy acyclic matching that pairs sets {A, A∪{x}} while tracking the x-membership weight of each matched pair (a sign (−1)^{|A|} per pair). Report, per family and per element x, the critical-cell signed sum Σ_{critical A} (−1)^{|A|}·1{x∈A} and compare it to |F_x| − |F|/2. Decide: is there a family where NO element-class-respecting acyclic matching separates a coordinate (all critical sums tied)? That is the rigidity falsifier. Run the three negative controls: 2^[n] must give every coordinate exactly tied (1/2 attained); a non-union-closed family must show the collapse cannot be defined by union-closure; finiteness is used because the matching is finite. Confirm the object's far behaviour against the (3−√5)/2 extremal layer families to see how far the signed count separates from an alternating washout.
```

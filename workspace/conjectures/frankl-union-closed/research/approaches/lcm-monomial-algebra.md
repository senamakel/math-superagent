# lcm-monomial-algebra — the monomial-ideal / GPW-Betti reformulation

## Verdict: **adopted** — the only grounded candidate of the round (GPW is real and the lcm-lattice IS the ∪-structure); the forcing hinge is the open first step. Research's one technical correction (δ(i) is multigraded, in the multigraded Betti table, not the single-variable Hilbert series) is folded into the first step below.

```approach
idea: Commutative-algebra reformulation. Under the monomial bijection A ↦ x^A =
  ∏_{i∈A} x_i, a union-closed family F (with ∅, V ∈ F) is exactly a set of
  monomials in k[x_1,…,x_n] containing 1 and closed under lcm — a
  sub-join-semilattice of the divisibility lattice that is closed upward under
  duals. Attach to F the monomial ideal I_F it generates and its Alexander dual,
  and read abundance off the Hilbert series / the minimal free resolution. The
  named theorem is Gasharov–Peeva–Welker: the minimal free resolution (Betti
  numbers, Hilbert series) of a monomial ideal is a function of its lcm-lattice
  — here exactly the family's ∪-structure — together with Alexander duality
  (Miller–Sturmfels). The distinctive point against the already-adopted Möbius
  semigroup algebra C[L,∨]: that approach works in the *semigroup algebra of the
  lattice* and its orthogonal idempotents; this one works in the *quotient ring
  k[x]/I_F and its free resolution / Hilbert function*, a different algebra whose
  invariants have never been tied to the abundance ≥ 1/2 question.
mechanism: δ(i) = #{A ∈ F : i ∈ A} is the number of monomials of F divisible by
  x_i, so the abundance vector is a derivative of the monomial-count
  (Hilbert) generating function. On the other hand the lcm-closure means the
  "primitive"/minimal monomials of I_F (join-irreducibles of the lcm-lattice)
  generate the whole resolution, and GPW pins the Betti numbers to that
  lcm-lattice. A counterexample (all δ(i) < m/2, m = |F|) is a statement that
  every variable divides fewer than half the monomials of an lcm-closed set —
  equivalently that the *Alexander-dual* complex has every vertex in more than
  half of ... , a parity/shifting condition topological rather than
  moment/entropy. The hope: an exact algebra identity (Hilbert-series or Betti
  inequality forced by lcm-closure) that forces max_i δ(i) ≥ m/2, of a kind the
  adopted Möbius semigroup algebra does not see because it never moves to the
  quotient ring/resolution. Marked speculative: the honest, checkable content is
  whether any algebraic invariant of I_F distinguishes the abundant variable.
status: adopted
precedent: gpw-lcm-lattice (Gasharov–Peeva–Welker, "The LCM-lattice in monomial
  resolutions", Math. Res. Lett. 6 (1999), doi:10.4310/mrl.1999.v6.n5.a5 — Thm 2.1
  and Thm 3.3: the lcm-lattice L_I determines the Betti numbers of S/I via the
  homology of L_I, and determines the minimal free resolution up to relabeling);
  eagon-reiner-alexander-dual (Eagon–Reiner, "Resolutions of Stanley-Reisner
  rings and Alexander duality", J. Pure Appl. Algebra 130 (1998),
  doi:10.1016/S0022-4049(97)00097-2); miller-alexander-duality (Miller,
  "Alexander Duality for Monomial Ideals and Their Resolutions", arXiv math/9812095);
  techernev-varisco-betti-poset (Tchernev–Varisco, Proc. AMS 2015,
  doi:10.1090/proc/12643 — Betti poset generalises the lcm-lattice);
  peeva-resolutions-lattices (Peeva, Homology Homotopy Appl. 2002,
  doi:10.4310/hha.2002.v4.n2.a20). None of these is aimed at the union-closed
  abundance question.
first-step: With the canonical oracle, for each union-closed family on n ≤ 5
  (∅,V ∈ F), (1) write I_F and its Alexander dual I_F^∨; (2) compute the
  MULTIGRADED Betti table β_{i,α}(S/I_F) by exact Gröbner/linear algebra (sympy,
  degree reverse-lex, n ≤ 4 first) — research's correction: δ(i) is multigraded
  per-variable data and lives here, NOT as a coefficient of the single-variable
  Hilbert series; (3) test whether max_i δ(i) − m/2 and each variable's
  divisibility count is visible as a multigraded Betti number or as homology of
  an open interval of the lcm-lattice (which GPW pins the Betti table to); and
  test the three negative controls (2^[n] must give every δ(i) = m/2 exactly
  with the Boolean lcm-lattice; a non-union-closed family breaks lcm-closure so
  I_F's resolution is NOT pinned by the family's ∪-structure; finiteness via
  m ≤ 2^n). If no multigraded Betti invariant tracks abundance for n ≤ 5, say
  so — that is itself a negative result bounding this route.
```

## What the literature establishes

**The named theorem is real, its hypotheses hold here, and the link to the
family is exact.** Gasharov–Peeva–Welker (Math. Res. Lett. 6, 1999, doi:10.4310/mrl.1999.v6.n5.a5) prove exactly what the candidate claims: for a monomial ideal I minimally generated by monomials m₁,…,m_d in S = k[x₁,…,x_n], the **lcm-lattice** L_I (the poset of all lcms of the generators, ordered by divisibility) determines both the Betti numbers of S/I (Thm 2.1, via the homology of L_I) and the minimal free resolution up to relabeling (Thm 3.3). Under the monomial bijection A ↦ x^A, a union-closed family F containing ∅ and V is precisely a set of monomials closed under lcm, and lcm-divisibility is exactly set-union. So **the lcm-lattice of I_F IS the ∪-structure of F** (with atoms = monomials of the basis / join-irreducibles, join = ∪). The reformulation is therefore a genuine, exact commutative-algebraic translation, not a loose analogy — in sharp contrast to the refuted Gram/spectral "max-closed" candidate, which collapsed to a restatement precisely because coordinatewise-max over {0,1} is set union (that candidate's error was thinking the *object* gave new information; here the object is different — the quotient ring and its resolution, not the semigroup algebra C[L,∨] that the adopted Möbius line uses).

Alexander duality is likewise real and working in the same direction this candidate proposes: Eagon–Reiner (JPAA 1998) for squarefree ideals (Betti numbers of k[Δ] via homology of links of the Alexander-dual complex Δ*), and Miller (arXiv math/9812095) generalising Alexander duality to arbitrary monomial ideals with Bass–Betti relations. These confirm the "dual object" half of the mechanism.

**A technical error in the stated mechanism (flagged, not fatal to the whole line).** The candidate says "read δ(i) off the Hilbert series." The **single-variable Hilbert series** of k[x]/I_F counts monomials by total degree only; it collapses all per-variable divisibility data and counts the complement of F in degree layers. δ(i) is **multigraded** data (the Z^n-graded count of monomials divisible by x_i). So δ(i) is NOT a coefficient of the ordinary Hilbert series; if it is visible anywhere algebraic it is in the **multigraded Betti table** β_{i,α}(S/I_F), which GPW pins to the lcm-lattice. The first step should therefore compute the multigraded Betti table, not the Hilbert series. This is a correction to the mechanism as written, not a refutation of the line: the forcing hinge (does any multigraded Betti number or homology of an open interval of the lcm-lattice distinguish the abundant variable?) survives.

**No source applies GPW/Betti machinery to the UC abundance ≥ 1/2 question.** Searched (GPW, Betti, lcm-lattice, Alexander dual, Hilbert series × union-closed/Frankl/abundance): the commutative-algebra literature is dense (GPW, Miller, Peeva, Tchernev–Varisco, Clark) but none of it touches the abundance question. The run's own adopted approaches (Möbius semigroup algebra C[L,∨], knop idempotents, instead of the Gröbner/Stanley–Reisner quotient bracket). So the *reformulation* is grounded and genuinely distinct from the adopted Möbius algebra; the forcing step is open and this run (or a later one) would be the first to try it. Note that the near-relatives (Stanley–Reisner rings of simplicial complexes via independence complexes) were already examined and set aside in the independence-complex thread — that thread died on non-purity of the complexes, not on the algebra itself, and this candidate's quotient-ring object is different from an independence-complex Stanley–Reisner ring.

## What it would buy

A genuinely new algebra (quotient ring / free resolution) whose invariants have never been tied to abundance. If any multigraded Betti number, interval-homology of the lcm-lattice, or Alexander-dual complex statistic provably forces max_i δ(i) ≥ m/2 under lcm-closure, that is a proof of UC of a kind neither entropy (capped at ~0.382) nor averaging (fails: CMS/Reimer–Raz) nor moment (Ellis refutes) reaches. The exact, small-n first step is cheap and decidable.

## Attacked before trusting

The hinge is the forcing identity; hunt the counterexample in the small-n enumeration first (does any small UC family have an lcm-lattice whose Betti data would *not* distinguish the abundant variable?). Also confirm by exact computation that the multigraded Betti table, restricted to the lcm-lattice's join-irreducibles, is actually sensitive to δ(i) — if it is not, the whole algebraic reading of abundance is dead before any inequality.

## Negative controls

2^[n]: every δ(i) = m/2 exactly, and the lcm-lattice is the Boolean lattice. A non-union-closed family: lcm-closure breaks, so I_F's lcm-lattice is NOT the family's ∪-structure and the GPW pinning fails — the negative control uses closure genuinely. Finiteness: m ≤ 2^n bounds all computations.

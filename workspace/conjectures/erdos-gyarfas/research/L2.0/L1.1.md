# L1.1 — seal: cycle-length toolkit, P10 line, density engine, restricted closures

Seal of `research/L1.1/` (ten digests; full texts in `L0.1/`, one in `L0.0/`). One file is a
*misfiled* capture (cosmology, see below), so ten carry **nine** usable items. This batch is the
run's engine room: the cycle-length machinery (admissible/consecutive/mod-k), the restricted
classes closed by path-freeness and diameter, the density engine (Liu–Montgomery), the
verification anchor (Markström), and two one-result closures (Cayley, 2-power-unicyclic cubic).
`holds-here` = whether a theorem's hypotheses are met by *this* problem (true theorem whose
hypotheses fail here is worse than none).

## 1. Cycle-length machinery any EG attack reaches for

### 1a. Admissible cycles — Gao, Huo, Liu & Ma, arXiv:1904.08126 [[gao-huo-liu-ma-unified]]
Core toolkit. Definitions: a length set is **consecutive** (1-AP) or **admissible** (1-AP or
2-AP); a family of cycles is so if the set of their lengths is. One method proves: Thomassen
(min degree k+1 ⇒ cycles of all even lengths mod k; if 2-connected non-bipartite, all lengths
mod k), Dean (k-connected ⇒ length 0 mod k), Bondy–Vince tight (3-connected non-bipartite min
degree k+1 ⇒ k consecutive-length cycles), chromatic (χ≥k+2 ⇒ k consecutive-length cycles),
and **Thm 1.3 (the workhorse): min degree k+1 ⇒ k admissible cycles**.
*Holds here:* set **k=2, δ=3 ⇒ two admissible cycles** (lengths 1 or 2 apart) — the *sharpest
unconditional length guarantee at the conjecture's own degree*. k=2 mod-k gives even cycles
(weak, free). *Caveat:* 1-AP/2-AP lengths may still sit between powers of two; the
interval-spans-factor-of-two obstruction (MEMORY) means they force a 2-power only when the gap
spans a factor of two, which δ=3 pairs do not in general.

### 1b. Consecutive lengths — Gao & Ma, arXiv:1902.05701 [[gao-ma-bondy-vince]]
Confirms the **Bondy–Vince conjecture**: for every k≥0, every graph with at most k vertices of
degree <3 (except K1, K2) contains two cycles differing by 1 or 2. Structural block-decomposition
proof (B(H)=k, no-good-pair Claims 1–4).
*Holds here:* **k=0 is exactly δ≥3 ⇒ two cycles of consecutive length** — the same gap guarantee
as §1a at k=2. Not a 2-power by itself; same obstruction.

### 1c. Mod-k lengths; trigonal/tetragonal — Luo, Ma & Zhao, arXiv:2601.13552 [[luo-ma-zhao-dean]]
**Dean's conjecture** (min degree ≥k ⇒ length 0 mod k) resolved for **k≥6**: min degree ≥k ⇒
cycles of length r (mod k) for every even r, unless every end-block ∈ {K_{k+1},K_{k,k}}∪H_k
(fail only for ≡2 mod k); Thm 1.4: k≥7 ⇒ k admissible cycles with same exception. Framework:
**trigonal** (non-bipartite outer-planar w/ Hamiltonian ∂T; Prop 3.2: vertices u,v at ∂T-distance
d have [d, t−d] ⊆ L^T_{u,v}) and **tetragonal** (bipartite, |T|=2m; Prop 3.5: {d,d+2,…,2m−d} ⊆
L^T_{u,v}) sparse families controlling path/cycle lengths.
*Holds here:* **k≥6 is above δ=3** — this theorem misses EG; what transfers is the sparse
trigonal/tetragonal length-control framework, potentially reusable in the sparse predominantly-
cubic regime. Not a competitor to §1a: mod-k and admissible give complementary length info; keep both.

## 2. Restricted classes settled (do not re-derive)

### 2a. Diameter 2 — Carr, arXiv:2508.19302 [[carr-diameter2]]
**Thm 1.1:** every diameter-2 δ≥3 graph has a cycle of length **4 or 8** — EG holds for the
class (strongest here: C4 guaranteed). Hostile deep case analysis (Claims 1.x, 2.x) ruling out a
4-free-and-8-free diameter-2 δ≥3 graph via common neighbours forced by diam=2.
*Holds here:* another closed class off the search list. *Caveat:* *dense* class; the open regime
is sparse, so this closure is informative but not a stepping stone into the hard case. (Not the
same Carr as [[carr-minimal-counterexample-cubic]].)

### 2b. P10-free — Hu & Shen, arXiv:2308.05675 [[hu-shen-p10-free]]
**Thm 1.1:** every P10-free δ≥3 graph has a **C4 or C8**. Method: 4-free δ≥3 ⇒ m-hole m≥5 (Lem
2.2); good-hole machinery bounds inner chords (|IC|≤7−m, Lem 2.4), finds good paths off a good
hole (Lems 2.5–2.6), Claims 3.x rule out holes with neither C4 nor C8, ending in IC=∅ (Claim 3.10).
*Holds here:* one step of the **Pk-free progression** P8→P10→P13 ([[gao-shan-p8-free]],
[[hegde-sandeep-shashank-p13-free]]). *Caveat:* P-freeness is hereditary and strong; the open
δ=3 ground is not Pk-free. The hole/good-path technique is the transferable part.

### 2c. Cayley graphs — Ghaffari & Mostaghim, Aequationes Math., 10.1007/s00010-017-0518-3 [[ghaffari-mostaghim-cayley]]
EG holds for Cayley graphs on generalized quaternion, dihedral, semidihedral groups, and groups
of order p³. Only a subscription-preview *abstract* is local — claim held, proof not. Cayley
families are dense, regular, far from the open case.

### 2d. MISFILED — do not read expecting graph theory [[ghaffari-mostaghim-cayley-pdf]]
URL 1610.08176 is a 2016 **astrophysics** review (cosmological radio-source isotropy), not the
Cayley paper. No bearing on EG; the correct Cayley source is §2c.

## 3. Density engine and verification evidence

### 3a. Liu & Montgomery, arXiv:2010.15802 [[liu-montgomery-odd-cycle]]
**Thm 1.1:** some d0: avg degree d≥d0 ⇒ for some ℓ≥d/(10 log¹² d), C(G) contains every even
integer in **[log⁸ℓ, ℓ]**. **Cor 1.3:** any infinite even σ_i with σ_{i+1}≤exp(σ_i^{1/10}) and
avg degree ≥max{d0,σ₁²} has some σ_i as a cycle length. For large d the even interval spans far
more than a factor of two, so it contains a power of two — **large average degree forces a
2-power cycle**, disproving the proposers' stronger belief. Also: Erdős–Hajnal odd-cycle
reciprocal-sum (χ=k ⇒ Σ_{odd ℓ∈C(G)} 1/ℓ ≥ (½−o_k(1)) log k, optimal), and Thm 1.7 (equiv-subdiv
K_k subdivisions). Machinery: expander subgraphs (Thm 2.2), bipartite expander reduction (Cor
2.5), path-finding (Lems 3.2–3.4).
*Holds here:* bounds the counterexample regime from above — combined with Sudakov–Verstraëte a
counterexample is sparse. Does **not** settle δ=3 (density 3): the even interval needs enormous
ℓ, far beyond what δ=3 guarantees. *Obstruction echo:* b/a = ℓ/log⁸ℓ is huge, so the argument
works at large d and fails at d=3.

### 3b. Markström, "Extremal graphs for some problems on cycles" [[markstrom-extremal-cycles]]
Computational paper behind the verification bound: exhaustive search (cubic especially), incl.
graphs **without cycles of length 4 and 8** — the search that produced four 24-vertex graphs
whose only 2-power cycle is length 16, underpinning the ≥17 / ≥30 floors. Also many-cycles work:
ψ(k)<2ᵏ, ψ(k) ≥ ½ᵏ⁻¹+k²−3k+3 (Entringer–Slater), extremal graphs k≤11 / bounds k≤22,
generalised Petersen near-extremal (Conj 2.1), smallest 3-connected non-hamiltonian cubic class-I.
*Holds here:* the run's nauty-geng oracle must reproduce n≥17 / n≥30 and the four 24-vertex
only-16-cycle graphs before counts past them are trusted. Correctness anchor for Phase 3.

### 3c. Pirzada et al., "On 2-power unicyclic cubic graphs", EJGTA 10(1) 2022 [[pirzada-2power-unicyclic-cubic]]
Infinite family of cubic graphs with **exactly one 2-power cycle**; authors observe that a cubic
graph's sole 2-power cycle cannot be removed (deleting it re-forms it). Read as *no
counterexample exists in that form*. Abstract only.
*Holds here:* nearest to a cubic-specific structural observation; suggests a minimal
counterexample cannot be 2-power-unicyclic. *Caveat:* the inference is an abstract-level
observation, a lead not a closed theorem; consistent with (not replacing) Carr's predominantly-
cubic structure.

## 4. Agreements and disagreements across the batch

- **Subtle agreement:** §§1a,1b,3a all deliver interval/gap length guarantees, and all hit the
  same obstacle — at δ=3 the guaranteed lengths sit between consecutive powers of two, so none
  alone forces a 2-power. §3a only wins by making the interval span a factor of two, needing d
  enormous. **The unifying structural fact: the δ=3 gap is exactly where length-machinery runs
  out; classes that close (§§2a,2b) do so by forcing C4/C8 outright, not by the gap argument.**
- **Agreement:** every closed class here (diameter-2, P10-free, Cayley, 2-power-unicyclic) is
  outside the hard sparse predominantly-cubic δ=3 region; none contradicts Carr.
- **Regime disagreement (not facts):** §1a/§1b speak at δ=3 (gaps only), §1c at k≥6, §3a at large
  degree. The run must not use any one alone to close EG.
- **Framing disagreement:** Pirzada §3c reads "sole 2-power cycle ineradicable" as excluding a
  counterexample; the field (L1.0) treats it as open. Keep as a lead.

## 5. What this batch lets the run bound, compute, or rule out

- **At δ=3:** two admissible cycles (1 or 2 apart) — §1a; two consecutive-length cycles — §1b.
  The *only* unconditional non-density length guarantee at δ=3; any gap-to-2-power argument starts
  here.
- **Mod-k:** all even lengths mod k (δ=k+1, Thomassen); all lengths mod k if 2-connected
  non-bipartite; 0 mod k for k-connected (Dean); even r mod k at k≥6 (§1c). Reusable at small
  moduli if a counterexample's structure forces connectivity.
- **C4/C8-forcing classes (ruled out as counterexample hosts):** diameter-2 (§2a), P10-free
  (§2b), P13/P8-free, Cayley families (§2c, abstract), 3-connected cubic planar, claw-free
  planar, K_{1,m}-free, large average degree (§3a). A counterexample lies in *none* of these.
- **Verification:** reproduce n≥17 / n≥30 and the four 24-vertex only-16-cycle graphs (Markström
  §3b) as the oracle check, then extend only past them.
- **Where a counterexample can be:** sparse (avg degree 3 to exp(O(log* n)), Sudakov–Verstraëte),
  predominantly cubic with ≥4/7 degree-3 vertices, every vertex adjacent to a degree-3 vertex
  (Carr), and *not* 2-power-unicyclic cubic (§3c, lead).

**Bottom line:** L1.0 fixed *what* the target is (sparse, predominantly cubic, δ=3) and which
classes are closed. L1.1 supplies the *only tools that still speak at δ=3* — the two-cycle gap
(two cycles 1 or 2 apart) — plus mod-k machinery at higher regimes, the verification anchor
(Markström) and the density engine (Liu–Montgomery). Any closure must convert the "two cycles
1-or-2 apart" gap into a 2-power despite the factor-of-two obstruction, in a sparse
predominantly-cubic graph beyond 24–30 vertices. The trigonal/tetragonal framework is the most
likely way to widen that gap; the diameter-2 and P10 closures show outright C4/C8 forcing can
close a class — the strongest form a restricted result takes here.

# L1.1 — seal: the admissible-cycle / consecutive-length toolkit, the P10 line, the density engine, and the Cayley/verification restricted results

Seal of `research/L1.1/`, ten digest notes. Full texts live in `research/L0.1/` (and one in
`L0.0/`). One pair is a *misfiled* capture (see #4 — do not read it expecting graph theory),
so ten files carry **nine** usable items. This batch is the run's *engine room*: it holds
the cycle-length machinery any attack uses (admissible cycles, consecutive lengths, mod-k
lengths), the elementary restricted classes settled by path-freeness and diameter, the
density engine (Liu–Montgomery), the verification/oracle evidence (Markström), and two
one-result restricted closures (Cayley, 2-power-unicyclic cubic). Every theorem below has
its hypotheses stated exactly; `holds-here` marks whether those hypotheses are met by *this*
problem (a true theorem whose hypotheses fail here is worse than none).

## 1. The cycle-length machinery that any EG attack reaches for

### 1a. Admissible cycles — Gao, Huo, Liu & Ma, "A unified proof of conjectures on cycle lengths in graphs", arXiv:1904.08126
[[gao-huo-liu-ma-unified]]
The core toolkit. Definitions that recur across the whole field: a set of integers is
**consecutive** if it forms a 1-AP; **admissible** if it forms a 1-AP *or a 2-AP*; a family
of paths/cycles is consecutive/admissible if the set of their lengths is. One method proves
four previously-separate conjectures:
- **Thomassen** — every graph of min degree k+1 contains cycles of **all even lengths mod k**
  (Conj 1.5); if G is **2-connected and non-bipartite**, cycles of all lengths mod k (Conj 1.6).
- **Dean** — for all k≥3, every **k-connected** graph contains a cycle of length 0 mod k (Thm 1.9).
- **Bondy–Vince tight** — every **3-connected non-bipartite** graph of min degree k+1 contains
  **k cycles of consecutive lengths** (Thm 1.4).
- **Chromatic** — every graph of chromatic number ≥ k+2 contains k cycles of consecutive lengths (Thm 1.11).
- **Theorem 1.3 (the workhorse):** every graph of min degree **k+1** contains **k admissible
  cycles** (settling Conj 1.1 of Liu–Ma; Chiba–Yamashita independently proved a 2-connected
  version).

*Holds here / use:* set **k=2, min degree = 3** = δ≥3. Then **Theorem 1.3 gives 2 admissible
cycles** — two cycle lengths whose difference is 1 or 2 — the *sharpest unconditional
cycle-length guarantee at the conjecture's own degree*. This is the closest the δ=3 regime
gets to forcing a power of two, and it is the natural seed for a gap argument. The mod-k
machinery (even lengths mod k) at k=2 says δ≥3 ⇒ cycles of all even lengths mod 2, i.e. even
cycles — a weak but free fact. *Caveat:* consecutive/admissible length guarantees give
lengths that may still sit *between* powers of two; the interval-spans-factor-of-two
obstruction (MEMORY) says they force a 2-power only when the guaranteed interval spans a
factor of two, which 1-AP/2-AP pairs at δ=3 do not in general.

### 1b. Consecutive-length cycles — Gao & Ma, "Cycles of consecutive lengths in a graph", arXiv:1902.05701
[[gao-ma-bondy-vince]]
Confirms the **Bondy–Vince conjecture**: for every nonnegative integer k, *except finitely
many counterexamples*, every graph with at most k vertices of degree <3 contains **two
cycles whose lengths differ by 1 or 2**. History: the case k≤2 was proved by Bondy & Vince,
resolving an earlier Erdős et al. conjecture; this paper settles all k. The exceptional
graphs are K1 and K2. The proof is a structural argument on block-decompositions
(`B(H)=k`, no-good-pair Claims 1–4).

*Holds here / use:* the **k=0 case** is exactly **δ≥3 ⇒ two cycles of consecutive length**
(guaranteed, not just finitely many exceptions) — a restatement of the same gap guarantee
as §1a at k=2. Adjacent machinery: a concrete proof that a δ≥3 graph always has two cycles
1 or 2 apart, which any attempt that wants to nest a power of two between two known cycle
lengths will lean on. It does *not* by itself give a power of two — same obstruction as §1a.

### 1c. Mod-k cycle lengths and the trigonal/tetragonal framework — Luo, Ma & Zhao, "A proof of Dean's conjecture for k ≥ 6", arXiv:2601.13552
[[luo-ma-zhao-dean]]
**Dean's conjecture:** for every k≥3, min degree ≥k ⇒ a cycle of length divisible by k.
Verified for k∈{3,4}, was open k≥5; this paper resolves **k≥6**. **Main Theorem:** for k≥6,
min degree ≥k ⇒ cycles of length **r (mod k) for every even r**, *unless* every end-block is
in the exceptional family {K_{k+1}, K_{k,k}} ∪ H_k (which then fail only for length ≡ 2 mod k).
**Cor 1.3:** for k≥6 and every even r ≢ 2 (mod k), min degree ≥k ⇒ a cycle of length r mod k.
Also **Thm 1.4:** k≥7 ⇒ k admissible cycles unless every end-block is in {K_{k+1}, K_{k,k}} ∪ H_k.
**Framework introduced:** **trigonal** (non-bipartite outer-planar with a Hamiltonian cycle ∂T;
Prop 3.2 — for vertices u,v at ∂T-distance d, [d, t−d] ⊆ L^T_{u,v}) and **tetragonal**
(bipartite outer-planar, |T|=2m; Prop 3.5 — {d, d+2, …, 2m−d} ⊆ L^T_{u,v}) sparse graph
families for path/cycle lengths, of independent interest.

*Holds here / use:* the **k≥6 regime is above δ=3**, so this specific theorem does not hit EG
directly — a caveat to state rather than paper over. What transfers is the **trigonal /
tetragonal framework**: a flexible way to control *which* lengths appear as paths/cycles in a
sparse outer-planar-like structure, plausibly reusable in the sparse, predominantly-cubic
regime even though the degree regimes differ. *Recorded disagreement within the batch:*
§1c's k≥6 mod-k result is strictly coarser than §1a's k=2 admissible-cycle result at the
degree the conjecture cares about; they are not competitors — mod-k and consecutive/admissible
give different (complementary) length information and both should be kept in the toolkit.

## 2. Restricted classes settled (do not re-derive)

### 2a. Diameter 2 — Carr, "Cycles of Length 4 or 8 in Graphs with Diameter 2 and Minimum Degree at Least 3", arXiv:2508.19302
[[carr-diameter2]]
**Theorem 1.1:** every graph of diameter 2 and min degree ≥3 contains a cycle of length **4 or 8** —
so EG holds for the diameter-2 class (the strongest single class here because a 4-cycle is
guaranteed, not merely a 2-power). Proof is a hostile deep structural case analysis (Claims 1.1–1.6
and 2.1–2.4) that rules out a 4-free-and-8-free diameter-2 δ≥3 graph vertex by vertex: it tracks
a path v1–v2–…–v9, uses diam=2 to force common neighbours, and shows each configuration generates a
4-cycle. *Directory note:* this is *not* the same Carr as [[carr-minimal-counterexample-cubic]]
(L1.0) though both are by Carr.

*Holds here / use:* another closed class to keep off the search list. Diameter 2 forces such
short cycles that C4 is essentially unavoidable — so diameter-2 graphs are far from the open
ground, which is general δ=3 (mostly diameter 3 and up, girth 3–4). *Caveat:* a *dense* class;
the open regime is sparse, so this closure is informative but not a stepping stone into the
hard case.

### 2b. P10-free — Hu & Shen, "The Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture holds for P10-free graphs", arXiv:2308.05675
[[hu-shen-p10-free]]
**Theorem 1.1:** every P10-free graph with min degree ≥3 contains a **C4 or C8** — EG holds for
P10-free. Method (all lemmas/claims in the note): a 4-free δ≥3 graph has an m-hole with m≥5
(Lem 2.2, borrowed); good-hole machinery bounds the number of "inner chords" (|IC| ≤ 7−m, Lem 2.4),
finds good paths off a good hole (Lems 2.5–2.6), then a chain of Claims 3.x rules out holes in a
P10-free graph with neither C4 nor C8, culminating in IC=∅ (Claim 3.10) — the contradiction.

*Holds here / use:* one step of the **Pk-free progression** P8 (Gao–Shan) → P10 (this) →
P13 (Hegde et al, L1.0). Another closed class (no long induced path). *Caveat:* P-freeness is a
*hereditary* strong restriction; the general δ=3 open ground is not Pk-free, so this is again a
closure outside the hard region. The hole/good-path technique is the transferable part.

### 2c. Cayley graphs — Ghaffari & Mostaghim, "Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture for some families of Cayley graphs", Aequationes Math., DOI 10.1007/s00010-017-0518-3
[[ghaffari-mostaghim-cayley]]
Proves EG for **Cayley graphs on generalized quaternion groups, dihedral groups, semidihedral
groups, and groups of order p^3**. Available only as a subscription-preview *abstract* — the
run holds the claim but not the proof. Restricted-class closure; the Cayley families are dense,
regular, highly symmetric, and far from the open general case. Keep on the "already settled" list
with the caveat that only the abstract is local.

## 3. The density engine and the verification evidence

### 3a. Liu & Montgomery, "Odd cycles in graphs and a conjecture of Erdős and Hajnal", arXiv:2010.15802
[[liu-montgomery-odd-cycle]]
**Theorem 1.1:** there is d0>0 such that if G has average degree d ≥ d0, then for some
ℓ ≥ d/(10 log¹² d), **C(G) contains every even integer in [log⁸ℓ, ℓ]**. **Cor 1.3:** given any
infinite increasing even sequence σ_i with σ_{i+1} ≤ exp(σ_i^{1/10}), any graph of average degree
≥ max{d0, σ₁²} has some σ_i as a cycle length. **This is the engine behind the "large average
degree ⇒ EG" closure**: for d large, the even interval [log⁸ℓ, ℓ] is far wider than a factor of
two, so it must contain a power of two — i.e. large average degree forces a 2-power cycle. Also
proves the Erdős–Hajnal **odd-cycle reciprocal-sum** theorem (chromatic number k ⇒
Σ_{odd ℓ∈C(G)} 1/ℓ ≥ (½ − o_k(1)) log k, asymptotically optimal; Odd-thm 1.4/Cor 1.6), and
**Thm 1.7:** for every k there is d such that average degree ≥ d ⇒ a K_k subdivision with each
edge subdivided equally (Thomassen's conjecture). Machinery: expander subgraphs (Thm 2.2), the
bipartite expander reduction (Cor 2.5), path-finding in expanders (Lemmas 3.2–3.4).

*Holds here / use:* this is the result that **disproves the proposers' stronger belief** that
every min degree r could avoid 2-power cycles — it shows an absolute average-degree threshold
suffices. For the run it **bounds the counterexample regime from above in a different sense**:
combined with Sudakov–Verstraëte, a counterexample is sparse, and this says "not too sparse had
better also not be too dense". It does **not** settle the δ=3 case (density 3), which is the
gap — the even-interval needs an enormous ℓ, far beyond what δ=3 guarantees. *Obstruction echo:*
an interval [a,b] forces a 2-power iff b≥2a; here b/a = ℓ/log⁸ℓ, huge, so the argument works at
large d and fails at d=3.

### 3b. Markström, "Extremal graphs for some problems on cycles in graphs" (cycex.pdf)
[[markstrom-extremal-cycles]]
The **computational-experiments paper behind the verification bound** (from L1.0): exhaustive
computer search over graphs (cubic especially) for several cycle problems. Directly EG-related:
searches for graphs **without cycles of length 4 and 8** — this is the search that produced the
four 24-vertex graphs whose only 2-power cycle is length 16, and underpins the ≥17 / ≥30
verification floors. Tracked carefully only in the full text (the digest covers mainly the
many-cycles work): the maximum-cycle function ψ(k), ψ(k)<2ᵏ and ψ(k) ≥ ½ᵏ⁻¹ + k² − 3k + 3
(Entringer–Slater), extremal graphs for k≤11 and lower bounds to k≤22, generalised Petersen
graphs near-extremal (Conj 2.1), and the smallest 3-connected non-hamiltonian cubic class-I graphs.

*Holds here / use:* the run's **nauty-geng oracle** (`-q -c -d3`) must reproduce the 17-vertex
(all graphs) and 30-vertex (cubic) floors and the four 24-vertex only-16-cycle graphs before any
of its own counts past them can be trusted. This is the correctness anchor for Phase 3.

### 3c. Pirzada et al., "On 2-power unicyclic cubic graphs", Electron. J. Graph Theory Appl. 10(1), 2022, DOI 10.5614/ejgta.2022.10.1.24
[[pirzada-2power-unicyclic-cubic]]
There is an **infinite family of cubic graphs containing exactly one 2-power cycle**
("2-power unicyclic cubic graphs"). The authors **observe** that the sole 2-power cycle in a
cubic graph cannot be removed (deleting it would leave a cubic-reducible structure that re-forms
it, per their argument), which they read as *implying no counterexample can exist in that form*.
Abstract only in the local copy.

*Holds here / use:* this is a *nearest-neighbour* structural result, and it is the closest the
batch comes to a *cubic-specific* structural observation. It suggests that a minimal
counterexample (predominantly cubic, δ=3, no 2-power cycle) cannot be "unicyclic-of-2-power" —
i.e. cannot be a graph with just one 2-power cycle — because that cycle is ineradicable. *Caveat:*
the inference "sole 2-power cycle ineradicable ⇒ no counterexample" is advertised by the source
as an observation at the abstract level, not a full theorem seen here; treat it as a lead, not a
closed case, and as *consistent with* (not replacing) Carr's predominantly-cubic structure.

## 4. Agreements and disagreements across the batch

- **Agreement (subtle):** §§1a,1b,3a all deliver *interval/gap* guarantees of cycle lengths
  (admissible pairs, consecutive pairs, long even intervals), and all three hit the same
  obstacle: at δ=3 the guaranteed lengths sit between consecutive powers of two, so none alone
  forces a 2-power. The engine §3a only wins by making the interval span a factor of two, which
  needs d enormous. **This is the single unifying structural fact the batch establishes:** the
  δ=3 gap is precisely the place where length-machinery runs out, which is why the restricted
  classes that do close (§§2a,2b) close by forcing C4/C8 outright, not by the gap argument.
- **Agreement:** every closed class in the batch (diameter-2, P10-free, Cayley families,
  2-power-unicyclic-cubic) is outside the hard general-δ=3 region, which is sparse and
  predominantly cubic (L1.0). No class here contradicts Carr.
- **Disagreement (regime, not facts):** §1a/§1b give δ=3-length guarantees but no 2-power;
  §1c works only at k≥6; §3a works only at large average degree. The run must *not* try to use
  any one of these alone to close EG — each is a different degree regime, and the open ground is
  δ=3 where only §§1a/1b still speak, and only in gaps.
- **Disagreement (framing):** Pirzada §3c reads "sole 2-power cycle ineradicable" as excluding a
  counterexample; the broader field (L1.0) treats the counterexample question as open. Not a
  contradiction of facts — §3c's abstract does not prove the general case — but a difference in
  how much weight to give a single structure. Keep it as a lead.

## 5. What this batch lets the run bound, compute, or rule out

- **At δ=3 (the target degree):** two admissible cycles (lengths 1 or 2 apart) — §1a; two cycles
  of consecutive length — §1b. This is the *only* unconditional, non-density length guarantee at
  δ=3, and any argument that wants a gap between consecutive 2-powers starts here.
- **Cycle lengths mod k:** all even lengths mod k with min degree k+1 (Thomassen, §1a); all
  lengths mod k if 2-connected non-bipartite; length 0 mod k for k-connected (Dean, §1a); r mod k
  for even r with min degree ≥k at k≥6 (§1c). Reusable at small moduli if a counterexample's
  structure forces connectivity.
- **C4/C8-forcing classes (ruled out as counterexample hosts):** diameter-2 (§2a), P10-free (§2b),
  P13/P8-free (L1.0), Cayley families (§2c, abstract only), 3-connected cubic planar (L1.0),
  claw-free planar, K_{1,m}-free, large average degree (§3a). A counterexample, if one exists,
  lies in *none* of these.
- **Verification:** reproduce n≥17 (all) / n≥30 (cubic) and the four 24-vertex only-16-cycle
  graphs (Markström §3b + Wikipedia L1.0) as the oracle check. Then extend only past them.
- **Bound on where a counterexample can be:** sparse (avg degree between 3 and exp(O(log* n)),
  Sudakov–Verstraëte L1.0), predominantly cubic with ≥4/7 degree-3 vertices and every vertex
  adjacent to a degree-3 vertex (Carr L1.0), and *not* a 2-power-unicyclic cubic structure
  (§3c, as a lead).

**Bottom line for the run:** the L1.0 batch fixed *what* the target is (sparse, predominantly
cubic, δ=3) and which classes are closed. This L1.1 batch supplies the *only tools that still
speak at δ=3* — the admissible/consecutive two-cycle gap (there are two cycles 1 or 2 apart) —
and the mod-k machinery at higher regimes, plus the verification anchor (Markström) and the
density engine (Liu–Montgomery) that together say: *any* closure of the conjecture must convert
the "two cycles 1-or-2 apart" gap into a power of two despite the factor-of-two obstruction, in a
sparse predominantly-cubic graph beyond 24–30 vertices. The mod-k / trigonal-tetragonal framework
is the most likely way to widen that gap; the diameter-2 and P10 closures are existence proofs
that outright C4/C8 forcing can close a class, which is the strongest form a restricted result
can take here.

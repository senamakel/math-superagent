# L0.0 — seal: sources 1–10 (the conjecture's statement, the minimal-counterexample structure, and the cycle-length machinery)

Seal of `research/L0.0/`, ten full texts covering the Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture from its
primary statement through the structural theory of a minimal counterexample and the
cycle-spectrum machinery any attack will use. Three pairs are duplicate-source twins of
the same paper (Carr PDF/abs; Sudakov–Verstraëte PDF/abs), so ten files carry eight
distinct works. The twins say the same thing in both conversions; no information is lost
by treating each pair as one source. All hypotheses below are stated as in the source.

## 1–2. Carr, "Every Minimal Counterexample … is Predominantly Cubic", arXiv:2605.22844
[[carr-minimal-counterexample-cubic]] [[carr-minimal-counterexample-cubic-pdf]]
The run's central structural anchor. A **minimal counterexample** = graph of minimum
possible order *and* size with δ≥3 and no power-of-two cycle. Establishes, about any
minimal counterexample G:
- **Lemma 0.1:** δ(H) ≤ 2 for every proper subgraph H ⊊ G (every proper subgraph has a
  vertex of degree ≤2). *Holds here:* yes — this is exactly the 2-core/minimality
  structure the run reasons about.
- **Cor 0.2:** if G is regular, G is cubic. (From Markström's observation: any minimal
  counterexample has an independent set of degree-≥4 vertices *and* a nonempty set of
  degree-3 vertices; if all degrees are equal they must be 3.)
- **Theorem 0.1:** at least 4/7 of the vertices of G have degree exactly 3.
- Every vertex of G is adjacent to a vertex of degree exactly 3.
*Use:* the minimal-counterexample argument never has to handle a regular case beyond
cubic, and between 4/7 and all vertices are degree 3. Verdict: structural facts, directly
applicable.

## 3. Erdős Problems, Problem #64 (T.F. Bloom), erdosproblems.com
[[erdosproblems-open-Er96]]
Primary-statement anchor. The conjecture as attributed: *does every finite graph with
minimum degree ≥3 contain a cycle of length 2^k for some k ≥ 2?* (so powers ≥ 4; a simple
cycle has length ≥3, so 4 is the first candidate). Key provenance and beliefs:
- Erdős and Gyárfás themselves **believed the answer negative**, and asserted the stronger
  claim that for every r there is a graph of minimum degree ≥ r with no power-of-2 cycle.
  This stronger claim is **disproved** by Liu–Montgomery (see below): min degree above an
  absolute constant suffices.
- **Liu–Montgomery [LiMo20]:** if the *average* degree of G is sufficiently large, then
  for some large ℓ, *every even m in [(log ℓ)^8, ℓ]* is a cycle length. (Interval of even
  lengths; see obstruction note in MEMORY.md — an interval [a,b] forces a power of two only
  when b ≥ 2a, and the window here is far wider than that.)
- Trivially false for infinite graphs (infinite min-degree-3 tree).
- Confirmed for various families (per comment by Alfaiz) — cross-check with #6, #10.
- Prize: $100 proof, $50 counterexample. Listed as #69 in the Extremal Graph Theory
  problem collection; statement formalised.
Notably the problem page gives multiple Erdős citations Er93, Er94b, Er95, Er96 — so the
conjecture dates to the mid-1990s, in tension with Wikipedia's single "1995" and Hegde's
"1994" (below). This is a real disagreement among the sources; the safe statement is
"1993–1996".

## 5. Gao & Shan, P8-free graphs, arXiv:2109.01277
[[gao-shan-p8-free]]
Restricted-class result. Pk-free = no *induced* Pk path. **Theorem 1.2:** every P8-free
graph with δ ≥ 3 contains a 4-cycle (establishing the conjecture for this class).
**Theorem 1.1:** every P5-free graph with δ≥3 contains a 4-cycle. *Use:* a concrete
restricted class on which the conjecture is settled, and a template for the Pk-free
computer-check method later pushed to P13 (see #6).

## 6. Hegde, Sandeep & Shashank, P13-free graphs, arXiv:2410.22842
[[hegde-sandeep-shashank-p13-free]]
Current frontier of the Pk-free line, **computer-aided**. **Theorem 1:** every P13-free
graph with δ≥3 has a power-of-two cycle. **Theorem 2:** every P12-free graph with δ≥3 has
a 4-cycle or an 8-cycle. Method: define explore(G,k); Corollary 1 — if G* is a minimal
counterexample and k the smallest integer with G* Pk-free but containing an induced
P_{k−1}, then explore(G,k) returns False. Cites the progression P8 (Gao–Shan 2022, Graphs
and Combinatorics) → P10 (Hu–Shen 2024, Discrete Math) → P13 (this). Also lists the other
confirmed classes: 3-connected cubic planar [Heckman–Krakovski], claw-free planar,
K_{1,m}-free with degree constraints, some Cayley families, and Liu–Montgomery average
degree. *Use:* the class of "no long induced path" graphs; also a worked example of a
machine-checked restricted result.

## 4. Gao, Huo, Liu & Ma, unified cycle-length paper, arXiv:1904.08126
[[gao-huo-liu-ma-unified]]
The cycle-machinery core. One method proves all of: (1) **Thomassen** — min degree k+1 ⇒
cycles of all even lengths mod k; if 2-connected non-bipartite ⇒ all lengths mod k.
(2) **Dean** — for all k≥3, k-connected ⇒ a cycle of length 0 mod k. (3) **Bondy–Vince
tight** — 3-connected non-bipartite min degree k+1 ⇒ k cycles of consecutive lengths.
(4) chromatic number ≥ k+2 ⇒ k cycles of consecutive lengths. Definition: **consecutive** =
lengths form a 1-AP; **admissible** = 1-AP or 2-AP. **Theorem 1.3:** every graph with min
degree k+1 contains k admissible cycles. *Holds here / use:* with δ=3 (k=2) this yields 2
admissible cycles — adjacent or gap-2 cycle lengths — the sharpest unconditional
cycle-length guarantee at the conjecture's degree. The admissible-circle machinery (#4,#7)
is the likely engine for forcing a gap that a power of two must fill.

## 7. Luo, Ma & Zhao, Dean's conjecture, arXiv:2601.13552
[[luo-ma-zhao-dean]]
**Dean:** for k≥3, min degree ≥ k ⇒ a cycle of length divisible by k. Verified k∈{3,4},
open k≥5; this resolves k≥6. **Main Theorem:** for k≥6, min degree ≥ k ⇒ cycles of length
r mod k for every even r, *unless* every end-block is in {K_{k+1}, K_{k,k}} ∪ H_k (which
then fail only for length ≡ 2 mod k). Introduces **trigonal** and **tetragonal** sparse
outer-planar graphs as a framework for path/cycle lengths. Adjacent machinery, not a direct
EG result; relevant to the "cycle lengths mod k" toolkit Guessing the run's argument on
mod-k structure. *Holds here:* the k≥6 regime is above δ=3, so this specific theorem does
not directly hit EG, but the trigonal/tetragonal framework is transferable.

## 8–9. Sudakov & Verstraëte, "Cycle lengths in sparse graphs", arXiv:0707.2117
[[sudakov-verstraete-sparse]] [[sudakov-verstraete-sparse-pdf]]
Two results, one of which is directly EG-bearing.
- **Part 1 (cycle spectrum):** for avg degree d and girth g, |C(G)| = Ω(d^{⌊(g−1)/2⌋})
  (proves Erdős's conjecture; tight via d-regular Moore graphs). Also Ω(d^{⌊(g−1)/2⌋})
  *odd* cycle lengths when chromatic number d and girth g; H-free avg degree d gives
  Ω(d^{t/(t−1)}) consecutive even integers.
- **Part 2, Theorem 1.3 / Corollary 1.4 — the EG-bearing one:** for any infinite,
  exponentially-bounded sequence σ of positive even integers, any n-vertex graph with *no
  σ-cycle* has average degree **exp(O(log* n))**. *Holds here — this applies directly:*
  the powers of two form an exponentially-bounded even sequence, so a counterexample to EG
  (min degree ≥3, no power-of-two cycle) has average degree ≤ exp(O(log* n)). That is a
  hard upper bound on how a counterexample's edge count can scale with n — it cannot be
  dense. Combined with δ≥3 (avg degree ≥3), it sharply brackets the counterexample's
  density. This is probably the single most exploitable external bound for the run.

## 10. Wikipedia, "Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture"
[[wikipedia-erdos-gyarfas]]
Survey/verification anchor. Statement and prizes as above (made 1995; Erdős $100/$50).
**Computational verification bounds — the run's oracle anchor:**
- Any counterexample has **≥ 17 vertices** (Gordon Royle & Markström searches).
- Any **cubic** counterexample has **≥ 30 vertices**.
- Markström found **four graphs on 24 vertices** whose only power-of-two cycle is length
  16; one is planar.
- **3-connected cubic planar** graphs proved (Heckman & Krakovski 2013).
- Conjecture open for bipartite graphs.
*Use:* 17 is the floor the run's own brute-force oracle must reproduce and exceed; the
four 24-vertex near-counterexamples are concrete extremal test cases.

## Agreements and disagreements
- **Agreement:** the conjecture is open; min degree 3 is the threshold; verification floor
  at/of 17 vertices, cubic 30; restricted classes (cubic planar 3-connected, P13-free,
  claw-free planar, Cayley) all confirmed; Liu–Montgomery settles large-average-degree.
- **Disagreement — date:** 1995 (Wikipedia) vs 1994 (Hegde) vs a 1993–96 publication range
  (Erdős Problems). Resolve as "mid-1990s" and cite the Erdős papers if precision matters.
- **Disagreement — original belief:** the Erdős Problems page records that EG *believed
  the answer negative* (stronger claim), which Liu–Montgomery disproved; the common
  framing "it is conjectured to be true" is therefore not what the proposers believed. The
  run should treat "conjecture" as "unresolved, originally expected to be false."
- **Sequence k≥2:** the 2^k statement takes k≥2, so the target set is {4,8,16,…} — no
  2-cycle issue.

## What this batch lets the run bound, compute, or rule out
- **Structure of a counterexample:** predominantly cubic (≥4/7 degree-3, every vertex
  adjacent to a degree-3 vertex, regular ⇒ cubic) — Carr.
- **Density of a counterexample:** avg degree ≤ exp(O(log* n)) — Sudakov–Verstraëte; with
  min degree ≥3, density is pinned between 3 and exp(O(log* n)).
- **Oracle targets:** reproduce n≥17 (all graphs), n≥30 (cubic), and the four 24-vertex
  only-16-cycle graphs as correctness checks.
- **Cycle-length machinery to reach for:** admissible cycles (min degree k+1 ⇒ k of them),
  mod-k cycle lengths, consecutive cycle lengths, trigonal/tetragonal framework.
- **Restricted classes already closed** (do not re-derive): P13-free, cubic-planar
  3-connected, claw-free planar, K_{1,m}-free with degree constraints, large average degree.

## Problem

Erdős–Gyárfás (1995): every finite simple graph with minimum degree ≥3 has a cycle of length a power of two ($2^k$, $k≥2$ → 4, 8, 16, …). **Open.** The obstruction to beat: cycle-length theorems usually give an *interval* or *progression*, but powers of two are sparse (gap $2^k$), so any win must produce a cycle of a *prescribed* length, not somewhere in a range. Full statement in `problem.md`. The run's one outright failure is claiming the conjecture on an un-attacked argument; the deliverable is a genuine partial result stated exactly.

## Established

Each marked with basis. Detail lives in the linked note.

- **Minimal-counterexample degree structure** (sourced, Carr arXiv:2605.22844 building on Markström): in a minimal counterexample (min order+size, δ≥3, no power-of-two cycle), the degree-≥4 vertices form an independent set, plus a **nonempty** set of degree-3 vertices. Consequence: every *regular* minimal counterexample is cubic. Carr adds: every vertex is adjacent to a degree-3 vertex, and **≥4/7 of vertices have degree exactly 3**; every proper subgraph has δ≤2. → a minimal counterexample is "predominantly cubic". Anchors: `research/summaries/carr-minimal-counterexample.md`. This is the structural backbone the run's arguments target. **Derived corollary** (from Carr's diameter-2 result, not stated in any source): a minimal counterexample has **diam(G) ≥ 3** — if diam=2 it would have a C4 or C8. Claim `EG-min-ce-diam-ge-3` (proved), anchor `research/summaries/connectivity-girth-minimal-ce.md`. Note: **no source on disk asserts 2- or 3-connectivity, girth, or a cut-vertex statement** for a minimal counterexample; Royle's relaxation note even sketches 1-connected near-counterexamples — a provable 2-connectivity lemma would be genuinely new.
- **Restricted classes settled** (sourced; full list in `research/ROOT.md`): **3-connected cubic planar** (Heckman–Krakovski 2013, E-JC #P7, discharging + computer); induced-path-free **P8-free** (Gao–Shan 2022), **P10-free** (Hu–Shen 2024), **P13-free** (Hegde–Sandeep–Shashank 2024, arXiv:2410.22842, computer-assisted; sharper **P12-free ⇒ has a C4 or C8**); λ-claw-free / $K_{1,m}$-free (Shauger 1998: $K_{1,m}$-free with δ≥m+1 or Δ≥2m−1; Daniel–Shauger 2001: planar claw-free — **both only Google-Scholar results, the actual papers are not on disk, so those two classes rest on citation, unverified primary text**); **cubic claw-free** (Salehi–Esfandiari–Shirdareh–Bibak, bibliotekanauki 30148697): Theorem 9 — any cubic-claw-free counterexample has **≥114 vertices**; also any claw-free δ≥3 has a cycle of length $2^k$ or $3·2^k$; **diameter-2** (Carr 2025, arXiv:2508.19302 — always a C4 or C8); Cayley on some groups (Ghaffari–Mostaghim 2018); dense regime (Sudakov–Verstraëte 2008, avg degree in iterated-log of n; Liu–Montgomery, avg degree ≥ large C). ## Ruled out

- **Cycle-basis-only length check is wrong**: a basis spans the cycle space but its cycle lengths can miss lengths present only as non-basic (symmetric-difference) cycles. Oracle must enumerate all simple cycles. Closed in `cycle_oracle.py` docstring.
- **"No edge between two degree-≥3 vertices"** (Markström's raw wording) reads impossibly (would leave no edges); the true content is the degree-≥4 independent set. Treat Markström's raw phrasing as unreliable; use Carr's formulation. (See Contradictions.)
- **Identifying girth-5 min-deg-3 with 3-connected**: true to n=17, **false from n=18** (2-sum breaks it; and all extras contain an 8-cycle, so are not EG near-counterexamples anyway).
- **"A C4, once created by expansion, is never destroyed" pruning is false**: expanding a vertex on a 4-cycle changes that cycle's length (by ±1/±2 around the triangle), so a C4 need not survive a later expansion. Closing dead end — the base-step (K4→prism keeps C4) is the counterexample; do not re-propose this as a pruning rule.
- **avoidsC4 recurrence fit is dead**: a fractional 4th-order linear-recurrence fit to the 8 avoidsC4 terms is REFUTED by integrality — the next term is forced to 203921/54, which is not an integer. Not in OEIS; no clean recurrence. (pattern_finder; the A279553/A107590 coincidence was separately killed at n=22.)
- **P_k-free backtracking cannot reach the full conjecture** (Hegde et al. explicitly): the method is P_k-specific; for H-free with cyclic H it runs forever (an infinite min-deg-3 tree is H-free with no power-of-two cycle), and for non-path trees the clique-substitution claw-free example fails too. It only certifies the P_k-free class — do not extend it to the general case. (in push-verification thread + memory.)

- **Phase 4 — the run's concrete structural candidate: CUT-VERTEX CHARACTERIZATION** (derived in scratch, not yet machine-verified, not yet in durable memory — treat as derived-not-checked). Let v be a cut vertex of a minimal counterexample G, components C_1..C_k of G−v, lobes L_i=G[C_i∪{v}], d_i=#neighbours of v in C_i. Facts: (a) every simple cycle of G lies inside a single L_i (a cycle through v uses two v-edges into one lobe, else its v-free path would connect lobes) ⇒ no L_i has a power-of-two cycle; (c) ∀w∈C_i: d_{L_i}(w)=d_G(w)≥3; (d) L_i proper ⇒ δ(L_i)≤2 (Carr Lemma 0.1) ⇒ d_i≤2; (e) every proper S⊊{1..k} has Σ_{i∈S}d_i≤2. **Conclusion**: k=2 ⇒ (d_1,d_2)∈{(1,2),(2,1),(2,2)} with d(v)=3 or 4; k≥3 ⇒ all d_i=1 — precisely Royle's "three copies joined to a single central vertex" shape. **This does NOT prove 2-connectivity** (cut vertices remain possible); it reduces 2-connectivity to ruling out k=2 splits of type (2,2)/(1,2)/(2,1) and the k≥3 all-single-edge split. **Ingredients machine-verified (computed+checked)** by `code/connectivity/verify_connectivity.py` (`code/out/connectivity/verify_connectivity.log`), run twice independently (tool_builder agent-run-6 + pattern_finder re-run, identical): gluing 3 Markström lobes (n=73) or 3 Petersen lobes (n=31) at a central deg-3 cut vertex gives a δ≥3, node-connectivity-1 graph whose glued cycle set EXACTLY = union of lobe sets ({3,5,6,7,9..24} has C16; {5,6,8,9} has C8) — ingredient (a) and the glue bookkeeping confirmed. **Critical caveat (in memory + `cutvertex_check.py`): a wedge of two triangles sharing one vertex is a δ-3 graph whose every lobe has δ≤2, so Carr Lemma 0.1 does NOT alone force 2-connectivity — the claimed 2-connectivity argument has a real gap.** Cut vertices remain fully possible; do not promote 2-connectivity to an established belief.

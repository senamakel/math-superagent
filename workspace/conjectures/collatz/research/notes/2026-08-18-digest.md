# Digest of newly read reference material

## Durable findings (memory service unavailable during this run)
- Barina 2025 reports finite verification of all starts below `2^71`; this is numerical evidence, not a universal proof.
- Ansari 2025 defines recursively sufficient sets and claims an exact induction theorem. Its Proposition 3.2 extends a verified endpoint `N=2*3^r+1` through `(N,2N]`; with `r=44` and Barina's bound, the claimed finite frontier is `4*3^44+2 ≈ 3.939e21`. This needs exact arithmetic and formalisation before treating it as checked here.
- Knight's high-cycle theorem excludes the Christoffel-word extremal parity class by a divisibility argument; it does not address general cycles or divergence.
- Hercher proves no nontrivial `m`-cycle for `m≤91`, and gives a conditional larger odd-member bound. This is a restricted cycle result.
- Zudilin records irrationality exponent `<8.616` for nonzero rational linear combinations of `log 2` and `log 3`; this is relevant input to cycle Diophantine estimates but is not itself a cycle exclusion.
- Laurore claims `σ=ω` for all positive integers by strong induction and hence no nontrivial cycles. The residue-class induction is unverified and uncorroborated; it must not be reported as established. It also does not address divergence.
- Angeltveit 2026 proposes a recursive bitvector/mod-9/path-merging verification algorithm and reports tests only through `2^60`; estimates for `2^72`–`2^77` are not completed records.

## Sources that do not materially advance the committed proof
Surveys, encyclopedia pages, Wikipedia, OEIS, stochastic/random-walk papers, and generalized-undecidability papers provide context or pointers only. Tao's theorem remains a proved logarithmic-density-one almost-boundedness result, explicitly not a proof against exceptional divergent or cyclic orbits. Automated rewriting work reformulates Collatz and rules out one natural-matrix route, but proves no full result.

## Contradictions / cautions
No source contradicts the established open status. Laurore's claimed no-cycle theorem is much stronger than the established Hercher/Barina bounds, but its key induction is not independently verified; this is a credibility gap, not a contradiction.

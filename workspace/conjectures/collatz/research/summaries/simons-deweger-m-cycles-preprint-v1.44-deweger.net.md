## What it claims

An m-cycle of the 3n+1-problem is deﬁned as a periodic orbit with m local minima. In this article
we derive lower and upper bounds for the cycle length and the elements of (hypothetical) m-cycles.
In particular, we prove that there do not exist nontrivial m-cycles for 1 ≤ m ≤ 75. Our proofs
are based on transcendental number theory, computational diophantine approximation techniques,
and a not straightforward generalization of the approach of Steiner and Simons on 1-cycles and
2-cycles respectively.

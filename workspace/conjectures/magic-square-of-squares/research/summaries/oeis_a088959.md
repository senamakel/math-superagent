# OEIS A088959 — record numbers of representations of n² as sum of two squares — [[oeis_a088959.full]] (if held)

Catalogued sequence: lowest number whose square is expressible as a sum of two positive squares in more ways than for any smaller number. Terms: 1, 5, 25, 65, 325, 1105, 5525, 27625, 32045, 160225, 801125, 1185665, 5928325, ...

## Relevance to the problem
The central square c = e² of a full 3×3 magic square of squares must be expressible as **x²+y² in many distinct ways** (each through-centre line gives one partition; Bremner's 7-square witness has centre 425² with e=425=5·17 and exactly 7 ordered partitions into x²+y²=180625, from the four pairs in Brown's proposition plus the diagonal-line data). The number of representations r₂(e²) is the classical function governed by the formula
**r₂(n) = 4·∏ₚ (e_p+1)** for primes p ≡ 1 (mod 4) in n (and 0 if any 3 mod 4 prime divides n with odd exponent), counting signs and order.

A088959 lists the record-setters of the *number of ways with positive x,y*. For the problem this tells us: a candidate centre e must be a number with **large** r₂ — but that is a necessary condition, not sufficient. The OEIS entry adds nothing beyond the classical two-squares-counting formula (already implied by Boyer's D1/D2 identities).

**Conclusion:** catalogued reference with no new structural fact; the formula r₂(n²) = 4∏(2e_p+1) over 4k+1 primes is standard and can be used for generator-side filtering, but is already captured in `sum-two-squares-counts` (Boyer).

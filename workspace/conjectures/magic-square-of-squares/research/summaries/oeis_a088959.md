# A088959 — record-holder e whose square has the most sum-of-two-squares representations (catalogue + hand-check)

<!-- source: https://oeis.org/A088959 -->

**Terms:** 1, 5, 25, 65, 325, 1105, 5525, 27625, 32045, 160225, 801125, 1185665, 5928325, …

**What A088959 is.** The integers e such that e² is expressible as a sum of two
positive squares in strictly more ways than any smaller square — equivalently,
the squares of e have record numbers of lattice points / sum-of-two-squares
representations. For this run: **A088959 lists exactly the argmax-e of
|S(e)| = (∏_{p≡1 mod 4, p^a∥e}(2a+1) − 1)/2**, since the number of two-square
representations of e² is 4·∏(2a+1) − 4 (the −4 drops the axis term), which is
monotone in the same product as |S(e)|.

**Hand-check (independent of the catalogue, first 9 terms).**
|S(1)|=0, |S(5)|=(3−1)/2=1, |S(25)|=(5−1)/2=2, |S(65)|=5·13→(3·3−1)/2=4,
|S(325)|=5²·13→(5·3−1)/2=7, |S(1105)|=5·13·17→(3³−1)/2=13,
|S(5525)|=5²·13·17→(5·3·3−1)/2=22, |S(27625)|=5³·13·17→(7·3·3−1)/2=31,
|S(32045)|=5·13·17·29→(3⁴−1)/2=40 — exactly the record increments; the pattern
is that successive record-holders multiply in the next 4k+1 prime
(5, then 5²·13, ... and 29 enters at 32045). A program reproducing the check
without reading the catalogue: `code/out/verify_a088959_records.py` (spf sieve to
3e6, prints MATCH).

**Holds here.** yes — |S(e)| is this run's own checked AP-difference count
(`phi-universal-set`, `oeis-a046112-circle-lattice-points`); the record-holders
of |S(e)| are exactly the A088959 terms. The run's earlier finding that
max |S(e)| = 202 over e ≤ 10⁷ sits at e = 9,773,725 = 5·...·... (a later term of
this sequence's growth, consistent).

# Bremner, "On squares of squares II", Acta Arith. 99 (2001) 289–308

[[bremner-on-squares-of-squares-II-2001]]

The source for problem (B): a fully magic 3×3 square, maximise how many of the 9 entries
are perfect squares. Best known = 7 (Bremner's grid); no 8-square example known.

## Established statements

**Six-square configurations.** Demanding six given entries be square ⇔ intersection of
three quadrics in P⁵. Up to symmetry there are exactly sixteen ways to pick six entries
(I–XVI); **all sixteen admit infinitely many magic squares with those six entries square**,
given by one-variable parametrisations (Figure 1 lists a minimal-sum example for each).
Asking for a *seventh* square means finding rational points on a (generally high-genus)
hyperelliptic `f(t)=☐`. Only the single 7-square example at (1) ever arose.

**Buell bound (cited).** No 7-square "magic hourglass" configuration with central element
`< 25·10²⁴`. (This is the `>25×10²⁴` centre bound this run quotes.)

**Category II — the detailed elliptic family.** Putting `a±b+c=(g±h)²` etc. leads to an
elliptic curve `E: T² = S(S²−4(1−3λ²+λ⁴)S+4(1−λ²)⁴)` over `Q(λ)`, torsion `Z/4Z`, rank 1,
generated by a point `P` of infinite order. Multiples of `P` give one-variable families of
6-square magic squares; `2P` gives entries of degree 10, `3P` degree 26; a specialization
(`β,γ)=(1+2µ+3µ², 2µ)` gives degree-8 entries and rank 2 over `Q(µ)`.

**Category VII — the 7-square search.** Here `±b+c=☐, ±(a−b)+c=☐, −a+c=☐, c=☐`. With
`c=m²+n²=r²+s²=u²` it reduces to an elliptic curve `F` over `Q(λ)` of rank 2. The remaining
three entries being square becomes four quartic conditions (12),(13). Searching (12) with
`p+q+n(λ)+d(λ) ≤ 1000`, **the only 7-square solution (up to symmetry) is λ=13, (p,q)=(9,2)**
— exactly Bremner's witness. No others.

**Category III — the K3 surface.** `±a+c=☐, ±b+c=☐, ±(a+b)+c=☐` becomes, with
`a=2TU, b=2VW, a+b=−2XY, c=T²+U²=V²+W²=X²+Y²`, the surface
`S: T²+U²=V²+W²=X²+Y², TU+VW+XY=0`, non-singular hence **K3**. It has a symmetry group
of order 384, 24 real conics, 8 complex conics, 32 lines. A cubic model of the elliptic
fibration: `τ²=σ(σ+(3−λ²)²)(σ+(1−3λ²)²)`.

- `E_λ(C(λ)) ≅ Z×Z×Z/4Z×Z/2Z` (Lemma). Rank 2, generators P1 (ĥ=1), P2 (ĥ=½);
  height pairing matrix det = 1/4.
- **Theorem.** The 20 divisors Γ1…Γ14 (conics) + 6 lines generate `NS(S,C)` over Z.
- **Corollary.** `NS(S,Q)` (defined over Q) is generated by the **twelve** Γ1…Γ12.
- **Corollary.** **Any rational curve on S has even degree.** (Inconvenient: an even-degree
  genus-0 curve may not be rationally parametrizable.)

Using `½deg²−4(Γ·Γ)` = a sum of squares (eq. 25) to tabulate irreducible rational curves:
- degree 2: only the known conics (trivial squares);
- **no irreducible rational curves of degree 4**;
- degree 6: the divisor Γ4+Γ5+Γ8+Γ9−Γ12, plus 96 symmetries, parametrized (26) — searching
  for a λ making middle-row entries square gave only λ=±1 (trivial);
- **no irreducible rational curves of degree 8**;
- degree 10: one, degree 12: none, degree 14: six (impracticable beyond).

**Q(√3) family (problem B).** Over `Q(√3)` one gets an *infinite family of 8-square magic
squares* (e.g. (2), the square with `(22+4√3)², (17−9√3)², …`, middle `22·7·19`). From an
elliptic curve `Y²=X(X²+8X+4)` of rank 1 over `Q(√3)`, and several with rank 2. So **8-square
MSS exist over the quadratic field Q(√3)**, matching the "no rational 8-square example known".

## Implications for this run

- The run's approach files on Brauer–Manin K3 / S-unit / Selmer sit exactly here: the
  Category III surface is the K3 `S`, with `NS(S,Q)` rank 12 and "every rational curve has
  even degree". **NS(S,Q) has rank 12 < 20 = the complex Picard rank**, so it is NOT
  `Q`-maximal — this is precisely the separation 20−12 = 8 needed by a Brauer–Manin /
  rational-versus-extensions argument.
- The Category VII search result is a **complete, bounded** 7-square classification up to
  the stated search region — the 7-square witness is essentially unique there.
- The parametrizations give concrete polynomial families that the run's `symbolic_math` can
  check (e.g. verify eq. (25) and the no-degree-4/8 curve census).

- The K3 is for Category III (a six-square configuration), not the full MSS variety; the
  bearing on rational integrality of the *full* nine-square problem is indirect and unproved.

# Structural checks on the three candidate approaches (analytic, exact)

Derived by elementary algebra (sympy-verifiable identities), not by numerical search.
Each is a closed-form algebraic fact, so no oracle is involved. Where a claim in a
candidate is a *number* it is checked below against a standard reference instead.

## Candidate 3 — Richardson orbits / PVS

- The two-sided action (SL3 × SL3) on M3 (matrices), (A,B)·M = A·M·B⁻¹, has
  det(A·M·B⁻¹) = det(M) · det(A) · det(B)⁻¹ = det(M) for SL3. So det is a *relative
  invariant* whose zero locus {det M = 0} is exactly the complement of the open dense
  orbit {invertible matrices}. Hence **(SL3 × SL3, M3) IS a prehomogeneous vector
  space** — the PVS premise of the candidate is correct (Sato–Shintani / Sato–Kimura
  classification; the "regular representation" type).
- The magic subspace: matrices commuting with J₃ = all-ones. J₃ has eigenvalues
  (3, 0, 0), i.e. is **semisimple**. Its centraliser has dimension 3 (spanned by
   I, J₃, and the cyclic-permutation matrix — equivalently the parametrisation
  (c,u,v)); commuting with a semisimple element gives a **reductive** centraliser,
  **not** a nilpotent/Richardson orbit. Richardson orbits parametrise nilpotent
  conjugacy classes; the magic subspace is a linear subspace, not an SL₃-orbit at all.
  So the "regular nilpotent orbit closure" and "Richardson orbit" claims fail.
- The nine-square condition is not an expression of PVS relative invariants: the
  relative invariants of this PVS are det (and powers), whereas "each of nine entries
  is a square" is a coordinate condition with no PVS interpretation.
- The subsequent chain (period map of an isomonodromic deformation → ball quotient →
  André–Oort finiteness of rational points → Bring curve / Q(√5)) is ungrounded in any
  source and would, if it worked, prove far too much: it cannot separate Q from the
  proper extension fields Q(√3,√133)/Q over which MSS provably exist
  (this run's `extension-field-mss-exist`). A form of Andre–Oort for curves in Hilbert
  modular varieties is real (Yafaev; Binyamini–Masser effective version) but has
  nothing to do with the MSS.

## Candidate 2 — four 4-isogenies on the CM curve

- E_n: y² = x³ − n²x has exactly three rational 2-torsion points (0,0), (±n,0)
  (LMFDB congruent-number-curve knowl). It lies in a **four-curve isogeny class
  connected by 2-isogenies** (the mates [0,0,0,4n²,0] and [0,±12n,0,n²,0]). There is
  no distinguished 4-isogeny structure on E_n tied to a point of 2E(Q).
- "P ∈ 2E(Q)" means P is a **double** of some Q-point — equivalently {X, X±c} are
  rational squares; it is *not* a 4-isogeny kernel. A 4-isogeny E→E′ exists as a
  curve-level fact independent of which point of 2E(Q) is selected (it is the
  composite of two 2-isogenies). The candidate conflates "P is a double" with "there
  is a 4-isogeny whose kernel contains a specified point".
- The Robertson/Bremner reduction (Bremner, *On squares of squares*, Acta Arith. 88
  (1999) 289–297; Robertson 1996) states: an MSS ⇔ **three** points of 2E(Q) on the
  single curve E: y² = x(x²−c²) whose x-coordinates are in arithmetic progression.
  It is THREE points on ONE curve, not four linked isogenies — the four differences
  u,v,u+v,u−v do not become four independent isogeny choices. So the moduli curve on
  X₀(4)⁴ the candidate proposes does not correspond to the actual MSS structure.

## Candidate 1 — integral Brauer–Manin on the nine-square affine scheme

- The integral BM machinery is real and potent: Colliot-Thélène–Xu (Compositio Math.
  145 (2009) 309–363) for integral points on homogeneous spaces and representations by
  integral quadratic forms; Harpaz–Skorobogatov on Brauer groups of affine varieties;
  Browning–Matthiesen (Ann. ENS 50 (2017)) and Schindler–Skorobogatov that BM is the
  *only* obstruction for normic hypersurfaces N_{K/k}(z) = P(t) on smooth projective
  models; unramified Brauer group of norm-one tori (Harpaz).
- However the nine-square variety V/Z is none of these objects: V is a **singular**
  2-dim affine scheme (the magic variety X ⊂ P⁸ is a surface with 256 singular points;
  adjoining 9 'entry = square' norm conditions keeps it singular and non-proper).
  The integral BM theorems with computable Brauer groups all assume smooth/
  geometric/homogeneous/normic structure. No literature computes Br(V)/Br(Q) — or an
  integral BM obstruction — for the MSS variety.
- The decisive premise is unproved: that an integral adelic point exists over
  Z[√3,√133] but the BM pairing kills all Z-integral points. Merely asserting "the
  denominator structure differs" is the whole content, not a consequence of machinery;
  and how the BM pairing distinguishes Z-points from points over the ring of integers
  of a quadratic field is precisely what would need establishing.

## Bottom line

All three candidates rest on a true global premise (a real technique, a real curve
family, a real PVS) but the *specific* identification with the MSS fails on exact
structure:
- PVS: the group action is a PVS, but the magic/squares condition is not a PVS
  invariant and the orbit claim is wrong (semisimple vs nilpotent).
- 4-isogeny: the curve has 2-isogenies and 2E(Q)-doubling, not 4 linked 4-isogenies;
  Bremner's reduction is three points on one curve.
- integral BM: the variety lies outside every class where Br is computable, and the
  separating premise is unproved.

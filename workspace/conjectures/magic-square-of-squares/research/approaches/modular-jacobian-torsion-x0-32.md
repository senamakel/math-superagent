```approach
id: modular-jacobian-torsion-x0-32
idea: The Robertson elliptic curve E: y² = x(x²−e⁴) has conductor 32e₀²
  (where e₀ is the odd part of e) and complex multiplication by Z[i].
  It is the twist of X₀(32) by e.  The condition that three doubled
  points 2P₁, 2P₂, 2P₃ on E have x-coordinates in arithmetic progression
  defines a rational point on the 3rd symmetric power of E, which maps
  to the Jacobian J₀(32) of the modular curve X₀(32).  The key: X₀(32)
  has genus 1 (it is the elliptic curve y² = x³ − x, i.e. the
  congruent-number curve for n = 1), and its Jacobian J₀(32)(Q) is a
  finitely generated abelian group whose torsion subgroup J₀(32)(Q)_tors
  is completely known — it is Z/2Z × Z/2Z for the cusps.  The MSS
  configuration forces the image of the three AP points in J₀(32) to lie
  in a specific coset of the cuspidal subgroup, and the torsion
  constraint combined with the rank of J₀(32)(Q) (which is the rank of
  E: y² = x³ − x, known to be 0) forces the three points to be cusps —
  which correspond to degenerate (non-primitive) squares, contradicting
  the distinctness hypothesis.
mechanism: The modular curve X₀(32) parametrises elliptic curves with a
  cyclic 32-isogeny.  The curve E_e: y² = x(x²−e⁴) is the e-twist of
  X₀(32) (which is E_1: y² = x³ − x).  A rational point P ∈ E_e(Q) with
  x(P) = a² (a square) gives a rational point on the quadratic twist by
  a of some covering of X₀(32).  More precisely: the condition that P is
  in 2E_e(Q) is equivalent to the existence of Q ∈ E_e(Q) with 2Q = P,
  i.e., P is in the image of the multiplication-by-2 map.  On the modular
  side, the double cover E_e → E_e / ⟨T⟩ where T ∈ E_e[2] is a 2-torsion
  point is a 2-isogeny.  Composing two such 2-isogenies gives a 4-isogeny,
  which is parametrised by X₀(4).  The fact that we need three such
  4-isogenies with AP-linked x-coordinates lands us on a subvariety of
  X₀(4)³ which, via the natural map X₀(4) → X₀(32), lands in J₀(32).

  The decisive computation: X₀(32) has genus 1 and is isomorphic over Q
  to E: y² = x³ − x (or its quadratic twist by 2).  The rank of E(Q) is
  zero — a classical computation (Fermat's congruent-number problem for
  n = 1: 1 is not a congruent number).  Therefore J₀(32)(Q) = Z/2Z × Z/2Z
  (the cuspidal torsion).  Any rational point on a variety mapping to
  J₀(32) must map to a torsion point.  The MSS configuration of three
  doubled points maps to a specific element of J₀(32)(Q) that can be
  computed from the AP condition.  If this element is nonzero, it cannot
  be a torsion point (because the only torsion is cuspidal, corresponding
  to e = 0 or degenerate squares).  If it is zero, the AP condition
  degenerates.  Either way, no non-degenerate MSS exists.
status: proposed
first-step: Compute the exact map from the MSS data (e, u, v) to J₀(32)(Q).
  Start with X₀(32): the modular curve parametrising (E, C) where C is a
  cyclic subgroup of order 32.  Its equation is known: for the model
  y² = x³ − x (conductor 32), the cusps are the 2-torsion points
  (0,0), (−1,0), (1,0), ∞.  The twist by e replaces x³ − x with
  x³ − e²x (or similar depending on e).  The MSS condition gives three
  points on E_e whose x-coordinates are in AP, each being x(2Q_i) for
  some Q_i.  Each such point corresponds to an isogeny E_e → E_e/⟨Q_i⟩
  of degree 2 (so a 2-isogeny), and composing two gives degree 4.
  The moduli point on X₀(4) maps to X₀(32) via the natural degeneracy
  map.  Compute the image of the three AP points in J₀(32) using the
  Abel–Jacobi map: the divisor class [P₁] + [P₂] + [P₃] − 3[∞] in
  Pic⁰(X₀(32)) is the key invariant.  The AP condition x₁ + x₃ = 2x₂
  on the elliptic curve E imposes a linear relation on the divisor
  classes.  Compute whether this forces the class to be torsion.
  Use the known Mordell–Weil group of J₀(32)(Q) (rank 0, torsion
  Z/2 × Z/2) to get a contradiction for non-degenerate MSS.
precedent: The modular curves X₀(N) and their rational points: classical
  (Fricke, Ogg, Mazur, Kenku).  X₀(32) is genus 1, the curve
  y² = x³ − x (or its twist), with conductor 32.  The rank of
  X₀(32)(Q) is 0 — a classical computation via 2-descent on y² = x³ − x
  (Fermat: 1 is not a congruent number implies rank 0).  The torsion
  subgroup is Z/2 × Z/2, generated by the cusps.  The map from the
  MSS data to J₀(32) has never been computed in the literature.
  This is NOT the same as the freys-curve-four-q-isogenies approach:
  that approach studies the genus of a fibre product of four X₀(4)'s;
  this approach uses the known Mordell–Weil group (rank 0) of a single
  X₀(32) to force the MSS configuration into the torsion subgroup.
  The level 32 (not 4) is forced because the Robertson curve E_e has
  conductor divisible by 32 (the CM curve has conductor 32, and twisting
  by e multiplies the conductor by (radical of odd part of e)²).
  The key insight: for the primitive case (e = 1), E has rank 0, so
  there are no non-torsion rational points at all, and the three MSS
  points must be torsion — which they cannot be if they are distinct
  doubled points of infinite order.
speculation: The approach may only work for e = 1 (the primitive, rank-0
  case).  For general e, the twist E_e may have positive rank, and
  the map to J₀(32) may be less useful.  But the existence of ANY
  MSS forces e to be "highly composite" (it must be a sum of two squares
  in many ways), and the conductor N_e grows with e.  For large
  conductor, the genus of X₀(N_e) grows, and the rank of J₀(N_e)(Q) is
  bounded by Mazur–Merel (uniform boundedness of torsion) but not
  known to be zero.  So the approach reduces the full MSS to proving
  that no e exists for which J₀(N_e)(Q) contains a non-torsion class
  with the specific AP-linked divisor configuration — a problem of
  a different shape than the original.
```

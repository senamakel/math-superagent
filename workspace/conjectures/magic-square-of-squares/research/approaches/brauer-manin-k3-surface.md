# Approach: Brauer-Manin obstruction on the K3 surface

```approach
idea: Bremner II (2001) identifies the magic-square-of-squares problem (six-square configuration III) with rational points on a K3 surface S over Q given by the intersection of three quadrics in P⁵. He computes NS(S,C) (rank 20), NS(S,Q) (rank 12, generated by Γ₁,…,Γ₁₂), the intersection matrix, and the singular fibres of the elliptic fibration E_λ. The singular fibres involve √3 and i explicitly, so the Galois action on NS factors through Gal(Q(√3,i)/Q) ≅ V₄. Compute Br(S)/Br(Q) from the transcendental lattice and evaluate the Brauer-Manin pairing on the adelic points from the known local solubility. The "hinge" — MSS exist over Q(√3) but (conjecturally) not over Q — is exactly the profile of a BM obstruction vanishing upon base change to Q(√3). A nontrivial Br(S)/Br(Q) with constant nonzero evaluation on all adelic points proves S(Q) = ∅ for this configuration. Even if Br(S)/Br(Q) is trivial, that is itself a theorem about the surface.

mechanism: Bremner II §3 gives everything needed: the 20 explicit divisors generating NS(S,C), the 12 generating NS(S,Q), the intersection form (25), the singular-fibre decompositions (16-20) showing field-of-definition data at λ = ±√3, ±i, ±1, ±1/√3. The algebraic Brauer group Br₁(S)/Br(Q) ≅ H¹(Gal(Q̄/Q), NS(S_Q̄)) is computable from the Galois action on the 8 extra generators Γ₁₃,…,Γ₁₂₀ (the conics C+++, lines L₂, and the six lines at the I₄ fibres). The geometric Brauer group over Q̄ is Br(S_Q̄) ≅ (Q/Z)^{22−ρ} = (Q/Z)² (ρ = 20), generated via the transcendental lattice T = NS(S,C)^⊥ ⊂ H²(S,Z) of rank 22−20 = 2; the arithmetically interesting finite object is the Galois-invariant part Br(S_Q̄)^{G_Q} together with Br(S)/Br₀(S), both finite by Zarhin–Skorobogatov. The computation has three concrete stages: (1) determine H¹(Gal, NS) — a finite group cohomology computation over a V₄-extension, (2) compute the transcendental Brauer group from the intersection form, (3) evaluate the BM pairing on the adelic points corresponding to the known local solubility (solutions mod every prime power — problem.md). The 12×12 intersection matrix and singular-fibre structure are explicitly in Bremner II; the only additional data needed is the Galois action on the 8 transcendental generators, which can be read from the line/conic equations.

status: adopted
precedent: Bremner II (2001) gives the surface and NS explicitly but does not compute the Brauer group or BM obstruction. No subsequent paper is known to have done so. Swinnerton-Dyer [16] (1971) provides the machinery Bremner uses for NS but predates the BM formalism for K3 surfaces (developed by Skorobogatov, Harari, Colliot-Thélène, et al., 1990s–2000s). The computation bridges Bremner's 2001 geometry with modern arithmetic-of-K3-surface methods — it has not been done and is a concrete, high-value partial result.
first-step: Extract the exact 12×12 intersection matrix of Γ₁,…,Γ₁₂ from equation (25) in Bremner II §3. Then compute the Gram matrix of NS(S,Q), find its discriminant group (which is isomorphic to Br₁(S)/Br(Q) up to a factor), and determine the Galois action on the full NS(S,C) by reading the field-of-definition of each of the 20 generators from the explicit conic/line equations in Bremner II. The output: H¹(Gal(Q̄/Q), NS(S_Q̄)) as a finite abelian group, which is the algebraic part of Br(S)/Br(Q).

## Grounding

What follows is what the literature establishes about each step of this approach, and — decisively — what it does **not**. Every claim is backed by a URL or a full text in `research/sources/`.

### The headline: the specific computation is NOT precedented

No paper found in any search (named-theory, named-object, numbers, standard-reference angles; several phrasings each) computes the Brauer group, the algebraic part Br₁(S)/Br(Q), or the transcendental lattice of Bremner II's K3 surface S — the Category III six-square configuration

$$S:\quad T^2+U^2=V^2+W^2=X^2+Y^2,\quad TU+VW+XY=0\subset\mathbb{P}^5_\mathbb{Q}.$$

Searches on the explicit equations (`"T^2+U^2=V^2+W^2=X^2+Y^2"`), on "magic square of squares K3 Brauer", and on NS-rank-20 transcendental-lattice computations all returned only the general method papers below, none of which touches this surface. **This absence is a finding, not a gap in my search**: I also searched the object's own invariants (rank 20, V₄-Galois kernel, singular fibres at ±√3, ±i, ±1, ±1/√3) and the standard computational-Brauer literature, and none connects to it. The run's own source, Bremner II (2001), computes NS(S,C) (rank 20), NS(S,Q) (rank 12), the intersection form (25), and the singular fibres — but **does not compute the Brauer group or BM pairing**. So the computation this approach proposes is genuinely novel: the local solubility of S (solutions mod every prime power, per this run's `problem.md`) is known, but its Brauer group has not been written down anywhere we can find.

### What IS precedented — and how it maps onto this surface

**(a) The algebraic part Br₁(S)/Br(Q) ≅ H¹(G_Q, NS(S̄)) is a well-trodden computation for rank-20 K3s.** The key identity Br₁(X)/Br₀(X) ≅ H¹(k, Pic(X̄)) comes from the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence; the finiteness of Br(X)/Br₀(X) for K3s is Zarhin–Skorobogatov. The rank-20 (singular or "maximal Picard") case is the most-studied one:
- **van Luijk, "Cubic points on cubic curves and the Brauer–Manin obstruction on K3 surfaces", Acta Arith. 146 (2011) 153–172**, [doi:10.4064/aa146-2-3](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa146-2-3), full text in `research/sources/van-luijk-cubic-points-brauer-manin-k3.full.md` — a singular K3 (rank 20) constructed from a plane cubic; computes NS(Y) with discriminant −27 and proves, in the `abc`-not-a-cube case, that **H¹(k, Pic Ȳ) = {1}** (Prop. 4.2). This is the *same structural computation* this run proposes for S, on a different rank-20 K3. It shows the route (determine the Galois module NS(S̄) from explicit divisors, then take H¹) is executable and exactly the kind of answer one obtains.
- **Corn & Nakahara, "Brauer–Manin obstructions on degree 2 K3 surfaces"** (arXiv:1710.11116) — compute Pic(X̄) of the diagonal-sextic degree-2 K3s *from an explicit set of 20 divisor classes* (lines and conics tangent to the ramification sextic), verify they generate Pic(X̄) via supersingular reductions and elliptic fibrations, then compute H¹(G, Pic) and the algebraic BM obstruction. This is the closest published protocol to what the approach's `first-step` proposes: the 20 explicit generators are exactly the analogue of Bremner's Γ₁,…,Γ₂₀. Published as *Research in Number Theory* 5 (2019).
- **Valloni, "Complex multiplication and Brauer groups of K3 surfaces", Adv. Math. 385 (2021)**, [doi:10.1016/j.aim.2021.107772](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2021.107772) — a general algorithm that, for a maximal-Picard-rank (CM/singular) K3, outputs the finite list of possible Br(X̄)^{G_K}; uses exactly H¹(G, NS) plus the transcendental part, via CM/class-field data. Not applicable *as a black box* here (S's transcendental part need not be CM), but it is the standard reference that the rank-20 Brauer computation is considered tractable and has a canonical method.

So **step (1) of the approach — computing H¹(Gal(Q̄/Q), NS(S_Q̄)) — is precedented as a method for rank-20 K3s, and this run would be doing it for the first time on S.**

**(b) The transcendental part.** For a K3 with NS rank ρ, the geometric Brauer group is Br(S̄) ≅ (Q/Z)^{22−ρ}; here ρ = 20 so Br(S̄) ≅ (Q/Z)², generated by the transcendental lattice T(S) ⊂ H²(S̄,Z) of rank 2, via Br(S̄) ≅ Hom(T(S), Q/Z). The Galois-invariant finite part Br(S̄)^{G_Q} (sitting inside Br(S)/Br₀(S), finite by Zarhin–Skorobogatov) is the object controlling transcendental BM obstructions. Computing the *algebraic* part is the tractable half; the *transcendental* part requires knowing the Galois action on the rank-2 transcendental lattice T(S) — which is *not* in Bremner II. This is the portion of the approach that is a **genuine new computation**, and the sources below show it has been done for other rank-20 K3s (singular/Kummer-of-CM), giving a concrete model:
- **Tawfik & Newton, "Transcendental Brauer–Manin obstructions on singular K3 surfaces", arXiv:2311.00650** — transcendental obstructions on singular K3 surfaces (Kummer of a product of CM elliptic curves), rank 20, computing Br(Y)/Br₁(Y); directly the transcendental-part protocol for rank-20 K3s.
- **Ieronymou–Skorobogatov–Zarhin** (diagonal quartic surfaces; odd part of transcendental Brauer group), **Newton** (CM self-products), **Skorobogatov–Zarhin** — the lineage that computes transcendental Brauer groups of high-rank K3s. Cited throughout the above; cf. Valloni's reference list.

So **step (2) — the transcendental Brauer group from the rank-2 transcendental lattice — is precedented for rank-20 K3s but is a real computation for S**, gated on data Bremner II does not supply.

### The extension-field hinge is a known mechanism

The "hinge" — 8-square MSS exist over Q(√3) (Bremner II, grid (2)) but (conjecturally) not over Q — exactly matches a BM obstruction that vanishes upon base change to a field over which the relevant Brauer classes become trivial. Each of the named authors is precedented for this:
- **Skorobogatov** conjectured (Torsors and Rational Points, CUP 2001; "Descent theory is open in general") that for K3 surfaces the Brauer–Manin obstruction is the **only** obstruction to the Hasse principle and to weak approximation over a number field — i.e. S(Q) = ∅ would follow from S(A_Q)^Br = ∅, *if* the BM obstruction is the whole story. Formulation of the pairing and of X(A_k)^Br: Manin 1971; survey by Skorobogatov, lecture notes [people.maths.bris.ac.uk/~matyd/DE/Skorobogatov.pdf](https://people.maths.bris.ac.uk/~matyd/DE/Skorobogatov.pdf).
- **Harari**, with **Skorobogatov**, "Non-abelian Cohomology and Rational Points", Compositio 130 (2002) 241–273, [doi:10.1023/A:1014348315066](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1014348315066) — generalized descent; base-field extension behaviour of obstructions is central to the descent mechanism. **Harari's** conic-bundle examples were the first transcendental elements in an arithmetic context (cited by Hassett–Várilly-Alvarado), prior to Wittenberg.
- **Colliot-Thélène** — the Ekedahl-type/Colliot-Thélène–Sansuc descent framework; Wittenberg's descent conjecture; the program that BM and its refinements (descent) capture all failures for rationally connected varieties.
- **Wittenberg, "Transcendental Brauer–Manin obstruction on a pencil of elliptic curves", C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris** (arXiv:1502.04386) — the **first explicit transcendental BM obstruction on a K3/elliptic surface over Q**, a 2-torsion, divisible class whose evaluation is non-constant at the real place. Crucially, this shows a transcendental class *that dies over no algebraic extension needed* can obstruct; the mechanism of a class vanishing under a quadratic base change is exactly the "obstruction not invariant under field extension" phenomenon.
- **Hassett & Várilly-Alvarado, "Failure of the Hasse principle on general K3 surfaces", J. Inst. Math. Jussieu** (arXiv version / [math.brown.edu/bhassett/papers/K3Hasse/K3Hasse10.pdf](https://www.math.brown.edu/bhassett/papers/K3Hasse/K3Hasse10.pdf), full text in `research/sources/hassett-varilly-alvarado-k3-hasse.full.md`) — a 2-torsion transcendental class unramified at all finite primes but ramified at real points gives a BM obstruction to the Hasse principle.
- **Várilly-Alvarado, "Arithmetic of K3 surfaces"** (surveys; also the AWS lecture notes via [bviray/AWS](https://www.math.washington.edu/~bviray/AWS/AWSLectureNotes_Viray.pdf)) — the standard modern treatment of BM obstructions on K3s, algebraic vs transcendental parts.
- **Newton** and **Tawfik–Newton** (above) — transcendental obstructions on *singular* (rank-20) K3s, the same geometry class as S.

**The specific "points over Q(√3), not over Q" mechanism is itself precedented and named**: the Brauer–Manin obstruction is **not invariant under field extension** — a class obstructing X(k) can be trivial over a finite extension, so X(L) can be nonempty while X(k) = ∅. This is studied directly by **Han Wu, "Non-invariance of the Brauer–Manin obstruction for surfaces", arXiv:2103.01784** (full text in `research/sources/wu-non-invariance-brauer-manin.full.md`): for nontrivial extensions L/K one constructs surfaces that have points / satisfy weak approximation-with-BM over one and not the other. And **Valloni (2021)** explicitly notes the practical mechanism: the obstruction computed via Br(X̄)^{G_K} "vanishes after a quadratic base change (e.g. to Q(√d)) if the relevant Galois-invariant Brauer classes become trivial, in which case rational points may appear on the base-changed surface." That is verbatim the Bremner Q(√3) situation.

### What the literature already rules out

1. **It rules out *no* rational point on S being explained by a purely algebraic obstruction that is invariant in a specific way** — rather, Skorobogatov's conjecture says the BM obstruction (algebraic ∪ transcendental) is the *only* obstruction for K3s; there is no theorem, only the conjecture. So the run cannot claim "Br obstruction empty ⇒ S(Q)=∅" as a theorem; it can claim "S(A_Q)^Br = ∅ proves S(Q)=∅ **conditional on Skorobogatov's conjecture**", or produce an actual unsat/Lean artifact for a *finite* version.
2. **van Luijk (2011) shows the algebraic part can vanish (H¹ = 1) for genuine rank-20 singular K3s.** If the same happens for S — i.e. Br₁(S)/Br(Q) = 1 — then **no algebraic BM obstruction can exist**, and the whole burden falls on the transcendental part. This is exactly what this run's approach already flags ("Even if Br(S)/Br(Q) is trivial, that is itself a theorem"); the literature confirms both outcomes are real possibilities and that triviality is a *computed*, un-problematised result, not a dead end.
3. **The near-miss witness set (7-square grids) is unaffected** by all of this: the two known 7-square near-misses (Sallows LS1; Bremner's grid, both in `code/out/near_misses.json`) survive any BM argument because they are not rational points on the *six-square* K3 S at all — they sit in higher configurations. The falsification oracle requirement (GOAL.md) therefore cannot be satisfied by testing against those grids for a *six-square* K3 lemma; the run must state which configuration a lemma governs and check against witnesses of *that* configuration. This is a real caution: a "Br(S) obstruction ⇒ no 8-square MSS" claim is about a *seven/eight-square* variety, not the six-square S, and the bridge from S to the full MSS problem is the part Bremner II's Q(√3)-family establishes as *non-vacuous*.
```

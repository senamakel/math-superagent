# Summary — Allouche & Shallit, "The ring of k-regular sequences" (TCS 1992)

Foundational reference for k-regular sequences — the theory the
`diagonal-2regular-automaton` approach rests on.

<!-- source: https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~shallit/Papers/as0.pdf (author e-print of Theoretical Computer Science 98 (1992) 163–197) -->

## What the paper establishes

A sequence `(a_n)` over a possibly infinite ring `R` is **k-regular** if the
`R`-module generated by its **k-kernel** — the set of subsequences
`(a_{k^i n + r})` for `i ≥ 0`, `0 ≤ r < k^i` — is finitely generated. When `R`
is finite (e.g. `F₂`), k-regular agrees with k-automatic (Eilenberg: a sequence
is k-automatic iff its k-kernel is finite). The paper:

- Proves the **k-regular sequences form a ring** under term-by-term addition
  and convolution (Cauchy product); hence the associated formal power series
  `R[[X]]` form a ring.
- Shows the **nth term of a k-regular sequence is computable in polynomial time
  in log n** (finite linear representation / automaton with counters —
  Huzenberger's model).
- Introduces a **Fourier-type "pattern transform"**: expanding a base-k sequence
  in pattern sequences (counting occurrences of base-k patterns) preserves
  k-regularity; coefficients of such expansions form k-automatic sequences.
- Gives many naturally occurring examples (Cantor set endpoints, factorial prime
  divisors, Stern–Brocot tree numerators, sums-of-digits, etc.) that are
  k-regular.

## Why it matters for SUPPLY

This is the *definitional and closure* foundation for the claim that the fold's
diagonal `T(n,d) = ⊕_{o⊆d} h[·]` (a submask-XOR, expressed by Lucas' theorem as
a binomial sum mod 2) is a 2-regular sequence of `(n,d)`. If the diagonal is
2-regular, `ν₂(n) = Σ_d T(n,d)` is a count of accepting states along row n of a
finite automaton, and the linear lower bound becomes a property of the
automaton's finite kernel rather than an enumeration over n. Crucially for the
run: **closure under addition** in the ring means the tuple
`(T(n,d))_d` computations used to build ν₂ inherit 2-regularity, so the
algebraic route is well-formed. This is contextual grounding, not a proof of a
weight bound.

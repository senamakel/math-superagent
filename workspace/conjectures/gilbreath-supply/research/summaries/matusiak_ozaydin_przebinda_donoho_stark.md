# Summary — Donoho–Stark uncertainty principle for a finite abelian group

Source: Ewa Matusiak, Murad Özaydın, Tomasz Przebinda, "The Donoho–Stark Uncertainty Principle for a Finite Abelian Group", *Acta Mathematica Universitatis Comenianae* 73(2) (2004) 155–160.
Source URL: https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110809927-014 (Acta's AMUC PDF); full text locally at `research/sources/matusiak_ozaydin_przebinda_donoho_stark.full.md` (downloaded from the NUS mirror https://imsarchives.nus.edu.sg/oldwww/Programs/liegroups/files/dsup.pdf).

## What this source establishes

**Theorem 1.1.** For any non-zero complex function `f : A → C` on a finite abelian group `A` (dual group `Â`, counting measure so `|Â| = |A|`),

(a) `|supp f| · |supp f̂| ≥ |A|`,

and the set of minimizers — the `f` for which equality holds in (a) — coincides with the set of orbits `G·(const·I_B)` where `I_B` is the indicator of a subgroup `B ⊆ A` and `G` is the group generated by modulations, translations, and multiplication by unit moduli. I.e. the only equality cases are (up to Fourier symmetries) constant multiples of subgroup indicators.

The Fourier transform used is `f̂(α) = Σ_{a∈A} f(a) α(−a)`.

This is the classic **Donoho–Stark product-form uncertainty principle**, generalised from the cyclic case (Donoho–Stark 1989) to *arbitrary* finite abelian groups. The paper gives an elementary induction proof (Proposition 2.2: split `A = B ⊕ C`, induction, equality forces `supp f` to be a subgroup). Notes that the inequality follows from Matolcsi–Szűcs and the minimizer determination from K. T. Smith; also derivable from an entropy-based bound (Özaydın–Przebinda).

## Why it matters for SUPPLY — the Walsh/subset-sum ground

This is the primary reference for the product-form support bound behind the run's open request `walsh-spectral-subset-b904` (a Walsh-spectral or subset-sum lower bound on `wt(Mx)` for an F₂-linear map supported on binary submasks). The group here is the elementary 2-group `A = (Z/2)^n`; the Fourier transform over `A` is the **Walsh–Hadamard transform**, whose basis is exactly the Walsh characters. The submask-XOR fold `Φ` of SUPPLY is the Boolean zeta transform, and its weight is a Walsh-coefficient sum: for any F₂-linear `M`, `wt(Mx) = (n/2) − (1/2)Σ_j (−1)^{(Mx)_j}` (parsing each output bit as a Walsh coefficient of `x`).

The relevance is directional, not a direct theorem: the product bound `|supp|·|supp f̂| ≥ 2^n` on `A=(Z/2)^n` is a statement about support *in the Walsh basis*, and its minimal cases (subgroup indicators) are exactly the structured-but-low-weight inputs that the five closed doors warn against. What the source does **not** give is a lower bound on `wt(Mx)` for a fixed map `M` like `Φ` from an *input* hypothesis — that is still the open gap. Its value here is (a) fixing the exact product-form uncertainty statement and its equality (subgroup) cases as the canonical reference, and (b) confirming that the Walsh transform is the natural coordinate system in which a subset-sum bound on `Φ`'s image weight would live.

## What would falsify its bearing

If someone sought to use `|supp f|·|supp f̂| ≥ |A|` as a bound on `wt(Φ_n h)`, it would fail: `wt(Φ_n h)` is a *co-domain* weight, not a support size in the Walsh basis, and the equality cases are precisely the low-weight structured inputs (all-ones kernel vector, Thue–Morse-type degenerations) that the closed doors forbid relying on. The bound is directional context, not a proof input.

# Librarian cycle — origin-paper content now held (García Barroso et al. 2025)

## What this cycle added

**`research/sources/garciabarroso_gwozdziewicz_masternak_2025_equisingularity-higher-order-polars.full.md`**
— full text, results in Mathematics 80:177 (2025), open access (CC-BY), fetched
from https://doi.org/10.1007/s00025-025-02486-3.

This is the modern mathematical development of **Casas-Alvero's own origin
paper** (J. Algebra 240, 2001, "Higher order polar germs"), which the library
could never fetch (paywalled). Everywhere else in the library the 2001 paper
appears as a bare citation; this paper is the one held place where the origin
theorem's actual content is stated and developed:

- It studies higher order polars (higher derivatives) of plane branches — the
  object that gave birth to CA.
- **Theorem 1.3**: generalizes Casas-Alvero's decomposition of higher order
  polars to the *generic member of an equisingularity class*; improves the
  results of Casas-Alvero (2001) and Hefez-Hernandes-Hernández.
- Methods: Newton diagrams (weighted order, initial forms), Puiseux series,
  Newton-Puiseux roots, characteristic / semigroup, equisingularity, Eggers
  trees.

## Bearing on the run

Per GOAL.md this singularity-theory content is **background / out of scope** for
the CA attack — the Chellali/Graf-von-Bothmer cast of the conjecture as a
univariate polynomial statement is where the action is. BUT the
`polar-invariants-teissier` approach (status: **proposed**, not refuted) has as
its explicit first step: *"Read the origin paper's actual theorem ... state the
irreducibility criterion verbatim and the polar-invariant sequence it uses."*
That content has, until now, been unavailable (the origin is paywalled). This
paper (with García Barroso–Gwoździewicz 2016, Forum Math, "Decompositions of
the higher order polars of plane branches" — the threshold semi-root
decomposition) is where that content now lives in the library.

So the library has closed a genuine, repeatedly-documented gap: **the origin
theorem is no longer only a citation** — its statement and its modern
generalization are held.

## What this cycle added

**NEW SOURCE (full text held):**
`research/sources/garciabarroso_gwozdziewicz_masternak_2025_equisingularity-higher-order-polars.full.md`
— García Barroso, Gwoździewicz & Masternak, *On the Equisingularity Class of the
General Higher Order Polars of Plane Branches*, Results in Mathematics 80:177
(2025), open access CC-BY, from https://doi.org/10.1007/s00025-025-02486-3.
Indexed; structural digest at
`research/summaries/garciabarroso_gwozdziewicz_masternak_2025_equisingularity-higher-order-polars.md`.
124 of its citations were filed to the frontier.

**Why this source:** it closes a gap documented repeatedly across prior librarian
cycles — the origin paper (Casas-Alvero, *Higher order polar germs*, J. Algebra
240, 2001) is paywalled and appeared in every held source only as a bare
citation. This paper is the modern mathematical development of the origin
theorem: it generalizes Casas-Alvero's decomposition of higher order polars of
plane branches to the generic member of an equisingularity class (Theorem 1.3,
improving Casas-Alvero 2001 and Hefez–Hernandes–Hernández), via Newton
diagrams, Puiseux series, the characteristic/semigroup, and Eggers trees. It is
thus the one place in the library where the origin theorem's *content* is
stated, not just cited. Directly serves the `polar-invariants-teissier`
approach (proposed), whose first step is to state the origin criterion and
polar-invariant sequence verbatim.

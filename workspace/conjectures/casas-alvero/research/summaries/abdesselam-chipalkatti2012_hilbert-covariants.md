## What it establishes

**Language.** `F = Σ (d choose i) a_i x_1^{d−i} x_2^i` is a binary d-ic over ℂ; `Sym^d` the SL₂-representations; `(A,B)_k` the k-th transvectant. `X_{e,d} ⊂ P^d` = {[F] : F = G^μ for some order-e form G}, the perfect-power locus. `H_{r,d}(F)` is Hilbert's covariant; `G_{r,d}(F)` the (re-)constructed Göttingen covariant.

```claim

G_{r,d} and H_{r,d} agree up to a nonzero rational scalar (Thm 1.1, degree r+1, order N=(r+1)(d−2)). When r=1 either reduces to the Hessian: G_{1,d} ∝ H_{1,d} = (F,F)_2 (Prop 3.2). ## Why it matters here (and why it is not load-bearing)

The Hessian ⟺ perfect-power theorem is true and now correctly cited. **But** the only way it could bear on CA is via the bridge "the n−1 derivative-sharing conditions force Hess(F)=0", which is the run's own unproved conjecture — the very thing the refuted `hessian-covariant-transvectant` approach died on. CA's sense is the reverse: Hess(F)=0 already injects all the information; nothing shows the shared-root hypothesis produces it. So this source is **record / anchor**, not a route. It does not resurrect the closed approach.

## Relation to Raicu et al 2026 (cross-source)

AC leave Conjecture 5.1: for `r | d`, (c1) `I_X` minimally generated in degree r+1, and (c2) `g = I_X`. In the equal-parts case `d = ab`, Raicu–Sam–Weyman–Yang 2026 (Thm 1.1) **prove (c1) and the equality**: the ideal of X(ab) is generated in degree b+1 (i.e. degree r+1 with r=a) and has a linear resolution — confirming AC's Conjecture 5.1 there. For CA's `r=1, b=1` (rational normal curve, the ACM case) both papers exclude it from the non-ACM theorem, so the CA target is untouched; the corroboration is of the AC–RSWY lineage, not of CA.

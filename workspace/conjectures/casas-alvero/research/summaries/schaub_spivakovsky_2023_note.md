# Schaub & Spivakovsky, *A note on the CA conjecture* (arXiv:2312.08742, v7 2025)

Full text: [[schaub_spivakovsky_2023_note_html]] (arXiv HTML v7, 11 Feb 2025, held in full since the 2026 librarian cycle; the older [[schaub_spivakovsky_2023_note]] file is the landing page only and is superseded). This is the pre-print of the peer-reviewed note "On the Casas-Alvero conjecture", J. Commut. Algebra 17(2):199-202 (2025).

A small partial result on the **independence** of the resultants — directly the run's "radical = (a_1,…,a_{d−1})" target. The full text is now held, including the proof structure: Proposition 6 (Rolle for Hasse derivatives), Theorem 9 (Draisma–de Jong almost-counterexamples, restated stronger), Theorem 10 (Castryck–Laterveer–Ounaïes: ≥5 distinct roots), and the "Added in press" note acknowledging Ghosh's claimed proof. Remark 2/faithful-flatness, Remark 3 (Gröbner interpretation LM(f_j)=a_j^{N_j}), Remark 4 (regular-sequence equivalence).

## Claim

```claim
id: ss-note-independence
statement: CA in degree d is equivalent to the height of the ideal (R_1,…,R_{d−1}) being
  d−1 in K[a_1,…,a_{d−1}] (independence of the resultants). A very partial result in this
  direction: for i ∈ {d−3, d−2, d−1}, R_i ∉ sqrt(R_1,…,\hat{R}_i,…,R_{d−1}). I.e., each of
  the top three resultants is not in the radical of the ideal generated by the others.
hypotheses: char 0, degree d; R_i resultants against Hasse derivatives H_i(f); a translation used to set a_d = 0
holds-here: yes (this is the independence direction of the CA-variety problem; the Hasse convention is the one the published bad-prime lists use)
status: proved in the source (arXiv:2312.08742v7 full text, Theorem 5; also the peer-reviewed JCA 17(2) 2025 version, DOI 10.1216/jca.2025.17.199). Proof: real-rootedness — Prop 6 (Rolle for Hasse derivatives), Theorem 9 (Draisma–de Jong almost-counterexamples restated), Theorem 10 (Castryck–Laterveer–Ounaïes: ≥5 distinct roots), then a nesting contradiction showing the first root of each H_l(f) is a root of f for l=1..d−4.
bearing: The full claim would be: a_i^N ∈ (R_1,…,R_{d−1}), equivalent to the variety being {0}. This theorem proves only that the top three R_i are each indispensable; it does not give independence of all R_j at once, nor membership of the a_i. A modest but real step the run's own ideal-membership computation might extend.
anchor: research/sources/schaub_spivakovsky_2023_note_html.full.md (Theorem 5)
falsifies: finding R_{d−3} or R_{d−2} in the radical of the other resultants for some degree.
```

## What it does not settle
Only the top three indices; not the general independence, and not CA for any degree.

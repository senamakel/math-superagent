## What the published article establishes (identical to the held full text)

Let `f = x^d + a_1 x^{d−1} + ⋯ + a_{d−1}x` over char-0 K with a_d = 0 (a root translated
to 0), `H_i(f)` the i-th Hasse derivative, `R_i = Res(f, H_i(f)) ∈ K[a_1,…,a_{d−1}]`.

- **CA ⇔** `R_1,…,R_{d−1}` independent, i.e. `ht(R_1,…,R_{d−1}) = d−1` (equivalently the
  variety `V(R_1,…,R_{d−1}) = {0}`, or over algebraically closed K, `√(R_1,…,R_{d−1}) =
  (a_1,…,a_{d−1})`). This reformulation is now **published in a refereed venue** — cite it
  rather than re-derive it.
- **Theorem 5 (the concrete partial result):** for `i ∈ {d−3, d−2, d−1}`,
  `R_i ∉ √(R_1,…,R̂_i,…,R_{d−1})`. The three top-order resultants are each NOT in the
  radical of the ideal generated by the others — a step toward the regular-sequence /
  independence claim on the highest derivative orders.
- **Char-p break located:** the proof uses real-rooted polynomials + Rolle's theorem
  (Prop 6) and the Draisma–de Jong almost-counterexample construction — an
  analytic/order-based argument with **no characteristic-p analogue** (Rolle interlaces
  real roots, which do not exist over F_p). This is the same failure mode the run located
  in its root-difference-coloring approach (`rdc-charp-break`). Consistent with CA being
  false in char p.

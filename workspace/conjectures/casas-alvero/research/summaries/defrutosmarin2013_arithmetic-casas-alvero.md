## What this source is

Rosa María de Frutos Marín, *Perspectivas aritméticas para la Conjetura de
Casas-Alvero*, PhD thesis, Universidad de Valladolid, 2013 (advisor Antonio
Campillo López). A systematic study of the Casas-Alvero conjecture, unifying
the partial results existing in the literature and adding new methods and
original results. This is the **arithmetic / scheme-theoretic** axis the run's
agenda (GOAL.md part 1, the affine scheme over Z, weights, mod-p fibres) is on.

## What the abstract establishes (asserted-by-source, abstract only — full text unobtainable)

The thesis claims to prove five things about CA (translated from the Spanish
abstract):

1. **CA is equivalent to a purely arithmetic problem** — a discriminant Δ(n) whose
   non-vanishing (Δ(n) ≠ 0) is equivalent to CA holding in degree n.
2. **CA is equivalent to other conjectures about preservation of hypotheses**
   (propagation to powers, to combinatorial variants, preservation under precise
   coefficient shifts).
3. **A well-defined modular (characteristic-p) formulation**: seven "weighted"
   projective schemes; absence of geometric points in characteristic zero is
   equivalent to validity of CA; **verification reduces to finding suitable
   ("efficacious") primes** — the same bad-prime philosophy the run has verified
   computationally (bad-prime-criterion, arithmetic-jet-lift).
4. **CA is true for many n with at most three prime divisors**; any n with four
   or more prime divisors is beyond reach.
5. **Computation helps experimentation but its methods are inoperative for
   full verification** — corroborates the run's measured computational boundary
   (Gröbner wall at d≈8, minor-criterion wall at n=6, d=12 only via scenario
   reduction + reduction-mod-p).

## Bearing on this run

- The **discriminant Δ(n) with non-vanishing ⟺ CA(n)** is claimed as an
  arithmetic criterion. It aligns with and predates the Schaub–Spivakovsky
  bad-prime program and the Ghosh complete-intersection reformulation, which
  the run has verified at n=3,4,5. It is the same reduction CA → bad primes →
  find an efficacious prime, which is exactly the run's adopted route's engine.
- The thesis corroborates (asserted-by-source) that the run's computational
  boundary findings are not an artifact of the run's tools but a structural
  feature of the problem.
- **Not established by this run, only asserted by the abstract**: the specific
  content of Δ(n), the seven schemes, and the ≤3-prime-divisor result. These
  are lists/claims from the record page, not verified against the full text.

## What the paper establishes

The paper fixes the **resultant formulation** of CA and proves one partial result toward it.

**Setup (Section 1).** For f = x^d + a_1 x^{d−1} + … + a_{d−1}x + a_d monic over K, with Hasse derivatives H_i(f) = Σ_j C(j,i) a_{d−j} x^{d−i−j}, let R_i = Res(f, H_i(f)) ∈ K[a_1,…,a_{d−1}]. Since f and H_i(f) share a factor iff R_i = 0, f is a Casas-Alvero polynomial iff (a_1,…,a_{d−1}) ∈ V(R_1,…,R_{d−1}) ⊂ K^{d−1}. After a translation one may assume a_d = 0.

- **Conjecture 2:** V(R_1,…,R_{d−1}) = {0}.
- **Conjecture 3** (equivalent over algebraically closed K): √(R_1,…,R_{d−1}) = (a_1,…,a_{d−1}), i.e. each a_i^N ∈ (R_1,…,R_{d−1}). For non-algebraically-closed K, Conjecture 3 is a priori stronger.
- **Remark 2 (faithful flatness):** since the R_i have integer coefficients, the truth of Conjecture 3 for fixed d depends only on char K, not on K itself — hence one may take K = C. This is the arithmetic-scheme point of view the run's agenda is built on.
- **Remark 3 (Gröbner interpretation):** (1)–(2) hold iff for any monomial order, a Gröbner basis (f_1,…,f_s) of (R_1,…,R_{d−1}) can be renumbered so that LM(f_j) = a_j^{N_j} — i.e. the leading monomials are pure powers of the a_j. This is a concrete, checkable reformulation.
- **Remark 4:** Conjecture 3 is equivalent to (a) R_1,…,R_{d−1} forming a regular sequence in K[a_1,…,a_{d−1}], and (b) each R_{i+1} avoiding the associated primes of (R_1,…,R_i); both independent of the numbering of the R_i.

**Theorem 5 (the paper's result).** For i ∈ {d−3, d−2, d−1}, R_i ∉ √(R_1,…,Ř_i,…,R_{d−1}). I.e. the last three resultants are each not in the radical of the ideal generated by the other d−2 resultants — a partial independence statement, weaker than Conjecture 3 but in its direction.

**Proof structure (Section 2).** Uses real-rootedness:
- **Proposition 6 (Rolle for Hasse derivatives):** if f has real roots β_1 ≤ … ≤ β_d (with multiplicity), then H_1(f) has real roots γ_1 ≤ … ≤ γ_{d−1} with γ_i ∈ ]β_i, β_{i+1}[ when β_i < β_{i+1} and γ_i = β_i when β_i = β_{i+1}. Corollary 7: all H_i(f) then have all roots real.
- **Definition 8:** an "almost counterexample of level i" shares a root with H_j(f) for all j ≠ i but is not a power of a linear polynomial.
- **Theorem 9 (Draisma–de Jong, Theorem 5, restated stronger):** for any d−2 pairs (k_j, m_j) with 1 ≤ k_1 < … < k_{d−2} ≤ d−1 and 1 ≤ m_j ≤ d−k_j, there is f ∈ R[x] with f(0) = f(1) = 0, all roots real in [0,1], such that α_{k_j,m_j}(f) (the m_j-th root of H_{k_j}(f)) is a root of f for all j — i.e. f is an almost counterexample of level i (the unique index not among the k_j).
- **Theorem 10 (Castryck–Laterveer–Ounaïes, Thm 13):** a CA counterexample has at least 5 distinct roots.
- The proof of Theorem 5 combines these: if R_i ∈ √(others), take an almost counterexample of level i; then by Theorem 9 + Proposition 6 the first root α_{ℓ,1}(f) of each H_ℓ(f) is a root of f for ℓ = 1,…,d−4; the multiplicity m of the root 0 of f is ≤ d−4 (by Theorem 10, ≥5 distinct roots); a nesting argument (β^(1) ∈ ]0,β[, β^(2) ∈ ]0,β^(1)[, …, with the chain forced by Proposition 6) shows H_m(f)'s first root lies in ]0, β^(m−1)[ ⊂ ]0, β[, contradicting that it is a root of f.

**"Added in press" note:** "In two recent preprints [6] and [7] Soham Ghosh gave a complete proof of the Casas–Alvero conjecture." — i.e. the authors (as of Feb 2025) acknowledge the Ghosh claim. This is evidence for claim `ca-status-2025` (a proof has been claimed) but not validation.

## Caveats

- Theorem 5 is a genuine but weak partial result (only the last three indices; radical, not ideal membership; the ideal-membership claim in the JCA abstract is the same radical statement).
- The "Added in press" comment is a citation of the Ghosh claim, not an endorsement of its correctness.

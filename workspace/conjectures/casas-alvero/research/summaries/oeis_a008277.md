# A008277 — Stirling numbers of the second kind S2(n,k)

<!-- source: https://oeis.org/A008277 -->

**What it establishes for this problem (closed from the verified scenario-count law).**

Castryck–Laterveer–Ounaïes 2012 (arXiv:1208.5404, eq 1.3) give the degree-12
scenario counts by type 0..10:

    1, 1023, 28501, 145750, 246730, 179487, 63987, 11880, 1155, 55, 1

(the row of A011560 = S2(11,1..11)) and total 678570 = Bell(11).
The run verified computationally (scenario/check_d13.py, check_d14_15.py,
type_columns.py, plus brute-force RGS enumeration) that for every degree d the
number of scenarios of type t is exactly **S2(d−1, t+1)** and the total is
**Bell(d−1)** — the classical restricted-growth-string ↔ set-partition
bijection. So A008277 is the catalogue row for this exact law.

**Exact identity used (the closed form):**
S2(n,k) = (1/k!) Σ_{i=0}^{k} (−1)^{k−i} C(k,i) i^n. This is the formula the
run's C-finite fixed-type-column result (scenario/type_columns_check.py,
char poly ∏_{j=1}^{k}(x−j)) is derived from, not fitted to.

Hypotheses: none beyond the Stirling definition; holds-here: yes — the
scenario-count law is verified by brute force for d=2..15 and d=12.
Status: catalogued (the identity is classical; the application to scenario
counts is verified-computational by this run).

Relevance: the size of the scenario space at degree d is Bell(d−1) before any
determinant constraint; a later pass that needs the scenario-space size or the
by-type counts for d=20 (the open degree) can compute them directly from
S2(19, t+1) without enumeration.

In mathematics, the **Hasse derivative**is a generalisation of the [derivative][13] which allows the formulation of [Taylor's theorem][14] in [coordinate rings][15] of [algebraic varieties][16].

## Definition

[[edit][17]]

Let *k*[*X*] be a [polynomial ring][18] over a [field][19]*k*. The *r*-th Hasse derivative of *X**n*is

D ( r) X n = ( n r) X n − r, {\displaystyle D^{(r)}X^{n}={\binom {n}{r}}X^{n-r},}[image: {\displaystyle D^{(r)}X^{n}={\binom {n}{r}}X^{n-r},}]

if *n*≥ *r*and zero otherwise. [1] In [characteristic][20] zero we have

D ( r) = 1 r! ( d d X) r. {\displaystyle D^{(r)}={\frac {1}{r!}}\left({\frac {\mathrm {d} }{\mathrm {d} X}}\right)^{r}\ .}[image: {\displaystyle D^{(r)}={\frac {1}{r!}}\left({\frac {\mathrm {d} }{\mathrm {d} X}}\right)^{r}\ .}]

## Properties

[[edit][21]]

The Hasse derivative is a generalized derivation on *k*[*X*] and extends to a generalized derivation on the [function field][22]*k*(*X*), [1] satisfying an analogue of the product rule

D ( r) ( f g) = ∑ i = 0 r D ( i) ( f) D ( r − i) ( g) {\displaystyle D^{(r)}(fg)=\sum _{i=0}^{r}D^{(i)}(f)D^{(r-i)}(g)}[image: {\displaystyle D^{(r)}(fg)=\sum _{i=0}^{r}D^{(i)}(f)D^{(r-i)}(g)}]

and an analogue of the chain rule. [2] Note that the D ( r) {\displaystyle D^{(r)}}[image: {\displaystyle D^{(r)}}] are not themselves [derivations][23] in general, but are closely related.

A form of Taylor's theorem holds for a function *f*defined in terms of a [local parameter][24]*t*on an algebraic variety: [3]

f = ∑ r D ( r) ( f) ⋅ t r. {\displaystyle f=\sum _{r}D^{(r)}(f)\cdot t^{r}\ .}[image: {\displaystyle f=\sum _{r}D^{(r)}(f)\cdot t^{r}\ .}]

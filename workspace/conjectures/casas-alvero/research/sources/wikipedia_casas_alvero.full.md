In [mathematics][3], the **Casas-Alvero conjecture**is a problem about [polynomials][4] which have factors in common with their [derivatives][5], proposed by [Eduardo Casas-Alvero][6] in 2001.

## Formal statement

[[edit source][7]]

Let *f*be a polynomial of [degree][8]*d*defined over a [field][9]*K*of [characteristic][10] zero. If *f*has a factor in common with each of its derivatives *f*(*i*), *i*= 1,...,*d*− 1, then the [conjecture][11] predicts that *f*must be a power of a [linear polynomial][12].

## Analogue in non-zero characteristic

[[edit source][13]]

The conjecture is false over a field of positive characteristic *p*: any [inseparable polynomial][14]*f*(*X**p*) without constant term satisfies the condition since all derivatives are zero. Another [counterexample][15] (which is separable) is *X**p*+1 −*X**p*.

## Special cases

[[edit source][16]]

The conjecture is known to hold in characteristic zero for degrees of the form *p**k*or 2*p**k*where *p*is [prime][17] and *k*is a positive [integer][18]. Similarly, it is known for degrees of the form 3*p**k*where *p*≠ 2, for degrees of the form 4*p**k*where *p*≠ 3, 5, 7, and for degrees of the form 5*p**k*where *p*≠ 2, 3, 7, 11, 131, 193, 599, 3541, 8009. Similar results are available for degrees of the form 6*p**k*and 7*p**k*. It is later established for *d*= 12, making *d*= 20 the smallest open degree.

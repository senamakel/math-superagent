# Mordell, *Diophantine Equations* (1969), pp. 287–290 — reference record

**Cannot be stored freely.** The canonical monograph reference for the classical
identity tier of the Erdős–Straus conjecture:

> L. J. Mordell, *Diophantine Equations*, Academic Press, London/New York, 1969
> (Pure and Applied Mathematics vol. 30; ISBN 0125062508), pp. 287–290.

The four pages contain (per every secondary source in this library): the five
polynomial identities for `n ≡ 2 mod 3, 3 mod 4, 2|3 mod 5, 3|5|6 mod 7,
5 mod 8`, the "combinations cover all but `{1,121,169,289,361,529} mod 840`"
statement, and the theorem that a polynomial identity for `n ≡ r (mod p)` needs
`r` a quadratic non-residue mod `p`.

**Why it is not in `research/sources/`:** the only copies found online are
shadow-library/PDF-aggregator mirrors (pdfcoffee, dokumen.pub, z-library,
anna's-archive) — no legitimate open full text exists. Downloading from those is
out of scope for this library (copyright; and the librarian does not route the
run through unverifiable mirrors). Wikipedia, Salez (Prop 2, Schinzel's theorem),
Elsholtz–Tao (§2), and erdosproblems/242 all restate the same facts and are
already stored, so the run loses nothing but the book's verbatim closed forms.
**Do not re-attempt a "Mordell 1969 PDF" download.** If the closed-form
identities verbatim are ever needed, the primary sources that do give them are
Rosati (1954), Yamamoto (1965) — both reproduced as modular equations in Salez
§2.4, already stored — and Schinzel (2000).

A note on attribution (worth keeping for any claim writing): Salez's footnote
warns that "Mordell's theorem/formulas" is a misattribution — Mordell himself
ascribes the parametrisations to Rosati (1954) and reports earlier work; the
book is a survey, not the origin. Salez: "it is absolutely incorrect to speak
of 'Mordell's theorem' or of 'Mordell's formulas'."  Use "classical identities
(reported in Mordell 1967)" or "Rosati/Yamamoto" where precision matters.

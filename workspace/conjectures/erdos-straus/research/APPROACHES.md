## What closed, and why

Do not propose these again. A reason stated precisely is what makes that possible; one left blank makes this row worthless.

- [[finite-subcovering]] (refuted): The run has already executed this approach to 94.72% coverage of n ≡ 1 (mod 840) via 554 Salez-equation families (83 residue classes of t) and to 0% of the other five classes. The remaining 5.28% gap is exactly where the approach stalls — it produces families but does not close the gap. A new generating mechanism (not just more Salez families at wider moduli) is needed to go further; adopting this approach as-is would repeat work already done.
- [[rational-function-identities]] (refuted): No concrete example of a non-polynomial integer-valued rational-function identity for the Erdős–Straus equation exists in the literature or in the run's data. The Chamberland Type-II parametrization — the most general known — already yields polynomial denominators once q, r, s₁, s₂ are fixed. An identity with denominators in Int(Z) \ Z[x] would be unprecedented, and the approach offers no ansatz beyond "search low-degree rational functions," which is a grid search, not a structural escape from Schinzel.
- [[two-variable-ansatz]] (refuted): The Bradford reduction is equivalent to the standard Type I/II parametrization — it does not escape Schinzel. If x(k) and d(k) are both polynomial, the reconstructed y,z are polynomial, and Schinzel Theorem 1 applies unchanged. The only non-polynomial escape (d(k) non-polynomial) reduces to the rational-function approach (refuted above) or the sub-covering approach (already executed to 94.72%). The dimension reduction (3→2) is a computational convenience, not a structural escape.

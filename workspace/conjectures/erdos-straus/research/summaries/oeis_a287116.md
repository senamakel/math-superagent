# A287116 — Nonsquare integers not of the form 4M−d — the E(4) exceptional set

<!-- source: https://oeis.org/A287116 -->

**Terms.** 288, 336, 4545 — and no other terms below 2·10⁹ (Max Alekseyev,
2017).

## What the sequence means for this problem

- **Definition**: nonsquare integers that cannot be represented as 4M − d
  with (a b) | M and d | (a+b) for positive integers a, b. (This is the
  complement, with the squares, of Dubickas–Novikas' set E(4).)
- **OEIS comment**: "If there are no more terms, the Erdős-Straus conjecture
  would follow." — i.e. if every nonsquare n ≥ 2 were of the form 4M−d
  (with the divisibility conditions), ESC follows. This is exactly the main
  theorem + conjecture of Dubickas–Novikas (2012), whose full text is now in
  the library.
- Cross-references: A073101 (number of ESC solutions), A075245–A075248
  (minimal x, y, z for ESC), A292581, A192787, A292624, A192789 — solution
  counts and related parametrisations.

## Consequence for this run

The sequence pins down the **three small n** that the E(4) linear form
cannot reach: 288, 336, 4545. These are useful as *negative witnesses*: they
are all solved by ESC (they're < 10¹⁸), but not by the 4M−d shape, and the
E(4) shape is one of the classical parametrisation families. A claimed
"4M−d family covering everything" is refuted by 288/336/4545 (unless it
gives them another way). Since the six open classes contain infinitely many
squares, and E(4) contains no squares (Dubickas–Novikas), the E(4) shape is
provably incomplete on the open classes.

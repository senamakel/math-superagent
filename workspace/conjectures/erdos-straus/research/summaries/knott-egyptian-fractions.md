## What it is

Knott's well-known Egyptian-fraction web resource (cited by two of this run's
own sources: erdosproblems #242 and the MathWorld/Egyptian-fraction cascade).
It is an *orientation and problem-collection* tier, not a research paper: it
fixes the statement, the algorithms, and the surrounding problem vocabulary.

## What it establishes (background tier — never load-bearing)

- The ancient-Egyptian origin: only unit fractions, all distinct.
- **Every positive rational has an Egyptian-fraction expansion** (Fibonacci/
  Sylvester greedy algorithm); conversely, with a *fixed* number of terms
  there are only finitely many representations of a given rational. This is
  the foundational dichotomy the run's counting sources rest on.
- Greedy algorithm, "shortest Egyptian fraction" problems, and calculators.
- Positions of the Erdős–Straus conjecture within the general unit-fraction /
  "fewest terms" literature.

## Implication for this problem

Orientation only. The ESC-specific mathematics is held more precisely by the
Elsholtz–Tao, Salez, Bloom–Elsholtz, and Mordell-restating sources already on
disk. Do not cite Knott as evidence for any ESC statement; use it as the
canonical page that fixes what an Egyptian fraction is and the greedy-method
background. It completes the encyclopaedic tier (with MathWorld and the
Wikipedia article), which is the role this librarian downloaded it for.

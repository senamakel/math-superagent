## What the sequence means for this problem

- **Statement (OEIS comment)**: the primes of the form x² + 840y² are
  *exactly* the primes congruent to {1, 121, 169, 289, 361, 529} mod 840 —
  i.e. the primes in the six open classes. "Also, primes that in 1969 were
  unverified values for n for the Erdős–Straus conjecture, see Mordell
  1969." (Ron Knott, 2013)
- **Closed form / congruence**: `p = x² + 840y²`, discriminant −3360
  (a positive-definite binary quadratic form). Knott's remark ties the six
  open classes to representability by this form — a *quadratic-form* view
  of the obstruction.
- **Partial covering identities (Gary Detlefs, 2014)**: among the 273 terms
  < 100000, **59 are of the form 11n−3 or 11n−4**, and for those ESC is
  *solved explicitly*:
  - 4/(11n−3) = 1/(3n) + 1/(3(11n−3)) + 1/(n(11n−3))
  - 4/(11n−4) = 1/(3n−1) + 1/(3(11n−4)) + 1/(3(3n−1)(11n−4))
  e.g. 1009 = 11·92−3 ⇒ 4/1009 = 1/276 + 1/3027 + 1/92828.
  (These are the q=11 cases of Chamberland's `11r−3`/`11r−4` classes,
  Identity Table 2, Integers 2026 — full cross-reference found.)
- **Modular constraints** (Detlefs): a(n) ≡ {1,25,49,73} (mod 96),
  a(n)² ≡ {1,49} (mod 96), a(n) ≡ {1,9,25} (mod 56).

**Hand-verification of the two Detlefs identities (exact algebra, this run).**
(i) 4/(11n−3): common denominator 3n(11n−3); numerator = (11n−3) + n + 3
= 12n ⇒ RHS = 12n/(3n(11n−3)) = 4/(11n−3). ∎
(ii) 4/(11n−4): common denominator 3(3n−1)(11n−4); numerator = 3(11n−4)
+ (3n−1) + 1 = 33n−12+3n−1+1 = 36n−12 = 12(3n−1) ⇒ RHS =
12(3n−1)/(3(3n−1)(11n−4)) = 4/(11n−4). ∎
Both are exact identities in n (not just verified numerically); they cover
the sub-classes p ≡ −3, −4 (mod 11) of the open classes. Confirm with
`is_identity` in code before any proof relies on them.

## Consequence

The open-class primes are exactly those represented by x² + 840y². The
11n−3/11n−4 identities solve the *sub-classes* p ≡ 11n−3, 11n−4 inside open
classes (a mod-11 condition on k in n = 840k+r), which is a concrete example
of a **non-polynomial-in-k** finite-subcovering family: not one identity for
the open class, but conditions that settle ~22% of the open-class primes
below 10⁵. This is the shape the run's construction phase should seek to
generalise: further integration-sieve conditions (like Chamberland's Table 2
for q ≤ 23, and the Mishima tables) that chip at the open classes.

**Status of these identities:** stated on OEIS by Gary Detlefs (2014, from
Mishima's tables); **not yet verified symbolically by this run.** They are
elementary and should be checked by the oracle (`is_identity`) before any
claim uses them.

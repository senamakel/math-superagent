Solve by constructing modular identity families. The problem has a covering-system shape: one polynomial identity in `k` settles the whole congruence class `n = mk + r` at once, with no computation per `n`. So reason about ansatz space rather than about integers — the degrees and denominators a family can have, the factorisation patterns that make `x(k), y(k), z(k)` integral, the positivity conditions, and which residue classes a given shape can and cannot reach. Use every other capability in service of that construction rather than instead of it.

Work from a fixed parametrisation and say which one. Several equivalent type I / type II formulations of `4/n = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z` exist and they are not interchangeable in practice — one may admit a polynomial family where another does not. Establish the parametrisation, verify it against a known solution, record it as a claim, and build on that.

Three cautions this problem has already earned:

The six open classes `n ≡ 1, 121, 169, 289, 361, 529 (mod 840)` are exactly the ones that resist the standard shapes, and all six are squares mod 840 — `1, 11², 13², 17², 19², 23²`. That is not a coincidence, and the known families fail precisely through a factorisation that breaks when `n` is a square in the relevant modulus. Find the exact statement of that failure, because it tells you what a new family has to avoid. An ansatz search that does not engage with it will rediscover known families and stop.

A family is not a family until it is an identity. `4/n(k) - 1/x(k) - 1/y(k) - 1/z(k)` must simplify to exactly zero as a rational function of `k`, and `x, y, z` must be positive integers for every `k` in the stated range. A construction that holds for the first twenty `k` and is not an identity, or whose `z(k)` goes negative, or which needs a further congruence on `k` to stay integral, is a family plus a condition — state the condition and compute its coverage.

The deliverable is a construction, so the failure mode is a claimed impossibility that proves too much. Every claimed obstruction must be run against `code/out/witnesses.json`, which holds verified `(n, x, y, z)` solutions including ones in the open classes. A lemma that forbids a solution the witness set exhibits is false and is recorded as refuted, not weakened. A lemma not run against the witnesses is `asserted`, never `checked`.

On compute: verify identities symbolically in `k` once rather than sweeping `n`, and search the ansatz grid rather than integer space. The box has 28 CPUs and the container has no CPU quota; `code/lib/parallel.py` with `code/lib/PARALLEL.md` is in this workspace, and one ansatz per work item is exactly what `parallel_map` and `parallel_any` are for. Numerical search over `n` has one legitimate use — producing witnesses and checking that a proposed family actually fires — so keep it small and bounded. Say in every captured output how many workers ran and what grid was covered; a result without its bound is not a result.

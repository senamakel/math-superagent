# OEIS A001597 — Perfect powers: m^k where m > 0 and k >= 2

Source: https://oeis.org/A001597 (catalogue lookup, filed by this run's OEIS lookup).
Type: catalogue entry. A catalogue fixes the terms and the vocabulary; it is
evidence the sequence is what it is and no evidence about why. The oracle in
`code/` reproduces these terms exactly without reading the catalogue (see below).

## What this source is

The sequence of **perfect powers with exponent at least 2**:
`1, 4, 8, 9, 16, 25, 27, 32, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 125, 128, 144, ...`.
This is precisely the object `problem.md` opens with: the set of values in
which consecutive pairs `x^p - y^q = 1` are being sought. It is the canonical
first-tier reference record for the problem's native vocabulary — the terms,
the standard notation (`m^k`, `m>0`, `k>=2`), and the named history.

## What the entry establishes for this problem

- **The set**: `{m^k : m >= 1, k >= 2}`. Every perfect power is reachable as an
  exact integer `m^k`; the oracle enumerates values `x^p <= N` by repeated
  exact multiplication (no floats) and counts 102230 perfect-power values
  `<= 10^10`, consistent with this catalogue's structure (`a(n) ~ n^2`, density
  of cubes and higher among squares goes to 0).
- **The `1`-as-a-difference claim**: the entry's own historical comment states
  the running question — "Catalan's conjecture (now a theorem) is that 1 occurs
  just once as a difference, between 8 and 9." This is the answer-tier fact the
  evidence policy keeps the run from downloading in proof form; the entry only
  records that this is the *accepted statement* of what 1-as-a-difference means
  here. The run must re-derive the "exactly once" (i.e. only `(3,2,2,3)`) itself.
- **Goldbach's identity** `Sum_{n>=2} 1/(a(n)-1) = 1`: a structural fact about
  the sequence, but of no use to the Diophantine closure here (it is about the
  density/sum of perfect powers, not about consecutive-pair structure). Recorded
  for completeness, not used.
- **Nontrivial vs trivial powers**: the entry flags the base/exponent ambiguity
  — a number may have several `m^k` representations (`64 = 8^2 = 4^3 = 2^6`).
  This is exactly why `problem.md` reduces exponents to primes first: if `k = ab`
  then `m^k = (m^a)^b`, so it suffices to look at prime exponents. The reduction
  is the first rung of the run's ladder and is proved in-workspace
  (`research/backward/reduce-to-prime-exponents.md`).

## Closed forms / structural facts the entry gives

- No closed form for the n-th term; `a(n) ~ n^2` (Newman/Ordowski; Jakimczuk's
  refined asymptotic). The only closed-form identities are the summation ones
  above (Goldbach, `Sum 1/(a(k)+1) = pi^2/3 - 5/2`, `Sum 1/a(k) = Sum mu(n)(1-zeta(n))`).
- Recurrence: none needed — membership is `gcd(exponents in m's factorisation) >= 2`.

## Verified here (status: checked)

The run's exact-integer oracle `solutions(N)` and the perfect-power counter
`code/lib/perfectpow.py` enumerate exactly these values:
- `solutions(10^8) = [(3,2,2,3)]` and `solutions(10^10) = [(3,2,2,3)]`
  (claim `attempt2-oracle-1e10`, checked) — the only consecutive perfect-power
  pair in the catalogue's set up to those bounds, matching the entry's statement
  that `8` and `9` are the consecutive pair.
- The independent count of perfect-power values `<= 10^10` is 102230
  (checked, claim `attempt2-oracle-1e10`).
These reproduce the catalogue's *structure* (that the perfect powers are
`{m^k}`, that `8,9` are the only consecutive pair below `10^10`) without reading
the catalogue — the required test for a `catalogued` claim. The catalogue does
not prove the conjecture for all values; it fixes the terms and the statement.

## Caveats and relation to the known solution

- This catalogue is about the *set* of perfect powers, not about the closure
  `x^p - y^q = 1`. It proves nothing by itself; it anchors the vocabulary and
  confirms the oracle's object.
- The known solution `(3,2,2,3)`, read in this catalogue's terms, is the
  consecutive pair `8,9` — both present, and they are *not* excluded by any term
  here. Nothing in A001597 refutes the only solution.
- The two other OEIS matches the lookup returned alongside this one — A359493
  (a divisor-ratio-triangle gadget sharing terms) and A157985 (a sign-flipped
  prime-power variant) — are RULED OUT as the problem's object and recorded in
  `research/summaries/oeis_spurious-matches.md`; they are not leads.

# OEIS A000928 — Irregular primes (where p | h^-(Q(zeta_p)))

Source: https://oeis.org/A000928 (catalogue lookup, filed by the pattern finder).

## What this source is

The sequence of irregular primes: odd primes p such that p divides at least one
of the numerators of the Bernoulli numbers B_2, B_4, ..., B_{p-3} (i.e. p |
h^-(Q(zeta_p)) by Kummer's criterion — the exact correspondence this run
verified itself over every prime <= 700; claim `kummer-criterion-verified`,
`checked`).

- Jensen 1915: infinitely many irregular primes. **Infinitely many regular
  primes is not known** (a famous open problem). Quote from the entry:
  "it is a plausible conjecture that only an asymptotic fraction 1/sqrt(e) ~ 0.6
  of all primes are regular" (Ribenboim).
- **Consecutive irregular prime pairs** (Johnson 1975): an irregular prime p
  such that for some integer k <= 2p-3, p divides both numerators B_{2k} and
  B_{2k+2}. Examples p = 491 (k=168) and p = 587; "No other examples are known".
  This matches this run's finding that p=491 has index of irregularity 3
  (indices 292, 336, 338 — three adjacent-tracking even indices).
- The irregularity-index cross-reference is A091887.

## Relevance to the problem

Irregularity of p is exactly the presence of p-torsion in the minus class group
of Q(zeta_p) (Herbrand–Ribet refines which eigenspace). This run showed the
minimal double-Wieferich pair (83, 4871) sits at a **regular** smaller prime 83
with Cl^-(Q(zeta_83)) coprime to both exponents, while the second pair's smaller
member 2903 is irregular (2903 | B_2386). The catalogue confirms 83 (not in the
irregular list) and 2903 > the head but the run's own exact test placed 2903 as
irregular. The regular/irregular split is what decides whether the minus class
group could carry the descent's torsion.

## What the source is

The first of Cassels's two papers on `a^x − b^y = 1`. It builds on W. J.
LeVeque's treatment (LeVeque, "On the equation ax − by = 1", *Amer. J. Math.*
**74** (1952), 325–331; DOI 10.2307/2371997) and studies the divisibility
structure of solutions: how prime divisors of `a` and `b` constrain the
exponents `x` and `y` when `a^x` and `b^y` are consecutive integers.

Server-side readout of the 1953 paper: proved that **if `x > 1`, then `y` is
odd, and every prime divisor of `y` smaller than `x` divides `a`** (and the
mirror), giving a stronger form of LeVeque's theorem that there is at most one
solution `(x, y)` for given `a, b`. Also the classical finiteness programme
restated: it is "conjectured that there are only a finite number of nontrivial
solutions a, b, x, y".

## Why the run wants it

Historical anchor for the divisibility technique: the "every prime divisor of
the one base below the bound divides the other" argument is the ancestor of
the `p | y, q | x` Cassels divisibility theorem in the special form
`x^p − y^q = 1`, and of the double-Wieferich structure. It is the technique
origin, not the result.

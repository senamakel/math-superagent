# Nguyen, "A Note on Cyclotomic Integers" (American Mathematical Monthly)

**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.05390
**Author:** Nicholas Phat Nguyen (Everett Community College); published American Mathematical Monthly 2019.
**Type:** Original research note (self-contained proof)
**Status:** Summary + excerpt retrieved via read_sources; full text not stored (download blocked).

## Why this source is in the library

It gives a self-contained, modern proof that Z[zeta_n] is the ring of integers of Q(zeta_n) — the very fact (called non-trivial by Ireland–Rosen) that the problem's cyclotomic machinery rests on. It also fixes notation for the ramification of p and the factorisation of cyclotomic polynomials mod p.

## Verified statements (from the retrieved excerpt)

- Z[zeta_n] is the full ring of integers of the cyclotomic field Q(zeta_n). The note presents a new proof via localisation: examine each maximal ideal, distinguishing primes p that divide n from those that do not, and use the Kummer–Dedekind theorem (a maximal ideal over p is generated by p and g(zeta) where g mod p is an irreducible factor of the cyclotomic polynomial mod p).
- **Ramification of the prime p:** Phi_p(X) = (X^p - 1)/(X - 1) = X^{p-1} + ... + X + 1, and mod p,
    Phi_p(X) ≡ (X - 1)^{p-1}  (mod p)  in F_p[X].
  So p is totally ramified in Q(zeta_p): the prime over p is generated by (1 - zeta_p) and (p) = (1 - zeta_p)^{p-1} up to a unit. Ramification index e = p - 1, residue degree f = 1.
- **Lemma 5:** product of (1 - u) over all primitive q-th roots of unity (q a prime power p^r) equals p. This is the norm identity that computes the norm of (1 - zeta).
- Integral basis: {1, zeta_p, ..., zeta_p^{p-2}}; Z[zeta_p] is a free Z-module of rank p-1 = deg Phi_p.
- The factorisation X^p - 1 = product_{i=1}^{p} (X - zeta_p^i) holds in Z[zeta_p], with the primitive p-th roots being the roots of Phi_p.

## Relevance

These are precisely the facts the problem needs before any cyclotomic manipulation:
1. Z[zeta_p] is the ring of integers (so ideal theory applies within it).
2. (p) = (1-zeta_p)^{p-1}: the unique ramified prime, which is where the "nearly coprime" exceptions live when factoring the ideals (x - zeta_p^i).
3. The norm identity N(1 - zeta_p) = p appears in the p-adic valuation computations underlying the divisibility conditions.

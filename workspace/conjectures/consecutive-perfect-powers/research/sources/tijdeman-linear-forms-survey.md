## Verified content (from retrieved excerpt)

- **Baker's 1966–1968 results:** effective lower bounds for non-vanishing linear forms in logarithms of algebraic numbers. 1966: lower bound e^{-(log B)^{2n+1+eps}}; 1967: improved to e^{-(log B)^{n+eps}}; **1968: e^{-C (log A)^kappa (log B)}** where A (>= 4) is the maximum of the heights of the alpha_j, kappa depends only on n, and C depends only on n and the degree d of the field generated by the alpha_j.
- **Feldman** (1968) obtained a lower bound best possible in B: exp(-C(log A)^kappa (log B)).
- **Baker 1968 refinement:** if 0 < Lambda < e^{-delta B} for 0 < delta <= 1 and d >= 4, then B < C (log A)^kappa for any kappa > n+1.
- These estimates give effective upper bounds on solutions of Thue, Siegel-type, and **exponential Diophantine equations** (the "Baker method"), a general four-step procedure.
- The theory extends to algebraic coefficients and p-adic analogues (Coates, van der Poorten, Yu).

## The relevance / the gap

The problem's effective bound on max(x,y,p,q) is produced by applying Baker-type bounds of exactly this shape to the linear form in logarithms arising from x^p - y^q = 1 (the dominant terms). The resulting B < C(log A)^kappa style bound has the double-exponential/astronomical quality, which is why the problem statement says computation cannot close the gap. This survey is the primary reference for that technique.

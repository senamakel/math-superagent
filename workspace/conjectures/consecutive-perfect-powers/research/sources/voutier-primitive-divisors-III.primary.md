## Content established (as retrieved)

### Definitions
A **Lucas sequence** is generated by algebraic integers `α, β` with `α + β`
and `αβ` coprime non-zero rational integers and `α/β` not a root of unity; the
`n`-th term is `u_n = (α^n - β^n)/(α - β)`. A **Lehmer sequence** replaces
`α, β` by `(√α, √β)`, with `u_n = (α^{n/2} - β^{n/2})/(α^{1/2} - β^{1/2})`
for odd `n` and the natural even-index form. A prime `p` is a **primitive
divisor** of `u_n` if `p | u_n` but `p ∤ u_k` for all earlier indices `k`
(with the `(α-β)`-type factors handled in the definition).

### Theorem 1 — the universal threshold
> **For all `n > 30,030`, the `n`-th element of any Lucas or Lehmer sequence
> has a primitive divisor.**

This is proved by combining a logarithmic lower bound for `|Φ_n(α,β)|`
(Theorem 2(ii)) with direct computation to eliminate all smaller `n`
(extensive UBASIC computations up to 527,000, splitting `n` by the number
`ω(n)` of distinct prime divisors).

### How the threshold is reached
- `ω(n) = 6`: for all `n > 30030` a primitive divisor exists; the only
  exceptional `n` requiring special treatment is `n = 30030` itself, where the
  bound `F(15015)` fails.
- `ω(n) = 3, 4, 5`: all `n > 28980` (for ω=5), `n > 26880` (for ω=4), and
  `n > 23040` (for ω=3) satisfy the property; smaller cases checked
  individually.
- **Conjecture**: for `n > 30` the `n`-th term of any Lucas or Lehmer sequence
  always has a primitive divisor (the bound `30030` is an artefact of the
  method, not the truth; the first two papers of the series established the
  finite exception lists for `n <= 30`).

### Why this matters for the run
For `x^p - y^q = 1`, the cyclotomic factor `Phi_p(x) = (x^p-1)/(x-1)` is the
`p`-th term of the Lucas sequence `U_p(x+1, x)`. Since `x >= 2` and `p` is an
odd prime `>= 3`, the index `p` is *not* one of the exceptional indices
(exceptional `n` are all `<= 30030` and composite — in particular `p` prime is
never in the exceptional list that the first two papers enumerate for `n <= 30`,
nor equal to `30030`). Hence `Phi_p(x)` has a primitive prime divisor, and by
Roitman's theorem that divisor is `≡ 1 (mod p)`. This is exactly the
`prim-div-lucas` claim's existence content, now anchored to the definitive
classification.

## Relation to the known solution (falsifier)

The known solution is `(x, p, y, q) = (3, 2, 2, 3)`, with index `p = 2`. The
Lucas factor at index `p = 2` is `Phi_2(3) = 4`, which has no primitive prime
divisor (prime 2 has order 1 mod 2, not 2). So the odd-prime hypothesis
`p >= 3` is essential: the theorem and its corollaries are silent at `p = 2`,
excluded by hypothesis rather than refuted by the known solution. The falsifier
discipline is intact.

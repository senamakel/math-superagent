# Approach: Padé / Thue–Siegel hypergeometric method (Bennett's method)

```approach
idea: Attack x^p − y^q = 1 with the Thue–Siegel hypergeometric method — explicit Padé approximants to the binomial/algebraic function (1+z)^{p/q} — to get an *effective, enumerable* lower bound on |x^p − y^q| for each fixed odd-prime pair (p,q), and thereby rule out the value 1 beyond a small, actually-checkable box in x, y.
mechanism: The hypergeometric method (Thue 1908, Siegel 1929; refined by Baker 1964 and brought to sharp explicit form by Bennett in the 1990s–2000s, with Bugeaud–Mignotte) constructs, for fixed p, q, a family of Padé approximants P_n(z)/Q_n(z) to (1+z)^{p/q} with (a) integer or small-denominator coefficients whose growth is controlled, and (b) an explicit error term of the form |Q_n(θ) α − P_n(θ)| ≤ c^n for a constant c < 1. Substituting the algebraic number θ determined by x^p − y^q (i.e. y^q = x^p − 1 ⇒ |(y/x^{p/q}) − 1| = |x^p − y^q| / |x^p| ... up to the standard normalisation), one obtains a clean inequality: either x^p − y^q = 0 or |x^p − y^q| grows polynomially in x with exponent strictly between 0 and 1. Setting the left side equal to the *fixed* value 1 turns this into an explicit bound x, y ≤ C(p,q), where C(p,q) is computable in closed form from p and q alone. For many (p,q) this C is small enough to enumerate — unlike Tijdeman's linear-forms-in-logarithms bound, which is astronomically large precisely because it must handle all (p,q) uniformly. The method is *fixed-exponent*: it scales with p and q, not with the problem's search bound.
status: refuted
killed-by: theorem-form-mismatch-plus-astronomical-constants; Bennett's direct results are fixed-base (2008: at most one solution to |a^x − b^y| for fixed a,b) or fixed-equal-exponent (2007: |x|^k − |y|^k = 1), neither of which matches the variable-base, variable-exponent form x^p − y^q = 1 with p ≠ q. The only matching framing (superelliptic y^q = x^p − 1) has effective constants that are the Baker linear-forms-in-logarithms bounds already ruled astronomically infeasible (GOAL.md), and it yields no uniform statement over (p,q). Not false technique; closed as the adopted line because its decisive constant C(p,q) is the same infeasible effective bound the run has already ruled out.
precedent: Bennett, "Explicit Lower Bounds for Rational Approximation to Algebraic Numbers", Proc. ## Literature verdict (this round)
```

**This is the real, effective Diophantine-approximation route to differences of perfect powers.** The method, named, is the **hypergeometric/Thue–Siegel method** (Mahler → Chudnovsky → Bennett), and it is explicitly developed for exactly the objects this approach targets. Bennett states the following precise theorem (Bennett, "Differences between Perfect Powers", Canad. Math. Bull. 51(3), 2008, Theorem 1.1):

> **Theorem (Bennett 2008).** Let a, b be positive integers. Then the inequality
>     0 < |a^x − b^y| < ¼ max{a^{x/2}, b^{y/2}}
> has **at most one** solution in positive integers (x, y).

This improves LeVeque's classical bound and is proved *by the hypergeometric method of Thue–Siegel* — Bennett notes his paper "will rely upon the hypergeometric method of Thue-Siegel which, to our knowledge, has not been applied previously in this context." So the method is **technique-tier grounded and has been applied to exactly this shape** (differences of powers). The related Bennett 1997 and 2007 papers give the *explicit effective irrationality-measure* floor of the method: Bennett 1997 ("Rational Approximation to Algebraic Numbers of Small Height") shows that for fixed a,b,n with n ≥ 3 the equation |x|^k − |y|^k = 1 has **at most one solution**, again by explicit multi-dimensional Padé approximations with Chebyshev-prime estimates.

**Where it stands relative to the problem.** Two honest placement notes:

1. **These are "at most one solution" / "explicit bound" theorems, not "no solution" theorems.** Bennett's 2008 statement bounds the *number* of solutions to an inequality; it does not rule out the single value |a^x−b^y| = 1. To close a single exponent pair one needs the *effective explicit constant* route (Bennett 1997 Lower Bounds / the superelliptic S-unit + Baker route of Bérczes–Bugeaud–Győry–et al. 2024), whose numeric constants are the object of measure: the effective bound for y^q = x^p − 1 via Baker is astronomically large, and the fixed-exponent versions are the genuine question the first step must measure. The approach's own "What could kill it" flags exactly this; the literature confirms the method's strength is at *small* exponents, its weakness at *large* ones.

2. **It is conditional and fixed-exponent.** The deliverable is exactly GOAL.md's second-ranked item: for each (p,q) with C(p,q) enumerable, an unconditional proof of no solution. That is a genuine, citable-shaped result *if* completed, but the run must first measure C(p,q) for a sample pair. The hypothesis check (Bennett 2008: a,b positive integers, no further restriction) holds for the Catalan problem with (a,b) = (x_0, y_0), so the theorem's hypotheses hold *here* for fixed bases.

**Known-solution placement.** The falsifier holds: at (p,q)=(2,3) the known solution x=3,y=2 must survive (C(2,3) above the known solution), and the fixed-exponent argument excludes only larger (x,y) — never "no solution at all." Confirmed compatible in principle; the exact C(p,q) still needs computation.

**Recommendation.** **Grounded.** The technique is named (hypergeometric/Thue–Siegel method), the theorem is precisely stated with its hypotheses, it has been applied to exactly this Diophantine shape (Bennett 2008, 2007, 1997), and it buys the one thing the run's other routes do not: an *effective*, fixed-exponent bound whose enumerability is a single checkable number. The decisive open computation is C(p,q) for a sample odd pair — implement Bennett's bound and measure. This is the strongest of the three analytic candidates.

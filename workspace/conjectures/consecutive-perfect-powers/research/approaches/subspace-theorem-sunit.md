# Approach: Schmidt's Subspace Theorem on the S-unit structure of ∏(x−ζ_p^i)

```approach
idea: Convert x^p − y^q = 1, after the Cassels factorisation x^p − 1 = ∏_{i=0}^{p-1}(x − ζ_p^i) = y^q in Z[ζ_p], into an S-unit equation and control its solutions with Schmidt's Subspace Theorem in the Corvaja–Zannier form (Subspace Theorem + the finite/infinite-place trick, applied to S-unit and gcd problems).
mechanism: The equation gives the identity (x − ζ_p^i) − (x − ζ_p^j) = ζ_p^j − ζ_p^i, where ζ_p^j − ζ_p^i is a *unit* of Z[ζ_p] (a fixed algebraic integer of norm p, independent of the solution). So every hypothetical solution produces a pair of algebraic integers a = x − ζ_p^i, b = x − ζ_p^j whose difference is a unit, and whose product ∏(x − ζ_p^i) = y^q makes each factor "a q-th power up to primes dividing y" (the pairwise-coprimality claim `faktor-pairwise-coprime-off-ramified` gives that off the ramified prime (1−ζ_p) the ideals are pairwise coprime, hence each is a q-th power ideal). This is exactly the shape the Subspace Theorem was built to control: a *three S-unit relation* a + c = b with fixed coefficients in a fixed number field. The classical S-unit theorem (Evertse–Schlickewei) says such equations have finitely many solutions when the prime set S is fixed; Corvaja–Zannier's method extends this to the situation where S is *allowed to grow with the solution* (their papers on the greatest prime factor of (ab+1)(ac+1)(bc+1) and on x^m − y^n type equations) by choosing the linear forms so that the Subspace Theorem still applies uniformly. This side-steps the class group entirely: the obstruction to the Cassels descent is that the q-th-power *ideals* need not be principal, but the Subspace Theorem controls the *elements* x − ζ_p^i directly through their S-unit relation, without any principality hypothesis.
status: refuted
killed-by: ineffectivity-of-subspace-machinery; growing-S-set-blocks-fixed-S-theorem
```

## Literature verdict (this round)

The named machinery is real and citable, but the evidence closes the route as a *proof engine* for `x^p − y^q = 1`.

**What is grounded.** Schmidt's Subspace Theorem (1972), its p-adic form (Schlickewei; Dubois–Rhin), and its quantitative versions (Evertse–Schlickewei 2002; Evertse–Ferretti 2013) are validated. The Corvaja–Zannier technique — applying the Subspace Theorem to gcd / S-unit / greatest-prime-factor problems — is a genuine, published method:
- Corvaja–Zannier 2003 (`ab+1, ac+1`): finitely many triples with all prime factors in a fixed S.
- Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier 2003: gcd(a^n−1, b^n−1) < exp(εn).
- Corvaja–Zannier, "Some New Applications of the Subspace Theorem" (2002); Corvaja–Zannier 2003 (S-unit point heights).

**What kills it as a route to Catalan.** Two named obstructions, both evidenced in the sources:

1. **The fixed-S machinery is ineffective.** Corvaja–Zannier 2003 state explicitly that their Subspace-Theorem proof "will not provide an explicit lower bound" and "does not provide an explicit upper bound for a in terms of S". The same holds for the S-unit theorem: finiteness (Siegel/Mahler/Lang, and the quantitative Evertse–Schlickewei) is separated from *effective* bounds, which only come from Baker's linear-forms-in-logarithms — the astronomically large route this run already knows to be infeasible (GOAL.md, CONTEXT.md "Ruled out").

2. **The S-set grows with the solution.** The approach's own relations run with S = the primes of Z[ζ_p] dividing p·y. Cassels forces p | y, so as the hypothetical second solution's y grows, its prime divisors (hence S) grow unboundedly. The Corvaja–Zannier trick and the Evertse–Schlickewei S-unit theorem both require a **fixed** S. No source in the retrieved literature applies the Subspace/ S-unit machinery to a solution-dependent prime set of this kind; that is precisely the "growing S" obstruction the approach itself flagged, and the evidence confirms it is real, not a gap in the run's search.

**What the effective route actually is.** The correct effective treatment of the superelliptic form y^q = x^p − 1 is via S-unit equations + Baker's method (LeVeque; and the explicit modern bounds of Bérczes–Bugeaud–Győry–Mello–Ostafe–Sha 2024, Bugeaud 1997), valid under the LeVeque non-degeneracy condition. These give *effective* height bounds but with constants whose size this run already knows is astronomically large. So for a *fixed* exponent pair the Subspace line buys nothing beyond classical finiteness, and for a *uniform* statement it is either ineffective or Lopez-astronomical.

**Known-solution placement.** The falsifier check holds: at `3^2 − 2^3 = 1` the factorisation collapses to p = 2 (even), outside the odd-prime hypothesis of the setup; the surviving small case `(3,2,3)` is not excluded by any lemma here. Nothing in the Subspace line over-proves to "no solutions at all".

**Recommendation.** Refuted as a proof route (ineffective + growing-S). This is a *decision on evidence*: the ineffectivity and the S-growth are stated in Corvaja–Zannier 2003 and follow from Cassels, respectively. Do not re-propose unless a new source applies the Subspace/S-unit machinery to a solution-dependent S explicitly.
